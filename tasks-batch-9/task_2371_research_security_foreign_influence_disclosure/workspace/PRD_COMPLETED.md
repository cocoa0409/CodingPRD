# Research Security Disclosure API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A university research security office sits at the intersection of two pressures that pull in opposite directions. Sponsors — the National Institutes of Health, the National Science Foundation, the Department of Defense, the Department of Energy and NASA — increasingly require that every foreign appointment, talent-programme membership, in-kind resource and outside support arrangement held by a funded investigator be disclosed, reconciled against what that investigator wrote on their current-and-pending support page, and managed under a documented plan when a conflict of commitment appears. Faculty, meanwhile, experience disclosure as a paperwork tax whose rules change per agency and whose consequences are invisible until an award is held up or a subpoena arrives. The office in the middle has historically run this work on spreadsheets, calendar reminders and institutional memory.

This document specifies the API for the system that replaces those spreadsheets. It is a single system of record for investigators, sponsored projects and their personnel rosters, the foreign organizations that appear in disclosures and subawards, the disclosure cycle itself, agency consistency checks, foreign travel and device security, controlled research area access, restricted-entity screening of collaborators, training compliance, and the investigation and sponsor notification of undisclosed relationships. Every one of these concerns feeds a small number of derived judgements — an investigator's research-security risk score and posture, a project's activation readiness and controlled-area posture — and the value of the system lies almost entirely in keeping those derived judgements correct and immediate as the underlying facts change.

Two design commitments follow from that and run through the whole specification. First, derived state is never lazily reconciled: every write that could change a score, a posture, a headcount or a blocker list recomputes the affected records before it returns, and cascades that recompute through the chain of dependants. Second, the office is a segregated-duties organisation: the officer who takes a disclosure under review may not issue its management plan, and the officer who substantiates an incident may not be the person who transmits the notification to the sponsor. The API enforces both mechanically rather than by policy memo. All resources are addressed as flat top-level collections at the service root; only personnel rosters and disclosure items are created beneath their parent.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts and cross-cutting principles that hold for every endpoint in the system. Where a rule here conflicts with an intuitive reading of a workflow in §3, the rule here governs.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user is acting. If either header is absent, empty or whitespace-only, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs; both header values are trimmed of surrounding whitespace before the role is matched and before `X-User-ID` is compared to anything or stored. The system recognises exactly five roles, spelled precisely as follows: `SystemAdmin`, `ResearchSecurityOfficer`, `ConflictReviewOfficial`, `AgencyLiaison`, and `Investigator`. A request whose `X-User-Role` is not one of those five, or whose role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Every read endpoint in the system is available to all five roles; write authority is role-scoped, as summarised in §4.

The five roles divide the work as follows. A `SystemAdmin` is the sponsored-programs administrator: they register investigators, create projects, add personnel to rosters, and drive the funding dimension of an award. A `ResearchSecurityOfficer` is the research security office proper: they register and designate foreign organizations, screen subawards, drive the security dimension of an award, manage controlled-area access grants, waive training, and open and investigate incidents. A `ConflictReviewOfficial` adjudicates disputed disclosure items and issues, waives or terminates conflict-of-commitment management plans. An `AgencyLiaison` is the sponsor-facing channel and holds exactly one write capability — transmitting an incident notification — and deliberately no investigative authority whatsoever. An `Investigator` is the disclosing researcher.

The meaning of `X-User-ID` depends on the declared role. When `X-User-Role` is `Investigator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Investigator` record; ownership checks on any resource that references an investigator compare that resource's `investigator_id` to `X-User-ID` directly. When `X-User-Role` is `SystemAdmin`, `ResearchSecurityOfficer`, `ConflictReviewOfficial` or `AgencyLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it where a workflow demands an accountable actor — as `reviewer_user_id` and `plan_approver_user_id` on a disclosure, and as `lead_investigator_user_id` on a security incident — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. An `Investigator`-role caller acting on a disclosure, a disclosure item or a travel registration that belongs to a different investigator is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

Three further identity checks exist on top of role membership, and all three return HTTP 403. `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_REVIEWER` guards continuity of review: once an officer has taken a disclosure under review, only that stored `reviewer_user_id` may return it for revision, certify it or reject it. `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` guards the two separations of duty the office is built around: the user issuing or waiving a management plan must not be the disclosure's stored `reviewer_user_id`, and the user recording a sponsor notification must not be the incident's stored `lead_investigator_user_id`. `ERR_NOT_INCIDENT_LEAD` guards the converse: only the stored `lead_investigator_user_id` may record an investigative finding on an incident.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field. Effort percentages, person-months and variances are carried there as JSON numbers rather than strings — a two-decimal quantity whose hundredths are zero may therefore serialise with fewer digits, `24.90` reading as `24.9` and `25.00` as `25.0` — while cent amounts, scores, counts, depths and points are JSON integers, and every timestamp is a string.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints; `total_count` is the size of the filtered set before the page is cut, and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of records actually returned on this page is still below `total_count`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. A `limit` outside the range 1 to 100 inclusive is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). `offset` is the number of records to skip before the page begins and defaults to 0. A `limit` or `offset` that is not a base-10 integer, and a negative `offset`, are equally `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). A filter query parameter whose value falls outside the enumerated member set that filter is drawn from is also `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a filter supplied empty or whitespace-only is ignored rather than rejected, and the free-text filters (`country_code`, `parent_organization_id` and every `*_id` filter) are matched literally without further validation.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list endpoint orders its records by `created_at` ascending, with records sharing a `created_at` ordered by insertion sequence, oldest first. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope described in §2.2.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system is evaluated against real wall-clock time. Deadlines, expiries, travel windows, grant windows and management-plan validity are all evaluated against a server-held **logical clock**. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; this startup default is mandatory and the server never falls back to real time. A `SystemAdmin` may advance or rewind the clock through an administrative test-driving capability that takes a single `current_utc` instant. That instant is stored verbatim: the call answers with a `data` of `{"current_utc": ...}` echoing the string exactly as supplied, and every field the server later stamps from the clock — `submitted_at`, `certified_at`, `plan_acknowledged_at`, `substantiated_at`, `closed_at`, `assigned_on`, `completed_on`, `waived_on`, `activated_at`, `revoked_at`, `executed_at`, `terminated_at` and `device_decision_recorded_at` — carries that same string rather than a re-normalised rendering of it.

Moving the clock changes no stored field. In particular a security incident's frozen `notification_due_on` is untouched by a clock move, and a management plan's stored `plan_expires_on` is untouched. What does change is every value that is derived at read time — most visibly the computed `status` of a training assignment, which is re-evaluated against the clock on the next read of that assignment and on every rule that consults training compliance.

### 2.5. Record Identity and Mutation Bookkeeping

Every resource in this system — investigators, foreign organizations, projects, personnel rows, disclosures, disclosure items, support statements, travel registrations, controlled access grants, security incidents, training assignments and subawards, without exception — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` — a server-minted UUID. There is no type prefix and no domain-flavoured encoding; the value is a bare UUID string.
- `created_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recording when the record was first persisted. Unlike the domain fields of §2.4, this bookkeeping pair is stamped from real wall-clock time and not from the logical clock, so moving the clock never changes it.
- `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the record.
- `version` — an integer that increments by one on every mutation of the record. A single request advances a given record's `version` at most once, however many recompute hops inside that request happen to touch it.

A recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation. When an investigator's `risk_score` is recomputed because one of their disclosure items became `VERIFIED`, that investigator's `updated_at` is refreshed and their `version` increments, even though the client never addressed the investigator record. The same holds for a project whose `activation_blockers` or `active_grant_count` shift as a consequence of a write somewhere else. This is what makes the concurrency control in §2.7 meaningful: an investigator's `version` genuinely tracks everything the office knows about them. Derived fields are additionally refreshed in place whenever a record is served — so a read is never stale even when no write has intervened, most visibly after a clock move — but a read alone never advances `updated_at` or `version`; only the recompute hops a workflow names do that.

### 2.6. Effort Arithmetic, Score Precision and Rounding Discipline

Money in this system is carried in integer cents (`award_amount_cents`, `amount_cents`, `compensation_usd_cents`, `in_kind_value_cents`) and needs no rounding rule. Effort percentages, person-months and scores do, and the **order** in which rounding is applied is deliberately observable rather than incidental. Wherever a value is rounded to two decimal places, the method is round half up.

Commitment percentages round **per contributing row, before summation, and the sum of the rounded parts is never rounded again**. An investigator's `committed_effort_pct` is the sum, over their personnel rows in `ACTIVE` status, of each row's `committed_person_months` divided by 12 months, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to two decimal places at that point. Their `outside_effort_pct` is the sum, over their `VERIFIED` disclosure items on disclosures that are not `REJECTED`, of each item's `annual_effort_hours` divided by 2,080 hours — the institution's definition of a full research year — multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to two decimal places at that point. `total_commitment_pct` is then the plain sum of three already-rounded terms: the investigator's own `institutional_base_effort_pct`, plus `committed_effort_pct`, plus `outside_effort_pct`. That sum is never re-rounded. `overcommitment_pct` is the amount by which `total_commitment_pct` exceeds 100, floored at zero, rounded half up to two decimal places.

The worked consequence matters, because it is the difference between two implementations that both look correct. Three `ACTIVE` personnel rows each committing 1.00 person-month contribute 8.33 apiece, for a `committed_effort_pct` of 24.99 — not the 25.00 that rounding the combined three person-months would produce. Three `VERIFIED` items of 100 `annual_effort_hours` each contribute 4.81 apiece, for an `outside_effort_pct` of 14.43 — not the 14.42 that rounding the combined 300 hours would produce.

Support-statement reconciliation rounds the other way round: person-months are accumulated unrounded and rounded once at the end, to two decimal places, half up. Outside effort inside that calculation is converted from hours at 160 hours per person-month and rounded to two decimal places before it is added to the personnel total, and the combined figure is rounded once more when it is stored.

Risk scoring is integer arithmetic with its own ordering rule. Each disclosure item's contribution is rounded half up to a whole number **before** it enters the sum; the per-item cap is applied to the already-summed integer; and the final clamp into the range 0 to 100 is applied last of all. Summing unrounded per-item products and rounding once at the end produces different, wrong answers. Completeness scores, severity points, screening risk points, risk flag counts, blocker positions and headcounts are integers throughout.

### 2.7. Stale-View Protection on Parent-Scoped Writes

Three write operations in this system are unconditional with respect to state — they will succeed against a perfectly valid parent record — and yet silently destroy information the client never observed. Those three carry optimistic concurrency control on the **parent's** `version`. Each takes a mandatory, non-optional version field in its request body, which must be present and a JSON integer — absent, null or non-integer is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at step 6, before any comparison is attempted; if the supplied value does not equal the parent record's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and the successful path increments the parent's `version` by one.

- `POST /disclosures` requires `expected_investigator_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Investigator`. A new disclosure for the same reporting cycle legitimately supersedes its predecessor, so no state precondition can catch a client that is working from a stale view: the request would succeed while discarding a disclosure the client never saw. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- `POST /controlled-access-grants` requires `expected_project_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Project`. Multiple `REQUESTED` grants on the same controlled area are legitimate, so a grant sized against a capacity picture that no longer exists would be accepted on state grounds alone. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- `POST /foreign-organizations/{organization_id}/designate` requires `expected_organization_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ForeignOrganization`. A designation write is an unconditional overwrite of `own_designation`, so a stale writer would clobber a designation change it never observed together with the entire subaward and project cascade that change produced. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field. In particular, replacing an organization's ownership parent, driving any status machine, reconciling a support statement and screening a subaward carry no version input and perform no concurrency check.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and the Order of Rejection

Path identifiers are resolved left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve determines the 404 that comes back. The path variables in use are `investigator_id`, `organization_id`, `project_id`, `personnel_id`, `disclosure_id`, `item_id`, `statement_id`, `registration_id`, `grant_id`, `incident_id`, `assignment_id` and `subaward_id`. Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved after the path, in the order the fields are declared in that endpoint's input list. The not-found codes are `ERR_INVESTIGATOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORGANIZATION_NOT_FOUND` (covering `organization_id`, `parent_organization_id`, `foreign_organization_id` and `host_organization_id`), `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND` (covering `project_id` and `required_for_project_id`), `ERR_PERSONNEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPORT_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRAVEL_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCESS_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRAINING_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_SUBAWARD_NOT_FOUND` — all HTTP 404. Resolution is what decides these codes, not shape: a mandatory body foreign key that is absent, null or not a string is treated as an identifier that does not resolve and therefore yields the matching 404 rather than `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An optional body foreign key is only resolved when it is present and non-null; supplying it as null is how a caller says "no link".

Because most write endpoints in this system are guarded by several independent checks at once, the **order** in which those checks run is part of the contract: a request that violates two rules must fail with the earlier rule's error, not the later one's. The evaluation order is the following twenty steps, and the first step that fails ends the request. Checks that are peculiar to one action rather than shared across the system are not given their own step; they sit where the workflow that owns them places them, always after step 7's transition-declaration check, and §3 spells each one out: `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_REVIEWER` runs immediately after the transition check and before the action's own gates; `ERR_COMPLETENESS_TOO_LOW`, then `ERR_PLAN_NOT_RESOLVED`, then `ERR_DISPUTED_ITEMS_OPEN` is the certification order; `ERR_PLAN_NOT_WARRANTED` follows the reviewer check; `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_CLEARED` then `ERR_PERSONNEL_NOT_ACTIVE` precede the posture gate on grant activation, which is in turn followed by the training gate and then the capacity projection; `ERR_OPEN_INCIDENTS` follows both the posture and the subaward gates on re-clearance; `ERR_PI_REQUIRED`, `ERR_ACCESS_GRANTS_OPEN`, `ERR_NO_CERTIFIED_DISCLOSURE`, `ERR_TRAINING_ALREADY_RESOLVED`, `ERR_REMEDIATION_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_AGENCY_NOTIFICATION_REQUIRED`, `ERR_REJECT_TOO_EARLY` and `ERR_INVALID_NOTIFICATION_DATE` are each the sole remaining gate on their own path. A role restriction that is narrower than the endpoint's own role list — because a single endpoint multiplexes actions or transitions that belong to different roles — is reported as `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403) and is evaluated where the action is dispatched: immediately after the `action` enum is read on `POST /disclosures/{disclosure_id}/plan`, and immediately after step 7 on `POST /disclosure-items/{item_id}/status` and `POST /travel-registrations/{registration_id}/status`.

1. Header presence — `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and non-empty, on every endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. Role admissibility — `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles and that role is listed for the endpoint, on every endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. Resource ownership for `Investigator`-role callers — `X-User-ID` equals the target investigator's `id`, or the owning investigator of the target disclosure, disclosure item or travel registration. This applies to `POST /disclosures`, `POST /disclosures/{disclosure_id}/items`, `POST /disclosures/{disclosure_id}/submit`, the `ACKNOWLEDGE` action of `POST /disclosures/{disclosure_id}/plan`, `POST /disclosure-items/{item_id}/status`, `POST /travel-registrations` and `POST /travel-registrations/{registration_id}/status`. Failure is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
4. Path existence — every path identifier resolves, left to right, on every endpoint that has path parameters; failure is the matching 404.
5. Body foreign-key existence — every `*_id` field in the request body resolves, in declaration order, on every endpoint whose input carries one; failure is the matching 404.
6. Request shape — required fields present, enum values inside their declared member sets, numeric ranges honoured (`parent_ownership_pct` between 0 and 100 inclusive, `committed_person_months` greater than 0 and at most 12, `annual_effort_hours` between 0 and 2,080 inclusive, `amount_cents` strictly greater than 0, `controlled_area_capacity` at least 0), and `limit` within 1 to 100. The conditionally required fields belong to this step too: `designated_on` must be supplied whenever `own_designation` is anything other than `NONE`, the ownership write must carry a `parent_organization_id` key at all (its value may be null, which detaches the organization) as well as a `parent_ownership_pct`, each expected-version field must be an integer, `lead_investigator_user_id` must accompany an `INVESTIGATING` incident transition, and `ISSUE` must carry all four plan fields. Numeric fields must arrive as JSON numbers, so a numerically-valued string is rejected here; a field explicitly supplied as null is treated exactly as if it had been omitted, taking its documented default where one exists and failing as absent where the field is required; instant fields must be strings that parse as ISO-8601 — a bare calendar date parses and is accepted — and whatever string parsed is what gets stored and returned, unnormalised; and a request body that is absent or is not a JSON object is read as an empty object, so it fails on the first required field rather than on the body itself. Applies to every endpoint; failure is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
7. Transition declaration — the target record is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, including the lockdown of terminal states. Applies to every status or action endpoint; failure is the entity's transition error at HTTP 409: `ERR_INVALID_DISCLOSURE_TRANSITION`, `ERR_INVALID_ITEM_TRANSITION`, `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_TRANSITION`, `ERR_INVALID_PERSONNEL_TRANSITION`, `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_TRANSITION`, `ERR_INVALID_TRAVEL_TRANSITION`, `ERR_INVALID_GRANT_TRANSITION`, `ERR_INVALID_INCIDENT_TRANSITION` or `ERR_INVALID_SUBAWARD_TRANSITION`.
8. Parent version match — the supplied expected-version field equals the parent's current `version`, on `POST /disclosures`, `POST /controlled-access-grants` and `POST /foreign-organizations/{organization_id}/designate`; failure is `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
9. Segregation of duties — the management-plan issuer or waiver differs from `reviewer_user_id`, and the agency notifier differs from `lead_investigator_user_id`. Applies to the `ISSUE` and `WAIVE` actions of `POST /disclosures/{disclosure_id}/plan` and to `POST /security-incidents/{incident_id}/notify`; failure is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
10. Incident lead identity — the officer recording a substantiated or unsubstantiated finding is the stored `lead_investigator_user_id`, on `POST /security-incidents/{incident_id}/status`; failure is `ERR_NOT_INCIDENT_LEAD` (403).
11. Investigator posture gate — a `SUSPENDED` posture blocks personnel activation, travel approval, project clearance and area access, and a `RESTRICTED` posture additionally blocks area access. Applies to `POST /project-personnel/{personnel_id}/status`, `POST /travel-registrations/{registration_id}/status`, `POST /projects/{project_id}/security-status` and `POST /controlled-access-grants/{grant_id}/status`; failure is `ERR_INVESTIGATOR_SUSPENDED` or `ERR_INVESTIGATOR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_ACCESS` (both 409).
12. Restricted-entity gate — a subaward whose `screening_designation` is `SDN` can never be mitigated, and a project carrying a `PENDING`, `FLAGGED` or `BLOCKED` subaward cannot be cleared. Applies to `POST /subawards/{subaward_id}/status` and `POST /projects/{project_id}/security-status`; failure is `ERR_RESTRICTED_ENTITY_BLOCKED` or `ERR_SUBAWARD_SCREENING_OPEN` (both 409). The `SDN` half of this gate is the exception to step 6's precedence: it is evaluated before the `mitigation_justification` presence check, so mitigating an `SDN` subaward with no justification reports `ERR_RESTRICTED_ENTITY_BLOCKED`.
13. Training gate — `CONTROLLED_AREA_SAFETY` must be satisfied before a grant activates, and `FOREIGN_TRAVEL_BRIEF` before a loaner-tier trip is approved; separately, no project-scoped assignment may be `ASSIGNED` or `EXPIRED` at award activation. Applies to `POST /controlled-access-grants/{grant_id}/status` and `POST /travel-registrations/{registration_id}/status`; failure is `ERR_AREA_TRAINING_INCOMPLETE` or `ERR_TRAVEL_BRIEFING_REQUIRED` (both 409).
14. Management-plan gate — a `LOANER_PLUS_ESCORT` trip requires a `PLAN_ACKNOWLEDGED` disclosure, and certification requires `plan_status` to be one of `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PLAN_WAIVED` or `PLAN_ACKNOWLEDGED`. Applies to `POST /travel-registrations/{registration_id}/status` and `POST /disclosures/{disclosure_id}/review`; failure is `ERR_MANAGEMENT_PLAN_REQUIRED` or `ERR_PLAN_NOT_RESOLVED` (both 409).
15. Uniqueness — an investigator's `email` is unique; a non-`SUPERSEDED` disclosure is unique per investigator and `cycle_label`; an unrevoked access grant is unique per project and investigator; a non-`SUPERSEDED` support statement is unique per investigator, project, `agency_code` and `document_type`. Applies to `POST /investigators`, `POST /disclosures`, `POST /controlled-access-grants` and `POST /support-statements`; failure is `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMAIL`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_DISCLOSURE_CYCLE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACCESS_GRANT` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUPPORT_STATEMENT` (all 409).
16. Ownership-graph sanity — the parent chain must not revisit a node already on the walked path and must not exceed eight controlling hops. Applies to `POST /foreign-organizations` and `POST /foreign-organizations/{organization_id}/ownership`; failure is `ERR_OWNERSHIP_CYCLE` or `ERR_OWNERSHIP_DEPTH_EXCEEDED` (both 422).
17. Interval sanity — a project's `period_end` is strictly after its `period_start`, a grant's `valid_until` is strictly after its `valid_from`, a registration's `return_on` is on or after its `depart_on`, and a plan's `plan_expires_on` is strictly after its `plan_effective_from`. Applies to `POST /projects`, `POST /controlled-access-grants`, `POST /travel-registrations` and `POST /disclosures/{disclosure_id}/plan`; failure is `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD`, `ERR_INVALID_GRANT_WINDOW`, `ERR_INVALID_TRAVEL_WINDOW` or `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_WINDOW` (all 422).
18. Travel timing — the closed-interval overlap check against the same investigator's `SUBMITTED` and `APPROVED` registrations, plus the rules that a trip cannot be cancelled after departure or completed before return. Applies to `POST /travel-registrations/{registration_id}/status`; failure is `ERR_TRAVEL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_TRAVEL_ALREADY_STARTED` or `ERR_TRAVEL_NOT_ENDED` (all 409).
19. Controlled-area capacity — the half-open peak-occupancy projection over the grant's window must not exceed the project's `controlled_area_capacity`. Applies to `POST /controlled-access-grants/{grant_id}/status`; failure is `ERR_AREA_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
20. Effort ceiling and activation readiness — an acknowledged, unexpired management plan caps `total_commitment_pct` at `plan_effort_cap_pct`, and a project may only reach `ACTIVE` with an empty `activation_blockers` list. Applies to `POST /project-personnel/{personnel_id}/status` and `POST /projects/{project_id}/award-status`; failure is `ERR_EFFORT_CAP_EXCEEDED` or `ERR_ACTIVATION_BLOCKED` (both 409).

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Field Reconciliation

Derived state is reconciled synchronously, inside the request that caused it, and in the dependency order the domain dictates rather than in whatever order is convenient. The characteristic chain runs: a fact changes about an investigator's relationships or incidents, so their `risk_score` is recomputed; because the score moved, their `disclosure_posture` is recomputed; because the posture may have left the set that permits controlled-area access, that investigator's open access grants may be suspended; because grants left `ACTIVE`, each affected project's `active_grant_count` is recomputed; because that count moved, each affected project's `controlled_area_posture` is recomputed. Every link in such a chain is a mutation in the sense of §2.5.

Parent-child and cross-aggregate cascades are equally mandatory. Closing or withdrawing an award terminates its subawards and removes its personnel, and removing personnel in turn pulls the effort those rows contributed back out of the investigator's commitment totals. Changing an organization's designation propagates down the ownership graph, re-screens the subawards that reference any affected organization, and can suspend the projects those subawards belong to. Certifying a disclosure changes which projects consider that investigator's disclosure obligation satisfied. Each of these is spelled out in the workflow subsection where it originates in §3; the principle to internalise here is that no client is ever expected to issue a follow-up call to make the system consistent.

### 2.10. The Restricted-Entity Ownership Graph

Foreign organizations form a directed graph: each record may name one `parent_organization_id` and the percentage of itself that parent holds, in `parent_ownership_pct`. A restricted-list designation attached directly to an organization is its `own_designation`, one of `NONE`, `UNVERIFIED_END_USER`, `ENTITY_LIST`, `FOREIGN_MILITARY`, `MALIGN_TALENT_PROGRAM` or `SDN`, and those six members are ranked by strictness in exactly that order — `NONE` least strict at rank zero, then `UNVERIFIED_END_USER` at one, `ENTITY_LIST` at two, `FOREIGN_MILITARY` at three, `MALIGN_TALENT_PROGRAM` at four, and `SDN` strictest at five.

An organization's `effective_designation` is the strictest designation found on the chain of control rooted at that organization, and `effective_designation_depth` is the number of ownership hops between the organization and the chain member that supplied it. The walk works as follows. Begin with the organization itself as the best-known designation at depth zero, and mark the organization as visited. Then, as long as the current node names a parent: if the current node's `parent_ownership_pct` is absent or is strictly below the controlling-interest threshold of 50 percent, stop walking — the chain of control is broken at that hop, and ancestors beyond it are excluded from the calculation even if one of them is `SDN`. Otherwise step to the parent; a `parent_organization_id` that names no stored organization likewise ends the walk quietly rather than raising. If that parent is already on the visited set, the graph is cyclic and the write is rejected with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_CYCLE` (HTTP 422). Add the parent to the visited set and increase the hop count by one; if the hop count now exceeds eight, the chain is too deep and the write is rejected with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_DEPTH_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). If the parent's `own_designation` is **strictly** stricter than the best designation recorded so far, adopt it along with the current hop count as the new best; a parent that merely ties the incumbent does not displace it, so ties always keep the nearer hop. Continue from the parent.

The 50 percent threshold is inclusive and is compared at two decimal places: a stake of exactly 50.00 percent conveys control and therefore inherits designation, while 49.99 percent does not. This calculation runs when an organization is created, when its own designation is changed, when its ownership parent or stake is changed, and on every descendant whenever an ancestor's designation changes.

### 2.11. Investigator Posture, Project Postures and What They Gate

Three enumerated postures act as master gates across the system, and all three are computed rather than set.

An investigator's `disclosure_posture` is one of `CLEAR`, `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, `RESTRICTED` or `SUSPENDED`. It is evaluated top-down and the first matching clause wins outright — no later clause can soften an earlier verdict. First: if the investigator has any security incident whose `status` is `AGENCY_NOTIFIED`, or whose `agency_notified_on` is set, or whose `finding` is `SUBSTANTIATED` with `severity` of `CRITICAL`, the posture is `SUSPENDED`. This clause reads the investigator's entire incident history including closed incidents, because an agency notification is permanent. Otherwise, if the investigator's `substantiated_incident_count` is at least 1, or their `risk_score` is at least 60, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise, if their `risk_score` is at least 30, or their `material_discrepancy_count` is at least 1, or any of their disclosures has `plan_status` of `PLAN_REQUIRED`, the posture is `REVIEW_REQUIRED`. Otherwise the posture is `CLEAR`. Both score thresholds are inclusive integer comparisons: a `risk_score` of 60 is `RESTRICTED` while 59 is `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, and a score of 30 is `REVIEW_REQUIRED` while 29 is `CLEAR`. `substantiated_incident_count` is the number of that investigator's security incidents whose `finding` is `SUBSTANTIATED`, and `material_discrepancy_count` is the number of their support statements whose `status` is `INCONSISTENT`.

A `SUSPENDED` posture blocks personnel activation, travel approval, project clearance and controlled-area access — the first three reported as `ERR_INVESTIGATOR_SUSPENDED`. Controlled-area access additionally requires the posture to be `CLEAR` or `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, so `RESTRICTED` blocks it too, reported as `ERR_INVESTIGATOR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_ACCESS`. A posture of `SUSPENDED` on any investigator holding an `ACTIVE` personnel row also keeps their projects out of activation.

A project's `controlled_area_posture` is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `LOCKED_DOWN`, evaluated top-down in this order: if the project's `security_status` is `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`, the posture is `LOCKED_DOWN`; otherwise if its `controlled_area_capacity` is 0, the posture is `LOCKED_DOWN`; otherwise if its `active_grant_count` is greater than or equal to its `controlled_area_capacity`, the posture is `RESTRICTED`; otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. The comparison is an inclusive integer one: an `active_grant_count` exactly equal to `controlled_area_capacity` yields `RESTRICTED`, not `LOCKED_DOWN`.

Finally, `training_compliant` is a boolean on the investigator that is true precisely when the investigator has no institution-wide training assignment — that is, no assignment whose `required_for_project_id` is null — whose computed `status` is `ASSIGNED` or `EXPIRED`. A newly registered investigator with no assignments at all is therefore compliant.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding the Research Population and the Foreign Ownership Graph

A `SystemAdmin` registers each researcher the office is responsible for. Registration takes the investigator's `full_name`, their institutional `email`, an `appointment_type` of `FACULTY`, `RESEARCH_STAFF` or `POSTDOC`, their `home_department`, and optionally an `institutional_base_effort_pct` — the share of a full year the investigator already owes the institution, which must be between 0 and 100 inclusive and defaults to 100 when omitted. The `email` is the institution's unique handle for the person; it is validated only as a non-empty string rather than against any address grammar, and registering a second investigator with an `email` already on file is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMAIL`, the comparison being an exact character-for-character one so that two addresses differing only in letter case are distinct handles. A freshly registered investigator starts with `committed_effort_pct` at 0, `outside_effort_pct` at 0, a `total_commitment_pct` equal to their `institutional_base_effort_pct`, `overcommitment_pct` at 0, `risk_score` at 0, `substantiated_incident_count` at 0, `material_discrepancy_count` at 0, `training_compliant` true, and a `disclosure_posture` of `CLEAR`. Reading an investigator returns all of those computed commitment, risk and posture fields; the roster of investigators can be narrowed by `appointment_type` and by `disclosure_posture`.

Foreign organizations are the counterparties that appear throughout the rest of the system — in disclosure items, in subawards, and as travel hosts. Either a `SystemAdmin` or a `ResearchSecurityOfficer` may register one, supplying its `legal_name`, a `country_code` of registration given as exactly two uppercase letters under ISO 3166-1 alpha-2, and an `org_type` of `UNIVERSITY`, `INSTITUTE`, `COMPANY`, `GOVERNMENT_AGENCY` or `TALENT_PROGRAM`. Registration may also carry the ownership and designation facts directly: a `parent_organization_id` naming the owning organization (null by default), a `parent_ownership_pct` giving the stake that parent holds (0 by default, between 0 and 100 inclusive), an `own_designation` (`NONE` by default), and a `designated_on` instant recording when that direct designation took effect (null by default, and forced to null whenever `own_designation` is `NONE` even if the caller supplied one, but mandatory — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` if omitted — whenever `own_designation` is anything else). The `effective_designation` and `effective_designation_depth` described in §2.10 are resolved for the new record before it is returned, and an ownership parent that would close a cycle or push the chain past eight controlling hops causes the create to be rejected rather than persisted. Organizations can be listed and filtered by `country_code`, by `own_designation`, by `effective_designation` and by `parent_organization_id` — that last filter narrowing the list to one owner's direct holdings — and a dedicated read returns the direct children of a given organization in the ownership graph — every organization naming it as `parent_organization_id`, whatever the size of the stake, so a 10-percent holding is listed as a subsidiary even though it is not a controlling hop for §2.10 purposes.

Only a `ResearchSecurityOfficer` may change what the restricted lists say. Attaching or changing a designation supplies a new `own_designation`, its `designated_on` instant, and the `expected_organization_version` demanded by §2.7; on success the organization's `version` increments by one and its `effective_designation` is resolved again. What follows is the longest cascade in the system, and every step of it is required. Because the organization's designation changed, `effective_designation` and `effective_designation_depth` are recomputed for every descendant reachable through hops of at least 50 percent ownership. Because those descendant designations changed, every subaward whose counterparty is an affected organization and whose `status` is `CLEARED` or `FLAGGED` is re-screened under the `applied_screening_variant` already stored on it — the variant is not re-chosen, and a subaward that was never screened, and so has no stored variant, is skipped entirely — which re-derives that subaward's `screening_designation`, `screening_matched_organization_id`, `screening_match_depth` and `screening_risk_points`; the subaward transitions to `BLOCKED` only where the re-screen actually yields `BLOCKED`, so a designation change that leaves the imputed outcome at `CLEARED` or `FLAGGED` refreshes the screening fields and stops there. Because subawards became `BLOCKED`, each affected project whose `security_status` is `CLEARED` transitions to `SUSPENDED` with a `security_status_reason` of `SUBAWARD_BLOCKED`, and gains `SUBAWARD_BLOCKED` in its `activation_blockers`. Because those projects were suspended, their access grants in `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE` transition to `SUSPENDED` with a `status_reason` of `PROJECT_SUSPENDED`, and each affected project's `active_grant_count` and `controlled_area_posture` are recomputed.

Ownership itself is also officer-maintained and separately addressable. Setting or replacing an organization's ownership position supplies the new `parent_organization_id` and `parent_ownership_pct`; both keys are mandatory — the `parent_organization_id` key must be present, though its value may be null to detach the organization from any owner, and `parent_ownership_pct` must be supplied outright — and unlike the designation write, it carries no expected-version field and performs no concurrency check. It recomputes the organization's own `effective_designation` and then every descendant's, and cascades into subaward screening state, project suspension and grant suspension exactly as the designation change does. A chain that revisits a node is refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_CYCLE` and one longer than eight controlling hops with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`, both HTTP 422, and in either case the organization is left exactly as it was — the attempted parent and stake are rolled back, and neither `version` nor `updated_at` moves; a `parent_organization_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_ORGANIZATION_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.2. Sponsored Awards, Roster Composition and the Effort Ledger

A sponsored project is created by a `SystemAdmin` with a `title`, an `agency_code` of `NIH`, `NSF`, `DOD`, `DOE` or `NASA`, a `sensitivity_level` of `STANDARD`, `CONTROLLED` or `RESTRICTED`, a total obligated `award_amount_cents` of at least 0, a `period_start`, a `period_end` that must fall strictly after `period_start` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD`, HTTP 422), and optionally a `controlled_area_capacity` — the maximum number of investigators allowed concurrent access to the project's controlled research area, at least 0 and defaulting to 0. Awards track two independent dimensions on the same record. The funding dimension, `award_status`, begins at `PROPOSED` and moves through `ACTIVATION_PENDING` to `ACTIVE`, ending at `CLOSED` or `WITHDRAWN`. The security dimension, `security_status`, begins at `PENDING_CLEARANCE` and moves among `CLEARED` and `SUSPENDED`, ending at `TERMINATED`; an accompanying `security_status_reason` records why, drawn from `NONE`, `OFFICER_ACTION`, `SUBAWARD_BLOCKED`, `INVESTIGATOR_SUSPENDED` and `INCIDENT_NOTIFICATION`, and starting at `NONE`. The two dimensions evolve independently and only meet at the activation gate; each `to_status` is read as an enumerated member of its own dimension's state set first, so a value outside that set is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` while a member the current state does not declare — including a `to_status` naming the state the record is already in — is `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_TRANSITION`. A new project computes its `activation_blockers` and its `controlled_area_posture` immediately, starts with `active_grant_count` at 0 and `open_incident_count` at 0, and — because a capacity of 0 means there is no controlled area to enter — a default-capacity project is `LOCKED_DOWN` from birth. Reading a project exposes `activation_blockers`, `active_grant_count`, `controlled_area_posture` and `open_incident_count`; the project list can be filtered by `agency_code`, `award_status`, `security_status` and `sensitivity_level`.

Investigators join a project through its personnel roster, which a `SystemAdmin` populates one row at a time. Each addition names an `investigator_id`, a `role_on_project` of `PI`, `CO_PI`, `SENIOR_PERSONNEL` or `KEY_PERSON`, and the `committed_person_months` promised annually, which must be strictly greater than 0, at most 12, and carry at most two decimal places. The row's `project_id` is taken from the containing project rather than from the payload, and the row is created in `PROPOSED` status. Creating it recomputes the project's `activation_blockers`, and a merely `PROPOSED` row does not satisfy the requirement for a principal investigator — `PERSONNEL_MISSING_PI` stays on the list. Personnel rows can be read individually and listed with filters on `project_id`, `investigator_id` and `status`; a `personnel_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_PERSONNEL_NOT_FOUND`.

Activating a roster row — the `PROPOSED → ACTIVE` transition, requested by a `SystemAdmin` or a `ResearchSecurityOfficer` with a `to_status` of `ACTIVE` — is where effort actually lands on the institution's books, so it is guarded three ways. The investigator's `disclosure_posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_INVESTIGATOR_SUSPENDED`. The project's `award_status` must be `PROPOSED`, `ACTIVATION_PENDING` or `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_TRANSITION` — a closed or withdrawn award takes on no new active personnel. And if the investigator holds a disclosure whose `plan_status` is `PLAN_ACKNOWLEDGED` and whose `plan_expires_on` is still ahead of the logical clock, their `total_commitment_pct` after this activation must be at most that plan's `plan_effort_cap_pct`, else `ERR_EFFORT_CAP_EXCEEDED` — a plan whose `plan_effort_cap_pct` is null imposes no ceiling and the check is skipped. On success the investigator's `committed_effort_pct` is recomputed as the per-row rounded sum over their `ACTIVE` rows described in §2.6, then `total_commitment_pct` and `overcommitment_pct` follow; because `committed_effort_pct` moved, `disclosure_posture` is recomputed; and the project's `activation_blockers` are recomputed.

Removal comes in two flavours, and they are deliberately not symmetric. Removing an `ACTIVE` row — `ACTIVE → REMOVED`, with `to_status` set to `REMOVED` — carries no preconditions and recomputes `committed_effort_pct`, `total_commitment_pct` and `overcommitment_pct` so that the conserved effort quantity returns to exactly its pre-activation value; it then revokes every access grant belonging to that same project and investigator whose `status` is `REQUESTED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, stamping each with a `status_reason` of `PERSONNEL_REMOVED`; because grants left `ACTIVE`, the project's `active_grant_count` is recomputed and then its `controlled_area_posture`; and finally `activation_blockers` are recomputed. Removing a `PROPOSED` row — `PROPOSED → REMOVED` — is also unconditioned but performs **no** effort recompute at all, because a `PROPOSED` row never contributed to `committed_effort_pct`; it recomputes only the project's `activation_blockers`. `REMOVED` is terminal: any further transition request on that row is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PERSONNEL_TRANSITION`.

### 3.3. The Disclosure Cycle: Drafting, Reportable Relationships and Submission

Disclosure is organised into cycles. An `Investigator` (acting for themselves) or a `ResearchSecurityOfficer` opens a disclosure by naming the `investigator_id`, a `cycle_label` such as `2026-ANNUAL`, a `disclosure_type` of `ANNUAL`, `EVENT_DRIVEN` or `PROPOSAL`, and the `expected_investigator_version` demanded by §2.7. The new record is created in review status `DRAFT` with plan status `NOT_REQUIRED`, `revision_round` at 0, `item_count` at 0, `late_item_count` at 0, `undisclosed_item_count` at 0, `completeness_score` at 100, `completeness_tier` of `A`, and `submitted_at`, `certified_at`, `reviewer_user_id`, `plan_effort_cap_pct`, `plan_effective_from`, `plan_expires_on`, `plan_approver_user_id` and `plan_acknowledged_at` all null, with `plan_required_actions` an empty list. The parent investigator's `version` increments by one.

Only one live disclosure may exist per investigator and `cycle_label`. If a `CERTIFIED` or `REJECTED` disclosure already exists for that pair, the create is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DISCLOSURE_CYCLE`. Otherwise any prior disclosure for the same pair whose review status is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` transitions to `SUPERSEDED` as a side effect of the create, its `plan_status` left exactly as it was, and the superseded record stops counting toward any project's `activation_blockers`; the investigator's `risk_score` is then recomputed and their `disclosure_posture` after it. `SUPERSEDED` is also declared as reachable from `CERTIFIED`: if a certified record ever stops being the operative one for its cycle, the projects on which that investigator holds an `ACTIVE` personnel row have their `activation_blockers` recomputed and `DISCLOSURE_NOT_CERTIFIED` reappears on them, and the investigator's `risk_score` and then `disclosure_posture` are recomputed. `CERTIFIED`, `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are the three terminal review states; a review action requested against a record already in one of them is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DISCLOSURE_TRANSITION`. Disclosures can be read individually — exposing `review_status`, `plan_status`, `completeness_score` and `completeness_tier` — and listed with filters on `investigator_id`, `review_status` and `plan_status`. An unresolvable `disclosure_id` yields `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_FOUND`.

Reportable relationships are added to a disclosure as items, by the owning `Investigator` or by a `ResearchSecurityOfficer`, and only while the parent disclosure's review status is `DRAFT` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`; adding one to a disclosure in any other review state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DISCLOSURE_TRANSITION`. Each item declares an `item_type` of `FOREIGN_APPOINTMENT`, `TALENT_PROGRAM`, `OUTSIDE_SUPPORT`, `IN_KIND_RESOURCE` or `VISITING_SCHOLAR`, the `foreign_organization_id` it relates to, and a `start_date`. Optionally it carries `annual_effort_hours` (0 by default, between 0 and 2,080 inclusive), `compensation_usd_cents` of annual cash compensation (0 by default), `in_kind_value_cents` of annual in-kind resources (0 by default), an `end_date` (null by default, meaning the relationship is ongoing), the boolean `is_paid` (false by default), and a `source`. The item's `disclosure_id` is taken from the containing disclosure. `source` is `SELF_REPORTED` by default, which is what an investigator declaring their own relationships produces; `INCIDENT_REMEDIATION` marks an item added specifically to remediate a security incident, and it is the presence of at least one such item on a certified disclosure that lets an incident close. Items are created in `DECLARED` status. Creating one recomputes the parent's `item_count`, `late_item_count`, `undisclosed_item_count`, `completeness_score` and `completeness_tier`, and computes the item's own `item_risk_points`. Items can be read individually and listed with filters on `disclosure_id`, `foreign_organization_id` and `status`; an unresolvable `item_id` yields `ERR_DISCLOSURE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`.

Three counters on the disclosure are maintained from its items and drive everything downstream. `item_count` is the number of items whose `status` is `DECLARED`, `VERIFIED` or `DISPUTED`. `late_item_count` is the number of items that were created while the disclosure's `revision_round` was already at least 1 — a relationship surfaced only after the office pushed back. `undisclosed_item_count` is the number of items whose `source` is `INCIDENT_REMEDIATION` — a relationship the investigator did not volunteer at all.

An item's `item_risk_points` is the integer weight that relationship carries in the investigator's risk score, computed before any cap applies. Start from the base weight of the `item_type`: 12 for `FOREIGN_APPOINTMENT`, 30 for `TALENT_PROGRAM`, 8 for `OUTSIDE_SUPPORT`, 6 for `IN_KIND_RESOURCE` and 10 for `VISITING_SCHOLAR`. Multiply that base by the counterparty's exposure multiplier, read from the referenced organization's `effective_designation`: 1.0 for `NONE`, 1.5 for `UNVERIFIED_END_USER`, 2.0 for `ENTITY_LIST`, 2.5 for `FOREIGN_MILITARY`, and 3.0 for both `MALIGN_TALENT_PROGRAM` and `SDN`. If the relationship is compensated — `is_paid` true — add a flat surcharge of 5 after the multiplication. Round the result half up to a whole number; that whole number is the item's contribution.

The investigator's `risk_score` assembles four terms in a fixed order. First, the item subtotal: sum the per-item whole-number contributions just described over every disclosure item whose `status` is `VERIFIED` that belongs to a disclosure of this investigator whose review status is not `REJECTED`, each item rounded to a whole number before it enters the sum, and then cap that subtotal at 60. Second, the incident subtotal: sum, over the investigator's security incidents whose `finding` is `SUBSTANTIATED`, a weight taken from the incident's frozen `severity` — 5 for `LOW`, 12 for `MODERATE`, 25 for `HIGH`, and 40 for `CRITICAL`. Third, the discrepancy subtotal: 15 points for each of the investigator's support statements whose `status` is `INCONSISTENT`. Fourth, a mitigation credit of 20 points, applied when the investigator holds a disclosure whose `plan_status` is `PLAN_ACKNOWLEDGED` and whose `plan_expires_on` is still ahead of the logical clock, and 0 otherwise. Add the capped item subtotal, the incident subtotal and the discrepancy subtotal; subtract the credit; then clamp the result into the range 0 to 100 — no lower than 0, no higher than 100. The cap of 60 applies only to the item term, and it applies to the already-summed integer, not to individual items.

Officers move items through their own small state machine with a `to_status`. A `ResearchSecurityOfficer` or `ConflictReviewOfficial` may verify a declared item (`DECLARED → VERIFIED`) or dispute it (`DECLARED → DISPUTED`); both require the parent disclosure's review status to be `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, else `ERR_INVALID_DISCLOSURE_TRANSITION`. Verification recomputes the investigator's `risk_score` and then their `disclosure_posture`. A dispute records no score change but has a hard consequence: while any item on a disclosure sits in `DISPUTED`, that disclosure cannot be certified. A `ConflictReviewOfficial` alone may resolve a dispute in the investigator's favour (`DISPUTED → VERIFIED`, unconditioned, recomputing `risk_score` then `disclosure_posture`) or strike the item entirely (`DISPUTED → WITHDRAWN`, unconditioned, recomputing the parent's `completeness_score` and then the investigator's `risk_score` and `disclosure_posture`). The owning `Investigator` may withdraw their own declared item (`DECLARED → WITHDRAWN`) only while the parent disclosure's review status is `DRAFT` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`, and only if `X-User-ID` matches the owning investigator, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; withdrawal recomputes the parent's `completeness_score` and then the investigator's `risk_score` and `disclosure_posture`. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal; any transition requested out of it, or any item transition not declared above, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ITEM_TRANSITION`. The endpoint itself admits all three roles, so the per-transition ownership named above is enforced in two places: an `Investigator` caller whose `X-User-ID` does not own the parent disclosure is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` before the `to_status` is even read, while a caller whose role is not among the roles that particular declared transition names — an `Investigator` verifying an item, or a `ResearchSecurityOfficer` resolving a dispute — is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403) evaluated after the transition itself is confirmed declared and before the parent disclosure's review status is consulted.

Submission is the investigator's own act and takes no payload. It carries a disclosure from `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` or, after a round of revision, from `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION → SUBMITTED`, and both paths are guarded identically: `X-User-ID` must equal the disclosure's `investigator_id`, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; the investigator's `disclosure_posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_INVESTIGATOR_SUSPENDED`; and no item on the disclosure may be in `DISPUTED`, else `ERR_DISPUTED_ITEMS_OPEN`. Both paths set `submitted_at` to the current logical clock — overwritten on each round, so it always names the most recent submission — recompute `completeness_score` and `completeness_tier`, recompute the investigator's `risk_score` and then their `disclosure_posture`, and recompute `outside_effort_pct` and `total_commitment_pct`. The stored `reviewer_user_id` survives the revision loop untouched, so the same officer must resume the review.

### 3.4. Officer Review, Completeness Scoring, Certification and Management Plans

Review is driven by a single officer capability that takes an `action` and an optional `review_note`, and is open to a `ResearchSecurityOfficer` or a `ConflictReviewOfficial`. The five admissible `action` values are `BEGIN_REVIEW`, `RETURN_FOR_REVISION`, `REQUIRE_PLAN`, `CERTIFY` and `REJECT`; any other value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and `review_note` when supplied need only be a string — it is validated and then discarded rather than stored. Every one of the five first checks that the action is declared from the disclosure's current `review_status`, and only then applies the assigned-reviewer guard, so an action aimed at the wrong review state reports `ERR_INVALID_DISCLOSURE_TRANSITION` even when the caller is the wrong officer as well.

`BEGIN_REVIEW` carries the disclosure from `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`. On the very first pass it stores the acting `X-User-ID` as `reviewer_user_id`; afterwards that value is never overwritten, and if `reviewer_user_id` is already set it must equal the caller's `X-User-ID`, else `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_REVIEWER`. `RETURN_FOR_REVISION` carries the disclosure from `UNDER_REVIEW → RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` and is likewise restricted to the stored `reviewer_user_id`; it increments `revision_round` by one and recomputes `completeness_score` and `completeness_tier`, and because the revision penalty grows with each round, a return can move the disclosure into a lower tier. `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION → SUBMITTED` closes the cycle, which a disclosure may traverse any number of times.

The `completeness_score` is computed in three ordered steps, and the ordering is observable. Step one produces a raw score: begin at 100; subtract 25 for each item counted in `undisclosed_item_count`; subtract 8 for each item counted in `late_item_count`; subtract 6 for each completed revision round counted in `revision_round`; then floor the result at 0. Step two selects `completeness_tier` **from that raw score**: `A` if the raw score is at least 90, otherwise `B` if the raw score is at least 70, otherwise `C`. Step three produces the final `completeness_score` by subtracting the tier's own penalty from the raw score and flooring at 0 — 0 for tier `A`, 5 for tier `B`, and 12 for tier `C`. The tier is never re-derived from the penalised score. Worked through: three late items and one completed revision round give a raw score of 100 minus 0 minus 24 minus 6, which is 70; 70 selects tier `B`; and the final `completeness_score` is 65. Re-deriving the tier from 65 would wrongly yield tier `C` and a score of 58, a value this API must never return — and because the certification floor sits at 60, that mistake also flips certification outcomes near the boundary. All three inputs are maintained on the disclosure record itself, so scoring never needs to scan items.

`CERTIFY` carries the disclosure from `UNDER_REVIEW → CERTIFIED` and is the most heavily guarded action in the review workflow. The caller must be the stored `reviewer_user_id`, else `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_REVIEWER`. The `completeness_score` must be at least 60, else `ERR_COMPLETENESS_TOO_LOW`. The `plan_status` must be one of `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PLAN_WAIVED` or `PLAN_ACKNOWLEDGED`, else `ERR_PLAN_NOT_RESOLVED` — so a disclosure sitting at `PLAN_REQUIRED`, `PLAN_ISSUED` or `PLAN_TERMINATED` cannot be certified. And no item may be in `DISPUTED`, else `ERR_DISPUTED_ITEMS_OPEN`. On success, `certified_at` is set to the current logical clock; every item still in `DECLARED` transitions to `VERIFIED`; because those items have entered the scoring set, the investigator's `outside_effort_pct`, `total_commitment_pct` and `risk_score` are recomputed; because `risk_score` changed, `disclosure_posture` is recomputed; and `activation_blockers` are recomputed for every project on which that investigator holds an `ACTIVE` personnel row, since certification is what removes `DISCLOSURE_NOT_CERTIFIED` from those lists.

`REJECT` carries the disclosure from `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED` and is available only to the stored `reviewer_user_id`. It additionally requires that `revision_round` be at least 2 — the office must have given the investigator two chances before abandoning the record — else `ERR_REJECT_TOO_EARLY`. On rejection, every item whose `status` is `DECLARED` or `DISPUTED` transitions to `WITHDRAWN`; because those items have left the scoring set, the investigator's `outside_effort_pct`, `total_commitment_pct` and `risk_score` are recomputed; and because `risk_score` changed, `disclosure_posture` is recomputed.

Running alongside review, on the very same disclosure record, is an independent management-plan dimension whose states are `NOT_REQUIRED` (the initial value), `PLAN_REQUIRED`, `PLAN_ISSUED`, `PLAN_ACKNOWLEDGED`, `PLAN_WAIVED` and the terminal `PLAN_TERMINATED`. The two dimensions advance separately; the only place they meet is the certification gate above.

Requiring a plan is a review action rather than a plan action: `REQUIRE_PLAN` moves the plan dimension `NOT_REQUIRED → PLAN_REQUIRED`. It demands that the disclosure's `review_status` be `UNDER_REVIEW` and that its `plan_status` still be `NOT_REQUIRED` — a plan dimension that has already moved anywhere else, including to `PLAN_WAIVED` or `PLAN_TERMINATED`, fails the same way — else `ERR_INVALID_DISCLOSURE_TRANSITION`; then that the caller be the stored `reviewer_user_id`, else `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_REVIEWER`; and then that a conflict-of-commitment trigger actually be present — meaning the investigator's `total_commitment_pct` exceeds 100, **or** the disclosure carries an item whose `item_type` is `TALENT_PROGRAM` with `status` of `DECLARED` or `VERIFIED`, **or** the investigator's `risk_score` is at least 30. If none of those three holds, the action is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_WARRANTED`. On success the investigator's `disclosure_posture` is recomputed, and because a `PLAN_REQUIRED` disclosure is itself a posture trigger, the posture becomes at least `REVIEW_REQUIRED`.

Everything after that runs through a separate plan capability taking an `action` of `ISSUE`, `ACKNOWLEDGE`, `WAIVE` or `TERMINATE`, plus the four plan fields `plan_effort_cap_pct`, `plan_required_actions`, `plan_effective_from` and `plan_expires_on`. The endpoint admits a `ConflictReviewOfficial` or an `Investigator`, but each action belongs to exactly one of them, and the mismatch is caught immediately after the `action` value is read — before the plan dimension's own state is consulted: `ACKNOWLEDGE` requires the `Investigator` role and the other three require `ConflictReviewOfficial`, and a caller in the wrong role is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). `ISSUE` moves the plan dimension `PLAN_REQUIRED → PLAN_ISSUED` and belongs to a `ConflictReviewOfficial`. All four plan fields are validated first: each must be present, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, with `plan_effort_cap_pct` at least 0 and `plan_required_actions` a list — possibly empty, which is accepted — of admissible members. Only then is `PLAN_REQUIRED` confirmed as the current plan state. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the disclosure's `reviewer_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the officer who reviewed the disclosure may not also write the plan that manages it; a disclosure whose `reviewer_user_id` is still null can be issued a plan by anyone in role. Last, `plan_expires_on` must fall strictly after `plan_effective_from`, else `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_WINDOW`. The four fields are stored on the disclosure and the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `plan_approver_user_id`. `plan_effort_cap_pct` is the effort ceiling the plan writes into force, and `plan_required_actions` is a list drawn from `DIVEST_APPOINTMENT`, `REDUCE_OUTSIDE_EFFORT`, `QUARTERLY_ATTESTATION`, `NO_FOREIGN_STUDENT_SUPERVISION` and `DEVICE_RESTRICTION`.

`ACKNOWLEDGE` moves the plan dimension `PLAN_ISSUED → PLAN_ACKNOWLEDGED` and is the subject investigator's own act: the caller's role must be `Investigator`, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, and `X-User-ID` must equal the disclosure's `investigator_id`, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — both ahead of the `PLAN_ISSUED` check. It carries no plan fields of its own; any supplied alongside it are ignored. It sets `plan_acknowledged_at` to the current logical clock; because an acknowledged plan is now in force, the investigator's `risk_score` is recomputed and the mitigation credit of 20 points applies while the logical clock remains before `plan_expires_on`; and because `risk_score` changed, `disclosure_posture` is recomputed.

`WAIVE` moves the plan dimension `PLAN_REQUIRED → PLAN_WAIVED` and also belongs to a `ConflictReviewOfficial` who is not the disclosure's `reviewer_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Waiving is the mirror image of requiring, though only of the first two of its three triggers: it demands that the investigator's `total_commitment_pct` be at most 100 **and** that the disclosure carry no item whose `item_type` is `TALENT_PROGRAM` in `DECLARED` or `VERIFIED` status. A `risk_score` of 30 or more does not by itself block a waiver, so a plan required on the strength of risk alone may be waived immediately. If a trigger is still present, the waiver is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_WARRANTED`. On success the investigator's `disclosure_posture` is recomputed.

`TERMINATE` is available to a `ConflictReviewOfficial` from either of two states, with no preconditions in either case. From `PLAN_ACKNOWLEDGED → PLAN_TERMINATED`, the plan stops being in force, so the investigator's `risk_score` is recomputed **without** the mitigation credit, and then `disclosure_posture` is recomputed. From `PLAN_ISSUED → PLAN_TERMINATED`, the credit was never applied in the first place, so the recompute of `risk_score` simply confirms the existing figure, and `disclosure_posture` is recomputed after it. `PLAN_TERMINATED` is terminal on this dimension, and a plan action requested from a state that does not declare it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DISCLOSURE_TRANSITION`.

### 3.5. Reconciling Sponsor Support Statements and Biographical Sketches

Every proposal an investigator submits to a sponsor carries a current-and-pending support page and a biographical sketch, and the office's job is to check those documents against what the institution actually knows. A statement is recorded by the owning `Investigator` or by a `ResearchSecurityOfficer`, naming the `investigator_id`, the `project_id` of the proposal the document accompanies, the `agency_code` it was submitted to, a `document_type` of `CURRENT_AND_PENDING` or `BIOSKETCH`, the total annual `declared_person_months` the document claims (at least 0, at most two decimal places), the `declared_appointment_count` of foreign appointments it lists (at least 0), and the `declared_support_count` of outside-support entries it lists (at least 0). The record is created in `SUBMITTED` status with `applied_variant`, `institutional_person_months`, `variance_person_months`, `omission_count` and `verdict` all null. Any prior statement for the same `investigator_id`, `project_id`, `agency_code` and `document_type` whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `RECONCILED` or `INCONSISTENT` transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, which stops it contributing to the investigator's `material_discrepancy_count` and to any activation blocker; a superseded `INCONSISTENT` statement in particular no longer contributes `SUPPORT_INCONSISTENT`, so the project's `activation_blockers` are recomputed. Both callers the endpoint admits are permitted to supersede — the owning `Investigator` acting on their own record and the `ResearchSecurityOfficer` acting on anyone's — so in practice every live statement for that combination is superseded and `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUPPORT_STATEMENT` is never returned; it remains in the catalogue as the code that would apply if a caller could not supersede. Creating a statement recomputes the investigator's `material_discrepancy_count` and `disclosure_posture` and the project's `activation_blockers`. Statements can be read individually — exposing the reconciliation verdict — and listed by `investigator_id`, `project_id` and `status`; an unresolvable `statement_id` yields `ERR_SUPPORT_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

Reconciliation is a `ResearchSecurityOfficer` act that takes a single `variant`, either `STRICT_AGENCY` or `INSTITUTIONAL_ROLLUP`, and the choice is recorded on the statement as `applied_variant`. A first reconcile out of `SUBMITTED` requires that the investigator hold at least one disclosure whose review status is `CERTIFIED`, else `ERR_NO_CERTIFIED_DISCLOSURE`; re-runs from `RECONCILED` or from `INCONSISTENT` carry no precondition at all. Reconciling a `SUPERSEDED` statement is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_TRANSITION`.

The two variants differ in four respects, and the difference is material enough that identical data can produce opposite verdicts. Under `STRICT_AGENCY` — the view a single sponsor takes of its own current-and-pending page — the institutional truth counts only those personnel rows of this investigator whose `status` is `ACTIVE` **and** whose project's `agency_code` equals the statement's `agency_code`; it draws disclosure items from exactly one source, the single newest disclosure of this investigator whose review status is `CERTIFIED`, selected by the latest `submitted_at`, breaking ties by the latest `created_at` and any remaining tie by the later insertion sequence, with a null `submitted_at` sorting before any real instant; it counts only items whose `item_type` is `FOREIGN_APPOINTMENT`; and it never converts outside hours into person-months, so the outside contribution is 0.00. Under `INSTITUTIONAL_ROLLUP` — the institution's total-effort view, used for a biographical-sketch consistency check — the institutional truth counts every personnel row of this investigator whose `status` is `ACTIVE` regardless of agency; it draws items from every disclosure of this investigator whose review status is neither `DRAFT` nor `REJECTED`; it counts items whose `item_type` is `FOREIGN_APPOINTMENT`, `TALENT_PROGRAM` or `VISITING_SCHOLAR`; and it converts outside effort into person-months by summing `annual_effort_hours` over those counted items whose `status` is not `WITHDRAWN` and dividing by 160 hours per person-month, rounded half up to two decimal places.

From there the arithmetic is common to both variants. `institutional_person_months` is the sum of `committed_person_months` across the selected personnel rows, plus the outside person-months figure derived above, with the total rounded half up to two decimal places. `variance_person_months` is `institutional_person_months` minus the document's `declared_person_months`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `omission_count` counts, in two buckets and never below zero per bucket, the counted non-`WITHDRAWN` items the document's own headcounts fail to account for: the appointment bucket is the number of counted items whose `item_type` is `FOREIGN_APPOINTMENT`, `TALENT_PROGRAM` or `VISITING_SCHOLAR` minus `declared_appointment_count`, floored at zero; the support bucket, which contributes only under `INSTITUTIONAL_ROLLUP` and is 0 under `STRICT_AGENCY`, is the number of non-`WITHDRAWN` items whose `item_type` is `OUTSIDE_SUPPORT` or `IN_KIND_RESOURCE` minus `declared_support_count`, floored at zero. The two floored buckets are added together.

The `verdict` follows from those two numbers, evaluated in order. If `omission_count` is at least 1, or the absolute value of `variance_person_months` is at least 1.00 person-months, the verdict is `MATERIAL_DISCREPANCY`. Otherwise, if the absolute value of `variance_person_months` is at least 0.25 person-months, the verdict is `MINOR_VARIANCE`. Otherwise the verdict is `CONSISTENT`. A verdict of `CONSISTENT` drives the statement's `status` to `RECONCILED`; both `MINOR_VARIANCE` and `MATERIAL_DISCREPANCY` drive it to `INCONSISTENT`. To make the variant difference concrete: an organization-heavy investigator whose talent-programme and visiting-scholar relationships sit outside the sponsor's own portfolio can reconcile as `CONSISTENT` under `STRICT_AGENCY` and as `MATERIAL_DISCREPANCY` under `INSTITUTIONAL_ROLLUP` on exactly the same stored data, because the rollup raises `institutional_person_months` and widens the counted item set.

Whichever direction the run lands, the same five fields — `applied_variant`, `institutional_person_months`, `variance_person_months`, `omission_count` and `verdict` — are stored or overwritten on the statement. A run out of `SUBMITTED` that yields `CONSISTENT` moves the statement to `RECONCILED` and recomputes the investigator's `disclosure_posture`. A run out of `SUBMITTED` that yields a variance moves it to `INCONSISTENT`, recomputes `disclosure_posture` — an inconsistent statement can by itself drive `REVIEW_REQUIRED` through `material_discrepancy_count` — and recomputes the statement's project's `activation_blockers`, where `SUPPORT_INCONSISTENT` now appears. Because reconciliation is re-runnable in both directions, `INCONSISTENT → RECONCILED` (after the underlying disclosures were fixed) and `RECONCILED → INCONSISTENT` (after new evidence surfaced) are both reachable and both unconditioned; each overwrites the stored reconciliation fields and recomputes the investigator's `disclosure_posture` and the project's `activation_blockers`. Every reconcile also recomputes the investigator's `material_discrepancy_count` and `risk_score`.

### 3.6. Counterparty Screening, Subaward Decisions and the Security Dimension of an Award

A `ResearchSecurityOfficer` proposes a subaward by naming the `project_id` it belongs to, the `organization_id` of the proposed collaborating entity, a `scope` summarising the statement of work, and an `amount_cents` that must be strictly greater than 0. The subaward is created in `PENDING` status with `applied_screening_variant`, `screening_designation`, `screening_matched_organization_id`, `screening_match_depth`, `screening_risk_points`, `mitigation_justification`, `executed_at` and `terminated_at` all null. Creation recomputes the project's `activation_blockers`, and because an unscreened counterparty is an open question, `SUBAWARD_BLOCKED` enters that list while the subaward remains `PENDING`. Subawards can be read individually and listed by `project_id`, `organization_id` and `status`; an unresolvable `subaward_id` yields `ERR_SUBAWARD_NOT_FOUND`.

Screening is an officer act taking a single `variant` of `DIRECT_MATCH` or `OWNERSHIP_TRANSITIVE`, recorded on the subaward as `applied_screening_variant`; it is admissible only while the subaward is still `PENDING`, and a screen requested against a subaward in any other status — including re-screening one already `CLEARED` or `BLOCKED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SUBAWARD_TRANSITION`, the re-screen of a designation change being a system cascade rather than something an officer can ask for. Under `DIRECT_MATCH` the screen compares only the named counterparty against the restricted-entity lists: it reads the counterparty's `own_designation`, always reports a `screening_match_depth` of 0, sets `screening_matched_organization_id` to the counterparty itself, and never inspects ancestors — so a counterparty whose `own_designation` is `NONE` always clears with 0 risk points no matter who owns it. Under `OWNERSHIP_TRANSITIVE` the screen honours ownership imputation through controlling parents: it reads the counterparty's `effective_designation`, reports that organization's `effective_designation_depth` as `screening_match_depth`, and sets `screening_matched_organization_id` to the chain member at that hop distance — which is the counterparty itself when the depth is 0.

Whichever designation the variant resolves, the outcome follows its strictness rank from §2.10. A rank of 2 or higher — that is, `ENTITY_LIST`, `FOREIGN_MILITARY`, `MALIGN_TALENT_PROGRAM` or `SDN` — yields an outcome of `BLOCKED`, with `screening_risk_points` equal to a base of 40 plus 10 multiplied by the strictness rank. A rank of exactly 1, meaning `UNVERIFIED_END_USER`, yields `FLAGGED` with `screening_risk_points` of 20. Anything else, meaning `NONE`, yields `CLEARED` with `screening_risk_points` of 0. The variant choice is therefore load-bearing: an organization whose `own_designation` is `NONE` but which is held 60 percent by an `SDN` parent screens as `CLEARED` with 0 risk points and depth 0 under `DIRECT_MATCH`, and as `BLOCKED` with 90 risk points and depth 1 under `OWNERSHIP_TRANSITIVE`, on identical stored data.

Every screen stores `applied_screening_variant`, `screening_designation`, `screening_matched_organization_id`, `screening_match_depth` and `screening_risk_points`, then moves the subaward from `PENDING` to the outcome state. `PENDING → CLEARED` and `PENDING → FLAGGED` each recompute the project's `activation_blockers` and do nothing further. `PENDING → BLOCKED` recomputes `activation_blockers` so that `SUBAWARD_BLOCKED` enters the list, and then — if the project's `security_status` is `CLEARED` — suspends that project with a `security_status_reason` of `SUBAWARD_BLOCKED`, which in turn suspends the project's grants in `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE` and recomputes its `active_grant_count` and `controlled_area_posture`. The same `BLOCKED` effects, in full, apply to the two system-driven paths `CLEARED → BLOCKED` and `FLAGGED → BLOCKED`, which fire when a designation change elsewhere in the ownership graph makes the counterparty's `effective_designation` restricted.

Officer decisions on a screened subaward come through a `to_status`. Mitigation is admissible from `FLAGGED → MITIGATED` and from `BLOCKED → MITIGATED`, and both paths carry exactly the same two conditions: `screening_designation` must not be `SDN`, else `ERR_RESTRICTED_ENTITY_BLOCKED` — a specially designated national is never mitigable, only terminated — and a `mitigation_justification` must be supplied, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Mitigating from `FLAGGED` recomputes the project's `activation_blockers`; mitigating from `BLOCKED` recomputes them too, and there `SUBAWARD_BLOCKED` leaves the list. Execution is admissible from `CLEARED → EXECUTED` and from `MITIGATED → EXECUTED`, and both paths require the project's `security_status` to be `CLEARED`, else `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_CLEARED`; both set `executed_at` to the current logical clock. Termination is admissible from `PENDING`, `CLEARED`, `FLAGGED`, `BLOCKED`, `MITIGATED` and `EXECUTED`, either by officer request or as a system cascade from an award's closure, withdrawal or security termination; every termination path, whichever state it leaves and whether officer-driven or cascaded, stamps `terminated_at` with the current logical clock — a subaward terminated straight out of `PENDING` carries a `terminated_at` just as an `EXECUTED` one does — termination out of `BLOCKED` recomputes `activation_blockers` such that `SUBAWARD_BLOCKED` leaves the list, and every termination path recomputes the project's `activation_blockers`. `TERMINATED` is terminal. Any subaward transition not declared above — mitigating an already-`EXECUTED` subaward, for instance, or anything at all out of `TERMINATED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SUBAWARD_TRANSITION`.

The security dimension of an award is driven exclusively by a `ResearchSecurityOfficer` supplying a `to_status` of `CLEARED`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`. Initial clearance, `PENDING_CLEARANCE → CLEARED`, requires two things, checked in the §2.8 order of posture before screening: no investigator holding an `ACTIVE` personnel row on the project may have a `disclosure_posture` of `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_INVESTIGATOR_SUSPENDED`; and no subaward on the project may be in `PENDING`, `FLAGGED` or `BLOCKED`, else `ERR_SUBAWARD_SCREENING_OPEN`. On success `security_status_reason` becomes `OFFICER_ACTION`, and both `activation_blockers` and `controlled_area_posture` are recomputed.

Suspension, `CLEARED → SUSPENDED`, has no preconditions and arrives either by officer request or as a system cascade — from a subaward that became `BLOCKED`, from an investigator who became `SUSPENDED`, or from an incident notification. `security_status_reason` records which: `OFFICER_ACTION` for the officer-driven call, and `SUBAWARD_BLOCKED`, `INVESTIGATOR_SUSPENDED` or `INCIDENT_NOTIFICATION` for the three system cascades respectively. Suspension then transitions every access grant on the project whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` with a `status_reason` of `PROJECT_SUSPENDED`; because grants left `ACTIVE`, the project's `active_grant_count` is recomputed; because that count changed, `controlled_area_posture` is recomputed; and `activation_blockers` are recomputed, where `SECURITY_NOT_CLEARED` now enters the list.

Re-clearance, `SUSPENDED → CLEARED`, closes the clearance cycle and carries three preconditions rather than two. As with initial clearance, and in that same order, no investigator with an `ACTIVE` personnel row may have a `disclosure_posture` of `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_INVESTIGATOR_SUSPENDED`, and no subaward may be in `PENDING`, `FLAGGED` or `BLOCKED`, else `ERR_SUBAWARD_SCREENING_OPEN`. Additionally, and last of the three, the project must carry no security incident whose `status` is `REPORTED`, `INVESTIGATING` or `SUBSTANTIATED`, else `ERR_OPEN_INCIDENTS`; this incident gate is unique to re-clearance and is not applied on the initial `PENDING_CLEARANCE → CLEARED` path. On success `security_status_reason` becomes `OFFICER_ACTION`, and a **conditional** restore runs: every access grant on the project whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` **and** whose `status_reason` is exactly `PROJECT_SUSPENDED` is revisited in ascending `created_at` order, and each one either returns to `ACTIVE` if the capacity projection still admits it, or else transitions to `REVOKED` with a `status_reason` of `CAPACITY_UNAVAILABLE` and `revoked_at` stamped from the current logical clock. Grants that were suspended for `INVESTIGATOR_RESTRICTED` or `OFFICER_ACTION` are deliberately **not** restored. Because grants returned to `ACTIVE`, the project's `active_grant_count` is recomputed and then its `controlled_area_posture`; `activation_blockers` are recomputed as well.

Security termination is admissible from `CLEARED → TERMINATED` and from `SUSPENDED → TERMINATED`, neither with preconditions, and the two behave identically: every access grant on the project whose `status` is `REQUESTED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` transitions to `REVOKED` with a `status_reason` of `PROJECT_TERMINATED`; because grants left `ACTIVE`, the project's `active_grant_count` is recomputed and then its `controlled_area_posture`; and every subaward on the project whose `status` is not already `TERMINATED` transitions to `TERMINATED` with its `terminated_at` stamped from the current logical clock. Security termination leaves `security_status_reason` exactly as the previous transition left it — it is not reset to `NONE` and not set to `OFFICER_ACTION` — so a project terminated out of `SUSPENDED` keeps recording why it was suspended. `TERMINATED` is terminal on the security dimension. A security transition that is not declared from the project's current security state — and equally an award-status transition not declared from its current funding state — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_TRANSITION`.

### 3.7. Physical and Device Security: Foreign Travel and Controlled-Area Access

Foreign travel is registered before it happens, by the travelling `Investigator` or by a `ResearchSecurityOfficer` acting for them. A registration names the `investigator_id`, a `destination_country_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, a `depart_on` instant, a `return_on` instant that must be on or after `depart_on` (else `ERR_INVALID_TRAVEL_WINDOW`, HTTP 422), and a `purpose` of `CONFERENCE`, `COLLABORATION`, `TEACHING` or `RECRUITMENT`. It may additionally name the `project_id` the trip is charged to (null when it is not charged to a project), the `host_organization_id` of the foreign host (null when there is no foreign host), and the boolean `carries_institutional_device`, which defaults to true. The record is created in `DRAFT` status with `device_decision_recorded_at` null, and the device projection below runs immediately so the traveller can see what will be required of them. Registrations can be read individually and listed by `investigator_id`, `project_id`, `host_organization_id` and `status`; an unresolvable `registration_id` yields `ERR_TRAVEL_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`.

The device requirement is derived from four independent risk flags, counted into `risk_flag_count`. One flag is raised if the `destination_country_code` is `CN`, `RU`, `IR` or `KP`. A second is raised if a `host_organization_id` is present and that organization's `effective_designation` has a strictness rank of at least 2 — that is, `ENTITY_LIST` or anything stricter. A third is raised if a `project_id` is present and that project's `sensitivity_level` is `CONTROLLED` or `RESTRICTED`. A fourth is raised if the traveller's `disclosure_posture` is `RESTRICTED` or `SUSPENDED`. The `device_requirement_tier` is then read off the flag count in this exact order, with the no-device short-circuit evaluated first: if `carries_institutional_device` is false **and** the flag count is exactly 0, the tier is `NONE`; otherwise if the flag count is 1 or fewer, the tier is `STANDARD`; otherwise if the flag count is 3 or fewer, the tier is `LOANER_REQUIRED`; otherwise, at 4 flags, the tier is `LOANER_PLUS_ESCORT`. The bands are inclusive on both ends — one flag is `STANDARD`, two and three are `LOANER_REQUIRED`, four is `LOANER_PLUS_ESCORT` — and declining to carry an institutional device only reaches `NONE` when there are no flags at all. `briefing_required` is true precisely when the tier is `LOANER_REQUIRED` or `LOANER_PLUS_ESCORT`.

Travel then moves through a status machine driven by a `to_status`. Submission, `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, is the traveller's own act: `X-User-ID` must equal the registration's investigator, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; `depart_on` must fall strictly before `return_on`, else `ERR_INVALID_TRAVEL_WINDOW`; and the trip must not collide with the traveller's other commitments. That collision test treats each trip as a **closed** interval running from `depart_on` through `return_on` inclusive, and it looks only at the same investigator's other registrations whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`. A conflict exists when the new trip's `depart_on` is on or before an existing trip's `return_on` **and** the new trip's `return_on` is on or after that existing trip's `depart_on`; if one exists the submission is refused with `ERR_TRAVEL_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Because the interval is closed, mere adjacency is a conflict: a new trip departing exactly on an existing trip's `return_on` is rejected, since a traveller cannot return and depart at the same instant. Registrations in `DRAFT`, `DENIED`, `CANCELLED` or `COMPLETED` release the window and are ignored by the test. Submission also re-runs the device projection, refreshing `risk_flag_count`, `device_requirement_tier` and `briefing_required` against current facts.

Approval, `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, belongs to a `ResearchSecurityOfficer` and carries three conditions. The traveller's `disclosure_posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_INVESTIGATOR_SUSPENDED`. If the `device_requirement_tier` is `LOANER_REQUIRED` or `LOANER_PLUS_ESCORT`, the traveller must hold a training assignment for `FOREIGN_TRAVEL_BRIEF` that is `COMPLETED` or `WAIVED`, else `ERR_TRAVEL_BRIEFING_REQUIRED`. And if the tier is `LOANER_PLUS_ESCORT` specifically, the traveller must hold a disclosure whose `plan_status` is `PLAN_ACKNOWLEDGED`, else `ERR_MANAGEMENT_PLAN_REQUIRED`. Both of those gates read the `device_requirement_tier` exactly as it is stored — as last refreshed by submission — rather than re-projecting it, so facts that changed after submission do not tighten the approval gates. On approval, `device_decision_recorded_at` is set to the current logical clock, locking in the tier that was in force at the decision, and the tier itself is not recomputed by the approval.

The remaining travel transitions are shorter. `SUBMITTED → DENIED` is an unconditioned officer act with no side effects. `SUBMITTED → CANCELLED` and `DRAFT → CANCELLED` are the traveller's own acts, requiring only that `X-User-ID` match the registration's investigator, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; cancelling out of `SUBMITTED` releases the window so that a previously conflicting registration can now be submitted. `APPROVED → CANCELLED` may be requested by the traveller or by an officer, but only while the logical clock is strictly before `depart_on`, else `ERR_TRAVEL_ALREADY_STARTED`; it too releases the window. `APPROVED → COMPLETED` is an officer act permitted only once the logical clock is on or after `return_on`, else `ERR_TRAVEL_NOT_ENDED`; it releases the window as well. `DENIED`, `CANCELLED` and `COMPLETED` are terminal, and any travel transition not declared above is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRAVEL_TRANSITION`. The endpoint admits both an `Investigator` and a `ResearchSecurityOfficer`, so the role attributions above are enforced per transition: an `Investigator` caller who does not own the registration is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` before the `to_status` is read, and a caller whose role is not among the roles that particular declared transition names — an officer submitting on the traveller's behalf, or a traveller approving their own trip — is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403) after the transition is confirmed declared and before any of the transition's own conditions run.

Controlled research area access is granted per project and per investigator, and only a `ResearchSecurityOfficer` may request one. A request names the `project_id` whose controlled area is being entered, the `investigator_id`, a `valid_from` instant, a `valid_until` instant that must fall strictly after `valid_from` (else `ERR_INVALID_GRANT_WINDOW`, HTTP 422), and the `expected_project_version` demanded by §2.7 — on success the project's `version` increments by one. The grant is created in `REQUESTED` status with a `status_reason` of `NONE` and `activated_at` and `revoked_at` null, and its `peak_concurrency_at_request` is stored from an informational run of the capacity projection **without** being enforced at this point. Only one unrevoked grant may exist for a given project and investigator pair; a second is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACCESS_GRANT`. Grants can be read individually — exposing `status`, `status_reason` and `peak_concurrency_at_request` — and listed by `project_id`, `investigator_id` and `status`; an unresolvable `grant_id` yields `ERR_ACCESS_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`.

Capacity is projected over **half-open** windows running from `valid_from` up to but excluding `valid_until`, which is deliberately the opposite convention from travel: two grants that merely touch do not overlap. The projection considers the other grants on the same project whose `status` is `REQUESTED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` — `REVOKED` grants never occupy capacity — and among those, only the ones whose window genuinely overlaps the candidate's, meaning the other grant's `valid_from` falls strictly before the candidate's `valid_until` and the candidate's `valid_from` falls strictly before the other grant's `valid_until`. The sweep then evaluates occupancy at the candidate's own `valid_from` and at each overlapping grant's `valid_from` that falls strictly inside the candidate's window; sweeping window starts alone is sufficient because occupancy can only rise at a start. At each swept instant, occupancy is 1 — the candidate always counts as one of the holders — plus the number of overlapping grants whose `valid_from` is on or before that instant and whose `valid_until` is strictly after it. The largest occupancy found across the swept instants is the peak, and it is stored as `peak_concurrency_at_request`. The check fails only on strictly greater: a peak exactly equal to the project's `controlled_area_capacity` is admissible, and a peak of one more than the capacity is refused with `ERR_AREA_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).

Activating a grant, `REQUESTED → ACTIVE`, is an officer act with a `to_status` of `ACTIVE` and is the single most heavily gated operation in the system. The project's `security_status` must be `CLEARED`, else `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_CLEARED`. The grant's investigator must hold an `ACTIVE` personnel row on the grant's project, else `ERR_PERSONNEL_NOT_ACTIVE`. That investigator's `disclosure_posture` must be `CLEAR` or `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, else `ERR_INVESTIGATOR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_ACCESS`. The investigator must hold a training assignment for `CONTROLLED_AREA_SAFETY` that is `COMPLETED` or `WAIVED`, else `ERR_AREA_TRAINING_INCOMPLETE`. And the capacity projection must not exceed the project's `controlled_area_capacity`, else `ERR_AREA_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On success, `activated_at` is set to the current logical clock, `status_reason` returns to `NONE`, the peak the projection just computed replaces the stored `peak_concurrency_at_request` — so that field always reports the most recent projection rather than the one taken at request time — the project's `active_grant_count` increments by 1, and because that count changed, the project's `controlled_area_posture` is recomputed.

A grant may oscillate between active and suspended any number of times. `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` has no preconditions and arrives either by officer request or as a system cascade from project suspension or investigator restriction; `status_reason` records `OFFICER_ACTION` for the officer call and `PROJECT_SUSPENDED` or `INVESTIGATOR_RESTRICTED` for the two cascades. It decrements the project's `active_grant_count` by 1 and recomputes `controlled_area_posture`. `REQUESTED → SUSPENDED` arises from those same system cascades and, being a declared transition on the officer endpoint too, may equally be requested directly by an officer, in which case `status_reason` records `OFFICER_ACTION`; either way it leaves `active_grant_count` unchanged, because a merely requested grant was never counted. `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` closes the cycle, arriving either by officer request or through the conditional restore that runs when a project is re-cleared; an officer-requested restore requires the project's `security_status` to be `CLEARED`, else `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_CLEARED`, and the investigator's `disclosure_posture` to be `CLEAR` or `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, else `ERR_INVESTIGATOR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_ACCESS`, and the `CONTROLLED_AREA_SAFETY` training to still be satisfied, else `ERR_AREA_TRAINING_INCOMPLETE`, and the capacity projection to fit. Unlike activation it does **not** re-check the personnel roster, so a restore succeeds even where the investigator's row is no longer `ACTIVE`, and it does not re-stamp `activated_at`, which keeps naming the first activation. The cascaded restore that rides on re-clearance applies the capacity projection alone — it revisits neither posture nor training, since re-clearance has already vetted the project's people. Where an officer's own restore request would exceed capacity it is refused with `ERR_AREA_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; where the system's restore cascade would exceed capacity the grant is instead sent to `REVOKED` with a `status_reason` of `CAPACITY_UNAVAILABLE` rather than the request failing. A successful restore resets `status_reason` to `NONE`, increments the project's `active_grant_count` by 1, and recomputes `controlled_area_posture`; an officer-requested restore additionally overwrites `peak_concurrency_at_request` with the peak it just projected, while the cascaded restore leaves that field as it stood.

Revocation is terminal and reachable from all three non-terminal states, by officer request or by system cascade from a capacity-unavailable restore, a personnel removal or a project termination; `status_reason` accordingly records `OFFICER_ACTION`, `CAPACITY_UNAVAILABLE`, `PERSONNEL_REMOVED` or `PROJECT_TERMINATED`. `ACTIVE → REVOKED` sets `revoked_at` to the current logical clock, decrements the project's `active_grant_count` by 1, and recomputes `controlled_area_posture`. `SUSPENDED → REVOKED` sets `revoked_at` but leaves `active_grant_count` unchanged, because a suspended grant was already decremented when it left `ACTIVE`. `REQUESTED → REVOKED` likewise leaves `active_grant_count` unchanged, because a requested grant was never counted, and it leaves `revoked_at` null as well: only a grant that had reached `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` carries a revocation timestamp. Any grant transition not declared above, including anything out of `REVOKED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_GRANT_TRANSITION`.

### 3.8. Incident Investigation, Agency Notification and Closure

When the research security office learns of a possible violation it opens a security incident. Only a `ResearchSecurityOfficer` may do so, naming the `investigator_id` the allegation concerns, an `incident_type` of `UNDISCLOSED_APPOINTMENT`, `UNDISCLOSED_TALENT_PROGRAM`, `UNREPORTED_TRAVEL`, `RESTRICTED_ENTITY_COLLABORATION` or `DEVICE_COMPROMISE`, the `discovered_on` instant at which the office learned of the issue, and a `summary` narrating the allegation. Two optional links may be supplied: a `disclosure_id`, null when the incident is not tied to a disclosure, and a `project_id`, null when no award is implicated. The incident is created in `REPORTED` status with `finding` set to `NONE` and with `severity_points`, `severity`, `lead_investigator_user_id`, `substantiated_at`, `notification_due_on`, `agency_notified_on`, `notification_reference` and `closed_at` all null and `notified_late` false. When a `project_id` was supplied, that project's `open_incident_count` — the number of security incidents on the project whose `status` is `REPORTED`, `INVESTIGATING` or `SUBSTANTIATED` — is recomputed. Incidents can be read individually, exposing `finding`, `severity`, `notification_due_on` and `notified_late`, and listed with filters on `investigator_id`, `disclosure_id`, `project_id` and `status`; an unresolvable `incident_id` yields `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`.

The incident status machine is driven by an officer supplying a `to_status` and, on one path only, a `lead_investigator_user_id`. Opening an investigation, `REPORTED → INVESTIGATING`, requires the `lead_investigator_user_id` to be present and a non-empty string, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and because that is a step 6 shape rule it is checked before the transition itself is looked up, so asking for `INVESTIGATING` from a state that does not declare it while also omitting the lead reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_INVALID_INCIDENT_TRANSITION`; a `lead_investigator_user_id` supplied alongside any other `to_status` is validated as a string and then ignored. The value is stored on the incident and from that moment locks the segregation-of-duties check that governs the eventual notification. An allegation may also be dismissed straight out of the gate: `REPORTED → UNSUBSTANTIATED` has no preconditions at all — nobody has been named lead yet, so no lead check is possible — and it merely sets `finding` to `UNSUBSTANTIATED` and recomputes the project's `open_incident_count`. The same dismissal from `INVESTIGATING → UNSUBSTANTIATED` is deliberately stricter: the calling `X-User-ID` must equal the stored `lead_investigator_user_id`, else `ERR_NOT_INCIDENT_LEAD`. Beyond that it behaves identically, setting `finding` to `UNSUBSTANTIATED` and recomputing `open_incident_count`, and it explicitly leaves the investigator's `disclosure_posture` untouched — an unsubstantiated finding never restricts anyone.

Substantiation, `INVESTIGATING → SUBSTANTIATED`, likewise requires the caller to be the stored lead, else `ERR_NOT_INCIDENT_LEAD`. It sets `finding` to `SUBSTANTIATED` and `substantiated_at` to the current logical clock, and it freezes the severity assessment described below. It then runs a four-hop cascade. The project's `open_incident_count` is recomputed. Because a substantiated finding now exists, the investigator's `disclosure_posture` is recomputed, which takes them to `RESTRICTED` — or all the way to `SUSPENDED` when the frozen `severity` is `CRITICAL`. Because that posture has left `CLEAR`/`REVIEW_REQUIRED`, every access grant belonging to that investigator whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE` transitions to `SUSPENDED` with a `status_reason` of `INVESTIGATOR_RESTRICTED`, spanning every project the investigator holds access on. Because those grants left `ACTIVE`, each affected project's `active_grant_count` is recomputed and then its `controlled_area_posture`. And when the `severity` is `CRITICAL`, one further hop fires: every project that has an `ACTIVE` personnel row for that investigator and whose `security_status` is `CLEARED` is suspended with a `security_status_reason` of `INCIDENT_NOTIFICATION`, which drags its own grant-suspension cascade behind it.

The severity assessment is computed once, at the substantiation moment, and never moves afterwards. It begins from the incident type: 20 points for `UNDISCLOSED_APPOINTMENT`, 40 for `UNDISCLOSED_TALENT_PROGRAM`, 15 for `UNREPORTED_TRAVEL`, 35 for `RESTRICTED_ENTITY_COLLABORATION` and 30 for `DEVICE_COMPROMISE`. Three surcharges may follow. If a `project_id` is present and that project's `award_status` is `ACTIVE`, 15 points are added. If a `project_id` is present and that project's `sensitivity_level` is `RESTRICTED`, 10 further points are added. Then the system counts the investigator's **other** incidents — excluding this one — whose `finding` is `SUBSTANTIATED`, multiplies that count by 10, caps the product at 20, and adds the result; so one prior adds 10, two priors add 20, and three or more priors still add only 20. The total is stored as `severity_points`, and `severity` is banded off it with inclusive floors: below 20 is `LOW`, below 40 is `MODERATE`, below 60 is `HIGH`, and 60 or above is `CRITICAL`. The floors bite exactly where they are written — an `UNREPORTED_TRAVEL` incident on an active, `RESTRICTED` project scores 15 plus 15 plus 10 for exactly 40 points and is therefore `HIGH`, while one point less would be `MODERATE`. Finally `notification_due_on` is set to `substantiated_at` plus the window the band earns: 60 days for `LOW`, 30 for `MODERATE`, 10 for `HIGH` and 5 for `CRITICAL`. Although every comparison against it is by calendar date, it is stored and returned as a full instant in the same rendering as any other timestamp — the substantiation instant advanced by whole days, keeping its time-of-day component — rather than as a bare date. Both `severity_points` and `notification_due_on` are frozen at this instant; a later change to the project's `award_status`, or a later incident added to the investigator's history, never moves an existing deadline.

Notifying the sponsor is a separate capability reserved to the `AgencyLiaison`, who supplies the `agency_notified_on` instant and the agency's `notification_reference`. It is admissible only from `SUBSTANTIATED`, and a notify call from any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INCIDENT_TRANSITION`. Two checks then apply. The caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from the stored `lead_investigator_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the officer who ran the investigation may not also certify to the sponsor that it was reported. And `agency_notified_on` must fall on or after the date of `substantiated_at`, else `ERR_INVALID_NOTIFICATION_DATE` (HTTP 422). On success the incident moves to `AGENCY_NOTIFIED`, `agency_notified_on` and `notification_reference` are stored, and `notified_late` is set to whether `agency_notified_on` is later than the frozen `notification_due_on`. That comparison is by date, so a notification landing exactly on `notification_due_on` is on time and `notified_late` stays false. Reaching `AGENCY_NOTIFIED` then recomputes the investigator's `disclosure_posture`, which becomes `SUSPENDED`; every remaining grant of that investigator in `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE` transitions to `SUSPENDED` with a `status_reason` of `INVESTIGATOR_RESTRICTED`; and each affected project's `active_grant_count`, `controlled_area_posture` and `activation_blockers` are recomputed.

Closure is a join point: both the notification branch and the remediation branch must be complete. `AGENCY_NOTIFIED → CLOSED` requires the incident's remediation leg to be finished — the incident's `disclosure_id` must not be null, the disclosure it names must have a `review_status` of `CERTIFIED`, and that disclosure must carry at least one item whose `source` is `INCIDENT_REMEDIATION` and whose `status` is not `WITHDRAWN` — else `ERR_REMEDIATION_INCOMPLETE`. On success `closed_at` is set to the current logical clock, `finding` deliberately **stays** `SUBSTANTIATED` so that the closed incident keeps contributing its 10-point prior-incident surcharge to any later incident, the project's `open_incident_count` is recomputed, and the investigator's `disclosure_posture` is recomputed but stays `SUSPENDED`, because the agency-notification history is permanent. The other approach to closure is declared purely so the join is explicit: `SUBSTANTIATED → CLOSED` is **always** rejected, with `ERR_AGENCY_NOTIFICATION_REQUIRED`, because a substantiated incident can never skip agency notification. `CLOSED` and `UNSUBSTANTIATED` are both terminal, and any incident transition not declared above — including anything at all out of those two states — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INCIDENT_TRANSITION`.

### 3.9. Training Compliance, Activation Readiness and Award Closure

A `SystemAdmin` or a `ResearchSecurityOfficer` assigns a required course by naming the `investigator_id`, a `course_code` of `RESEARCH_SECURITY_BASE`, `EXPORT_CONTROL`, `INSIDER_THREAT`, `FOREIGN_TRAVEL_BRIEF` or `CONTROLLED_AREA_SAFETY`, and a `due_on` deadline. An optional `required_for_project_id` scopes the assignment to one award; leaving it null makes the requirement institution-wide. On creation `assigned_on` is set from the current logical clock and `completed_on` and `waived_on` are null. Creation recomputes the investigator's `training_compliant`, and for a project-scoped assignment it also recomputes that project's `activation_blockers`. Assignments can be read individually and listed by `investigator_id`, `required_for_project_id` and `course_code`; an unresolvable `assignment_id` yields `ERR_TRAINING_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

An assignment has no stored status. Its `status` is derived on every read, and on every rule that consults training compliance, from its own `waived_on`, `completed_on` and `due_on` against the current logical clock, in exactly this order: if `waived_on` is not null the status is `WAIVED`; otherwise if `completed_on` is not null the status is `COMPLETED`; otherwise if the clock has reached or passed `due_on` the status is `EXPIRED`; otherwise the status is `ASSIGNED`. The deadline comparison is inclusive, so a clock sitting exactly on `due_on` reads as `EXPIRED` while one second earlier reads as `ASSIGNED`. Because `completed_on` is tested before the deadline, a completion recorded while the assignment was still live stays `COMPLETED` forever, however far the clock later advances.

Completion may be recorded by the assigned `Investigator` or by a `SystemAdmin` and takes no body; it sets `completed_on` from the current logical clock. A completion recorded after `due_on` has passed is still accepted and still yields a status of `COMPLETED`, for the same ordering reason. Waiver is reserved to a `ResearchSecurityOfficer`, also takes no body, and sets `waived_on` from the current logical clock; a waiver satisfies every gate that a completion satisfies. Neither operation checks ownership: the `Investigator` role is admitted to completion by role alone, so any investigator-role caller may record a completion against any assignment, and `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` never arises on either path. Both operations refuse to act twice: if the assignment already has either `completed_on` or `waived_on` set, the call fails with `ERR_TRAINING_ALREADY_RESOLVED`. Both recompute the investigator's `training_compliant` and, for a project-scoped assignment, the project's `activation_blockers`.

The two consumers of training data read disjoint slices of it, which is worth stating plainly. An investigator's `training_compliant` is true exactly when they have **no institution-wide** assignments — those whose `required_for_project_id` is null — whose derived status is `ASSIGNED` or `EXPIRED`; project-scoped assignments never affect it. Conversely a project's `TRAINING_INCOMPLETE` blocker looks only at assignments whose `required_for_project_id` is that project and whose derived status is `ASSIGNED` or `EXPIRED`; institution-wide assignments never raise it. An investigator can therefore be `training_compliant` while blocking an award's activation, and vice versa.

A project's `activation_blockers` list is recomputed on every change to its personnel, subawards, disclosures, support statements, training assignments or `security_status`, and is returned with the project on read. Seven independent conditions may contribute a code. `SECURITY_NOT_CLEARED` is emitted when the project's `security_status` is anything other than `CLEARED`. `DISCLOSURE_NOT_CERTIFIED` is emitted when some investigator holding an `ACTIVE` personnel row on the project has no disclosure at all whose `review_status` is `CERTIFIED`. `TRAINING_INCOMPLETE` is emitted when some assignment scoped to this project has a derived status of `ASSIGNED` or `EXPIRED`. `SUBAWARD_BLOCKED` is emitted when some subaward on the project has a `status` of `PENDING`, `FLAGGED` or `BLOCKED`. `SUPPORT_INCONSISTENT` is emitted when some support statement on the project has a `status` of `INCONSISTENT`. `PERSONNEL_MISSING_PI` is emitted when the project has no personnel row whose `role_on_project` is `PI` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. `INVESTIGATOR_SUSPENDED` is emitted when some investigator holding an `ACTIVE` personnel row has a `disclosure_posture` of `SUSPENDED`. The resulting list is always ordered by the fixed sequence `SECURITY_NOT_CLEARED`, `DISCLOSURE_NOT_CERTIFIED`, `TRAINING_INCOMPLETE`, `SUBAWARD_BLOCKED`, `SUPPORT_INCONSISTENT`, `PERSONNEL_MISSING_PI`, `INVESTIGATOR_SUSPENDED` — never in the order the conditions happened to be discovered — and no code appears twice even when several rows trigger it. A project is ready for activation exactly when the list is empty.

The funding dimension of an award is driven exclusively by a `SystemAdmin` supplying a `to_status` of `ACTIVATION_PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `CLOSED` or `WITHDRAWN`. Moving `PROPOSED → ACTIVATION_PENDING` requires at least one personnel row on the project whose `role_on_project` is `PI` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_PI_REQUIRED`; it recomputes `activation_blockers`. Moving `ACTIVATION_PENDING → ACTIVE` requires `activation_blockers` to be empty, else `ERR_ACTIVATION_BLOCKED`, whose message names the first code in the ordered list — which is why the ordering above is fixed rather than incidental; on success it recomputes `open_incident_count` and `controlled_area_posture`.

Closing an award, `ACTIVE → CLOSED`, carries two preconditions. No access grant on the project may have a `status` of `REQUESTED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_ACCESS_GRANTS_OPEN` — every grant must already be `REVOKED`. And the project's `open_incident_count` must be 0, else `ERR_OPEN_INCIDENTS`. On success every subaward on the project whose `status` is `PENDING`, `CLEARED`, `FLAGGED`, `BLOCKED`, `MITIGATED` or `EXECUTED` transitions to `TERMINATED` with `terminated_at` stamped; every personnel row whose `status` is `ACTIVE` transitions to `REMOVED` — and unlike the roster's own `ACTIVE → REMOVED` call, this cascade does **not** revoke that investigator's access grants, which is consistent with closure already requiring every grant to be `REVOKED`; because those rows left `ACTIVE`, each affected investigator's `committed_effort_pct` and `total_commitment_pct` are recomputed; and because `committed_effort_pct` changed, each affected investigator's `overcommitment_pct` and `disclosure_posture` are recomputed in turn.

Withdrawal is available from the two pre-award states and nowhere else: `PROPOSED → WITHDRAWN` and `ACTIVATION_PENDING → WITHDRAWN` both have no preconditions and both run exactly the same cascade. Every subaward on the project transitions to `TERMINATED` regardless of its current status, each with `terminated_at` stamped, and every personnel row whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` transitions to `REMOVED` — with the `ACTIVE` rows additionally triggering the same effort recompute chain of `committed_effort_pct`, `total_commitment_pct`, `overcommitment_pct` and `disclosure_posture`, while `PROPOSED` rows carried no effort and so trigger nothing. Withdrawal likewise leaves any access grants on the project exactly as they stand rather than revoking them. `CLOSED` and `WITHDRAWN` are both terminal: an active award can never be withdrawn, only closed, and neither terminal state can be reopened. Any award-status transition not declared from the project's current funding state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_TRANSITION`, the same error the security dimension uses.

## 4. API Endpoint Definitions

All endpoints live directly at the root of the API namespace; there is no version or service prefix. A path that matches no route below, and a method that the matched route does not accept, both answer with the standard error envelope carrying `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — at HTTP 404 and HTTP 405 respectively rather than the 400 the catalogue in §5 lists for that code — and a trailing slash on any path is treated as equivalent to its absence. Endpoints that create a resource return HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`; every other successful endpoint returns HTTP 200 with the affected resource, or an array of resources for list endpoints, in `data`. Every endpoint listed below is subject to the authentication, authorization, existence, validation and state checks of Section 2.8, evaluated in the order given there.
