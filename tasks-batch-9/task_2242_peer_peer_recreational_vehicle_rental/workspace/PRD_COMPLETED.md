# RV Share Marketplace API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Recreational vehicles are among the most expensive assets a household owns and among the least used: a motorhome or fifth-wheel trailer that sits in a driveway for fifty weeks a year is a depreciating liability. The RV Share Marketplace exists to convert that idle capital into rental income by matching private owners with travellers who want a rig for a week without buying one. What makes the business hard is not the matching — it is everything around it. A rig must be documented and insured before it can be advertised. A renter must be old enough, licensed cleanly enough and, for towable trailers, experienced enough to be entrusted with a thirty-foot vehicle. Money must be held before the keys change hands, metered against mileage and generator-hour allowances during the trip, reconciled afterwards, and split three ways between owner, renter and platform in a way that always balances to zero.

This document specifies the backend API that governs that entire arc. The service is mounted under `/rv-marketplace` and is organised around a small number of long-lived aggregates — owners, renters, rig listings, and bookings — surrounded by configuration catalogues (protection tiers, cancellation tiers, seasonal pricing windows, campgrounds) and by the evidentiary record of a trip (vehicle documents, inspection and trip photographs, meter readings, damage claims, ledger rows). Two properties of the design deserve emphasis up front. First, a booking carries **two independent state dimensions**: a trip dimension that tracks physical custody of the rig, and a settlement dimension that tracks money. They advance on different triggers and must never be conflated. Second, the platform is a **ledger-reconciliation system**: every money movement is an append-only signed row, and the signed rows of any single booking must sum to exactly zero at every observable moment, including after a settlement has been reversed.

The requirements below are written for the engineers who will build the service. Section 2 fixes the contract-level behaviour that every endpoint shares — headers, envelopes, pagination, identity, rounding, concurrency guards and the strict order in which a request is judged. Section 3 works through the business narrative in the order the marketplace actually operates, from onboarding a rig to closing the books on a completed trip. Sections 4 and 5 are lookup tables: the HTTP surface and the error catalogue. Where Section 3 describes a rule, Section 4 tells you which route carries it; neither section is complete without the other.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold everywhere. Where a Section 3 workflow appears to conflict with something stated here, this section governs.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). The same rejection applies when `X-User-Role` carries a value outside the four recognised role names, which are exactly `Owner`, `Renter`, `PlatformAdmin` and `ClaimsAdjuster`. Once the headers are accepted, the acting role must appear in the authorised-role list published for the requested route in Section 4; a role acting outside its list is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). The most consequential instance of that rule is deliberate: a `PlatformAdmin` may configure the marketplace, review listings and post settlements, but may **not** adjudicate a damage claim — attempting to do so yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, because the party that decides a claim must never be the party that pays it out.

Two of the four roles are backed by persisted entities. When `X-User-Role` is `Owner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Owner`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Owner` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a listing is owned when its `owner_id` equals `X-User-ID`; a booking is owned when the `owner_id` of the listing named by the booking's `listing_id` equals `X-User-ID`; and a delivery assignment or damage claim is owned when the same chain, traced through the row's `booking_id`, arrives at the acting owner. When `X-User-Role` is `Renter`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Renter`; ownership checks compare the target booking's `renter_id` — directly for booking-scoped actions, and through the row's `booking_id` for meter readings, trip photos and claim responses — to `X-User-ID`. A mismatch on either chain is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403), which is distinct from `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`: the role was permitted, the specific resource was not.

The other two roles are not entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and consequently no ownership check ever constrains a `PlatformAdmin`. When `X-User-Role` is `ClaimsAdjuster`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it but does not compare it to any entity's `id`, so an adjuster's authority over a claim derives entirely from the role, never from a relationship to the owner, the renter or the rig.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from wall-clock time, so it moves only when an operator repositions the clock.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` skips a number of leading rows and defaults to 0; it may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) rather than being silently clamped; so is a `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer literal, and so is any declared filter query parameter carrying a value outside its enumeration. Query parameters an endpoint does not declare are ignored.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Because the logical clock stands still between repositionings, rows routinely share an identical `created_at`, so a second key is always in play: rows tied on `created_at` are ordered by ascending server-assigned creation sequence — simply the order in which the rows were inserted. That insertion sequence is also the final tie-break of every other ordering rule in this document: wherever an explicitly listed ordering chain (a cascade's sibling processing order, the waitlist-promotion ranking, the meter-reading walk, the newest-driving-record selector, the seasonal-window selector, the reversal walk) leaves two rows tied, insertion order decides. Two collections rely on that default in a load-bearing way: seasonal pricing windows are read back in the same creation order that the seasonal-multiplier selector uses as its tie-break, and ledger rows are read back in the same creation order in which they were posted.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied; `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Semantics

Every resource the API creates is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix — a booking id and a listing id are indistinguishable by shape, and clients must never parse them. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the moment of creation as read from the logical clock described in §2.5. Server-minted timestamps take that three-fractional-digit form, but client-supplied dates and instants — `date_of_birth`, `expires_on`, `checked_at`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `window_start`, `window_end` and `recorded_at` — are stored and echoed back exactly as the client wrote them, never renormalised.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 and increments by one on every mutation of that resource. Owners, renters, listings, vehicle documents, protection plans, cancellation policies, pricing seasons, campgrounds, bookings, delivery assignments and damage claims all carry both fields. Four record types are append-only evidence rather than mutable state, and they carry **only** `id` and `created_at`: renter qualification checks, meter readings, trip photos and ledger entries have no `updated_at` and no `version` at all, and clients must not expect either field on them. Every free-text reason or note a transition accepts is retained on the resource that transition names and echoed on every later read of it — `decision_note` and `suspend_reason` on a listing, `cancel_reason` and `reversal_reason` on a booking, `void_reason` on a delivery assignment, and `info_request_note`, `response_note` and `withdraw_reason` on a damage claim — each present and null from the moment the resource is created until its own transition sets it, and never cleared by a later transition.

A mutation for these purposes includes any change to a computed field, not only to a client-supplied one. When a settlement recomputes an owner's `payout_balance_cents`, when a document upload flips a listing's `insurance_status`, or when a cascade decrements a campground's `scheduled_assignment_count`, the affected resource's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments — even though no client asked for that resource to change. Where a workflow in Section 3 states that a particular resource's version increments, that statement is a specification of observable behaviour, not an implementation hint.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock as the Only Source of "Now"

Every date, deadline, expiry, lookback window and elapsed-time computation in this system evaluates against a **logical clock**, never against real wall-clock time. The server's startup default logical clock is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; until an operator repositions it, every rule that asks what time it is receives that instant. A `PlatformAdmin` may reposition the clock by supplying the target instant as `to_iso8601`, a required field whose value must parse as an ISO 8601 instant or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the response's `data` carries exactly one field, `current_utc`, reporting the resulting clock. Repositioning the clock mutates no entity, refreshes no `updated_at` and bumps no `version` — it is purely a test-driving capability, and it may move the clock backwards as freely as forwards.

Because the clock is authoritative, the following are all logical-clock reads: a document's expiry comparison, a qualification's expiry comparison, the thirty-six-month driving-record lookback, the age-in-whole-years computation at a booking's start date, the hours-remaining-before-pickup computation on a cancellation, the minutes-late computation on a return, the ninety-day utilisation window on a pricing-guidance read, and the `last_reconciled_at` stamp written at settlement.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in **cents**. There are no fractional cents anywhere in the model, in any request body, or in any response. Rates and shares are expressed in **basis points** — one basis point is one ten-thousandth, so 10000 basis points is exactly the whole amount, 1200 basis points is twelve percent, and 20000 basis points is two hundred percent.

Wherever a cent amount is derived by multiplying by a basis-point figure, the product is divided by 10000 and the result is rounded to a whole number of cents using **round half up**: a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero to the next whole cent. Section 3 names each rounding site explicitly, and the placement matters. The nightly-rate computation rounds once per night *before* the nights are summed, not once after; the pricing-guidance suggestion rounds exactly once at the very end of a two-factor multiplication, not after each factor. An implementation that moves a rounding step produces a different, wrong number.

Generator hours are the single non-integer quantity in the model. They are carried to one decimal place — every generator-hour value supplied to or returned by the API is a multiple of 0.1 — and whenever billable generator hours are derived, the billable quantity is first raised to the next multiple of 0.1 (a ceiling to the nearest tenth, so 2.01 becomes 2.1 and 2.10 stays 2.1) and only then multiplied by the per-hour rate and rounded half up to whole cents.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Parent Aggregates

Two creation endpoints are guarded against acting on a stale view of their parent aggregate. Both guards work the same way: the request must carry the client's last observed `version` of the parent, and if that value does not equal the parent's current `version`, the write is refused with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (412).

Creating a delivery assignment requires `expected_booking_version` as a non-optional input field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Booking` named by `booking_id`. If the booking's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (412). This guard is load-bearing precisely because a second assignment against the same booking is legitimate product behaviour — the newer assignment supersedes the prior one, which is voided and gives its campground slot back — so nothing about the request itself reveals that the client's picture of the booking's `delivery_fee_cents`, `hold_amount_cents` and `quoted_total_cents` is out of date. Only the version check reveals it.

Uploading a vehicle document requires `expected_listing_version` as a non-optional input field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Listing` named by `listing_id`. If the listing's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the upload is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (412). Here too the reason is that the operation is destructive in a way the payload hides: a replacement document of the same kind supersedes its predecessor and can flip the listing's `insurance_status`, so a client acting on a stale listing would silently retire a document it never saw.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. In particular, none of the state-transition actions on bookings, listings, assignments or claims takes an expected-version input, and supplying one to them has no defined effect.

### 2.8. Request Evaluation Order and Identifier Resolution

Because many rules can fail on the same request, the order in which they are evaluated is part of the contract: the error a client receives must be the *first* failure in the ladder below, not an arbitrary one. Implementations must evaluate in exactly this sequence, stopping at the first failure.

1. **Header presence** — both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Header validity** — `X-User-Role` is one of `Owner`, `Renter`, `PlatformAdmin`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Role authorisation** — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised-role list in Section 4, otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
4. **Payload shape** — request body and query parameters satisfy the declared field types, enum memberships, numeric ranges and required-field presence, and pagination bounds are respected (`limit` between 1 and 100, `offset` not negative), otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint. This step also enforces the document-shape pairing: a vehicle document whose `doc_type` is `INSPECTION_PHOTO` must carry `zone` and must not carry `expires_on`, while a document whose `doc_type` is `TITLE`, `REGISTRATION` or `INSURANCE_POLICY` must carry `expires_on` and must not carry `zone`. Fields the operation does not declare are ignored rather than rejected, so a stray key — including an expected-version field offered to an endpoint that declares none — never by itself makes a request malformed.
5. **Path identifier resolution** — every identifier embedded in the URL path resolves to an existing row, validated strictly left to right, so the first unresolvable segment determines the error. The concrete code is the per-entity 404: `ERR_OWNER_NOT_FOUND` for `ownerId`, `ERR_RENTER_NOT_FOUND` for `renterId`, `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND` for `listingId`, `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND` for `documentId`, `ERR_QUALIFICATION_NOT_FOUND` for `qualificationId`, `ERR_CAMPGROUND_NOT_FOUND` for `campgroundId`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND` for `bookingId`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` for `assignmentId`, `ERR_READING_NOT_FOUND` for `readingId`, `ERR_PHOTO_NOT_FOUND` for `photoId`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` for `claimId` and `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND` for `entryId`. Applies to every path-parameterised endpoint.
6. **Body foreign-key resolution** — every foreign key carried in the request body resolves, checked in the order the fields are listed for that operation in Section 3. The concrete code is again the per-entity 404: `owner_id` yields `ERR_OWNER_NOT_FOUND`, `renter_id` yields `ERR_RENTER_NOT_FOUND`, `listing_id` yields `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND`, `protection_plan_id` yields `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `cancellation_policy_id` yields `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `campground_id` yields `ERR_CAMPGROUND_NOT_FOUND` and `booking_id` yields `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`. This step applies to the eight creation operations that carry foreign keys: listing creation, vehicle-document upload, qualification recording, booking request, delivery-assignment creation, meter-reading recording, trip-photo upload and damage-claim intake.
7. **Resource ownership** — for the two entity-backed roles, the acting `X-User-ID` owns the target as described in §2.1, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to every creation, transition and adjustment endpoint whose target is `Owner`- or `Renter`-scoped — including listing creation, where the body's `owner_id` must equal the acting owner, and booking request, where the body's `renter_id` must equal the acting renter — and, among reads, to exactly three: the single-owner read, the single-renter read and the pricing-guidance read. Every other read is deliberately unfiltered: the single-resource reads of listings, vehicle documents, qualification checks, campgrounds, bookings, delivery assignments, meter readings, trip photos, damage claims and ledger rows, and every list endpoint without exception, perform no ownership check and return rows irrespective of who owns them.
8. **Parent version** — where the endpoint declares an expected-parent-version input, it equals the parent's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (412). Applies to delivery-assignment creation and vehicle-document upload only.
9. **Booking trip state** — the booking's `trip_status` is a legal origin for the requested trip transition, otherwise `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to owner approval, renter confirmation, handover, return, settlement and cancellation.
10. **Booking settlement state** — the booking's `settlement_status` is a legal origin for the requested settlement transition, otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to settlement and to settlement reversal.
11. **Listing state** — the listing's `listing_status` is a legal origin for the requested listing transition, otherwise `ERR_LISTING_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to submit-for-review, the review decision, suspension, reinstatement and archival. Two additional conditions carry the same code: archival additionally requires that no booking on the listing sits in `REQUESTED`, `OWNER_APPROVED`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_TRIP` or `RETURNED`, and reinstatement additionally requires that the owner's `owner_penalty_points` is strictly below the twelve-point suspension threshold.
12. **Claim state** — the claim's `status` is a legal origin for the requested claim transition, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to opening review, requesting information, responding to an information request, adjudication and withdrawal — and also to settlement, which carries the same code when a claim on the booking is still sitting in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED`.
13. **Assignment state** — the delivery assignment's `status` is a legal origin for the requested transition, otherwise `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to voiding an assignment, and to handover, which carries the same code when a booking whose `delivery_option` is `CAMPGROUND_DROPOFF` has no `SCHEDULED` assignment.
14. **Listing bookable** — the listing referenced by the request has `listing_status` equal to `LISTED`, otherwise `ERR_LISTING_NOT_BOOKABLE` (409). Applies to booking request, owner approval and renter confirmation.
15. **Claim window open** — at damage-claim intake the booking's `settlement_status` is either `RECONCILING` or `DISPUTED`; at every other claim-workflow operation it is exactly `DISPUTED`. Otherwise `ERR_BOOKING_SETTLEMENT_CLOSED` (409). Applies to claim intake, opening review, requesting information, responding and adjudication — five operations in total. A booking already `SETTLED` or `REFUNDED` accepts no claim activity whatsoever.
16. **Renter age** — `ERR_RENTER_AGE_BELOW_MINIMUM` (422), at booking request.
17. **Driving-record validity** — `ERR_DRIVING_RECORD_INVALID` (422), at booking request.
18. **Driving-record point ceiling** — `ERR_DRIVING_RECORD_POINTS_EXCEEDED` (422), at booking request.
19. **Towing experience present** — `ERR_TOWING_EXPERIENCE_MISSING` (422), at booking request.
20. **Towing tier sufficient** — `ERR_TOWING_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` (422), at booking request.
21. **Renter not blocked** — `ERR_RENTER_BLOCKED` (422), at booking request.
22. **Listing insurance verified** — the listing's `insurance_status` is `VERIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_LISTING_INSURANCE_NOT_VERIFIED` (422). Applies to three operations: booking request, an approving review decision, and reinstatement.
23. **Photo baseline present** — `ERR_PHOTO_BASELINE_MISSING` (422), at damage-claim intake.
24. **Listing dates available** — `ERR_LISTING_DATES_UNAVAILABLE` (409), at booking request.
25. **Drop-off window free** — `ERR_DROPOFF_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409), at delivery-assignment creation.
26. **Date range sane** — `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` (422). Applies to booking request and delivery-assignment creation.
27. **Rig fits the campground** — `ERR_RIG_LENGTH_EXCEEDS_CAMPGROUND` (422), at delivery-assignment creation.
28. **Inspection photo coverage** — `ERR_INSPECTION_PHOTOS_INCOMPLETE` (422), at submit-for-review.
29. **Required documents present** — `ERR_REQUIRED_DOCUMENT_MISSING` (422), at submit-for-review.
30. **Owner in good standing** — the owner's `status` is `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_OWNER_SUSPENDED` (422). Applies to two operations: submit-for-review and owner approval of a booking.
31. **Meter monotonicity** — `ERR_METER_READING_REGRESSION` (422), at meter-reading recording.
32. **Information-round budget** — `ERR_INFO_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED` (422), at requesting information.
33. **Override justified** — `ERR_ADJUDICATION_OVERRIDE_REASON_REQUIRED` (422), at adjudication.
34. **Plan covers the class** — `ERR_PLAN_CLASS_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422), at booking request.
35. **Delivery offered** — `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_OFFERED` (422), at booking request.
36. **Delivery applicable** — `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_APPLICABLE` (422), at delivery-assignment creation.
37. **Meter readings complete** — `ERR_METER_READINGS_INCOMPLETE` (422). Applies to handover and return.
38. **Trip photos complete** — `ERR_TRIP_PHOTOS_INCOMPLETE` (422). Applies to handover and return.
39. **Renter solvent** — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RENTER_FUNDS` (402). Applies to three operations: renter confirmation, settlement and wallet adjustment.

The exact condition behind each of items 16 through 39 is stated in the Section 3 subsection that owns the workflow. Note the consequences of the ordering: a booking request whose dates collide with a live booking reports `ERR_LISTING_DATES_UNAVAILABLE` even if the requested range is also inverted, because the collision check sits ahead of the range check; and a booking request from a renter who is both under-age and blocked reports `ERR_RENTER_AGE_BELOW_MINIMUM`, because age precedes the penalty gate.

### 2.9. Cascade Propagation Across the Marketplace

Several operations in this system do far more than change the state of the resource they name. Suspending a listing, cancelling a booking as its owner, adjusting an owner's penalty points, and handing a rig over can each set off a chain of consequences that reaches other bookings, other renters' wallets, campground capacity and other bookings' quotes. These chains are causal and ordered: each level fires because the previous level fired, and no level may be skipped because its inputs "look unchanged".

Two ordering rules make the chains deterministic. When a cascade must process a set of sibling bookings, they are processed in ascending `created_at` order. When a cascade must process a set of sibling listings, they are likewise processed in ascending `created_at` order. Every resource that a cascade touches — the booking whose `trip_status` it flips, the renter whose `wallet_credit_cents` it credits, the campground whose `scheduled_assignment_count` it decrements, the owner whose `listed_listing_count` it adjusts — has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as if a client had asked for that change directly.

Cascades are also the mechanism by which the platform, acting as the system rather than as a named operator, may transition a resource with no client request naming it. A booking may find itself `CANCELLED_BY_PLATFORM` because a listing above it was suspended; a waitlisted delivery assignment may find itself `SCHEDULED` because an unrelated assignment released a slot; a booking's `settlement_status` may return from `DISPUTED` to `RECONCILING` because the last open claim was decided. Clients must treat such server-initiated transitions as normal.

### 2.10. The Per-Booking Ledger Invariant

Every movement of money in this marketplace is recorded as an append-only `LedgerEntry` row scoped to exactly one booking through its `booking_id`. A row names the side of the marketplace it lands on in `party`, which is `RENTER`, `OWNER` or `PLATFORM`; it names the kind of movement in `entry_type`; it carries a signed `amount_cents`, where a positive value credits that party and a negative value debits it; and it carries a `memo`, a human-readable reason recorded by whichever transition produced the row. A row that reverses an earlier row records the reversed row's identifier in `reversal_of_entry_id`; forward rows leave `reversal_of_entry_id` null, and the field is not a foreign key to any other entity type.

The recognised values of `entry_type` are `TRIP_CHARGE`, `OWNER_PAYOUT`, `SERVICE_FEE`, `PROTECTION_PREMIUM`, `OWNER_CANCEL_FEE`, `DAMAGE_RENTER_CHARGE`, `DAMAGE_OWNER_CREDIT`, `DAMAGE_PLAN_PAYMENT`, `TRIP_CHARGE_REVERSAL`, `OWNER_PAYOUT_REVERSAL`, `SERVICE_FEE_REVERSAL` and `PROTECTION_PREMIUM_REVERSAL`.

The governing invariant is conservation: **for any booking, the sum of `amount_cents` over all of its ledger rows — every party, every entry type, forward and reversal rows alike, with no status filter of any kind, because ledger rows have no status — is exactly zero at all times.** Every transition that appends rows must append a balanced set. The invariant is checked after every ledger-producing transition and again after a settlement reversal. Its sharpest expression is the compensation guarantee: a settlement followed by its reversal takes a booking's ledger row count from four to eight, leaves the booking's ledger sum at zero, and returns the owner's `payout_balance_cents` to the exact value it held before the settlement.

An owner's `payout_balance_cents` is not stored independently of the ledger; it is the sum of `amount_cents` over every ledger row whose `party` is `OWNER` and whose booking belongs to a listing that owner owns. Whenever ledger rows are appended or reversed, that sum is recomputed; the owner's `updated_at` and `version` move on that recomputation only when the recomputed sum actually differs from the value already stored, so a recomputation that lands on the same number is not by itself a mutation.

### 2.11. Campground Slot Conservation

Campground capacity is the second conserved quantity in the system. A campground's `scheduled_assignment_count` is by definition the number of delivery assignments referencing that campground whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` — assignments in `REQUESTED`, `WAITLISTED`, `COMPLETED` or `VOIDED` are excluded from the count entirely. Any transition into or out of `SCHEDULED` adjusts the count so that this identity holds after every delivery-assignment status change.

Capacity itself is enforced per calendar date, not per campground overall. For a campground and a UTC calendar date, the available slots are the campground's `daily_dropoff_capacity` minus the number of assignments on that campground with that `dropoff_date` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`. That figure may never go negative — the system schedules an assignment only while a slot remains, and waitlists it otherwise.

The compensation guarantee here mirrors the ledger's: scheduling an assignment and then voiding it, on a campground whose waitlist for that drop-off date is empty, returns `scheduled_assignment_count` to exactly its pre-scheduling value.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Marketplace Participants, Wallets and Configuration Catalogues

Nobody self-registers on this platform. A `PlatformAdmin` onboards an RV owner by supplying a `display_name` between 1 and 80 characters, an `email` that must contain an at-sign and must be unique across all owners, and a `payout_state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters identifying the US state of the owner's payout account. Every uniqueness and cross-field constraint stated anywhere in this subsection — a duplicate owner or renter `email`, a second protection plan or cancellation policy on a `tier` already defined, an `end_month_day` sorting before its `start_month_day`, a `date_of_birth` not strictly before the clock date — is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) at step 4 of the ladder. The new owner opens with `status` set to `ACTIVE`, `owner_penalty_points` at 0, `payout_balance_cents` at 0 and `listed_listing_count` at 0. Owner `status` takes one of two values, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and a `SUSPENDED` owner may neither submit a listing for review nor have a suspended listing reinstated. `listed_listing_count` tracks the number of that owner's listings whose `listing_status` is `LISTED`, and `owner_penalty_points` — which may never fall below 0 — accumulates from owner-initiated cancellations and from operator adjustments.

Renter onboarding is likewise operator-driven, taking a `display_name` of 1 to 80 characters, a unique `email` containing an at-sign, a `date_of_birth` as a `YYYY-MM-DD` date that must fall strictly before the current logical-clock date, and a `license_state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters naming the state that issued the licence on file. A fresh renter opens with `wallet_credit_cents` at 0, `renter_penalty_points` at 0, `driving_record_status` at `NONE` and `towing_verified_tier` at `NONE`; `driving_record_points` likewise starts at 0. The wallet, `wallet_credit_cents`, is the renter's platform balance in cents: it funds holds, absorbs refunds and pays post-trip balances, and it may never go negative.

Operators keep three levers on participant standing. An owner penalty adjustment takes a `points_delta` and a `reason`; the delta is added to `owner_penalty_points` and the result is floored at 0, and the owner's `version` increments. If the resulting total reaches or exceeds twelve points, the owner's `status` becomes `SUSPENDED` and every one of that owner's `LISTED` listings cascades to `SUSPENDED` with all the downstream consequences described in §3.3. If instead the resulting total falls back below twelve, the owner's `status` returns to `ACTIVE` — but previously suspended listings are **not** automatically reinstated; each must be reinstated deliberately. A renter penalty adjustment takes the same `points_delta` and `reason` pair, adds the delta to `renter_penalty_points`, floors the result at 0 and increments the renter's `version`. A wallet adjustment takes an `amount_cents_delta` and a `reason`, adds the delta to `wallet_credit_cents` and increments the renter's `version`; if the resulting balance would be negative the adjustment is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RENTER_FUNDS` and nothing changes.

Four configuration catalogues underpin every priced trip, and all four are operator-owned. A **protection plan** is defined by a `tier` — one of `BASIC`, `STANDARD` or `PREMIUM`, unique across all plans — together with a `daily_premium_cents` floor, a `premium_pct_of_base_bp` share expressed in basis points between 0 and 5000, a `deductible_cents` ceiling on what a renter can be charged for approved damage, a `coverage_ceiling_cents` cap on the gross damage the plan will recognise for one sharing period, an `excluded_causes` array drawn from `COLLISION`, `THEFT`, `WATER_INTRUSION`, `TIRE_BLOWOUT`, `AWNING_WIND`, `INTERIOR_STAIN` and `APPLIANCE_FAILURE`, and a non-empty `eligible_vehicle_classes` array drawn from `CLASS_A`, `CLASS_C`, `CAMPER_VAN`, `TRAVEL_TRAILER` and `FIFTH_WHEEL`. All seven plan fields are required — `excluded_causes` must be supplied, though an empty array is perfectly legal, whereas `eligible_vehicle_classes` may not be empty — and `daily_premium_cents`, `deductible_cents` and `coverage_ceiling_cents` are integers of zero or more. A **cancellation policy** is defined by a `tier` — one of `FLEXIBLE`, `MODERATE` or `STRICT`, unique across all policies — a `full_refund_hours_before` threshold of zero or more hours, a `partial_refund_pct_bp` share in basis points from 0 to 10000, a `renter_cancel_penalty_points` figure from 0 to 10, an `owner_cancel_penalty_points` figure from 0 to 12, and an `owner_cancel_fee_bp` share in basis points from 0 to 5000.

A **pricing season** names a `season_code` of `PEAK`, `SHOULDER` or `OFF` and a year-independent inclusive window given as `start_month_day` and `end_month_day`, each a two-digit month and two-digit day joined by a hyphen; `end_month_day` must not sort before `start_month_day` when the two strings are compared character by character, which is to say no season row wraps across the turn of the year. The `multiplier_bp` for the window is a basis-point figure between 1000 and 30000. To find the multiplier that applies to a calendar date, the platform formats that date as its two-digit month and two-digit day, then collects every season row whose `start_month_day` is less than or equal to that string and whose `end_month_day` is greater than or equal to it — both bounds inclusive, compared as strings. If no row matches, the multiplier is 10000 basis points, meaning the owner's list price stands unmultiplied, and the reported season code is `OFF`. If one or more rows match, the winner is the one with the earliest `created_at`, and both its `multiplier_bp` and its `season_code` are taken from that single row. Overlapping season windows are therefore resolved by creation order, not by specificity.

A **campground** is registered with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, a `max_rig_length_ft` between 10 and 60 feet, a `daily_dropoff_capacity` of at least 1, a `dropoff_buffer_minutes` figure from 0 to 240, and an optional `status` that defaults to `ACCEPTING` and may otherwise be `CLOSED`. Only an `ACCEPTING` campground may receive drop-offs. A newly registered campground opens with `scheduled_assignment_count` at 0.

### 3.2. Rig Listings: Draft, Documentation and Platform Review

An `Owner` creates a listing for a rig by supplying `owner_id`, a `nickname` of 1 to 80 characters, a `vehicle_class` of `CLASS_A`, `CLASS_C`, `CAMPER_VAN`, `TRAVEL_TRAILER` or `FIFTH_WHEEL`, a `gvwr_lbs` gross vehicle weight rating between 1000 and 30000 pounds, a `length_ft` between 10 and 45 feet, a `base_nightly_rate_cents` list price of at least 1000 cents, an `included_miles_per_night` allowance of zero or more, a `mileage_overage_rate_cents_per_mile` of zero or more, an `included_generator_hours_per_night` allowance of zero or more expressed to one decimal place, a `generator_overage_rate_cents_per_hour` of zero or more, and a `min_renter_age` between 21 and 30 whole years. Three delivery fields are optional: `delivery_offered` defaults to false, and `delivery_base_fee_cents` and `delivery_per_mile_cents` each default to 0 and must be non-negative. The supplied `owner_id` is resolved first — an unknown one yields `ERR_OWNER_NOT_FOUND` — and must then name the acting owner: a listing created with another owner's `owner_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

The listing is born in `listing_status` `DRAFT`, with `insurance_status` at `PENDING`, `inspection_photo_zone_count` at 0, `completed_night_count` at 0 and `last_reconciled_at` null. One field is derived rather than supplied: `requires_towing` is true exactly when `vehicle_class` is `TRAVEL_TRAILER` or `FIFTH_WHEEL`, and false for the three motorised classes. That single boolean is what later forces a renter to hold a towing-experience credential and what opens the door to the towing surcharge on the protection premium.

Documentation is attached to a listing as separate records. An `Owner` uploads one by naming the `listing_id`, a `doc_type` of `TITLE`, `REGISTRATION`, `INSURANCE_POLICY` or `INSPECTION_PHOTO`, an `image_ref` of 1 to 200 characters holding the opaque storage reference for the scan or photograph, and `expected_listing_version` as described in §2.7. Two fields are conditional and mutually exclusive: `zone` — one of `EXTERIOR_FRONT`, `EXTERIOR_REAR`, `INTERIOR`, `ROOF` or `UNDERCARRIAGE` — is required when `doc_type` is `INSPECTION_PHOTO` and forbidden otherwise, while `expires_on`, a `YYYY-MM-DD` date, is required for `TITLE`, `REGISTRATION` and `INSURANCE_POLICY` and forbidden for `INSPECTION_PHOTO`. Getting that pairing wrong is a malformed request and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Each document opens with `verification_status` at `PENDING` and `superseded` false.

Uploading a document is a supersession event, and it has three effects beyond creating the row. Any prior non-superseded document on the same listing with the same `doc_type` — and, for inspection photographs, the same `zone` as well — has its `superseded` flag set to true and its `verification_status` moved to `SUPERSEDED`, whatever that status had been, and its own `version` increments and `updated_at` refreshes as a consequence. The listing's `insurance_status` and `inspection_photo_zone_count` are then recomputed. Finally the listing's `version` increments by one — unconditionally, whether or not either derived field actually changed — which is exactly why the next upload needs a fresh `expected_listing_version`. A `PlatformAdmin` verifies a document by setting its `verification_status` to `VERIFIED`; verification carries no state precondition of its own, so it succeeds from `PENDING`, from `VERIFIED` again, and even from `SUPERSEDED`, in every case landing on `VERIFIED` without touching the `superseded` flag. That action recomputes the listing's `insurance_status`, increments the document's `version` and increments the listing's `version` as well. The full set of `verification_status` values is therefore `PENDING`, `VERIFIED` and `SUPERSEDED`.

Both listing-level derived fields follow mechanically from the document set. `insurance_status` is `VERIFIED` when the listing has a document whose `doc_type` is `INSURANCE_POLICY`, whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED`, whose `superseded` is false and whose `expires_on` is on or after the current logical-clock date; it is `EXPIRED` when such a verified, non-superseded insurance document exists but its `expires_on` falls before the clock date; and it is `PENDING` in every other case, including the case of no insurance document at all. Its permitted values are exactly `PENDING`, `VERIFIED` and `EXPIRED`. `inspection_photo_zone_count` is the number of **distinct** `zone` values across the listing's non-superseded inspection photographs — so five photographs of the roof still count as one zone. Both fields are re-evaluated against the logical clock on every read of the listing, single or listed, and again at every gate that consults them, so an insurance document that lapses because the clock moved forward shows up as `EXPIRED` on the very next read; that re-evaluation is not a mutation and neither refreshes `updated_at` nor increments `version`.

Onboarding culminates in review. An `Owner` submits a `DRAFT` listing, advancing `listing_status` from `DRAFT` to `IN_REVIEW`, and three preconditions guard the move. The acting owner must be the listing's owner. The owner's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_OWNER_SUSPENDED`. For each of `TITLE`, `REGISTRATION` and `INSURANCE_POLICY` there must exist a document on the listing of that type whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED`, whose `superseded` is false and whose `expires_on` is on or after the clock date; a missing, unverified, superseded or lapsed document of any of the three types yields `ERR_REQUIRED_DOCUMENT_MISSING`. And the listing's `inspection_photo_zone_count` must equal five — that is, all five rig zones must be photographed, with nothing missing and nothing extra to count — or the submission is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_PHOTOS_INCOMPLETE`. Whatever order they are described in here, the three are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.8 — photo-zone coverage first, then the three required documents, then the owner's standing — so a suspended owner submitting an undocumented listing reports `ERR_INSPECTION_PHOTOS_INCOMPLETE` rather than `ERR_OWNER_SUSPENDED`. The successful submission recomputes `insurance_status` and `inspection_photo_zone_count`, sets `listing_status` to `IN_REVIEW`, increments the listing's `version` and refreshes its `updated_at`.

The review decision is a fork on a single operator action, and the two branches differ in more than the destination state. A `PlatformAdmin` supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` together with an optional `decision_note`. On `APPROVE`, the listing moves `IN_REVIEW → LISTED`, the owner's `listed_listing_count` increases by one, the listing's `version` increments and the owner's `version` increments; approval additionally requires that the listing's `insurance_status` be `VERIFIED` at that moment, and an approval attempted against a `PENDING` or `EXPIRED` insurance status is refused with `ERR_LISTING_INSURANCE_NOT_VERIFIED`. On `REJECT`, the listing moves `IN_REVIEW → DRAFT` — the revert branch, which exists so that the owner can repair the documentation and resubmit — and the listing's `version` increments, but the owner's `listed_listing_count` is left untouched and the owner's `version` is not bumped. Rejection carries no preconditions of its own.

### 3.3. Keeping Listings Bookable: Suspension, Reinstatement and Archival

A listing's full set of states is `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `LISTED`, `SUSPENDED` and `ARCHIVED`, of which `ARCHIVED` is terminal. `SUSPENDED` and `LISTED` form a cycle: a listing may be suspended and reinstated any number of times over its life.

Suspension is the most far-reaching operation in the system. A `PlatformAdmin` suspends a `LISTED` listing with a `suspend_reason`, and the same transition also fires as a link in the owner-cancellation chain described in §3.10. The listing moves `LISTED → SUSPENDED`, the owner's `listed_listing_count` decreases by one, and both the listing's and the owner's `version` increment. From there the consequences cascade in two further causally chained levels. Because the listing entered `SUSPENDED`, every booking on that listing whose `trip_status` is `REQUESTED`, `OWNER_APPROVED` or `CONFIRMED` — processed in ascending `created_at` order — transitions to `CANCELLED_BY_PLATFORM`; for each such booking the `settlement_status` advances to `REFUNDED`, `refund_cents` is set equal to the booking's `hold_amount_cents`, that amount is added to the renter's `wallet_credit_cents`, and `hold_amount_cents` is then set to 0 — but the renter is credited, and their `updated_at` and `version` move, only when that refund is non-zero, so a booking cancelled out of `REQUESTED` or `OWNER_APPROVED` leaves its renter's record entirely untouched. Because those bookings entered `CANCELLED_BY_PLATFORM`, every delivery assignment belonging to any of them whose `status` is `REQUESTED`, `SCHEDULED` or `WAITLISTED` transitions to `VOIDED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order; and for each assignment that was `SCHEDULED`, the campground's `scheduled_assignment_count` decreases by one and the waitlist-promotion procedure of §3.6 runs for that campground and that `dropoff_date`. Voiding by cascade differs from voiding by request in one respect: the cancelled booking's own `delivery_fee_cents` is left exactly as it stood and its quote is not recomputed, because the booking is terminal and its quote no longer means anything. No penalty points are assessed against anybody by a platform-driven cancellation.

Reinstatement runs the cycle backwards. A `PlatformAdmin` returns a `SUSPENDED` listing to `LISTED`, which increases the owner's `listed_listing_count` by one and increments both the listing's and the owner's `version`. Two preconditions guard it. The owner's `owner_penalty_points` must be strictly below twelve, and a reinstatement attempted while the owner still sits at or above that threshold is refused with `ERR_LISTING_STATE_INVALID`. The listing's `insurance_status` must be `VERIFIED`, and a reinstatement attempted against a `PENDING` or `EXPIRED` insurance status is refused with `ERR_LISTING_INSURANCE_NOT_VERIFIED`. Reinstatement does not resurrect the bookings that suspension cancelled; those are terminal.

Archival is the terminal exit and is available from `DRAFT` and from `SUSPENDED` — never directly from `LISTED` or `IN_REVIEW`. Either the owning `Owner` or a `PlatformAdmin` may archive. Both source states share the same single precondition: no booking on the listing may sit in `REQUESTED`, `OWNER_APPROVED`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_TRIP` or `RETURNED`, because an archived rig must have no live commitments; an attempt with any such booking outstanding is refused with `ERR_LISTING_STATE_INVALID`. Both source states share the same single side effect as well: `listing_status` becomes `ARCHIVED` and the listing's `version` increments.

### 3.4. Renter Qualification and Risk Scoring

Qualification evidence is recorded by a `PlatformAdmin` on behalf of a renter, one third-party check at a time. Each record names the `renter_id`, a `check_type` of either `DRIVING_RECORD` or `TOWING_EXPERIENCE`, a `result`, an optional `violation_points` count defaulting to 0 and bounded between 0 and 20, a `checked_at` instant recording when the third party ran the check, and an `expires_on` date after which the check no longer satisfies a booking gate. The `result` vocabulary spans both check types — `CLEAR`, `MINOR`, `MAJOR`, `VERIFIED_BASIC`, `VERIFIED_ADVANCED` and `UNVERIFIED` — but the values are not interchangeable: `CLEAR`, `MINOR` and `MAJOR` belong only to a `DRIVING_RECORD` check, while `VERIFIED_BASIC`, `VERIFIED_ADVANCED` and `UNVERIFIED` belong only to a `TOWING_EXPERIENCE` check. A towing check must carry `violation_points` of 0. Qualification records are append-only evidence: they carry `id` and `created_at` and nothing else from the common set. Recording one recomputes the renter's `driving_record_points`, `driving_record_status` and `towing_verified_tier`, and increments the renter's `version`. Those same three fields are additionally re-evaluated against the logical clock on every read of the renter, and again when a booking request freezes its towing tier, so a check that lapses because the clock moved forward is reflected on the next read; unlike the recording of evidence, neither of those re-evaluations refreshes `updated_at` or increments `version`.

The renter's `driving_record_status` reflects the single newest driving-record check, selected by descending `checked_at` and, where two checks share an instant, by descending `created_at`. When the renter has no driving-record check at all the status is `NONE`. When the newest check's `expires_on` falls before the current logical-clock date the status is `EXPIRED`, whatever that check said. Otherwise the status is that check's own `result`, so the permitted values are `NONE`, `CLEAR`, `MINOR`, `MAJOR` and `EXPIRED`. The renter's `towing_verified_tier` is the highest tier among the renter's towing-experience checks whose `expires_on` is on or after the clock date, where `ADVANCED` outranks `BASIC`; a renter with no unexpired towing check has `towing_verified_tier` of `NONE`, and the field's permitted values are `NONE`, `BASIC` and `ADVANCED`. A check whose `result` is `VERIFIED_ADVANCED` supplies the `ADVANCED` tier and one whose `result` is `VERIFIED_BASIC` supplies the `BASIC` tier; an `UNVERIFIED` check supplies no tier at all.

The most subtle piece of this subsection is that the platform maintains **two different driving-record risk numbers from the same evidence**, computed differently and used for different purposes. Both draw only on checks whose `check_type` is `DRIVING_RECORD`; towing checks are excluded and in any case always carry zero points.

The renter-level number, `driving_record_points`, is a rolling window sum. Its window opens thirty-six months — three full years — before the current logical clock and closes at the clock itself, and both endpoints are inclusive. `driving_record_points` is the sum of `violation_points` over every driving-record check whose `checked_at` falls on or after the window's opening instant and on or before the clock. Because it sums, three separate two-point checks inside the window total six points, and a fourth breaches the ceiling.

The booking-level number, `renter_record_points_snapshot`, is frozen onto each booking at the moment the booking is requested and reads only the **single newest** driving-record check — the same selector that `driving_record_status` uses, descending `checked_at` with descending `created_at` as tie-break — taking that one check's `violation_points`, or 0 when the renter has no driving-record check at all. It is the number the insurer underwrites on. The two numbers routinely disagree, and that disagreement is intended: the very same renter can show six points on the rolling gate and two on the snapshot.

### 3.5. Requesting a Sharing Period: Eligibility, Availability and the Provisional Quote

A `Renter` requests a sharing period by naming, in this order, the `listing_id` of the rig, the `renter_id` of the renting party, the `protection_plan_id` of the protection tier to be pinned for the trip, and the `cancellation_policy_id` of the cancellation tier — those four foreign keys are resolved in that order, so an unresolvable listing reports `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND` before an unresolvable plan would have reported `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`. Once all four resolve, the `renter_id` must name the acting renter; a request naming somebody else is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and that check sits after the whole foreign-key round and ahead of every business gate below. The request also carries a `start_date` and an `end_date` as `YYYY-MM-DD` dates, a `delivery_option` of either `PICKUP` or `CAMPGROUND_DROPOFF`, a non-negative `declared_miles` mileage estimate, and a non-negative `declared_generator_hours` estimate expressed to one decimal place. The sharing period is the half-open date range that includes `start_date` and excludes `end_date`, so `nights` is simply the number of days between the two dates. Scheduled pickup is `start_date` at 15:00:00 UTC and scheduled return is `end_date` at 11:00:00 UTC — both fixed platform-wide, and both consumed later by the cancellation and late-return rules.

Eleven business gates run at request time, in the ladder order given in §2.8. The listing must be `LISTED`, otherwise `ERR_LISTING_NOT_BOOKABLE`. The renter's whole-year age at the booking's `start_date` — recorded on the booking as `renter_age_years_at_start`, computed from the renter's `date_of_birth` to `start_date` and truncated downward to whole years — must be at least the listing's `min_renter_age`, otherwise `ERR_RENTER_AGE_BELOW_MINIMUM`. The newest driving-record check, selected by descending `checked_at` with descending `created_at` as tie-break, must have a `result` of either `CLEAR` or `MINOR` and an `expires_on` falling on or after the booking's `end_date`; a renter with no driving-record check, a newest check reading `MAJOR`, or a newest check expiring before the trip ends is rejected with `ERR_DRIVING_RECORD_INVALID`. The renter's rolling `driving_record_points` must be at most six, and a renter above six is rejected with `ERR_DRIVING_RECORD_POINTS_EXCEEDED` — note that this gate reads the rolling three-year sum, not the snapshot.

Towing is gated in two steps, and both steps evaluate the renter's towing credentials against checks whose `expires_on` is on or after the booking's `end_date`. When the listing's `requires_towing` is true, the renter's `towing_verified_tier` must be something other than `NONE`, otherwise `ERR_TOWING_EXPERIENCE_MISSING`. When `requires_towing` is true **and** the listing's `gvwr_lbs` is strictly greater than 10000 pounds — five short tons, the point at which a trailer stops being a weekend proposition — the tier must be exactly `ADVANCED`, so a renter holding only `BASIC` is rejected with `ERR_TOWING_TIER_INSUFFICIENT`. A heavy trailer therefore rejects a `BASIC` renter at the second step even though the first step passed.

The remaining gates concern standing, insurance, availability and configuration fit. The renter's `renter_penalty_points` must be strictly below eight, and a renter at or above eight points is rejected with `ERR_RENTER_BLOCKED`. The listing's `insurance_status` must be `VERIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_LISTING_INSURANCE_NOT_VERIFIED`. The requested dates must not collide with any live booking on the same listing — that is, any booking whose `trip_status` is `REQUESTED`, `OWNER_APPROVED`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_TRIP` or `RETURNED`. Collision is evaluated on the half-open date range extended by a one-night turnaround buffer after the existing booking's checkout: the two bookings collide when the new booking's `start_date` falls strictly before one day after the existing booking's `end_date`, **and** the new booking's `end_date` falls strictly after the existing booking's `start_date`. A collision is reported as `ERR_LISTING_DATES_UNAVAILABLE`. Separately, `start_date` must fall strictly before `end_date` and must be on or after the current logical-clock date; violating either yields `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. The listing's `vehicle_class` must be a member of the chosen protection plan's `eligible_vehicle_classes`, otherwise `ERR_PLAN_CLASS_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. And when `delivery_option` is `CAMPGROUND_DROPOFF`, the listing's `delivery_offered` must be true, otherwise `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_OFFERED`.

A booking that clears all of that is created with `trip_status` `REQUESTED` and `settlement_status` `NONE`. Four values are frozen onto it at that instant and never recomputed afterwards: `nights`, `renter_age_years_at_start`, `renter_record_points_snapshot` under the newest-check rule of §3.4, and `towing_verified_tier`, copied from the renter's current tier — the clock-date evaluation of §3.4, not the end-date evaluation the two towing gates just performed, so a credential that is unexpired today but lapses before the trip ends can gate one way and freeze the other. That frozen `towing_verified_tier` — with permitted values `NONE`, `BASIC` and `ADVANCED` — is what later drives the towing branch of the protection premium, so a renter who upgrades their credential after requesting a booking still pays the surcharge on that booking.

The provisional quote is then computed. The **nightly subtotal**, `base_subtotal_cents`, is built one night at a time: for each night of the sharing period, starting at `start_date` and running through the night before `end_date`, the platform resolves that night's seasonal multiplier by the selector of §3.1, multiplies the listing's `base_nightly_rate_cents` by that multiplier in basis points, divides by 10000, rounds the result half up to a whole number of cents, and adds that whole-cent figure to the running subtotal. The rounding happens **once per night, before the addition**. Summing the unrounded per-night products and rounding the total once at the end produces a different number and is not the rule.

The **protection premium**, `premium_cents`, is a floor, a surcharge and a ceiling applied in that order. First the platform takes the larger of two candidates: the plan's `daily_premium_cents` multiplied by the booking's `nights`, and the booking's `base_subtotal_cents` multiplied by the plan's `premium_pct_of_base_bp`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up to whole cents. The larger of those two is the tier premium. Next, when the listing's `requires_towing` is true and the booking's frozen `towing_verified_tier` is exactly `BASIC`, a towing surcharge equal to 2500 basis points — a quarter — of the tier premium is computed, divided by 10000 and rounded half up; in every other case, including an `ADVANCED` tier or a rig that needs no towing, the surcharge is 0. Finally the premium is capped: `premium_cents` is the smaller of the tier premium plus the surcharge, and 1500 basis points — fifteen percent — of the plan's `coverage_ceiling_cents`, that second figure being divided by 10000 and rounded half up.

The **grand total** is assembled in six pinned steps, and the order is not negotiable because the discount depends on a subtotal, the fee depends on the discounted subtotal, and the total depends on the fee. Step one adds `base_subtotal_cents`, `premium_cents` and `delivery_fee_cents` into a pre-discount figure; at request time `delivery_fee_cents` is 0, because no delivery assignment exists yet. Step two selects the `loyalty_tier` from that **pre-discount figure and from nothing else**: a pre-discount figure of at least 150000 cents selects `LOYALTY_TIER_2` at 700 basis points, a figure of at least 100000 cents but below 150000 selects `LOYALTY_TIER_1` at 400 basis points, and anything below 100000 cents selects `NONE` at 0 basis points. Keying the tier on the post-fee grand total instead is the classic mistake here, and it produces the wrong tier on exactly the bookings that sit near a band edge. Step three computes `loyalty_discount_cents` as the pre-discount figure multiplied by the selected basis points, divided by 10000 and rounded half up. Step four subtracts that discount from the pre-discount figure to give the discounted subtotal. Step five computes `service_fee_cents` as the discounted subtotal multiplied by 1200 basis points — a flat twelve percent — divided by 10000 and rounded half up; the fee base is the discounted subtotal and **excludes overages entirely**. Step six computes `estimated_overage_cents` from the declared estimates as described in §3.7 and sets `quoted_total_cents` to the discounted subtotal plus `service_fee_cents` plus `estimated_overage_cents`.

At request time the booking's remaining money fields are all still at rest: `hold_amount_cents`, `final_overage_cents`, `trip_total_overage_cents`, `late_return_fee_cents`, `final_total_cents`, `balance_due_cents` and `refund_cents` are all 0, `delivery_fee_cents` is 0, `loyalty_discount_cents` is whatever step three produced, and `info_round_count` — the number of completed information rounds across every damage claim on this booking — is 0.

### 3.6. Delivery and Campground Drop-off Coordination

A rig destined for a campground rather than a driveway needs a drop-off window, and either the owning `Owner` or a `PlatformAdmin` may create one. The request names the `booking_id` and the `campground_id`, gives the inclusive `window_start` and `window_end` instants bounding the drop-off window, states the `distance_miles` of road mileage from the rig's home base to the campground as a whole number from 0 to 500, optionally supplies a `setup_fee_cents` flat on-site levelling and hookup charge between 0 and 50000 defaulting to 0, and carries `expected_booking_version` as described in §2.7.

Four checks gate the creation beyond the version guard. The booking's `delivery_option` must be `CAMPGROUND_DROPOFF`; creating an assignment for a `PICKUP` booking is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_APPLICABLE`. The drop-off window must be coherent and correctly placed: `window_end` must fall strictly after `window_start`, and the UTC calendar date of `window_start` must equal the booking's `start_date`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. The rig must fit and the site must be open: the listing's `length_ft` must be no greater than the campground's `max_rig_length_ft` and the campground's `status` must be `ACCEPTING`, or the request is refused with `ERR_RIG_LENGTH_EXCEEDS_CAMPGROUND`. And the window must not collide with an existing `SCHEDULED` window on the same campground. Despite the order in which they read here, the four are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.8: the collision test first, then the window-coherence test, then the rig-fit and campground-status test, and the `delivery_option` test last — so an assignment offered against a `PICKUP` booking on an already-taken window reports `ERR_DROPOFF_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, not `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_APPLICABLE`.

The **collision test** treats windows as closed minute intervals and applies the campground's `dropoff_buffer_minutes` **symmetrically to both ends** of every existing scheduled window. Only windows on the same campground **and bearing the same `dropoff_date`** are compared; a scheduled window in a different date bucket is never a collision partner however close the buffer would bring it. A new window collides with an existing one when the new `window_start` falls at or before the existing `window_end` plus the buffer, **and** the new `window_end` falls at or after the existing `window_start` minus the buffer. Because the interval ends are closed and the buffer is applied on both sides, two windows that merely touch at a shared instant collide, and so do two windows separated by exactly the buffer. A collision is reported as `ERR_DROPOFF_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

Creation is also a supersession event, and the supersession is deferred until the request has earned it. Any earlier delivery assignment on the same booking whose `status` is `REQUESTED`, `SCHEDULED` or `WAITLISTED` is voided — releasing its campground slot if it held one, zeroing the booking's `delivery_fee_cents` with a quote recomputation, and running the waitlist-promotion procedure — but only after all four gates above have passed, so a creation refused for a window conflict, an incoherent window, a rig that does not fit or a `PICKUP` booking leaves those earlier assignments standing untouched. Correspondingly, the collision test that gates the request disregards the booking's own soon-to-be-superseded windows: a client rescheduling the same booking never collides with itself. Once the priors are voided the collision test is applied once more against whatever windows still hold `SCHEDULED` — a promotion triggered by the release can take the slot back — and a conflict at that point is likewise `ERR_DROPOFF_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, with the supersession already performed and not rolled back. The new row's `delivery_fee_cents` is then computed as the listing's `delivery_base_fee_cents`, plus the listing's `delivery_per_mile_cents` multiplied by the assignment's `distance_miles`, plus the assignment's `setup_fee_cents`; every input is a whole number of cents or a whole mile count, so no rounding arises. The row's `dropoff_date` is derived as the UTC calendar date of `window_start` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and that date is the capacity bucket key. Creating an assignment increments the parent booking's `version` by one.

The new assignment's landing state is a fork on a single action, and the two branches have genuinely different side effects. An assignment is born `REQUESTED` and resolves immediately. When the campground's `status` is `ACCEPTING` and the number of `SCHEDULED` assignments already holding that campground and that `dropoff_date` is strictly fewer than the campground's `daily_dropoff_capacity`, and the window passes the collision test, the assignment lands in `SCHEDULED`: the campground's `scheduled_assignment_count` increases by one, the campground's `version` increments, the booking's `delivery_fee_cents` is set to the assignment's `delivery_fee_cents`, and the booking's quote is recomputed through all six steps of §3.5 with that new delivery fee in play. When the campground is `ACCEPTING` but the bucket for that `dropoff_date` already holds at least `daily_dropoff_capacity` scheduled assignments, the assignment lands in `WAITLISTED` instead — which is a normal outcome and not an error. A waitlisted assignment consumes no slot, leaves `scheduled_assignment_count` unchanged, and does not bill: the booking's `delivery_fee_cents` stays 0. The assignment's `promoted_from_waitlist` flag is false on creation and its full status vocabulary is `REQUESTED`, `SCHEDULED`, `WAITLISTED`, `COMPLETED` and `VOIDED`, of which `COMPLETED` and `VOIDED` are terminal.

Slots are released in three ways, and every release runs the same **promotion procedure** for the affected campground and `dropoff_date`. The procedure first filters candidates: only assignments on that campground with that `dropoff_date` whose `status` is `WAITLISTED` and whose booking's `trip_status` is `CONFIRMED`, `OWNER_APPROVED` or `REQUESTED` are eligible — a waitlisted assignment hanging off an already-cancelled or already-started booking is not. Candidates are then ranked by ascending `created_at`; where two share a `created_at`, by ascending `window_start`; and where they still tie, by ascending `distance_miles`. The procedure walks the ranking in order and promotes the first candidate whose window passes the buffer-based collision test against the windows still holding `SCHEDULED` status on that campground. If the candidate list is empty, or if every candidate's window conflicts, no promotion occurs and the campground's `scheduled_assignment_count` simply remains at its decremented value — this is a normal outcome, not an error.

A promoted assignment moves `WAITLISTED → SCHEDULED`, sets `promoted_from_waitlist` to true, and increases the campground's `scheduled_assignment_count` by one. It then bills: its booking's `delivery_fee_cents` is set to the assignment's `delivery_fee_cents` and that booking's quote is recomputed through the six steps. If that booking's `settlement_status` is already `HELD`, the hold is trued up in the same breath — the difference between the newly computed `quoted_total_cents` and the old `hold_amount_cents` is debited from that renter's `wallet_credit_cents`, and `hold_amount_cents` is reset to the new `quoted_total_cents`. A renter who has already funded a booking can therefore see their wallet move because somebody else's rig left a campground. The true-up carries no solvency guard of its own: it is applied unconditionally, never refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RENTER_FUNDS`, and is the one path on which `wallet_credit_cents` may be driven below zero — the promotion is a system-initiated consequence and cannot be held up by a third party's balance.

Voiding is the manual release. Either the owning `Owner` or a `PlatformAdmin` may void an assignment with a `void_reason`, and voiding is available from `SCHEDULED` and from `WAITLISTED`. The two source states share the destination but not the consequences. Voiding a `SCHEDULED` assignment sets its `status` to `VOIDED`, decreases the campground's `scheduled_assignment_count` by one, increments the campground's `version`, sets the booking's `delivery_fee_cents` back to 0 and recomputes the booking's quote, and runs the promotion procedure for that campground and `dropoff_date`. Voiding a `WAITLISTED` assignment sets its `status` to `VOIDED` and sets the booking's `delivery_fee_cents` to 0 with a quote recomputation, but leaves `scheduled_assignment_count` untouched and runs **no** promotion, because no slot was released. Voiding carries no precondition beyond the state check and, when the actor is an `Owner`, the requirement that the acting owner own the listing behind the assignment's booking — a check that runs ahead of the state check, so voiding somebody else's already-completed assignment reports `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` rather than `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`. Either way the assignment's `version` increments. The booking's does not: although a void rewrites the booking's `delivery_fee_cents` and every quote field downstream of it, the booking's `updated_at` and `version` are deliberately left where they were, so a client holding a booking version may void an assignment and still create the replacement assignment with the same `expected_booking_version`. The third and largest release path is cascade: any booking cancellation, from any of the three cancelling roles, voids every one of that booking's assignments sitting in `REQUESTED`, `SCHEDULED` or `WAITLISTED`, with the same slot release and promotion for each formerly-scheduled row.

### 3.7. Confirmation, Handover, Metering and Return

A booking's trip dimension runs `REQUESTED` → `OWNER_APPROVED` → `CONFIRMED` → `IN_TRIP` → `RETURNED` → `CLOSED`, with three cancellation exits — `CANCELLED_BY_RENTER`, `CANCELLED_BY_OWNER` and `CANCELLED_BY_PLATFORM` — and all four of `CLOSED`, `CANCELLED_BY_RENTER`, `CANCELLED_BY_OWNER` and `CANCELLED_BY_PLATFORM` terminal. Alongside it the settlement dimension runs `NONE` → `HELD` → `RECONCILING` → `SETTLED`, with a `DISPUTED` detour off `RECONCILING`, a `RECONCILING` return from `SETTLED`, and `REFUNDED` as its terminal cancellation state. **The two dimensions advance on different triggers and neither implies the other.** Handover moves `trip_status` and leaves `settlement_status` completely untouched; reversing a settlement moves `settlement_status` back to `RECONCILING` while `trip_status` stays `CLOSED`.

The owning `Owner` accepts a request, advancing `trip_status` from `REQUESTED` to `OWNER_APPROVED` and incrementing the booking's `version`. Approval requires that the acting owner own the listing behind the booking, that the owner's `status` be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_OWNER_SUSPENDED` — and that the listing still be `LISTED`, otherwise `ERR_LISTING_NOT_BOOKABLE`.

The `Renter` then confirms, which is where money first moves. Confirmation requires that the acting renter be the booking's renter, that the listing still be `LISTED`, and that the renter's `wallet_credit_cents` be at least the booking's `quoted_total_cents`; an underfunded wallet is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RENTER_FUNDS` (402). On success the quote is recomputed from scratch — `base_subtotal_cents`, `premium_cents`, `delivery_fee_cents`, `loyalty_tier`, `loyalty_discount_cents`, `service_fee_cents`, `estimated_overage_cents` and `quoted_total_cents` all through the six steps of §3.5, picking up any delivery fee that landed in the meantime — and `hold_amount_cents` is set to the resulting `quoted_total_cents`. The recomputation reads the listing's rates, allowances and delivery fees and the seasonal window rows **as they stand now**, not as they stood at request time, so an owner who repriced in between is honoured; the only inputs still frozen are the four values §3.5 pinned onto the booking, `nights`, `renter_age_years_at_start`, `renter_record_points_snapshot` and `towing_verified_tier`, the last of which still drives the towing surcharge. The `delivery_fee_cents` it picks up is that of the booking's earliest assignment whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`, and 0 when the booking has no such assignment — a waitlisted or voided assignment contributes nothing. The funds check is made against that freshly computed `quoted_total_cents`, not against any earlier one. That amount is subtracted from the renter's `wallet_credit_cents` and the renter's `version` increments. The trip dimension moves `OWNER_APPROVED → CONFIRMED`, the settlement dimension moves `NONE → HELD` on the strength of `quoted_total_cents` having been computed, and the booking's `version` increments.

Metering and photography are recorded as evidence throughout the trip, by either the owning `Owner` or the booking's `Renter`. A meter reading names the `booking_id`, a `reading_type` of `PRE_TRIP` at handover, `MID_TRIP` for an interim reading, or `POST_TRIP` at return, an absolute non-negative `odometer_miles` value in whole miles, an absolute non-negative `generator_hours` hour-meter value to one decimal place, and a `recorded_at` instant. Readings are append-only and carry only `id` and `created_at`. Both meters must be non-decreasing: a new reading must not carry an `odometer_miles` or a `generator_hours` value below that of the reading immediately preceding it when the booking's readings are ordered by ascending `recorded_at`, and its `recorded_at` must not equal that of an existing reading on the same booking. Either violation is refused with `ERR_METER_READING_REGRESSION`; the comparison looks only at that immediately preceding reading, so a reading slotted in ahead of existing later readings is judged against what came before it and never against what comes after. Recording a reading changes no money field on the booking; the numbers are consumed at return. Neither metering nor photography carries any booking-state precondition — a reading or a photograph of either phase may be recorded while the booking sits in any `trip_status` or `settlement_status` whatever, including `REQUESTED` before the trip exists and `CLOSED` after it is settled — and neither creation touches the booking's `updated_at` or `version`.

A trip photo names the `booking_id`, a `phase` of `PRE_TRIP` or `POST_TRIP`, a `zone` of `EXTERIOR_FRONT`, `EXTERIOR_REAR`, `INTERIOR`, `ROOF` or `UNDERCARRIAGE`, an optional `damage_flagged` boolean defaulting to false that records whether the uploader marked visible damage in the frame, and an `image_ref` of 1 to 200 characters. Trip photos are also append-only, carrying only `id` and `created_at`. The `PRE_TRIP` set is the damage baseline; the `POST_TRIP` set is the comparison set.

Handover is performed by the owning `Owner` and advances the trip dimension `CONFIRMED → IN_TRIP` with a `version` increment. Four preconditions guard it. The acting owner must own the listing. There must be at least one meter reading on the booking whose `reading_type` is `PRE_TRIP`, otherwise `ERR_METER_READINGS_INCOMPLETE`. The booking's `PRE_TRIP` trip photos must cover at least four **distinct** `zone` values, otherwise `ERR_TRIP_PHOTOS_INCOMPLETE` — and note that this is four of the five zones, deliberately one fewer than the five that listing onboarding demands, so a trip may legitimately begin and end with one zone undocumented. And when the booking's `delivery_option` is `CAMPGROUND_DROPOFF`, there must be a delivery assignment on the booking whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Handover then sets off a four-level chain. Because the booking entered `IN_TRIP`, the delivery assignment on that booking whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` transitions to `COMPLETED`. Because that assignment left `SCHEDULED`, the campground's `scheduled_assignment_count` decreases by one and the promotion procedure of §3.6 runs for that campground and `dropoff_date`, moving the selected waitlisted assignment to `SCHEDULED` with `promoted_from_waitlist` set true. Because that promotion happened, the promoted assignment's own booking has its `delivery_fee_cents` set to the promoted assignment's `delivery_fee_cents` and its quote recomputed through the six steps; and if that booking's `settlement_status` is `HELD`, the difference between its new `quoted_total_cents` and its old `hold_amount_cents` is debited from its renter's `wallet_credit_cents` and its `hold_amount_cents` is reset to the new `quoted_total_cents`. Handover does not touch the starting booking's own `settlement_status`.

Return may be performed by either the booking's `Renter` or the owning `Owner`, and advances the trip dimension `IN_TRIP → RETURNED` with a `version` increment. It requires a meter reading on the booking whose `reading_type` is `POST_TRIP`, otherwise `ERR_METER_READINGS_INCOMPLETE`, and `POST_TRIP` trip photos covering at least four distinct `zone` values, otherwise `ERR_TRIP_PHOTOS_INCOMPLETE`. The zone that a renter is permitted to leave undocumented at return is precisely the zone that will later fail the claim-intake baseline check of §3.8.

Return is where the meters are converted into money, and the platform computes the overage **two different ways from the same readings**. In both computations the booking's readings are ordered by ascending `recorded_at`, with ascending `created_at` as the tie-break.

The **billed** figure, `final_overage_cents`, is computed segment by segment and includes readings of every type, `MID_TRIP` rows among them. The platform walks each consecutive pair of readings in that order. For a pair, the segment's night count is the number of days between the calendar date of the earlier reading and the calendar date of the later one; when those dates are the same the segment counts as one night rather than zero. The segment's mileage allowance is that night count multiplied by the listing's `included_miles_per_night`, and the segment's mileage use is the later reading's `odometer_miles` minus the earlier one's. Whatever the use exceeds the allowance by — and zero when the allowance is not exceeded — is multiplied by the listing's `mileage_overage_rate_cents_per_mile` and added to the running total. The segment's generator allowance is that same night count multiplied by the listing's `included_generator_hours_per_night`, and the segment's generator use is the later reading's `generator_hours` minus the earlier one's. Whatever the use exceeds the allowance by — and zero when it does not — is raised to the next multiple of 0.1 hours, multiplied by the listing's `generator_overage_rate_cents_per_hour`, rounded half up to whole cents, and added to the running total. Because each segment carries its own allowance, **frugality in one segment does not offset excess in another**: a renter who drives nothing on Monday and 400 miles on Tuesday pays for Tuesday.

The **reporting** figure, `trip_total_overage_cents`, pools the whole trip's allowance and **ignores `MID_TRIP` readings entirely**. It takes the earliest `PRE_TRIP` reading by ascending `recorded_at` and the latest `POST_TRIP` reading by descending `recorded_at`. The trip's mileage allowance is the booking's `nights` multiplied by `included_miles_per_night` and its generator allowance is `nights` multiplied by `included_generator_hours_per_night`. The figure is then the amount by which the odometer difference between those two readings exceeds the mileage allowance — zero if it does not — multiplied by `mileage_overage_rate_cents_per_mile`, plus the amount by which the generator-hour difference exceeds the generator allowance — zero if it does not — raised to the next multiple of 0.1 hours, multiplied by `generator_overage_rate_cents_per_hour` and rounded half up to whole cents. This figure is retained purely for reconciliation transparency and is **never billed**; because it pools the allowance, it can report zero on a trip where the segment-wise figure bills a real overage.

The same pooled shape, applied to the renter's declarations rather than to readings, produces `estimated_overage_cents` at quote time: the amount by which `declared_miles` exceeds `nights` multiplied by `included_miles_per_night` — zero if it does not — multiplied by `mileage_overage_rate_cents_per_mile`, plus the amount by which `declared_generator_hours` exceeds `nights` multiplied by `included_generator_hours_per_night` — zero if it does not — raised to the next multiple of 0.1 hours, multiplied by `generator_overage_rate_cents_per_hour` and rounded half up to whole cents.

Return also prices lateness. Scheduled return is the booking's `end_date` at 11:00:00 UTC. The lateness in minutes is the whole number of minutes from that instant to the logical clock at the moment of the return, truncated downward, and floored at zero so an early return is never negative. A rig returned within one full hour of grace — that is, a lateness of 60 minutes or fewer, the boundary being inclusive so exactly 60 minutes late is not late — carries a `late_return_fee_cents` of 0. Beyond the grace hour, the platform counts the minutes past the grace window and divides by 60, rounding **up** to a whole number of blocks, so a single minute into the second hour buys a whole block. Each block costs 7500 cents. The total is capped at 20000 basis points of the listing's `base_nightly_rate_cents` — twice the nightly rate — that cap being divided by 10000 and rounded half up, and `late_return_fee_cents` is the smaller of the block charge and the cap.

With overages and lateness in hand, return computes the trip's final figures. The discounted subtotal is `base_subtotal_cents` plus `premium_cents` plus `delivery_fee_cents` minus `loyalty_discount_cents`. The damage component is the sum of `renter_charge_cents` over every damage claim on this booking whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `PARTIAL`; claims in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `DENIED` or `WITHDRAWN` contribute nothing, so at return time, before any claim has been adjudicated, the damage component is 0. `final_total_cents` is the discounted subtotal plus `service_fee_cents` plus `final_overage_cents` plus `late_return_fee_cents` plus the damage component; every input is already a whole number of cents, so no rounding occurs. `balance_due_cents` is `final_total_cents` minus `hold_amount_cents` — positive when the renter still owes money, negative when a refund is owed. The settlement dimension advances `HELD → RECONCILING` on the strength of `final_total_cents` having been computed, the trip dimension moves `IN_TRIP → RETURNED`, and the booking's `version` increments.

### 3.8. Damage Claims: Intake, Review Cycles and Adjudication

Only the owning `Owner` may file a damage claim, and only against a returned rig. The claim names the `booking_id`, a `cause_code` from `COLLISION`, `THEFT`, `WATER_INTRUSION`, `TIRE_BLOWOUT`, `AWNING_WIND`, `INTERIOR_STAIN` and `APPLIANCE_FAILURE`, a `damage_zone` from `EXTERIOR_FRONT`, `EXTERIOR_REAR`, `INTERIOR`, `ROOF` and `UNDERCARRIAGE`, a `claimed_amount_cents` asserted repair cost of at least 1 cent, and a `description` narrative of 1 to 500 characters. Intake requires the booking's `settlement_status` to be either `RECONCILING` or `DISPUTED` — the second value is deliberate, so that a further claim may be filed while an earlier one is still open — and any other settlement state is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_SETTLEMENT_CLOSED`. It also requires that the claimed zone have a usable evidentiary baseline: there must be at least one `PRE_TRIP` trip photo **and** at least one `POST_TRIP` trip photo on the booking for that exact `damage_zone`. A zone with no baseline photo, or no comparison photo, is refused with `ERR_PHOTO_BASELINE_MISSING` — and this is exactly the boundary that the four-of-five photo coverage rule at handover and return leaves reachable.

At intake the platform freezes a photo comparison onto the claim, and the frozen values never change afterwards. It restricts attention to the booking's trip photos in the claim's `damage_zone`, ignoring every other zone entirely. `pre_photo_count` is the number of those photos whose `phase` is `PRE_TRIP`, and `post_photo_count` the number whose `phase` is `POST_TRIP`. The `comparison_verdict` follows a pinned precedence: if any of the pre-trip photos in that zone has `damage_flagged` true, the verdict is `PRE_EXISTING`; otherwise, if any of the post-trip photos in that zone has `damage_flagged` true, the verdict is `NEW_DAMAGE`; otherwise the verdict is `NO_VISIBLE_CHANGE`. A flagged pre-trip photograph therefore beats a flagged post-trip photograph — the damage was already there. The claim is created in `status` `SUBMITTED`, with `info_rounds_used` at 0 and `gross_amount_cents`, `renter_charge_cents`, `plan_paid_cents` and `owner_payout_cents` all at 0, and `override_amount_cents` and `override_reason` both null. Intake also moves the booking's settlement dimension `RECONCILING → DISPUTED` and increments the booking's `version`; a second claim filed while the booking is already `DISPUTED` leaves that dimension where it is and increments the `version` all the same.

A claim's states are `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `PARTIAL`, `DENIED` and `WITHDRAWN`, of which `APPROVED`, `PARTIAL`, `DENIED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal. A `ClaimsAdjuster` — never a `PlatformAdmin` — opens a submitted claim, moving it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and incrementing its `version`; opening requires the booking's `settlement_status` to be `DISPUTED`, otherwise `ERR_BOOKING_SETTLEMENT_CLOSED`.

Review may cycle. The adjuster may pause for more information with an `info_request_note`, moving the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED` and incrementing its `version`; the pause is permitted only while the claim's `info_rounds_used` is strictly below two, and a third information request on a claim that has already used its two rounds is refused with `ERR_INFO_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED`. Either the owning `Owner` or the booking's `Renter` may answer with a `response_note`, moving the claim `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, incrementing the claim's `info_rounds_used` by one, incrementing the booking's `info_round_count` by one, and incrementing both the claim's and the booking's `version`. `UNDER_REVIEW` and `INFO_REQUESTED` thus form a bounded revision cycle: at most two completed round trips per claim.

Adjudication is the adjuster's decision, legal only while the claim sits in `UNDER_REVIEW` — from `SUBMITTED`, from `INFO_REQUESTED` or from any terminal state it is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` — and it takes two optional inputs: an `override_amount_cents` manual gross figure, which must be an integer of zero or more, and an `override_reason` justification. Whenever `override_amount_cents` is supplied, `override_reason` must be present, otherwise the adjudication is refused with `ERR_ADJUDICATION_OVERRIDE_REASON_REQUIRED`; that same requirement guards all three adjudication outcomes identically. The 422 is reserved for an `override_reason` that is absent altogether: one supplied as an empty string fails the shape check of ladder step 4 and reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` instead. Both inputs are recorded verbatim on the claim as `override_amount_cents` and `override_reason`, and stay null on an adjudication that supplied neither.

The adjudicated gross is decided by a three-way waterfall in strict order. If the claim's `cause_code` appears in the booking's protection plan's `excluded_causes`, `gross_amount_cents` is 0 — **exclusion beats every verdict**, including a flagged post-trip photograph and including a supplied override. Otherwise, if `override_amount_cents` was supplied, `gross_amount_cents` is the smaller of that override and the plan's `coverage_ceiling_cents`. Otherwise the platform takes the eligible amount as the smaller of the claim's `claimed_amount_cents` and the plan's `coverage_ceiling_cents`, selects a verdict factor from the frozen `comparison_verdict` — 10000 basis points, the full amount, for `NEW_DAMAGE`; 5000 basis points, half, for `NO_VISIBLE_CHANGE`; and 0 basis points, nothing at all, for `PRE_EXISTING` — multiplies the eligible amount by that factor, divides by 10000 and rounds half up to whole cents.

The gross is then split by a deductible waterfall: the renter's bucket fills first and the plan absorbs whatever remains. `renter_charge_cents` is the smaller of `gross_amount_cents` and the plan's `deductible_cents`. `plan_paid_cents` is `gross_amount_cents` minus `renter_charge_cents`. `owner_payout_cents` is the full `gross_amount_cents` — the owner is made whole regardless of how the cost is divided behind the scenes.

The gross also selects the outcome, and the three outcomes differ sharply in what they post. When `gross_amount_cents` is 0 the claim becomes `DENIED`. When `gross_amount_cents` is at least the claim's `claimed_amount_cents` it becomes `APPROVED`. Otherwise — a positive gross below the claimed amount — it becomes `PARTIAL`. `APPROVED` and `PARTIAL` are treated identically for money purposes: each appends three ledger rows to the booking — a `RENTER` row of type `DAMAGE_RENTER_CHARGE` carrying the negation of `renter_charge_cents`, a `PLATFORM` row of type `DAMAGE_PLAN_PAYMENT` carrying the negation of `plan_paid_cents`, and an `OWNER` row of type `DAMAGE_OWNER_CREDIT` carrying the positive `owner_payout_cents` — the only difference being the reduced gross in the `PARTIAL` case. `DENIED` appends **no ledger rows at all**, because a denied claim moves no money. All three outcomes increment the claim's `version` and the booking's `version`.

An `Owner` may withdraw a claim before it is decided, and withdrawal is available from `SUBMITTED` and from `INFO_REQUESTED`. Both source states behave identically: the claim's `status` becomes `WITHDRAWN` with a `withdraw_reason` recorded, the claim's `version` increments and the booking's `version` increments. Withdrawal requires that the acting owner own the listing behind the claim's booking, and posts no ledger rows.

Every terminal outcome — `APPROVED`, `PARTIAL`, `DENIED` and `WITHDRAWN` alike — triggers the same check on the booking. When no claim on the booking remains in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED`, the settlement dimension returns `DISPUTED → RECONCILING`, and the booking's `final_total_cents` and `balance_due_cents` are recomputed to fold in the sum of `renter_charge_cents` over the booking's claims now sitting in `APPROVED` or `PARTIAL`. While even one claim remains open, the booking stays `DISPUTED` and cannot be settled.

### 3.9. Settlement, Payout and the Compensating Reversal

Settlement is a `PlatformAdmin` action and is the trip dimension's last forward step, `RETURNED → CLOSED`, taken in lockstep with the settlement dimension's `RECONCILING → SETTLED`. Three preconditions guard it. The booking's `settlement_status` must be `RECONCILING`, otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATE_INVALID`. No damage claim on the booking may sit in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED`, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. And when the booking's `balance_due_cents` is positive, the renter's `wallet_credit_cents` must be at least that amount, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RENTER_FUNDS` (402).

Settlement posts exactly four ledger rows, in a pinned order. First the platform computes the owner's payout as the booking's `final_total_cents` minus its `service_fee_cents` minus its `premium_cents` — the owner keeps the trip's value less the platform's cut and the insurance premium. Then, in this sequence: a `RENTER` row of type `TRIP_CHARGE` carrying the negation of `final_total_cents`; an `OWNER` row of type `OWNER_PAYOUT` carrying the positive owner payout; a `PLATFORM` row of type `SERVICE_FEE` carrying the positive `service_fee_cents`; and a `PLATFORM` row of type `PROTECTION_PREMIUM` carrying the positive `premium_cents`. By construction those four signed amounts sum to exactly zero, which is how the conservation invariant of §2.10 survives settlement.

Settlement then moves the surrounding state. When `balance_due_cents` is positive, that amount is subtracted from the renter's `wallet_credit_cents`; when it is negative, the absolute value of it is added back to the renter's `wallet_credit_cents`. The owner's `payout_balance_cents` is recomputed from the ledger rows whose `party` is `OWNER`. The listing's `completed_night_count` increases by the booking's `nights` and the listing's `last_reconciled_at` is stamped with the logical clock. The settlement dimension reaches `SETTLED`, the trip dimension reaches `CLOSED`, and the booking's `version` increments.

Because settlements can be posted in error, the platform provides a compensating action rather than a mutation. A `PlatformAdmin` reverses a posted settlement with a `reversal_reason`; the reversal requires the booking's `settlement_status` to be exactly `SETTLED`, and an attempt against a booking still `RECONCILING` is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATE_INVALID`. For every forward ledger row of the booking whose `entry_type` is `TRIP_CHARGE`, `OWNER_PAYOUT`, `SERVICE_FEE` or `PROTECTION_PREMIUM` and whose `reversal_of_entry_id` is null, processed in ascending `created_at` order, the platform appends the exact inverse row: same `party`, same magnitude with the sign flipped, `entry_type` being the forward type with the suffix `_REVERSAL` — so `TRIP_CHARGE_REVERSAL`, `OWNER_PAYOUT_REVERSAL`, `SERVICE_FEE_REVERSAL` and `PROTECTION_PREMIUM_REVERSAL` — and `reversal_of_entry_id` set to the identifier of the row being reversed.

The reversal also undoes the surrounding movements. The wallet motion of settlement runs backwards: when `balance_due_cents` is positive that amount is added back to the renter's `wallet_credit_cents`, and when it is negative the absolute value of it is subtracted. The owner's `payout_balance_cents` is recomputed, and the listing's `completed_night_count` decreases by the booking's `nights`, floored at 0. The listing's `last_reconciled_at` is rolled back to the logical-clock stamp of the most recent settlement still standing on that listing, and to null when the reversal leaves the listing with no settled booking at all. The settlement dimension returns `SETTLED → RECONCILING` while the trip dimension **stays `CLOSED`** — the rig came back whichever way the money went — and the booking's `version` increments. The observable guarantee is the one stated in §2.10: the booking's ledger row count goes from four to eight, its signed sum stays zero, and the owner's `payout_balance_cents` returns to its exact pre-settlement value.

### 3.10. Cancellation, Refunds and the Penalty Cascade

Cancellation is a single action available to three roles with a `cancel_reason`, and what happens next depends entirely on who is cancelling. A `Renter` may cancel from `REQUESTED`, from `OWNER_APPROVED` or from `CONFIRMED`, and must be the booking's renter. An `Owner` may cancel from `CONFIRMED` only, and must own the listing. A `PlatformAdmin` may cancel from `REQUESTED`, `OWNER_APPROVED` or `CONFIRMED` with no further precondition, and the same three transitions also fire as a cascade link out of a listing suspension.

The refund share is computed against the scheduled pickup instant, which is the booking's `start_date` at 15:00:00 UTC. The platform takes the whole number of hours from the logical clock at the moment of cancellation to that pickup instant, truncated downward — a value that goes negative once pickup is already past. For a **renter** cancellation, when that hours-remaining figure is greater than or equal to the cancellation policy's `full_refund_hours_before`, the refund share is the full 10000 basis points; the boundary is inclusive, so cancelling at exactly the threshold still earns a complete refund. Otherwise the refund share is the policy's `partial_refund_pct_bp`. `refund_cents` is the booking's `hold_amount_cents` multiplied by that share, divided by 10000 and rounded half up. When the share is anything less than the full 10000 basis points — that is, on any inside-window renter cancellation — the policy's `renter_cancel_penalty_points` are added to the renter's `renter_penalty_points`. For an **owner** or **platform** cancellation the refund share is always the full 10000 basis points and `refund_cents` equals `hold_amount_cents` exactly, with no rounding involved.

Every cancellation, whatever the actor, credits `refund_cents` to the renter's `wallet_credit_cents`, then sets the booking's `hold_amount_cents` to 0, advances the settlement dimension to `REFUNDED`, records the supplied `cancel_reason`, voids every delivery assignment on the booking whose `status` is `REQUESTED`, `SCHEDULED` or `WAITLISTED` in ascending `created_at` order — with each formerly-`SCHEDULED` row decrementing its campground's `scheduled_assignment_count` and triggering the promotion procedure, and with the cancelled booking's own `delivery_fee_cents` and quote left exactly as they stood — and increments the booking's `version`. On a directly requested cancellation the renter's `updated_at` and `version` move whether or not the refund is non-zero and whether or not any penalty was assessed, which is the one place a renter's record advances on a zero-cent credit. A cancellation from `REQUESTED` or `OWNER_APPROVED` finds `settlement_status` at `NONE` and no hold ever taken, so `refund_cents` is 0 and the settlement dimension moves `NONE → REFUNDED`; a cancellation from `CONFIRMED` finds `settlement_status` at `HELD`, so the refund is a real wallet credit and the dimension moves `HELD → REFUNDED`. A `PlatformAdmin` cancellation assesses **no penalty points against anybody**, which is what distinguishes it from the two peer-initiated exits.

An owner cancellation is the deepest chain in the system: five causally linked levels from a single request. At the first level the booking's `trip_status` becomes `CANCELLED_BY_OWNER`, `refund_cents` is set to `hold_amount_cents`, that amount is credited to the renter's `wallet_credit_cents`, `hold_amount_cents` becomes 0 and the settlement dimension advances to `REFUNDED`. Because the booking entered `CANCELLED_BY_OWNER`, the second level assesses the owner: the policy's `owner_cancel_penalty_points` are added to the owner's `owner_penalty_points`, and an owner cancellation fee is computed as the booking's `base_subtotal_cents` multiplied by the policy's `owner_cancel_fee_bp`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up. That fee is posted as a balanced pair of ledger rows — an `OWNER` row of type `OWNER_CANCEL_FEE` carrying the negation of the fee, and a `PLATFORM` row of type `OWNER_CANCEL_FEE` carrying the positive fee — after which the owner's `payout_balance_cents` is recomputed. Both the booking's and the owner's `version` increment.

Because those penalty points may push the owner over the line, the third level fires when the owner's `owner_penalty_points` reaches or exceeds twelve: the owner's `status` becomes `SUSPENDED`, and every listing of that owner whose `listing_status` is `LISTED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, transitions `LISTED → SUSPENDED`. From there the fourth and fifth levels are exactly the cascade already specified in §3.3 — each newly suspended listing cancels its own live bookings into `CANCELLED_BY_PLATFORM` with full refunds to their renters, and each of those cancellations voids its delivery assignments, releases campground slots and runs waitlist promotions. One owner backing out of one weekend can therefore refund several unrelated renters and reshuffle the queue at several campgrounds.

### 3.11. Owner Pricing Guidance and Utilisation Analytics

Owners need to know whether their rig is priced correctly, and the platform answers with a read-only guidance computation that either the owning `Owner` or a `PlatformAdmin` may request for a listing. It mutates nothing and is evaluated fresh on every call.

The analysis window opens on the logical clock's calendar date and runs for ninety days inclusive, so the window's last date is eighty-nine days after its first. Within that window the platform counts **booked nights**: the number of dates in the window covered by at least one booking on this listing whose `trip_status` is `CONFIRMED`, `IN_TRIP`, `RETURNED` or `CLOSED`, where a booking covers every date from its `start_date` up to but not including its `end_date`. Bookings in `REQUESTED` or `OWNER_APPROVED`, and all three cancelled states, are excluded entirely — an unconfirmed request is not demand. A date covered by two qualifying bookings counts once, so the tally can never exceed ninety.

Utilisation is that count expressed in basis points: the booked-night count multiplied by 10000, divided by 90, rounded half up to whole basis points. Utilisation then selects a pricing adjustment on a four-band ladder, evaluated from the top down. At or above 7000 basis points — seventy percent occupancy or better — the adjustment is a positive 1500 basis points, a fifteen percent uplift. At or above 4000 but below 7000, the adjustment is a positive 500 basis points. At or above 2000 but below 4000, the adjustment is 0 and the owner's list price stands. Below 2000 basis points the adjustment is a negative 1000 basis points, a ten percent markdown suggested to stimulate demand.

The seasonal component looks ahead rather than at today: the platform resolves the seasonal multiplier and season code for the date thirty days after the window's opening date, using the selector of §3.1 with all its inclusive-bound and earliest-created-at tie-break behaviour. The suggested rate is then the listing's `base_nightly_rate_cents` multiplied by the sum of 10000 and the utilisation adjustment, multiplied again by the seasonal multiplier in basis points, divided by one hundred million — the product of the two basis-point scales — and rounded half up to whole cents **exactly once, at the very end**. Rounding after the utilisation factor and again after the seasonal factor yields a different, wrong number.

The response reports the listing's identifier as `listing_id`, the window bounds as `window_start_date` and `window_end_date`, the tally as `booked_nights`, the utilisation as `utilization_bp`, the selected adjustment as `util_adj_bp`, the look-ahead season as `season_code` and `season_multiplier_bp`, and the recommendation as `suggested_nightly_rate_cents`. The guidance is advisory: nothing about it changes the listing's stored `base_nightly_rate_cents`.

Two other stored fields serve owner analytics and are worth naming here because they move only at settlement time. A listing's `completed_night_count` is the sum of `nights` over the bookings on that listing whose `trip_status` is `CLOSED`, and its `last_reconciled_at` holds the logical-clock value at the most recent settlement of a booking on that listing — null until the listing's first settlement, and rolled back along with the night count whenever a settlement is reversed.
