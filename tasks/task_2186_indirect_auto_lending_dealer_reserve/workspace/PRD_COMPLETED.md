# Indirect Auto Lending Dealer Reserve API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Indirect auto lending is a business conducted almost entirely at arm's length. A franchised or independent dealership sits between our institution and the retail borrower: the dealership takes the credit application, quotes a rate, prints the retail installment contract, and only afterwards sends us a funding package for purchase. Everything we know about that transaction — the applicant's credit profile, the vehicle's book value, the rate the customer was actually quoted — arrives through the dealer. The commercial arrangement that makes this work is **dealer participation**, colloquially called *dealer reserve*: the dealership is permitted to mark the retail contract up above the buy rate we publish, and it keeps a share of that markup as compensation for originating the paper. Reserve is what motivates a dealer to send us volume, and it is simultaneously the single largest source of legal, credit, and reputational risk in the portfolio.

This document specifies the API for the platform that administers that relationship end to end. It covers the dealer agreement that establishes program eligibility and the participation cap; the tiered rate sheets against which applications are decisioned; the tier-assignment and buy-rate engine, including the loan-to-value and term demotions that push a marginal deal down a tier; the funding-package document verification that gates disbursement; the accrual of participation and volume incentive into a per-dealer reserve ledger; the pro-rata clawback of that reserve when a contract pays off early or defaults inside the contractual chargeback window; the dealer performance scorecard that converts portfolio outcomes into a letter grade; the fair-lending analysis that tests whether markup was distributed evenhandedly across monitoring cohorts; and, at the end of the line, dealer suspension, termination, and the repurchase of contracts already on our books.

Three constraints shape nearly every requirement that follows. First, **money is never approximated** — reserve is a contractual entitlement and a clawback is a receivable, so every cent is computed by a pinned formula with a pinned rounding order. Second, **terms are pinned at the moment of the decision, not read at the moment of the question** — a contract booked under one agreement and one rate sheet continues to be governed by those documents even after both have been replaced, because the dealer's entitlement was earned under the terms then in force. Third, **the same person may not both underwrite and fund** — segregation of duties is a supervisory expectation, not a preference, and the platform enforces it structurally. All resources are mounted under the `/lending` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, empty or whitespace-only — both values are trimmed before the emptiness test — or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the five roles defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before any other; a request with no headers at all never reaches path resolution, body validation, or business rules.

The five roles are `PortfolioAdmin`, `CreditAnalyst`, `FundingSpecialist`, `DealerPrincipal`, and `ComplianceOfficer`.

A `PortfolioAdmin` is the lender-side program manager. Dealer onboarding, dealer lifecycle transitions, agreement drafting, rate-sheet authoring and publication, portfolio-event recording, scorecard computation and finalization, repurchase demands, manual reserve adjustments, and the logical-clock control all belong to this role exclusively. A `CreditAnalyst` is the underwriter; decisioning an application is theirs alone. A `FundingSpecialist` staffs the funding desk: verifying or rejecting individual funding-package documents and releasing funds against a contract are their two exclusive operations. A `DealerPrincipal` is the dealership owner acting for their own store — countersigning agreements, submitting and resubmitting applications, booking contracts, uploading documents, and answering repurchase demands. A `ComplianceOfficer` runs the fair-lending function: creating and transitioning markup disparity reviews, and placing or releasing the dealer compliance hold. Every role may read through every `GET` endpoint.

When a request's role is not listed for the endpoint it addresses, the response is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Two endpoints are multi-action and are additionally gated on the action itself rather than only on the endpoint. On the dealer status endpoint, the actions `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE`, and `TERMINATE` require `PortfolioAdmin`, while `COMPLIANCE_HOLD` and `RELEASE_HOLD` require `ComplianceOfficer`; a `ComplianceOfficer` attempting `TERMINATE`, or a `PortfolioAdmin` attempting `RELEASE_HOLD`, receives `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. On the buyback demand action endpoint, `ACKNOWLEDGE` and `DISPUTE` require `DealerPrincipal`, while `SETTLE` and `WITHDRAW` require `PortfolioAdmin`, with the same rejection code for a mismatch. On both endpoints the action-level gate is applied only to a recognized action token; a missing or unrecognized `action` is an ordinary body-shape failure, reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` in its usual place in the order of §2.7 — that is, after path resolution.

**Identity semantics.** `DealerPrincipal` is the only entity-backed role: when `X-User-Role` is `DealerPrincipal`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Dealer`, and ownership checks on any resource that references a dealership compare that resource's dealer foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, the compared field is `Dealer.id` itself for a dealer resource, `dealer_id` on an agreement, an application, a contract, a scorecard, a review, or a repurchase demand, and — for a document — the `dealer_id` of the contract the document hangs from. The same equality must hold for any `dealer_id` value supplied in a request body. A `DealerPrincipal` who addresses or names a dealership other than their own is rejected with `ERR_DEALER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403), and their list reads are restricted to resources whose dealership is their own.

The other four roles have opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `PortfolioAdmin`, `CreditAnalyst`, `FundingSpecialist`, or `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition writes it — for example as `decisioned_by_user_id` on an application, as `funded_by_user_id` on a contract, or as `verified_by_user_id` on a document — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. Those recorded values matter in exactly one place: the segregation-of-duties rule described in §2.10 compares two of them to each other.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum accepted value of 1 and a maximum accepted value of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A value outside those bounds, or one that is not an integer, is a request-shape failure and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The one deliberate exception is the per-dealer scorecard listing, which is ordered by `period_start` descending — ties broken by `created_at` ascending — so that the most recent scoring window is read first, and the per-sheet tier listing, which is ordered by `min_credit_score` descending and tie-broken by `tier_code` ascending — the same ordering the demotion walk uses, so that what a caller reads is what the pricing engine walks.

List responses populate four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used for the query, `total_count` reports how many records match the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Auditing, and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied value is ignored. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC string with three fractional digits, stamped at insertion and never changed. That canonical three-fractional-digit form is used for every instant the server itself writes — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `decisioned_at`, `verified_at`, `countersigned_at`, `published_at`, `finalized_at`, `closed_at`, and the `api_request_timestamp` of §2.2. An instant supplied by a client, by contrast, is stored and echoed back **verbatim** in exactly the string the client sent — `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `contract_date`, `funding_date`, `event_date`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `window_start`, `window_end`, and the `settled_at` taken from a settlement date — with only its parsed value used for the comparisons §2.9 describes.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is refreshed to the current instant on every mutation of that record, and `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on each mutation. Three entity types are exceptions and are deliberately immutable once written: `RateSheetTier`, `ReserveEntry`, and `ChargebackEvent` carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and neither field appears in their response payloads. A tier row, a ledger entry, and a portfolio-event record are historical facts; correcting one means posting another, not editing the original.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, not wall-clock time. Every effectivity comparison, every seasoning computation, every window test, and every system-stamped timestamp reads that clock. At server startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it continues to read that instant until it is repositioned; it never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `PortfolioAdmin` repositions it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-control capability, which answers with the resulting `current_utc` and mutates no entity. The clock may be moved forward or backward freely, which is how a test harness ages a book of contracts.

### 2.5. Money, Basis Points, Percentages, and Rounding

All monetary quantities are integers denominated in **cents**; there are no fractional cents anywhere in the system. All rate quantities are integers denominated in **basis points**, where one basis point is one hundredth of one percent, so a 4.75% APR is 475 basis points and a 250-basis-point participation cap is a cap of two and a half percentage points of markup.

Wherever a computation must be reduced to an integer, it is rounded **half up** — a value exactly halfway between two integers rounds to the one of larger magnitude away from zero. Two ratio fields on an application, `ltv_pct` and `dti_pct`, are the only non-integer numeric fields in the system; they are carried to **two decimal places, rounded half up**.

The **rounding order is part of the specification, not an implementation detail**. Where a formula in §3 says that each component is rounded on its own before the components are summed, rounding the sum instead produces a different and incorrect answer, and the system is expected to produce the specified one. Likewise, where a formula chains several multiplications and says a single rounding is applied at the end, intermediate results are carried at full precision and rounded once.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on the Dealer Record

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the `Dealer` record, because both are operations where a client is acting on a picture of the dealership that may already be out of date.

Drafting a dealer agreement (`POST /lending/dealer-agreements`) requires a non-optional `expected_dealer_version` field in the request body, carrying the `version` value the client last observed on that `Dealer`. If it does not equal the dealership's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEALER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Nothing in the agreement state machine forbids a second agreement — a successor agreement legitimately supersedes its predecessor — so no state precondition would catch a writer who is drafting against a program configuration that has since changed. The version check is the only thing that does.

Posting a manual reserve adjustment (`POST /lending/dealers/{id}/reserve-adjustments`) is guarded identically: the request body must carry a non-optional `expected_dealer_version` matching the dealership's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEALER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Repeated adjustments of the same amount are ordinary ledger activity, so no state rule rejects the second one; the version check is what stops a writer from posting an amount it derived from a reserve balance that has since moved.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and none should be invented. Note that a great many operations increment `Dealer.version` as a side effect — funding, chargeback events, buyback settlement, agreement countersignature, scorecard finalization, remediation orders, and every dealer status change — so a client that reads a dealership, waits, and then drafts an agreement will very often find its expectation stale.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and the Order of Rejection

Path parameters are resolved **left to right**. When a request addresses a nested resource and an outer identifier does not resolve, the outer failure is reported and the inner identifier is never examined. A `DealerPrincipal`'s scope check precedes path resolution only where the acting dealership is named directly — the `{id}` of a dealer-rooted path, or a `dealer_id` carried in the request body. Where the dealership is reached indirectly, through whatever the path or body identifier resolves to — an agreement, application, contract, document, demand, review, scorecard, or ledger entry — the scope check necessarily runs after that identifier resolves, so an unresolvable identifier yields its not-found code rather than `ERR_DEALER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Each entity type has its own not-found code: `ERR_DEALER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_SHEET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUYBACK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCORECARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_RESERVE_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, each returned with HTTP 404.

The global order in which a request is evaluated is fixed, and it matters whenever a single request would fail more than one check. Authentication of the headers comes first (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`), then the endpoint's role list and any action-level role gate (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`), then the dealer-scope check for a `DealerPrincipal` (`ERR_DEALER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`), then left-to-right resolution of path identifiers (the not-found codes above), then request-body shape and range validation (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), then resolution of foreign keys named in the request body (`ERR_DEALER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate, on agreement creation, application submission, contract booking, buyback issuance, and fair-lending review creation), then the state precondition for the requested transition (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`), then the operation-specific state gates for funding readiness (`ERR_DOCS_NOT_VERIFIED`) and application approval (`ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_APPROVED`), then the optimistic-concurrency check (`ERR_STALE_DEALER_VERSION`), then the compliance hold (`ERR_DEALER_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`), then segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), then dealer activity status (`ERR_DEALER_NOT_ACTIVE`), and only then the domain-specific referential, temporal, computational and fiscal rules described throughout §3, in the order in which they are introduced there.

Request-body shape validation covers required fields being present, values being of the declared type, enum members being drawn from the declared set, numbers lying inside the ranges §3 states for them, and every interval ending strictly after it starts — an agreement's `effective_to` strictly after its `effective_from`, a rate sheet's `effective_to` strictly after its `effective_from`, a scorecard's `period_end` strictly after its `period_start`, a review's `window_end` strictly after its `window_start` — plus the requirement that `chargeback_window_days` be an exact multiple of 30. Any of these failures is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

### 2.8. The Dealer Roll-Up

The `Dealer` record carries a block of derived fields that summarize the dealership's standing, and they are maintained by the server rather than supplied by any client. `reserve_balance_cents` is the signed sum of `amount_cents` across every ledger entry whose `dealer_id` is this dealership's, taken over all entry types, and it is recomputed on every ledger posting. `outstanding_principal_cents` is the sum of `unpaid_principal_cents` across this dealership's contracts whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED`, using each contract's currently persisted value — a contract's unpaid principal is never re-amortized merely because someone read the dealer record. `funded_contract_count` is the number of this dealership's contracts whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED`. Both of these last two are fully recomputed at funding and again at every subsequent change of a contract's `funding_status`.

Alongside those sit a set of counters. `total_funded_ever_count` increases by one on every successful funding and is never decreased, even when the contract later pays off, defaults, or is repurchased. `early_payoff_count` counts this dealership's contracts that reached `PAID_OFF` with a seasoning below the early-payoff threshold; `default_count` counts those that reached `DEFAULTED`; `repurchase_count` counts those that reached `REPURCHASED` through a settled repurchase demand; and `chargeback_count` counts the clawback entries posted against this dealership. From two of those, `early_payoff_rate_bps` is derived: when `total_funded_ever_count` is greater than zero, it is ten thousand multiplied by `early_payoff_count`, divided by `total_funded_ever_count`, rounded half up to a whole number of basis points; when `total_funded_ever_count` is zero it is zero. It is recomputed whenever either input changes.

Two further fields track quality supervision. `latest_scorecard_grade` holds the `grade` of the most recently finalized scorecard for this dealership and is null until one is finalized; its permitted values are `A`, `B`, `C`, `D`, and `UNRATED`. `scorecard_stale` is a boolean, false by default, set to true whenever `reserve_balance_cents` moves because a `CHARGEBACK` entry was posted — by that entry type alone, so a `BUYBACK_OFFSET` or a `REMEDIATION` posting moves the balance without raising the flag — and cleared back to false whenever any scorecard for this dealership is finalized — it is the flag that tells a portfolio manager the dealership's published grade no longer reflects its ledger.

A newly onboarded dealership is created with every counter and every derived amount at zero, `compliance_hold` false, `suspension_reason` null, `latest_scorecard_grade` null, `scorecard_stale` false, and `version` 1.

### 2.9. Pinning, Effectivity, and the Two Interval Conventions

The system uses two different interval conventions, and confusing them is the most common way to get an answer wrong.

**Rate sheets use a half-open window.** A sheet is effective when its `effective_from` is at or before the clock **and** the clock is strictly before its `effective_to`. A sheet whose `effective_to` is exactly the current instant is no longer effective. **Dealer agreements use a closed window.** An agreement is in force when its `effective_from` is at or before the clock **and** the clock is at or before its `effective_to`; an agreement whose `effective_to` is exactly the current instant is still in force on that instant. Scorecard trailing windows and fair-lending review windows are likewise half-open over the contract's `funded_at`: at or after the start, strictly before the end.

Once a decision is made, the documents that governed it are **pinned**. An application records `agreement_id`, `rate_sheet_id`, and `assigned_tier_id` at decision time, and a contract copies `agreement_id` forward at creation. Every reserve accrual and every chargeback computation for that contract reads the pinned agreement — its `participation_method`, its `max_participation_bps`, its `flat_fee_cap_cents`, its `chargeback_window_days` — and never the dealership's currently in-force agreement, even after the pinned one has moved to `SUPERSEDED` or `TERMINATED`. Likewise an application that was decisioned under one rate sheet keeps its `rate_sheet_id` and `assigned_tier_id` when that sheet is later retired.

### 2.10. Compliance Holds and Segregation of Duties

`compliance_hold` is a boolean on the dealership that is independent of `status`. It can be raised or lowered directly by a `ComplianceOfficer` through the dealer status endpoint's `COMPLIANCE_HOLD` and `RELEASE_HOLD` actions, neither of which changes `status`, and it is raised automatically when a fair-lending review is published with an adverse finding. While `compliance_hold` is true, three operations are refused with `ERR_DEALER_COMPLIANCE_HOLD` (HTTP 409): decisioning any application of that dealership, booking a contract for that dealership, and funding one of its contracts. Reads, document uploads, document verification, portfolio events, scorecards, and buyback handling are all unaffected.

Segregation of duties is enforced at exactly one point. The `X-User-ID` of the `FundingSpecialist` releasing funds must differ from the `decisioned_by_user_id` recorded on the application the contract was booked from. When they are equal, the funding is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). Note that the check is on the identifier, not on the role: the same human operating under two roles with one identifier is precisely what the rule exists to catch.

### 2.11. Ledger Discipline

The reserve ledger is append-only. Each `ReserveEntry` names the `dealer_id` whose ledger it moves and, for contract-attributable movements, the `contract_id` it belongs to. Two entry types are dealer-level and carry a null `contract_id`: `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT` and `REMEDIATION`. The four contract-level types are `PARTICIPATION_ACCRUAL`, `INCENTIVE_ACCRUAL`, `CHARGEBACK`, and `BUYBACK_OFFSET`. Each entry carries a signed `amount_cents` — positive credits the dealership, negative claws back — a `reason` string of one to one hundred and twenty characters, and a `balance_after_cents` that captures `reserve_balance_cents` immediately after the entry was posted. Only a `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT` takes its `reason` from the request; every entry the platform posts of its own accord carries a `reason` equal to its own `entry_type` string.

Two fiscal floors apply. A manual reserve adjustment must not drive `reserve_balance_cents` below zero, and neither may a fair-lending remediation posting; either attempt is refused with `ERR_RESERVE_BALANCE_NEGATIVE` (HTTP 409). Accruals, chargebacks, and buyback offsets arising from the ordinary contract lifecycle are not subject to that floor — they are consequences of contracts already booked, and refusing them would misstate the receivable.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding a Dealership and the Agreement Chain

A dealership enters the system when a `PortfolioAdmin` onboards it. The onboarding payload names the store's `legal_name` — between 1 and 120 characters, and not blank — and its `state_code`, exactly two uppercase letters identifying the US state where the dealership is licensed. Two further inputs are optional. `program_code` names the rate-sheet program the dealership buys under and defaults to `PS-STANDARD` when omitted; it is the value matched against published rate sheets at decision time. `funding_line_cents` sets the maximum aggregate unpaid principal the lender is willing to carry for this dealership at any one moment, must be zero or greater, and defaults to 50,000,000 cents — half a million dollars of exposure. A new dealership is created in `PROSPECT`, which is a status that permits nothing: no application may be submitted, decisioned, or funded for a `PROSPECT` dealership. The four dealer statuses are `PROSPECT`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, and `TERMINATED`.

Program eligibility begins only when a countersigned agreement exists. A `PortfolioAdmin` drafts one by naming the `dealer_id` it binds, the `participation_method` by which reserve will be earned — either `RATE_PARTICIPATION` or `FLAT_FEE` — the `max_participation_bps` cap on retained markup, which must lie between 0 and 250 inclusive, and the `effective_from` and `effective_to` bounds of the agreement's in-force window. Three inputs are optional: `flat_fee_cap_cents`, a per-contract ceiling that applies only under `FLAT_FEE` and is ignored entirely under `RATE_PARTICIPATION`, which must be zero or greater and defaults to 0; `chargeback_window_days`, the number of days after funding during which reserve remains clawable, which must be an exact multiple of 30 lying between 60 and 720 inclusive and defaults to 360 — twelve months of exposure; and `supersedes_agreement_id`, naming the prior agreement this one is intended to replace, which is null for a dealership's first agreement and for any successor that does not replace an incumbent. The draft is also guarded by the optimistic-concurrency check of §2.6 through its mandatory `expected_dealer_version`, and drafting an agreement itself advances `Dealer.version` by one. A freshly drafted agreement sits in `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN` with `countersigned_at` and `signer_name` both null.

**The window rules for agreements differ depending on whether the draft supersedes anything, and the two branches are not symmetric.** When `supersedes_agreement_id` is null, the draft must respect a mandatory notice buffer of 30 days against every existing agreement of the same dealership whose status is `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN` or `ACTIVE`: the new agreement's `effective_from` must be at or after that existing agreement's `effective_to` plus 30 days. Windows that merely touch — a new agreement starting on the day the incumbent ends — are **rejected**, because the buffer is mandatory rather than a mere non-overlap requirement. When `supersedes_agreement_id` is set, the buffer does not apply at all; instead the new agreement's `effective_from` must be strictly after the superseded agreement's `effective_from` and at or before the superseded agreement's `effective_to`, so that a successor is genuinely nested inside the term it replaces. Either branch failing yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

Countersignature is the dealer's act. Supplying a `signer_name`, the `DealerPrincipal` moves the agreement `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN → ACTIVE`; the agreement's `countersigned_at` is stamped with the current logical instant and `signer_name` is retained on the record. The agreement must genuinely be in `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN` — countersigning one that is already `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED`, or `TERMINATED` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — its `dealer_id` must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, and the dealership itself must be in `PROSPECT`, `ACTIVE`, or `SUSPENDED`. Three side effects follow, and all three matter. When the dealership was in `PROSPECT`, it advances `PROSPECT → ACTIVE`, which is how program eligibility actually begins. When `supersedes_agreement_id` is not null and the named predecessor is genuinely `ACTIVE`, it moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` and its `effective_to` is truncated to one day before the successor's `effective_from` — a server-written value, so it is rewritten in the canonical timestamp form of §2.4 rather than in the string the predecessor was drafted with; a predecessor in any other status is left untouched and the countersignature still succeeds; contracts already booked under the predecessor keep their pinned reference to it and continue to be priced and charged back under its `chargeback_window_days` and `max_participation_bps`, exactly as §2.9 requires. A successor may only supersede an agreement of the same dealership whose `effective_from` is earlier than its own; both conditions are enforced at drafting time, not here, and either failure is `ERR_AGREEMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. And, as with every dealer-touching operation, `Dealer.version` advances by one. The four agreement statuses are `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN`, `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED`, and `TERMINATED`. Agreements can be listed and narrowed by owning `dealer_id`, by the `supersedes_agreement_id` they name, and by `status`.

### 3.2. Rate Sheets, Tiers, and Program Pricing

Pricing is published, not negotiated per deal. A `PortfolioAdmin` creates a rate sheet by naming the `program_code` it prices and the `effective_from` and `effective_to` bounds of its half-open effectivity window. The sheet is born in `DRAFT` with `tier_count` at zero and `published_at` null; the three sheet statuses are `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED`, and `RETIRED`.

Tier rows are appended to a `DRAFT` sheet one at a time and are immutable once written. Each row carries a `tier_code` — one of `T1`, `T2`, or `T3`, and unique within its sheet — together with the inclusive bounds of its credit-score band, `min_credit_score` and `max_credit_score`, both between 300 and 900 with the maximum at or above the minimum. Three of the row's fields describe the risk the tier will tolerate before it pushes a deal down: `max_ltv_pct`, the highest loan-to-value percentage acceptable at this tier, between 50 and 150; `max_term_months`, the longest contract term acceptable at this tier, between 12 and 96; and `advance_rate_pct`, the maximum percentage of collateral value the lender will advance at this tier, also between 50 and 150. The remaining two are commercial: `buy_rate_apr_bps`, the lender's own buy rate for the tier expressed in basis points of APR and lying between 0 and 3000, and `acquisition_fee_cents`, a flat fee — zero or greater — deducted from dealer proceeds at funding for any contract booked at this tier. Appending a row advances the owning sheet's `tier_count`. Because a tier row is an immutable historical fact, it carries only its `id` and `created_at`. All eight of the row's fields are required. A repeated `tier_code` is a body-shape failure, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and is judged alongside the other field validations — that is, before the owning sheet's `DRAFT` status is tested; appending to a sheet that is `PUBLISHED` or `RETIRED` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Publication is where a sheet becomes usable and where the calendar is policed. A sheet must be in `DRAFT`, or the attempt is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. It must carry at least one tier row, or the attempt is `ERR_RATE_SHEET_NO_TIERS` (HTTP 422). And its window must not intersect the window of any other `PUBLISHED` sheet for the same `program_code`. Intersection is tested under half-open semantics: two windows intersect when the existing sheet's `effective_from` is strictly before the new sheet's `effective_to` **and** the new sheet's `effective_from` is strictly before the existing sheet's `effective_to`. Windows that merely touch — the new sheet beginning at the exact instant the existing one ends — do **not** intersect and are accepted. This is the opposite of the agreement rule in §3.1, where touching is forbidden. An intersection is refused with `ERR_RATE_SHEET_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409).

On a successful publication the sheet moves `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` and `published_at` is stamped with the current logical instant. Publication also performs the housekeeping that keeps the program's history tidy: every other sheet of the same `program_code` that is currently `PUBLISHED` and whose `effective_to` is at or before the current instant moves `PUBLISHED → RETIRED`, processed in ascending `effective_from` order and tie-broken by ascending `created_at`. Retirement is bookkeeping only — an application already decisioned keeps its pinned `rate_sheet_id` and `assigned_tier_id` regardless.

### 3.3. Application Intake, Decisioning, and the Revision Cycle

A `DealerPrincipal` submits a retail credit application on behalf of their own store, naming the `dealer_id` — which must equal their own `X-User-ID` — and an `applicant_reference`, a dealer-supplied opaque reference of 1 to 64 characters standing in for the retail applicant, since no personally identifying data is stored here. The payload also carries a `monitoring_cohort`, either `CONTROL` or `MONITORED`, a regulatory label used by nothing except the fair-lending markup analysis of §3.8. The credit and deal facts follow: `credit_score` between 300 and 900, `monthly_income_cents` strictly greater than zero, `monthly_debt_cents` zero or greater, `amount_financed_cents` strictly greater than zero, `collateral_value_cents` strictly greater than zero, `term_months` between 12 and 96, `vehicle_model_year` between 1990 and 2030, and `requested_apr_bps` — the APR the dealership intends to quote the customer — between 0 and 3600. Submission requires the dealership to be `ACTIVE`; otherwise `ERR_DEALER_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409).

Two ratios are derived at submission and again on every revision. `ltv_pct` is `amount_financed_cents` multiplied by 100, divided by `collateral_value_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `dti_pct` is `monthly_debt_cents` multiplied by 100, divided by `monthly_income_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places. A new application is created in `SUBMITTED`, with `agreement_id`, `rate_sheet_id`, `assigned_tier_id`, `base_tier_code`, `assigned_tier_code`, `buy_rate_apr_bps`, `max_contract_apr_bps`, `held_from_status`, `decisioned_by_user_id`, `decisioned_at`, and `decline_reason` all null, and `demotion_count`, `resubmission_count`, and `stipulation_count` all zero. The eight application statuses are `SUBMITTED`, `CONDITIONED`, `RESUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, `DECLINED`, `ON_HOLD`, `WITHDRAWN`, and `CONTRACTED`. Applications can be listed and narrowed by `dealer_id`, by `status`, by the pinned `agreement_id`, by the pinned `rate_sheet_id`, and by the `assigned_tier_id`.

A `CreditAnalyst` decisions the file by supplying an `outcome` of `APPROVE`, `CONDITION`, or `DECLINE`, optionally a `stipulation_count` — an integer of zero or greater — and optionally a `decision_note`. Whichever outcome is chosen, the analyst's `X-User-ID` is stamped onto `decisioned_by_user_id` and the current logical instant onto `decisioned_at` — and that instant starts the funding clock of §3.5. All three outcomes require the dealership to be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_DEALER_NOT_ACTIVE`) and its `compliance_hold` to be false (`ERR_DEALER_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`). `APPROVE` and `CONDITION` are permitted only from `SUBMITTED` or `RESUBMITTED`; `DECLINE` is permitted from `SUBMITTED`, `RESUBMITTED`, or `CONDITIONED`. Any other originating status is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

A `DECLINE` closes the file: the application moves to `DECLINED` and `decline_reason` is set to the supplied `decision_note`, or to the sentinel `ANALYST_DECLINE` when no note was given. A `CONDITION` returns the file to the dealership with stipulations: the application moves to `CONDITIONED`, `stipulation_count` is set to the supplied value or to 1 when it is omitted, and — importantly — `agreement_id` is pinned to the in-force agreement at this moment, exactly as an approval would pin it. A `CONDITION` therefore requires that an in-force agreement exist (`ERR_NO_INFORCE_AGREEMENT`, HTTP 422) but does **not** require a rate sheet, run tier assignment, or pin `rate_sheet_id` or `assigned_tier_id`.

An `APPROVE` runs the full pricing engine, and the order of its steps is pinned because different orders produce different rejections for the same file.

**Step one — sheet selection.** Among all rate sheets, the eligible ones are those whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`, whose `program_code` equals the dealership's `program_code`, and whose half-open window contains the current logical instant: `effective_from` at or before the instant and `effective_to` strictly after it. `DRAFT` and `RETIRED` sheets are never eligible. Eligible candidates are ranked by `effective_from` descending, tie-broken by `created_at` descending, and the first is selected. When no candidate exists, the decision fails with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_RATE_SHEET` (HTTP 422).

**Step two — agreement selection.** Among the dealership's agreements, the eligible ones are those whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose closed window contains the current instant: `effective_from` at or before it and `effective_to` at or after it. `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN`, `SUPERSEDED`, and `TERMINATED` agreements are never eligible. Candidates are ranked by `effective_from` descending, tie-broken by `created_at` descending, and the first is selected. When no candidate exists, the decision fails with `ERR_NO_INFORCE_AGREEMENT` (HTTP 422).

**Step three — base tier banding.** The selected sheet's tier rows are ordered by `min_credit_score` descending and tie-broken by `tier_code` ascending — richest band first. Walking that ordered list, the base tier is the first row whose band contains the applicant's `credit_score`: the score is at or above `min_credit_score` and at or below `max_credit_score`. Its position in the ordered list is the base index, and its `tier_code` is recorded as `base_tier_code`. If no row's band contains the score, the decision fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TIER` (HTTP 422).

**Step four — demotion.** `demotion_count` starts at zero. It increases by one when `ltv_pct` is strictly greater than the **base** tier's `max_ltv_pct`, and by one again when `term_months` is strictly greater than the **base** tier's `max_term_months`, so its value is 0, 1, or 2. Both tests are evaluated **once, against the base tier's own caps** — the caps of the tier a deal has been demoted into are deliberately not re-checked, so a deal never cascades further than two steps. The final index is the base index plus `demotion_count`. If the final index runs past the last row of the ordered tier list, the decision fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TIER` (HTTP 422).

**Step five — the affordability gate, evaluated only after a tier has resolved.** If `dti_pct` is strictly greater than 45.00 percent, the decision fails with `ERR_DTI_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Because this gate runs last, a file that both bands into no tier and exceeds the debt-to-income ceiling reports the tier failure, not the affordability failure.

On success the row at the final index becomes the assigned tier. The application moves to `APPROVED` and pins `rate_sheet_id` to the selected sheet, `agreement_id` to the selected agreement, `assigned_tier_id` to the assigned tier row, `assigned_tier_code` to that row's `tier_code`, and `buy_rate_apr_bps` to that row's `buy_rate_apr_bps`. Finally, `max_contract_apr_bps` is pinned as the assigned `buy_rate_apr_bps` plus whichever is smaller of the agreement's `max_participation_bps` and the program-wide markup ceiling of 250 basis points. That single number is the highest APR the dealership may lawfully contract at, and §3.4 enforces it.

The revision cycle closes the loop. A `DealerPrincipal` whose application sits in `CONDITIONED` may return it with revised terms, moving it `CONDITIONED → RESUBMITTED`. The revision payload may carry any of `credit_score`, `amount_financed_cents`, `collateral_value_cents`, `term_months`, and `requested_apr_bps`; every field supplied overwrites the stored value and every field omitted leaves the stored value untouched. `resubmission_count` increases by one, and `ltv_pct` and `dti_pct` are recomputed from the merged values. The application must genuinely be `CONDITIONED` (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`), its `dealer_id` must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, its dealership must be `ACTIVE`, and `resubmission_count` must be strictly less than 2 — a third revision attempt is refused with `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). A resubmitted file can be decisioned again, and a second `CONDITION` returns it to `CONDITIONED`, so the cycle `CONDITIONED → RESUBMITTED → CONDITIONED` may run until the revision limit bites. An approval issued from `RESUBMITTED` behaves identically to one issued from `SUBMITTED` and retains the `stipulation_count` set by the earlier conditioning — which is what causes an income document to be required at funding.

### 3.4. Booking the Retail Contract and Assembling the Funding Package

Once a file is `APPROVED`, the dealership books the retail installment contract it actually signed with the customer. The booking payload is short: the `application_id` of the approved file, the `contract_apr_bps` actually contracted with the retail customer, and the `contract_date` on which the paper was signed at the dealership. `contract_apr_bps` carries no range bound of its own; the only band it must satisfy is the pricing gate below. Everything else about the contract is derived, because everything else was already decided upstream.

Three gates guard the booking, and they are evaluated in this order. The application must be in `APPROVED`; a file in any other status — including one already `CONTRACTED` — is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409). The dealership must then be free of a compliance hold (`ERR_DEALER_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`) and, tested after that, `ACTIVE` (`ERR_DEALER_NOT_ACTIVE`) — the hold before the status, exactly as §2.7 orders them. Then the two pricing gates fire. First, `contract_apr_bps` must be at or above the application's pinned `buy_rate_apr_bps` and at or below its pinned `max_contract_apr_bps`; a rate below the buy rate or above the participation ceiling is refused with `ERR_MARKUP_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Second, the advance limit: the application's `amount_financed_cents` must be at or below the applicant's `collateral_value_cents` multiplied by the **assigned** tier's `advance_rate_pct`, divided by 100 and rounded half up — the tier used is the post-demotion tier the decision assigned, never the base tier. Exceeding it is `ERR_ADVANCE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

Booking a contract advances the application `APPROVED → CONTRACTED` and creates the contract with `funding_status` at `PENDING_DOCS` and `reserve_status` at `NONE`. The contract copies forward from the application its `dealer_id`, its pinned `agreement_id`, its `amount_financed_cents`, its `term_months`, its `buy_rate_apr_bps`, and its `monitoring_cohort`, and it copies `acquisition_fee_cents` from the assigned tier row. Two values are computed at creation: `markup_bps`, which is `contract_apr_bps` less `buy_rate_apr_bps`, and `required_doc_types`, the array of document types the funding package must contain. The base requirement is three types — `RETAIL_INSTALLMENT_CONTRACT`, `TITLE_APPLICATION`, and `INSURANCE_BINDER` — and a fourth, `INCOME_VERIFICATION`, is added when the originating application's `stipulation_count` is greater than zero. That array is pinned at creation and does not change afterwards, even if the application is later touched. `verified_doc_count`, `doc_defect_count`, `reserve_accrued_cents`, `incentive_accrued_cents`, `dealer_proceeds_cents`, `unpaid_principal_cents`, and `months_seasoned` all begin at zero, and `funded_at`, `funded_by_user_id`, and `payoff_months_seasoned` all begin null.

A contract carries **two independent state dimensions**, and confusing them is a category error. `funding_status` tracks documents and the credit lifecycle across `PENDING_DOCS`, `DOCS_VERIFIED`, `FUNDED`, `PAID_OFF`, `DEFAULTED`, and `REPURCHASED`, of which the last three are terminal. `reserve_status` tracks the dealer's participation entitlement across `NONE`, `ACCRUED`, `HELD`, `CHARGED_BACK`, and `EARNED`, of which the last two are terminal. They advance independently except where a rule explicitly names both: issuing a repurchase demand moves `reserve_status` without touching `funding_status`, and rejecting a document moves `funding_status` without touching `reserve_status`. Where §3.6 and §3.7 write `reserve_status`, they write it outright rather than only from the origin named: a portfolio event recorded against a contract whose reserve is `HELD` under an open repurchase demand still drives that reserve straight to `CHARGED_BACK` or `EARNED` on the seasoning test. Contracts can be listed and narrowed by `dealer_id`, by originating `application_id`, by pinned `agreement_id`, and by either dimension — `funding_status` or `reserve_status`.

The `DealerPrincipal` uploads the funding package one document at a time, each upload naming a `doc_type` — one of `RETAIL_INSTALLMENT_CONTRACT`, `TITLE_APPLICATION`, `INSURANCE_BINDER`, or `INCOME_VERIFICATION` — and a `document_reference`, the dealership's own imaging reference of 1 to 64 characters. A given `doc_type` may appear at most once per contract; a second upload of a type already present is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Each document is created in `RECEIVED`, with `defect_code`, `verified_by_user_id`, and `verified_at` null. The three document statuses are `RECEIVED`, `VERIFIED`, and `REJECTED`. A contract's documents can be listed and narrowed by `status` and by `doc_type`.

Verification is the `FundingSpecialist`'s act and is where the document dimension and the contract dimension meet. The verifier supplies an `outcome` of either `VERIFY` or `REJECT`, and optionally a `defect_code`. Verification is permitted only while the owning contract's `funding_status` is `PENDING_DOCS` or `DOCS_VERIFIED`; a contract already `FUNDED` or terminal cannot have its package re-worked, and the attempt is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

A `VERIFY` outcome is accepted on a document in `RECEIVED` or in `REJECTED` — the second case being the cure of an earlier defect, closing the cycle `VERIFIED → REJECTED → VERIFIED`. In both cases the document moves to `VERIFIED`, its `defect_code` is cleared to null, `verified_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`, and `verified_at` with the current logical instant. When the document's `doc_type` is in the contract's `required_doc_types`, the contract's `verified_doc_count` increases by one; and when that verification brings every type in `required_doc_types` to `VERIFIED`, the contract advances `PENDING_DOCS → DOCS_VERIFIED`, with `verified_doc_count` then equal to the number of required types. A verification of a document whose type is not in the required set — an income document on a contract that never carried a stipulation — is accepted and stamped, but moves neither counter nor contract.

A `REJECT` outcome is accepted on a document in `RECEIVED` or in `VERIFIED`. The document moves to `REJECTED` and its `defect_code` is set to the supplied value, or to the sentinel `UNSPECIFIED_DEFECT` when none was supplied; `verified_by_user_id` and `verified_at` are stamped exactly as on a verification. The contract's `doc_defect_count` increases by one — and never decreases, because it is a permanent record of how much rework this package took, and §3.7 reads it. When the rejected document had been `VERIFIED`, the contract's `verified_doc_count` decreases by one; and when that document's `doc_type` is in `required_doc_types`, the contract reverts `DOCS_VERIFIED → PENDING_DOCS`. A contract that has reverted cannot be funded again until the document is re-verified.

### 3.5. Funding, Dealer Participation, and the Reserve Ledger

Funding is the moment money leaves and the dealer's entitlement is created. A `FundingSpecialist` supplies a single input — the `funding_date` — and the platform runs six checks before any of it happens, in this order. The contract's `funding_status` must be `DOCS_VERIFIED`, meaning every type in `required_doc_types` has a document in `VERIFIED`; otherwise `ERR_DOCS_NOT_VERIFIED` (HTTP 409). The dealership's `compliance_hold` must be false (`ERR_DEALER_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`). The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the originating application's `decisioned_by_user_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`). The dealership must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_DEALER_NOT_ACTIVE`). The supplied `funding_date` must fall no more than 30 days after the application's `decisioned_at`, and must also be at or after the contract's `contract_date`; either failure is `ERR_FUNDING_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). A `funding_date` earlier than `decisioned_at` is not itself a failure of the thirty-day test — that test bounds the gap from above only, and the `contract_date` floor is the sole constraint from below. And the dealership's current `outstanding_principal_cents` plus this contract's `amount_financed_cents` must be at or below its `funding_line_cents`; exceeding the line is `ERR_DEALER_FUNDING_LINE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).

**Computing dealer participation.** The reserve calculation begins by capping the markup three ways: the **eligible markup** is the smallest of the contract's `markup_bps`, the pinned agreement's `max_participation_bps`, and the program-wide ceiling of 250 basis points. Note again that the agreement consulted is the one pinned on the contract, never the dealership's currently in-force agreement.

Under a pinned `participation_method` of `RATE_PARTICIPATION`, the gross participation is `amount_financed_cents`, multiplied by the eligible markup expressed as a fraction of ten thousand, multiplied by a number of basis months divided by twelve, multiplied by the participation factor of 0.55 — and the result is rounded half up to whole cents **exactly once, at the very end**, with every intermediate product carried at full precision. Under a pinned `participation_method` of `FLAT_FEE`, the gross participation is instead the smaller of the agreement's `flat_fee_cap_cents` and `amount_financed_cents` multiplied by 150 basis points expressed as a fraction of ten thousand, that product rounded half up to whole cents. Whichever branch applies, `reserve_accrued_cents` is the smaller of that gross figure and the program reserve cap of 250,000 cents — no single contract may accrue more than two thousand five hundred dollars of participation.

Separately, a volume incentive accrues on every funded contract regardless of participation method. `incentive_accrued_cents` is the smaller of 15,000 cents and `amount_financed_cents` multiplied by 25 basis points expressed as a fraction of ten thousand, that product rounded half up to whole cents — one quarter of one percent of the amount financed, capped at one hundred and fifty dollars.

The rate-participation branch runs in two variants that differ **only** in the number of basis months used. The **scheduled-yield** variant, which runs at funding, uses the contract's full `term_months`; it values the participation over the whole scheduled term and it is the figure actually posted to the ledger. The **earned-to-date** variant, which runs on every portfolio event in §3.6, uses that event's `months_seasoned` instead; it values participation over elapsed seasoning only and is written to `earned_to_date_reserve_cents` on the event record as a disclosure figure. It is never posted to the ledger, and for the same contract it is deliberately **not** equal to `reserve_accrued_cents` less `participation_chargeback_cents`, because the chargeback prorates over the agreement's chargeback window while this variant prorates over the contract's term. Under a pinned `participation_method` of `FLAT_FEE` there is no second variant at all: the flat computation ignores basis months entirely, so the earned-to-date figure written to an event equals the accrued figure whatever the seasoning. Either way the program reserve cap of 250,000 cents is applied to the earned-to-date figure exactly as it is applied to the posted one.

On a successful funding the contract records `funded_at` as the supplied `funding_date` and `funded_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, and advances `DOCS_VERIFIED → FUNDED` on the first dimension and `NONE → ACCRUED` on the second. `dealer_proceeds_cents` is computed once here as `amount_financed_cents`, less `acquisition_fee_cents`, plus `reserve_accrued_cents`, plus `incentive_accrued_cents` — what the dealership is actually paid. `unpaid_principal_cents` is set to `amount_financed_cents`.

Two ledger entries are posted, **in this order**: first a `PARTICIPATION_ACCRUAL` entry crediting `reserve_accrued_cents`, then an `INCENTIVE_ACCRUAL` entry crediting `incentive_accrued_cents`. Each names the dealership's `dealer_id` and this `contract_id`, carries a signed `amount_cents`, and stamps `balance_after_cents` with the dealership's `reserve_balance_cents` as it stood immediately after that entry was applied — so reading the two entries back shows the balance stepping twice, not once. The dealership's `reserve_balance_cents`, `outstanding_principal_cents`, and `funded_contract_count` are then recomputed, `total_funded_ever_count` increases by one, and `Dealer.version` advances by one.

**The amortization convention.** Whenever a funded contract's `unpaid_principal_cents` must be recomputed — at each subsequent change of `funding_status`, and when a repurchase demand takes its snapshot — it is derived on a straight line. The elapsed seasoning is the number of whole days between `funded_at` and the reference instant, divided by 30 and rounded **down** to a whole number of months; that figure is then capped at the contract's `term_months`, so a contract seasoned past its own term amortizes to nothing rather than to a negative balance. The principal repaid is `amount_financed_cents` multiplied by those capped months, divided by `term_months`, rounded half up to whole cents, and `unpaid_principal_cents` is `amount_financed_cents` less that repaid amount.

A `PortfolioAdmin` may also move a dealership's ledger directly, posting a manual adjustment that names a signed `amount_cents`, a `reason` of 1 to 120 characters, and the `expected_dealer_version` of §2.6. The resulting entry has `entry_type` `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT` and a null `contract_id`, because it belongs to the dealership rather than to any single deal; `balance_after_cents` is stamped, `reserve_balance_cents` is recomputed, and `Dealer.version` advances by one. An adjustment that would drive `reserve_balance_cents` below zero is refused with `ERR_RESERVE_BALANCE_NEGATIVE` (HTTP 409). The ledger as a whole can be read and narrowed by `dealer_id`, by `contract_id`, and by `entry_type`, in `created_at` ascending order — the order in which the entries were actually posted.

### 3.6. Portfolio Events and the Reserve Chargeback

A funded contract eventually leaves the book, and how it leaves determines whether the dealership keeps the reserve it accrued. A `PortfolioAdmin` records the departure by lodging a portfolio event against the contract, naming an `event_type` of either `EARLY_PAYOFF` or `DEFAULT` and the `event_date` on which it was recorded. The contract's `funding_status` must be `FUNDED` — an event against a contract that is still in documents, or already terminal, is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and the `event_date` must be at or after the contract's `funded_at`. Equality is accepted and simply yields a seasoning of zero; an earlier date is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_DATE` (HTTP 422). Recorded events can be read back per contract and narrowed by `event_type`.

**The seasoning test.** The event's `window_months` is the pinned agreement's `chargeback_window_days` divided by 30 — which is why that field is constrained to exact multiples of 30, and why a default of 360 days means a twelve-month clawback exposure. The event's `months_seasoned` is the number of whole days between the contract's `funded_at` and the `event_date`, divided by 30 and rounded down to a whole number. `within_window` is true when `months_seasoned` is **strictly less than** `window_months`, and false otherwise — a contract that seasons to exactly the window length has escaped it.

When `within_window` is false, nothing is clawed back: `participation_chargeback_cents`, `incentive_chargeback_cents`, and `chargeback_total_cents` are all zero, no ledger entry of any kind is created, and the contract's `reserve_status` advances `ACCRUED → EARNED`. The dealership has kept the money.

When `within_window` is true, the unearned fraction is `window_months` less `months_seasoned`, divided by `window_months`, and it is carried as an exact ratio rather than being rounded. That fraction is then applied to each of the contract's two positive accrual entries **separately**, and each product is rounded half up to whole cents **on its own, before the two are added together** — summing first and rounding once produces a different and wrong figure. `participation_chargeback_cents` is the rounded product of the contract's `PARTICIPATION_ACCRUAL` entry's `amount_cents` and the unearned fraction; `incentive_chargeback_cents` is the rounded product of the contract's `INCENTIVE_ACCRUAL` entry's `amount_cents` and the same fraction; and the two are processed in that fixed order. `chargeback_total_cents` is their sum, then capped so that it never exceeds the contract's net posted reserve — the signed sum of `amount_cents` across every ledger entry naming this `contract_id`, taken over every entry type. Only the two accrual entries feed the proration base, but `CHARGEBACK`, `BUYBACK_OFFSET`, `REMEDIATION`, and `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT` entries all count toward that cap. The contract's `reserve_status` advances `ACCRUED → CHARGED_BACK`, and a single ledger entry of `entry_type` `CHARGEBACK` is posted for the negative of `chargeback_total_cents`, with the dealership's `chargeback_count` increasing by one, `reserve_balance_cents` recomputed, and `scorecard_stale` set to true — the dealership's published grade no longer reflects its ledger.

Whichever branch was taken, the event record also carries `earned_to_date_reserve_cents`: the earned-to-date variant of the participation calculation described in §3.5, evaluated at this event's `months_seasoned`. It is written for disclosure only, is never posted, and — for identical contract inputs — is materially smaller than `reserve_accrued_cents` less `participation_chargeback_cents`, because the two figures prorate over different denominators.

**The early-payoff branch.** An `EARLY_PAYOFF` event advances the contract `FUNDED → PAID_OFF`. Its `months_seasoned` and its `payoff_months_seasoned` are both set to the seasoning computed above. When `payoff_months_seasoned` is **strictly less than** twelve months, the dealership's `early_payoff_count` increases by one — a payoff at exactly twelve months of seasoning is not early and does not count. That change forces a recomputation of `early_payoff_rate_bps`, and that recomputation is what can suspend the dealership: when the dealership is `ACTIVE`, its `total_funded_ever_count` is at least 2, and its recomputed `early_payoff_rate_bps` is at or above 3000 — thirty percent of everything it has ever funded having paid off inside the first year — the dealership advances `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to the sentinel `AUTO_EARLY_PAYOFF_RATE`, and every one of its applications in `SUBMITTED`, `CONDITIONED`, `RESUBMITTED`, or `APPROVED` captures its current status into `held_from_status` and moves to `ON_HOLD`. All of this happens inside the same request that recorded the payoff — the chargeback posting, the counter update, the rate recomputation, the suspension, and the application holds are one atomic cascade of four links.

**The default branch.** A `DEFAULT` event advances the contract `FUNDED → DEFAULTED`. Its `months_seasoned` is set to the seasoning computed above, but `payoff_months_seasoned` stays null, because nothing was paid off. The dealership's `default_count` increases by one. A default **never** touches `early_payoff_count` and therefore **never** triggers the probation suspension, no matter how bad the book looks — that supervisory lever belongs to the scorecard of §3.8 instead. Everything else is identical to the payoff branch: the same seasoning test, the same clawback arithmetic, the same `CHARGEBACK` entry when `within_window` is true and the same move to `EARNED` when it is false, and the same `scorecard_stale` flag when a clawback posted.

On either branch the contract's own `months_seasoned` is set to the seasoning computed above, its `unpaid_principal_cents` is recomputed on the straight line of §3.5, the recomputed value is consumed by the dealership roll-up, and only then is it set to zero — so `outstanding_principal_cents` and `funded_contract_count` are recomputed from the correct final position rather than from a balance already blanked. `Dealer.version` advances by one.

### 3.7. Repurchase Demands and Settlement

When a contract turns out to be defective, fraudulent, or simply the wrong paper, the lender demands that the dealership buy it back. A `PortfolioAdmin` issues the demand against a funded contract, naming its `contract_id` and a `reason` of `DOC_DEFECT`, `EARLY_DEFAULT`, `FRAUD`, or `DEALER_TERMINATION`. The named contract must have `funding_status` `FUNDED` and must not already carry another demand in `ISSUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, or `DISPUTED`; either failure is `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FUNDED` (HTTP 409). Issuance copies the contract's `dealer_id` onto the demand and moves the contract's `reserve_status` `ACCRUED → HELD` — the entitlement is frozen, not yet clawed back, and no ledger entry is written. The contract's `funding_status` stays `FUNDED` throughout, which is the clearest demonstration of the two dimensions' independence. Demands can be listed and narrowed by `dealer_id`, by `contract_id`, and by `status`. The five demand statuses are `ISSUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `DISPUTED`, `SETTLED`, and `WITHDRAWN`, of which the last two are terminal.

Issuance freezes a settlement figure that is never recomputed. Elapsed seasoning is taken as the whole days between the contract's `funded_at` and the **current logical instant**, divided by 30 and rounded down, then capped at the contract's `term_months`. The demand's `unpaid_principal_cents` is `amount_financed_cents` less the straight-line repaid principal — `amount_financed_cents` multiplied by those capped months, divided by `term_months`, rounded half up to whole cents. Its `reserve_offset_cents` is the signed sum of `amount_cents` across every ledger entry naming this `contract_id`, over every entry type, evaluated at issuance. Its `admin_fee_cents` is the flat repurchase administration fee of 25,000 cents. And its `buyback_amount_cents` is `unpaid_principal_cents` plus `reserve_offset_cents` plus `admin_fee_cents`. That snapshot is frozen on the demand at issuance and is used verbatim at settlement, however much the ledger or the calendar has moved in between.

The dealership answers the demand. Acknowledgement, by the `DealerPrincipal` whose `X-User-ID` matches the demand's `dealer_id`, is accepted from `ISSUED` or from `DISPUTED` and moves the demand to `ACKNOWLEDGED` with no ledger movement. Dispute, by the same principal, is accepted from `ISSUED` or from `ACKNOWLEDGED`, moves the demand to `DISPUTED`, and records the supplied `dispute_reason` — an optional field, so a dispute lodged without one writes null over whatever stood there — which is otherwise retained on the record even after the dispute is later resolved. The cycle `ISSUED → DISPUTED → ACKNOWLEDGED → DISPUTED` is permitted to run indefinitely until the demand is settled or withdrawn; an action attempted from a status that does not permit it is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Settlement is the `PortfolioAdmin`'s act and is permitted **only** from `ACKNOWLEDGED` — a disputed demand must be acknowledged before it can settle — and only while the contract is still `FUNDED`. The admin supplies a `settlement_date`, which is recorded as the demand's `settled_at`, and the demand moves `ACKNOWLEDGED → SETTLED`. The contract advances `FUNDED → REPURCHASED` on the first dimension and `HELD → CHARGED_BACK` on the second. A single ledger entry of `entry_type` `BUYBACK_OFFSET` is posted for the negative of the demand's frozen `reserve_offset_cents`, clawing back everything the contract ever credited. The contract's `unpaid_principal_cents` is recomputed on the straight line, consumed by the roll-up, and then set to zero; the dealership's `repurchase_count` increases by one; `reserve_balance_cents`, `outstanding_principal_cents`, and `funded_contract_count` are recomputed; and `Dealer.version` advances by one.

Withdrawal is the release valve. A `PortfolioAdmin` may withdraw a demand from `ISSUED`, from `ACKNOWLEDGED`, or from `DISPUTED`, moving it to `WITHDRAWN`. No ledger entry is written; the contract's `reserve_status` simply reverts `HELD → ACCRUED`, restoring the cycle `ACCRUED → HELD → ACCRUED`, and the contract remains `FUNDED` and fully eligible for the ordinary chargeback treatment of §3.6.

### 3.8. Portfolio Quality: Scorecards, Fair Lending, and Remediation

Two supervisory instruments read the same book of contracts and reach different conclusions about it. The scorecard asks whether a dealership's paper performs; the fair-lending review asks whether its markup was distributed evenhandedly.

**The dealer scorecard.** A `PortfolioAdmin` computes one by naming a `period_start`, a `period_end` strictly after it, and a `basis` of either `TRAILING_WINDOW` or `INCEPTION_TO_DATE`. The scorecard is created in `DRAFT` with every metric written immediately; the dealership itself is not mutated by the computation. Its two statuses are `DRAFT` and `FINALIZED`. Scorecards can be listed per dealership and narrowed by `basis` and by `status`.

The population is the dealership's contracts whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED`, `PAID_OFF`, `DEFAULTED`, or `REPURCHASED`; contracts still in `PENDING_DOCS` or `DOCS_VERIFIED` are excluded from every variant, because unfunded paper says nothing about performance. The `basis` then decides how the period bounds are used, and the two variants are genuinely different populations. Under `TRAILING_WINDOW`, the population is further filtered to contracts whose `funded_at` lies at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`; a contract funded before `period_start` is invisible, so an older default cannot depress the score. Under `INCEPTION_TO_DATE`, `period_start` and `period_end` are ignored entirely and every eligible contract the dealership has ever had funded is included. For the same dealership at the same instant the two bases yield a different `population_count`, a different `default_rate_bps`, and in general a different `grade`.

When `population_count` is zero, `default_rate_bps`, `early_payoff_rate_bps`, `doc_defect_rate_bps`, and `avg_markup_bps` are all zero, `composite_score` is null, and `grade` is `UNRATED`. Otherwise three counts are taken over the population: the number of contracts in `DEFAULTED`; the number in `PAID_OFF` whose `payoff_months_seasoned` is strictly less than twelve months; and the number whose `doc_defect_count` is greater than zero. Each becomes a rate in whole basis points — ten thousand multiplied by the count, divided by `population_count`, rounded half up — giving `default_rate_bps`, `early_payoff_rate_bps`, and `doc_defect_rate_bps` respectively. `avg_markup_bps` is the sum of every population contract's `markup_bps` divided by `population_count`, rounded half up.

The composite is a deduction from a perfect thousand, and **each deduction is rounded to a whole point on its own before any subtraction happens**. The default deduction is 6.0 multiplied by `default_rate_bps` divided by 100, rounded half up. The early-payoff deduction is 2.0 multiplied by `early_payoff_rate_bps` divided by 100, rounded half up. The document-defect deduction is 1.5 multiplied by `doc_defect_rate_bps` divided by 100, rounded half up. The markup deduction is 1.0 multiplied by the excess of `avg_markup_bps` over the 150-basis-point markup benchmark — floored at zero, so a dealership pricing at or below the benchmark suffers no deduction — divided by 10, rounded half up. `composite_score` is a thousand less all four deductions, then clamped to lie between 0 and 1000 inclusive. `grade` is `A` when `composite_score` is at least 850, `B` when it is at least 700, `C` when it is at least 550, and `D` otherwise.

Finalization is a separate act, permitted only from `DRAFT` (`ERR_INVALID_STATE` otherwise). The scorecard moves `DRAFT → FINALIZED` and stamps `finalized_at` with the current logical instant. The dealership's `latest_scorecard_grade` is set to this scorecard's `grade` and its `scorecard_stale` is cleared to false — including when the grade is `UNRATED`. And when the `grade` is `D` and the dealership is currently `ACTIVE`, the dealership advances `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to the sentinel `AUTO_SCORECARD_GRADE_D`, and every one of its applications in `SUBMITTED`, `CONDITIONED`, `RESUBMITTED`, or `APPROVED` captures its status into `held_from_status` and moves to `ON_HOLD`. `Dealer.version` advances by one on every finalization.

**The fair-lending review.** A `ComplianceOfficer` runs one by naming the `dealer_id`, a `window_start`, and a `window_end` strictly after it. The population snapshot is taken **once**, at creation: the dealership's contracts whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED`, `PAID_OFF`, `DEFAULTED`, or `REPURCHASED` and whose `funded_at` lies at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`. Contracts in `PENDING_DOCS` or `DOCS_VERIFIED` are excluded, as are contracts funded outside that half-open window. If `population_count` is fewer than 4 contracts, the review is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REVIEW_POPULATION` (HTTP 422) — a disparity computed over three deals is noise. Reviews can be listed and narrowed by `dealer_id` and by `status`; their four statuses are `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED`, `REMEDIATION_ORDERED`, and `CLOSED`.

**The order of evaluation is pinned.** `benchmark_markup_bps` is computed first, over the **whole** population — including the `MONITORED` contracts that may be about to be remediated. It is dollar-weighted: the sum, across every population contract, of that contract's `markup_bps` multiplied by its `amount_financed_cents`, divided by the sum of `amount_financed_cents` across the same population, rounded half up to whole basis points. It is frozen on the review row at creation and is **never** recomputed — not after a remediation entry posts, and not by any later review that re-reads the same contracts. A benchmark recomputed over the post-remediation book, or over the control cohort alone, is a different and wrong number.

The two cohort averages are unweighted. `control_avg_markup_bps` is the sum of `markup_bps` over population contracts whose `monitoring_cohort` is `CONTROL`, divided by the number of such contracts, rounded half up — and zero when there are none. `monitored_avg_markup_bps` is the same figure over contracts whose `monitoring_cohort` is `MONITORED`, likewise zero when there are none. `disparity_bps` is `monitored_avg_markup_bps` less `control_avg_markup_bps`, and it is signed: a monitored cohort priced more cheaply than the control cohort yields a negative disparity. `finding` is `EXCEEDS_THRESHOLD` when `disparity_bps` is **strictly greater than** 25 basis points, and `WITHIN_TOLERANCE` otherwise — a disparity of exactly 25 basis points is within tolerance.

`remediation_pool_cents` is zero when the `finding` is `WITHIN_TOLERANCE`. When the `finding` is `EXCEEDS_THRESHOLD`, it is built contract by contract over the `MONITORED` contracts only, processed in ascending `funded_at` order and tie-broken by ascending `created_at`. Each contract contributes its `amount_financed_cents`, multiplied by the excess of its own `markup_bps` over the frozen `benchmark_markup_bps` expressed as a fraction of ten thousand — floored at zero, so a monitored contract priced at or below the benchmark contributes nothing — multiplied by its `term_months` divided by twelve, multiplied by the participation factor of 0.55. **Each contract's contribution is rounded half up to whole cents on its own, and only then are the contributions summed**, exactly the discipline the chargeback rule of §3.6 uses.

Publication and closure both arrive through the review's single transition endpoint, whose only body field is an `action` naming either `PUBLISH` or `CLOSE`; any other value, or none, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Publication forks on that computed finding, and the two branches are very different. Publication is permitted only from `DRAFT`. When the `finding` is `WITHIN_TOLERANCE`, the review advances `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`, `published_at` is stamped with the current logical instant, no ledger entry is written, and no compliance hold is placed. When the `finding` is `EXCEEDS_THRESHOLD`, the review advances `DRAFT → REMEDIATION_ORDERED` instead — but only if `remediation_pool_cents` is at or below the dealership's current `reserve_balance_cents`; a pool larger than the balance would drive the ledger negative and is refused with `ERR_RESERVE_BALANCE_NEGATIVE` (HTTP 409). The fork is on the finding alone and never on the size of the pool: an adverse finding whose pool computes to zero still takes this branch, still posts a `REMEDIATION` entry — for zero cents — and still raises the hold. On success `published_at` is stamped, a single ledger entry of `entry_type` `REMEDIATION` is posted for the negative of `remediation_pool_cents` with a null `contract_id` — remediation is a dealer-level obligation, not attributable to one deal — the dealership's `compliance_hold` is set to true, `reserve_balance_cents` is recomputed, and `Dealer.version` advances by one.

Closing a review is permitted from `PUBLISHED` and from `REMEDIATION_ORDERED`, and both move the review to `CLOSED` with `closed_at` stamped at the current logical instant. Closing from `PUBLISHED` does nothing else. Closing from `REMEDIATION_ORDERED` additionally clears the dealership's `compliance_hold` back to false — but only when no other fair-lending review of the same dealership remains in `REMEDIATION_ORDERED`, so that two concurrent adverse findings require two closures before origination resumes — and advances `Dealer.version` by one whether or not the hold was actually cleared.

### 3.9. Suspension, Reinstatement, and the Termination Cascade

Dealer lifecycle actions all arrive through a single control surface that takes an `action` and an optional `reason`. Two of the five actions belong to the `ComplianceOfficer` and never touch `status` at all: `COMPLIANCE_HOLD` sets `compliance_hold` to true and `RELEASE_HOLD` clears it to false, with the consequences §2.10 describes. Neither carries a state precondition — either may be applied to a dealership in any of the four statuses, `TERMINATED` included — and both are idempotent, simply writing the flag and advancing `Dealer.version` by one. The other three belong to the `PortfolioAdmin`.

`SUSPEND` is permitted only from `ACTIVE`. The dealership advances `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, `suspension_reason` is set to the supplied `reason` or to the sentinel `MANUAL_SUSPENSION` when none was supplied, every application of the dealership in `SUBMITTED`, `CONDITIONED`, `RESUBMITTED`, or `APPROVED` captures its current status into `held_from_status` and moves to `ON_HOLD`, and `Dealer.version` advances by one. This is exactly the same hold treatment the automatic suspensions of §3.6 and §3.8 apply — a manual suspension, an early-payoff probation breach, and a grade-`D` scorecard all reach the same place by different routes.

`REINSTATE` is permitted only from `SUSPENDED`, and it is gated twice: the dealership's `compliance_hold` must be false, and no fair-lending review of this dealership may remain in `REMEDIATION_ORDERED`. Either gate failing is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, the same code a wrong originating status yields. When both hold, the dealership advances `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, `suspension_reason` is cleared to null, and every application in `ON_HOLD` is restored to whichever of `SUBMITTED`, `CONDITIONED`, `RESUBMITTED`, or `APPROVED` its `held_from_status` recorded, with `held_from_status` then cleared to null. `Dealer.version` advances by one. The cycle `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` may run any number of times.

`TERMINATE` is permitted from `ACTIVE` and from `SUSPENDED`, and both origins run an identical five-link cascade. **Link one:** the dealership advances to `TERMINATED`, which is terminal and from which nothing returns, and `suspension_reason` is set to the supplied `reason` or to the sentinel `TERMINATION`. **Link two, caused by link one:** every agreement of this dealership whose `status` is `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN` or `ACTIVE` transitions to `TERMINATED`; no ledger movement accompanies this. **Link three, caused by link two:** every application pinned to an agreement that link two just terminated — together with every application of this dealership whose `agreement_id` is still null — whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `CONDITIONED`, `RESUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, or `ON_HOLD` transitions to `WITHDRAWN`, with `held_from_status` cleared to null. **Link four, also caused by link two:** for every contract of this dealership whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED`, whose seasoning — whole days from `funded_at` to the current logical instant, divided by 30 and rounded down — is strictly less than the window implied by its pinned agreement's `chargeback_window_days`, and which does not already carry a demand in `ISSUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, or `DISPUTED`, a repurchase demand is issued with `reason` `DEALER_TERMINATION`, and those demands are issued in ascending contract `funded_at` order, tie-broken by ascending contract `created_at`, each taking the same frozen settlement snapshot §3.7 describes. **Link five, caused by link four:** each demand so issued moves its contract's `reserve_status` `ACCRUED → HELD`. `Dealer.version` advances by one for the whole cascade.
