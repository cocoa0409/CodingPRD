# Creator Monetization Payouts API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Creators who earn on our platform are paid from four independent revenue surfaces — advertising, subscriptions, tips and commerce — each carrying its own commercial terms, and each subject to a different set of policy, tax and solvency constraints before a single cent may leave the platform. Today those constraints live in spreadsheets and in the heads of the payments team: whether a channel has crossed the monetization thresholds, whether its tax documentation is still valid, whether a demonetization decision on one video should retroactively unwind revenue that was already accrued, and whether an open trust-and-safety investigation should freeze a disbursement that is already in flight. The consequence is that creators cannot predict their own earnings, and the finance team cannot defend a number to an auditor.

This document specifies the Creator Monetization Payouts service, mounted under `/monetization`, which turns those judgements into an auditable machine. The service holds the creator's monetization eligibility and re-evaluates it periodically, computes a per-event revenue share against per-product terms and content-level advertiser suitability, collects tax documentation and derives a withholding rate from country and treaty rules, aggregates each attribution window into a statement, and disburses money on a threshold, schedule and rail chosen by the creator. It also holds the unpleasant half of the same problem: demonetization events with appeals, revenue holds during policy investigations, and retroactive clawback of revenue earned inside a substantiated violation window.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **every movement of money is a ledger entry** — the service never stores a balance it cannot rebuild by replaying the ledger, and every cascade that touches a creator's money is expected to leave a matching, signed, append-only trail. Second, **policy authority and money authority are separated**: the reviewer who opens an investigation may not resolve it, the reviewer who demonetized a video may not decide its appeal, and no reviewer may create or settle a payout at all. Both commitments exist so that when a creator disputes a statement, an auditor can reconstruct exactly which decision produced which cent.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set that principal acts under. If either header is absent or blank, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is inspected. If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the five recognized roles — `Creator`, `PolicyReviewer`, `FinanceOperator`, `PlatformAdmin`, `Auditor` — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). When the role is recognized but is not among the roles authorized for the addressed operation (see the authorization column of the endpoint table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles differ not only in what they may do but in what `X-User-ID` *means*:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Creator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting creator record; ownership checks on any resource that references a creator compare the resource's creator reference to `X-User-ID`. If `X-User-ID` does not match the `id` of an existing creator record, the request is rejected with `ERR_CREATOR_IDENTITY_UNKNOWN` (HTTP 401) — this check runs immediately after role recognition and before any authorization decision. A creator may act only inside its own subtree: its own creator record, its tax profiles, content items, revenue events, appeals, earnings statements, payouts, investigations, fund allocations and ledger entries. On any single-resource address, if the addressed record's creator reference (or, for the creator record itself, its `id`) is not equal to `X-User-ID`, the request fails with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403); the same error is returned when a creator supplies a `creator_id` in a request body that is not its own `X-User-ID`. List reads behave differently and never produce this error: they are silently narrowed to the rows the acting creator owns, so a creator listing creators receives exactly its own single row. Two collections are global configuration and are **not** narrowed for a creator: the revenue-product catalogue and the earnings-period calendar. A creator may submit and revise its own tax form, open an appeal, respond to an information request, and dispute a statement, but it may never rate content, decide an appeal, close a period, finalize a statement, or settle a payout.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PolicyReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted record. The server records it — as `opened_by_user_id` on an investigation the reviewer opens, as `last_rated_by_user_id` on a content item the reviewer rates, as `resolved_by_user_id` on an investigation the reviewer resolves, and as `decided_by_user_id` on an appeal the reviewer decides — but never compares it to any record's `id`. A reviewer's reach is unscoped across creators: it sees every creator's content, appeals and investigations. It is constrained instead by two per-record segregation-of-duties comparisons, both of which reject with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), described in §2.9 and §3.4/§3.8. A reviewer has no money authority whatsoever: it cannot create, submit, settle or retry a payout, cannot close a period, and cannot finalize or adjust a statement — even though its own decisions are what cause clawbacks and holds. A reviewer is likewise not permitted to read earnings statements, payouts, ledger entries or creator funds: policy judgement is deliberately blind to the money it moves.
- When `X-User-Role` is `FinanceOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted record; the server may record it in audit context but never compares it to any record's `id`. This is the only role that moves money: it verifies tax documentation, ingests revenue, closes a period, finalizes a statement, resolves a dispute, creates, submits, settles and retries payouts, and allocates, voids or disburses a creator fund. There is no segregation-of-duties rule constraining a finance operator, so the same operator may legitimately close a period, finalize its statements and settle the resulting payout. A finance operator may not rate content, may not open or resolve an investigation, and may not change platform configuration.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted record and is never compared to any record's `id`. Administrators own configuration and the data plane: the product catalogue, the period calendar, fund declarations, creator registration, metrics ingestion and the logical clock. `PlatformAdmin` is deliberately **not** a superset role — it holds no money-moving and no policy-judgement operation, so a scenario that requires a period closed and a payout settled requires a `FinanceOperator` regardless of administrative rights.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Auditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted record and is never compared to any record's `id`. An auditor may read every collection in the domain, including the creator ledger and the fund allocation rows, and may mutate nothing. Every write attempted by an auditor is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403) at the authorization stage — that is, before existence checks, before state checks, and before any version check — so an auditor probing a resource that does not exist still receives 403 rather than 404.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and has a minimum of 0 with no upper bound. When a supplied `limit` is not an integer between 1 and 100 inclusive, or a supplied `offset` is not an integer of at least 0, the read is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific collection declares a different ordering in §3, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Four collections carry a domain-specific ordering that clients and auditors depend on: a creator's ledger entries are returned by `occurred_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; revenue events are returned by `occurred_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; earnings statements are returned by their period's `period_start` ascending and then `created_at` ascending — the same order the clawback waterfall and payout attachment use; and the allocation rows of a fund are returned by `rank` ascending.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the current window. Single-resource reads carry no pagination fields.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, Versions and the Logical Clock

Every resource is identified by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix, and no rule anywhere in this specification may use an `id` as a decision input: every ordering, ranking and tie-break in the service resolves on `created_at` ascending or on a named business field, never on identifier order.

Every resource records `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, fixed at creation. Every resource except the ledger entry additionally records `updated_at`, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Ledger entries are the one exception: because the ledger is append-only and a posted entry is never edited, a ledger entry carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all.

Time in this service is *logical*, not wall-clock. A single server-wide logical clock is the sole source of every timestamp field the business rules read — tax-form expiry, strike lapse, the period-close pipeline, settlement times and every `*_at` field written by a transition. Its startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and before the clock has ever been moved the service reads that value; it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. An administrator-only test-driving capability advances the clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant. The clock is monotonic: a supplied instant earlier than the current logical time is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422), while an instant exactly equal to the current logical time is accepted as a no-op. Moving the clock mutates no entity row.

### 2.5. Money, Rates and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this domain are signed integer **cents**; no floating-point amount is ever persisted. All rates are integer **basis points**, where 10000 basis points is 100 percent. When a rate is applied to money, the exact rational product is computed first and only the final amount is rounded — to a whole cent, half-up — unless a rule below explicitly names a different rounding direction. Two rules deliberately round differently and the difference is observable: the disbursement fee computation always rounds its percentage and tier components *up* to the next whole cent, and the effective revenue share truncates its basis-point product *down* to a whole basis point before it is ever applied to money. Basis-point-on-basis-point arithmetic is floored to an integer basis-point value; money produced from an exact rational is rounded half-up.

### 2.6. The Creator Ledger and the Four Derived Balances

Every cent that moves for a creator is recorded as an append-only ledger entry. An entry carries the `creator_id` whose balance it moves, an `entry_type` naming the economic reason, a signed `amount_cents` (credits positive, debits negative), a `balance_after_cents` snapshot of that creator's ledger balance immediately after the entry was appended, a polymorphic pair of `source_entity` and `source_id` naming the row that caused the movement, an `occurred_at` equal to the logical time of the append, and a fixed machine-readable `memo` string describing the causing transition. An entry whose `amount_cents` would be zero is never written at all.

`entry_type` takes exactly one of eleven values: `ACCRUAL`, `ACCRUAL_REVERSAL`, `ACCRUAL_RESTORE`, `WITHHOLDING`, `WITHHOLDING_TRUE_UP`, `FUND_CREDIT`, `PAYOUT_DEBIT`, `PAYOUT_FEE`, `PAYOUT_REVERSAL`, `CLAWBACK` and `DISPUTE_ADJUSTMENT`. `source_entity` takes exactly one of five values — `REVENUE_EVENT`, `EARNINGS_STATEMENT`, `PAYOUT`, `INVESTIGATION` or `FUND_ALLOCATION` — and `source_id` holds the identifier of the row of that type; because the target type varies, `source_id` is not a declared foreign key.

A creator's money is reported through four derived quantities, recomputed from source rows on every read of a creator and on every write that needs a balance, and never trusted from a cache:

- `ledger_balance_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over **all** of that creator's ledger entries — every entry type, every point in time, signed, with no filter of any kind.
- `held_cents` is the sum of `hold_amount_cents` over that creator's investigations that are in `OPEN` state and were opened with holds enabled.
- `statement_payable_cents` is the sum, over that creator's earnings statements whose status is `FINALIZED` or `ADJUSTED` and which are not attached to any payout, of the statement's `net_payable_cents` minus its `clawback_applied_cents` plus its `dispute_adjustment_cents`. Statements that are `PROVISIONAL`, `UNDER_DISPUTE` or `PAID` contribute nothing, and neither does a statement already attached to a payout — that money has already left the ledger as a payout debit.
- `available_balance_cents` is `statement_payable_cents` plus the creator's `fund_credit_balance_cents` minus `held_cents`. It may legitimately be zero or negative; the rules that guard payout creation (§3.7) reject non-positive values.

Four conservation invariants hold across the whole domain and are the auditor's primary reconciliation tool. **(I1) A payout round trip conserves the ledger.** For any payout, its `PAYOUT_DEBIT` entry of minus `amount_cents`, its `PAYOUT_FEE` entry of minus `fee_cents`, and its `PAYOUT_REVERSAL` entry of plus the sum of `amount_cents` and `fee_cents` add to exactly zero, so a creator's `ledger_balance_cents` after a payout is voided, fails or is abandoned equals its value immediately before that payout was created, and `fund_credit_balance_cents` is restored to its pre-payout value by the fund-credit restore. A retry re-debits the identical two amounts, so any number of failures followed by the same number of reversals also nets to zero. **(I2) A fund pool is conserved.** A fund's `allocated_cents` plus its `unallocated_remainder_cents` always equals its `total_pool_cents`, after a first allocation run, after a superseding run, and after allocations are voided; and at disbursement the sum of the appended `FUND_CREDIT` entry amounts equals `allocated_cents`. **(I3) An accrual round trip is reversible.** For any revenue event, the sum of its `ACCRUAL`, `ACCRUAL_REVERSAL` and `ACCRUAL_RESTORE` entries equals its current `creator_share_cents`, so a re-rating from `GREEN` to `RED` followed by an upheld appeal restoring `GREEN` returns the creator's `ledger_balance_cents` to its pre-rating value, provided the creator's lifetime-paid tier bucket did not change in between. **(I4) A rejected dispute is a no-op.** A statement that cycles `FINALIZED` → `UNDER_DISPUTE` → `FINALIZED` with the dispute rejected appends no ledger entry and leaves `dispute_adjustment_cents` at its prior value, so both `ledger_balance_cents` and `statement_payable_cents` return to their exact pre-dispute values.

### 2.7. Two Independent State Dimensions on a Creator

A creator row carries two enumerated state dimensions that evolve on the same row without gating each other. The **monetization dimension**, `eligibility_status`, is born `PENDING_REVIEW` and takes one of `PENDING_REVIEW`, `ELIGIBLE`, `INELIGIBLE` or `SUSPENDED`; it gates revenue *accrual* only, and is written exclusively by the eligibility evaluation described in §3.1. The **disbursement dimension**, `payout_state`, is born `ACCRUING` and takes one of `ACCRUING`, `HOLD`, `PAYOUT_IN_FLIGHT` or `ARREARS`; it gates *disbursement* only. Neither dimension is a precondition of the other: a `SUSPENDED` creator may still be paid out, and a creator in `ARREARS` may still be re-evaluated to `ELIGIBLE`. The eligibility evaluation neither reads nor writes `payout_state`, and the disbursement resolution neither reads nor writes `eligibility_status`.

`payout_state` is not a stored decision but a pure function of stored state, recomputed at the end of every request that touches the creator's `outstanding_clawback_cents`, any of that creator's investigations' state or hold flag, or any of that creator's payouts' state. The resolution walks four clauses in strict precedence and the **first matching clause wins**: if `outstanding_clawback_cents` is greater than zero the state is `ARREARS`; otherwise, if the creator has at least one investigation in `OPEN` state that was opened with holds enabled the state is `HOLD`; otherwise, if the creator has at least one payout in `PENDING` or `PROCESSING` state the state is `PAYOUT_IN_FLIGHT`; otherwise the state is `ACCRUING`. Because the rule never reads the previous value of `payout_state`, it is idempotent.

That precedence has consequences the implementation must reproduce exactly. `ARREARS` masks an open holding investigation, so clearing that investigation while arrears remain leaves the creator in `ARREARS`. `HOLD` masks an in-flight payout, so opening a holding investigation on a creator who already has a `PENDING` payout flips that creator from `PAYOUT_IN_FLIGHT` to `HOLD`, and handing that payout to the processing rail is then refused. Payouts in `PAID`, `FAILED`, `VOIDED` or `ABANDONED` state are ignored by the resolution entirely, which is why a failed payout awaiting retry leaves its creator `ACCRUING` and re-payable.

### 2.8. Stale-View Rejection on Tax-Document and Fund Writes

Two writes in this service cannot be protected by a state precondition, because a second sequential attempt is legitimate product behaviour in both cases. Both are therefore guarded by an explicit stale-view check against the parent record's `version`.

The first is **tax profile creation**. A newly collected profile legitimately supersedes the creator's prior profile, so a second create is valid and no state check can distinguish a caller working from an out-of-date picture of the creator. The create request therefore requires a non-optional `expected_creator_version` field carrying the `version` of the target creator as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the creator's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CREATOR_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — the caller's view of the creator, including which profile is currently in force, is out of date.

The second is **fund allocation**. Allocation is idempotent in shape but not in content: a fund that is already `ALLOCATED` accepts a superseding run that replaces its entire allocation set, so a second sequential allocation would itself succeed under the product rules. Both the allocation run and the void-allocations operation therefore require a non-optional `expected_fund_version` field carrying the `version` of the target fund as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the fund's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FUND_VERSION` (HTTP 412); this is the only signal that the caller computed its intent from a candidate pool or pool size that no longer holds.

No other operation in this service accepts a version field. Endpoints not named above take no `expected_creator_version`, no `expected_fund_version`, and no other version input.

### 2.9. The Order in Which a Request Is Rejected

When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned and no side effect fires. The rules form a **total order**, and the earliest violated rule in that order produces the response. The order is:

1. **Authentication and authorization, in four steps.** Both auth headers present and non-blank (`ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`, 401); then `X-User-Role` recognized (`ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`, 401); then, when the role is `Creator`, `X-User-ID` resolving to an existing creator record (`ERR_CREATOR_IDENTITY_UNKNOWN`, 401); then the role appearing among the roles authorized for the addressed operation (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, 403).
2. **Request shape, in five steps.** Pagination bounds on list reads (`ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`, 400); then every required input field present and non-null (`ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD`, 422, on every write); then declared JSON types, enum membership and ISO 8601 UTC datetime parsing (`ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`, 422); then numeric range constraints (`ERR_FIELD_OUT_OF_RANGE`, 422) — `gross_amount_cents` strictly greater than 0, `total_pool_cents` strictly greater than 0, `payout_threshold_cents` at least 1000, `follower_count` at least 0, `watch_time_minutes_365d` at least 0, `creator_share_bps` from 0 to 9000 inclusive, `platform_fee_bps` from 0 to 10000 inclusive, `treaty_rate_bps` from 0 to 3000 inclusive, `adjustment_cents` at least 0, `max_allocation_cents` strictly greater than 0, `min_watch_time_minutes` at least 0; then free-text non-blankness and length bounds (`ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`, 422) — `display_name` 1 to 120 characters, `title` 1 to 200, `statement_text` 1 to 2000, `scope` 1 to 500, `label` 1 to 60, `name` 1 to 120, and each of `finding`, `reason`, `rationale`, `info_request`, `response_statement` and `dispute_reason` 1 to 2000.
3. **Existence of addressed rows, then of body references.** Every identifier in the URL path must resolve to an existing row, checked left to right, yielding the 404 that names the missing type: `ERR_CREATOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TAX_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTENT_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYOUT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_FUND_NOT_FOUND`. Only then are references supplied in a request body resolved, in the order the endpoint lists them — `creator_id` first, then `product_id`, then `content_item_id`, then `period_id` — yielding `ERR_CREATOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTENT_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND` (all 404). Body-reference resolution applies to tax-profile creation, content-item publication, revenue-event ingestion, appeal creation, payout creation, investigation opening and fund declaration.
4. **Creator ownership scoping** (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, 403), on every operation whose authorized roles include `Creator`, exactly as described in §2.1.
5. **State preconditions**, including cross-record state preconditions. Within this band the order is: tax profile must be `DRAFT` to submit (`ERR_TAX_PROFILE_NOT_DRAFT`, 409); must be `SUBMITTED` to receive a verification decision (`ERR_TAX_PROFILE_NOT_SUBMITTED`, 409); must be `REJECTED` to be revised (`ERR_TAX_PROFILE_NOT_REJECTED`, 409); a product must be `ACTIVE` to be retired and `ACTIVE` for its terms to be amended (`ERR_PRODUCT_ALREADY_RETIRED`, 409, for both); a rating change must actually change the rating (`ERR_RATING_UNCHANGED`, 409); an appeal may only be opened against a content item whose monetization state is `LIMITED` or `DEMONETIZED` (`ERR_CONTENT_NOT_APPEALABLE`, 409) and only when that item has no appeal already in flight (`ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_OPEN`, 409); an appeal must be `SUBMITTED` to enter review (`ERR_APPEAL_NOT_SUBMITTED`, 409), `UNDER_REVIEW` to receive an information request or a decision (`ERR_APPEAL_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, 409), and `INFO_REQUESTED` to receive a creator response (`ERR_APPEAL_NOT_INFO_REQUESTED`, 409) with rounds remaining (`ERR_APPEAL_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, 409); a period must be `OPEN` to close (`ERR_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`, 409); a statement must be `PROVISIONAL` to finalize (`ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_PROVISIONAL`, 409) and its period must be `CLOSED` (`ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`, 409); a statement must be `FINALIZED` or `ADJUSTED` to be disputed (`ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_DISPUTABLE`, 409) and must not be attached to a payout (`ERR_STATEMENT_ALREADY_ATTACHED`, 409); a statement must be `UNDER_DISPUTE` for its dispute to be resolved (`ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_UNDER_DISPUTE`, 409); an investigation must be `OPEN` to be substantiated or cleared (`ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_OPEN`, 409); a payout must be `PENDING` to be handed to the rail (`ERR_PAYOUT_NOT_PENDING`, 409) and its creator must not be in `HOLD` (`ERR_PAYOUT_HELD`, 409); a payout must be `PROCESSING` for a settlement to be recorded (`ERR_PAYOUT_NOT_PROCESSING`, 409); a payout must be `FAILED` to be retried (`ERR_PAYOUT_NOT_FAILED`, 409) with retries remaining (`ERR_PAYOUT_RETRIES_EXHAUSTED`, 409); a payout may only be created for a creator whose `payout_state` is `ACCRUING` (`ERR_CREATOR_NOT_PAYABLE`, 409); a revenue event may only be ingested for a creator whose `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE` (`ERR_CREATOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, 409) against a product that is `ACTIVE` (`ERR_PRODUCT_RETIRED`, 409); a fund must be `DRAFT` or `ALLOCATED` to run an allocation (`ERR_FUND_NOT_ALLOCATABLE`, 409); and a fund must be `ALLOCATED` to have its allocations voided or to be disbursed (`ERR_FUND_NOT_ALLOCATED`, 409).
6. **Stale-view version checks**, deliberately *after* every state precondition above: `ERR_STALE_FUND_VERSION` (412) on the allocation and void-allocations operations, then `ERR_STALE_CREATOR_VERSION` (412) on tax profile creation. Because these run after the state band, a stale version supplied against a wrongly-stated fund reports the state error rather than the version error.
7. **Safety and compliance.** Segregation of duties on investigation resolution and on appeal decisions (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403, in that order); then reserved rating provenance (`ERR_RATING_SOURCE_RESERVED`, 422); then verified tax documentation before disbursement (`ERR_TAX_DOCUMENTATION_MISSING`, 422); then the bar on accruing revenue inside a window under active investigation (`ERR_ACCRUAL_UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, 409).
8. **Cross-field and cross-record consistency**, in this order: `ERR_CONTENT_CREATOR_MISMATCH`, `ERR_CONTENT_LINK_REQUIRED`, `ERR_CONTENT_LINK_FORBIDDEN`, `ERR_TAX_COUNTRY_MISMATCH`, `ERR_TAX_FORM_COUNTRY_INVALID`, `ERR_TREATY_RATE_REQUIRED`, `ERR_TREATY_RATE_FORBIDDEN`, `ERR_APPEAL_RATING_NOT_IMPROVEMENT`, `ERR_REJECTION_REASON_REQUIRED`, `ERR_REJECTION_REASON_FORBIDDEN`, `ERR_FAILURE_CODE_REQUIRED`, `ERR_FAILURE_CODE_FORBIDDEN`, `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_OUT_OF_BOUNDS` (all 422).
9. **Uniqueness and closure constraints**, in this order: `ERR_SHARE_SPLIT_NOT_WHOLE` (422), `ERR_FUND_PERIOD_FINALIZED` (409), `ERR_FUND_ALREADY_EXISTS_FOR_PERIOD` (409), `ERR_FUND_CAP_BELOW_FLOOR` (422).
10. **Interval validity and overlap**, in this order: `ERR_PERIOD_WINDOW_INVALID` (422), `ERR_PERIOD_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409), `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSE_OUT_OF_ORDER` (409), `ERR_INVESTIGATION_WINDOW_INVALID` (422), `ERR_INVESTIGATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409), `ERR_NO_OPEN_PERIOD_FOR_EVENT` (422), `ERR_PUBLISHED_IN_FUTURE` (422), `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (422).
11. **Balance, threshold, pool and capacity checks**, last of all, in this order: `ERR_NO_PAYABLE_BALANCE` (422), `ERR_BELOW_PAYOUT_THRESHOLD` (422), `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE_FOR_RETRY` (422), `ERR_DISPUTE_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_STATEMENT` (422), `ERR_FUND_POOL_EMPTY` (422), `ERR_FUND_NO_RECIPIENTS` (422).

Five conflicts are pinned by that order and are each reachable by a single request violating both rules at once. A payout creation for a creator whose `payout_state` is `ARREARS` and whose `available_balance_cents` is zero returns `ERR_CREATOR_NOT_PAYABLE`, not `ERR_NO_PAYABLE_BALANCE`, because the state band precedes the fiscal band. A substantiation sent by the opening reviewer against an investigation that is already `SUBSTANTIATED` returns `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_OPEN`, not `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A payout creation for a creator with no `VERIFIED` tax profile whose `available_balance_cents` is below `payout_threshold_cents` returns `ERR_TAX_DOCUMENTATION_MISSING`, not `ERR_BELOW_PAYOUT_THRESHOLD`. A revenue event whose `content_item_id` belongs to a different creator *and* whose `occurred_at` falls outside every open period returns `ERR_CONTENT_CREATOR_MISMATCH`, not `ERR_NO_OPEN_PERIOD_FOR_EVENT`. And an allocation run against a `DISBURSED` fund carrying a stale `expected_fund_version` returns `ERR_FUND_NOT_ALLOCATABLE` (409), not `ERR_STALE_FUND_VERSION` (412).

### 2.10. Address Resolution, Cascades and Derived-Field Reconciliation

Every collection in this service is a peer collection directly under the service mount; parent-child links are carried in request bodies, never in nested URL segments. Only three reads are addressed as sub-resources of a parent — a creator's ledger entries, a creator's fund allocations, and a fund's allocation rows — because those children are append-only or derived and have no standalone collection of their own. Whenever an address contains more than one identifier, the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, so a malformed parent reference is reported before anything is checked about the child.

Cascades are pervasive in this domain, and the specification states them as *hops* precisely because an implementation that fires only the first hop will look correct on the addressed row and be wrong everywhere else. Whenever a hop mutates a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented by one — including rows the caller never addressed, such as a creator whose balance changed because a statement it owns absorbed a clawback. Two compound states are derived from children rather than written directly: a period becomes `FINALIZED` when the condition over its child statements holds, and a content item's `monetization_state` is derived strictly from its `suitability_rating`.

One read is also a reconciliation point. The creator earnings-summary read recomputes both the four derived balances and the disbursement-state resolution; if the recomputed `payout_state` differs from the stored value, the stored value is corrected in place and the creator's `version` is incremented by one. Every other read is side-effect-free: derived money fields are recomputed for presentation but nothing is written.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering a Channel and Deciding Whether It May Earn

A `PlatformAdmin` registers a channel by supplying its public `display_name` (1 to 120 characters, non-blank), its tax residence `country_code` — one of `US`, `GB`, `DE`, `IN`, `BR` or `JP` — and the two ingestion metrics that drive the monetization decision: `follower_count` and `watch_time_minutes_365d`, the trailing-365-day watch minutes, both of which must be at least zero. Two disbursement preferences may be supplied at registration or left to their defaults: `payout_threshold_cents`, the minimum balance the channel wants to accumulate before money is disbursed, defaulting to 10000 cents and never permitted below 1000 cents; and `payout_method`, the disbursement rail, defaulting to `BANK_TRANSFER` and otherwise one of `INSTANT_DEBIT` or `CHECK`. A newly registered channel is born with `version` 1, `eligibility_status` `PENDING_REVIEW`, `payout_state` `ACCRUING`, and every system counter at its default: `active_strike_count` 0, `last_strike_at` null, `consecutive_payment_failures` 0, `fund_credit_balance_cents` 0, `outstanding_clawback_cents` 0, `lifetime_paid_cents` 0, `evaluation_count` 0, `last_evaluated_at` null and `last_evaluation_failed_criteria` an empty array. No evaluation runs at registration, which is why a brand-new channel can neither accrue revenue nor be paid.

Metrics arrive later, by administrative ingestion, overwriting `follower_count` and/or `watch_time_minutes_365d` and incrementing the creator's `version`. Ingestion deliberately does **not** re-evaluate eligibility: `eligibility_status`, `last_evaluation_failed_criteria` and `evaluation_count` are all left untouched, so a channel whose followers just crossed the threshold stays `INELIGIBLE` until a finance operator asks for an explicit evaluation or the next period close sweeps it up.

**The eligibility evaluation** is a three-criterion threshold test, and its most important property is that the policy criterion outranks both metric criteria and short-circuits them. If the channel's `active_strike_count` is at least 1, the evaluation stops immediately: `eligibility_status` becomes `SUSPENDED` and `last_evaluation_failed_criteria` is set to an array containing exactly the single element `POLICY_COMPLIANCE` — the metric criteria are *not* appended in this branch even when they also fail. Otherwise the two metric criteria are both evaluated, and neither short-circuits the other: the code `FOLLOWER_MINIMUM` is appended when `follower_count` is strictly below 10000 followers, and then the code `WATCH_TIME_MINIMUM` is appended when `watch_time_minutes_365d` is strictly below 240000 watch minutes — that is, below four thousand hours of trailing-year watch time. When both metric criteria pass, `eligibility_status` becomes `ELIGIBLE` and `last_evaluation_failed_criteria` is the empty array; when either fails, `eligibility_status` becomes `INELIGIBLE` and `last_evaluation_failed_criteria` carries the failing codes in the fixed emission order `FOLLOWER_MINIMUM`, `WATCH_TIME_MINIMUM`, `POLICY_COMPLIANCE`. Both thresholds are inclusive at the boundary: a `follower_count` of exactly 10000 passes and 9999 fails, and a `watch_time_minutes_365d` of exactly 240000 passes.

Whichever branch runs, the evaluation closes by incrementing `evaluation_count` by one and setting `last_evaluated_at` to the current logical time. Both counters advance on **every** evaluation, including one that leaves `eligibility_status` exactly where it was: an `ELIGIBLE` channel re-evaluated to `ELIGIBLE` still increments `evaluation_count`. The evaluation reads exactly one creator row and consults no child record, and it never touches `payout_state`.

An on-demand evaluation, requested by a `FinanceOperator` and moving the channel from `PENDING_REVIEW` to `ELIGIBLE`, from any of `PENDING_REVIEW`, `ELIGIBLE` or `SUSPENDED` to `INELIGIBLE`, or from any of `PENDING_REVIEW`, `ELIGIBLE` or `INELIGIBLE` to `SUSPENDED`, reads `active_strike_count` exactly as stored: no strike lapse is applied first, so a channel whose only strike is older than the ninety-day strike window still evaluates to `SUSPENDED` on demand. The lapse is applied only inside the period-close pipeline (§3.5), which is why identical stored channel state can yield `SUSPENDED` on demand and `ELIGIBLE` or `INELIGIBLE` at close. That same close pipeline is the only route from `SUSPENDED` back to `ELIGIBLE`: it requires that the channel's `last_strike_at` plus ninety days be at or before the close instant, so that all strikes lapse together and `active_strike_count` becomes 0 before the re-evaluation runs, and that `follower_count` be at least 10000 while `watch_time_minutes_365d` is at least 240000.

**Policy strikes** are recorded by a `PolicyReviewer` against a channel with a supplied `reason` (1 to 2000 characters, non-blank), and the operation is a deliberate two-hop cascade. The first hop increments `active_strike_count` by one and sets `last_strike_at` to the current logical time. The second hop follows automatically because `active_strike_count` is now at least 1: the eligibility evaluation re-runs, writes `eligibility_status` `SUSPENDED` and `last_evaluation_failed_criteria` containing only `POLICY_COMPLIANCE`, and increments `evaluation_count`. The creator's `version` increments by one and `payout_state` is untouched — a suspended channel is still perfectly payable for money it has already earned.

Disbursement preferences may be changed later by the channel itself or by a `FinanceOperator`, supplying a new `payout_method` and/or a new `payout_threshold_cents`. When the supplied `payout_method` differs from the stored value, `consecutive_payment_failures` is reset to 0 — a new rail deserves a fresh run of attempts. Existing payout rows are unaffected: each keeps the rail and fee it snapshotted at creation, and nothing is recomputed.

### 3.2. Collecting Tax Documentation and Resolving a Withholding Rate

A tax profile is collected either by the channel itself or by a `FinanceOperator`, addressed to a `creator_id` and carrying a `form_type` of `W9` or `W8BEN`, the `country_code` declared on the form, a `tin_provided` boolean stating whether a taxpayer identification number was supplied (default false), a `treaty_claimed` boolean stating whether a reduced treaty rate is claimed (default false), an optional `treaty_rate_bps` carrying the claimed rate in basis points, and the `expected_creator_version` described in §2.8. The declared `country_code` must equal the channel's own `country_code`, or the collection is rejected with `ERR_TAX_COUNTRY_MISMATCH`. Form type and country must agree: `W9` requires a `country_code` of `US`, and `W8BEN` requires a `country_code` other than `US`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_TAX_FORM_COUNTRY_INVALID`, and the same rule is enforced again on any revision that changes `form_type`. A `treaty_rate_bps` is mandatory whenever `treaty_claimed` is true — on collection, on revision, and again as a precondition of submission — and its absence is rejected with `ERR_TREATY_RATE_REQUIRED`; conversely it must be null whenever `treaty_claimed` is false, and supplying it anyway is rejected with `ERR_TREATY_RATE_FORBIDDEN`. When present it must lie between 0 and 3000 basis points inclusive.

Collecting a profile has a supersession side effect that is easy to miss: **every** existing tax profile of that channel whose `status` is not already `SUPERSEDED` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, with its `withholding_rate_bps` frozen at whatever value it then carried, and it is excluded from rate selection forever afterwards. The new profile is created with `status` `DRAFT`, its `submitted_at`, `verified_at`, `expires_at` and `rejection_reason` all null, and its `withholding_rate_bps` resolved to the backup rate. The creator's `version` increments by one.

A profile's lifecycle runs `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED` → either `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED`, with `REJECTED` → `DRAFT` available for correction, `SUPERSEDED` reachable from any of `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED`, and `EXPIRED` reachable only from `VERIFIED`; `SUPERSEDED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal. The channel submits its own form, which sets `submitted_at` to the current logical time, re-resolves `withholding_rate_bps` — still the backup rate, because only a `VERIFIED` profile earns a statutory or treaty rate — and increments the profile's `version`. A `FinanceOperator` then supplies a `decision` of either `VERIFY` or `REJECT`. On `VERIFY`, `status` becomes `VERIFIED`, `verified_at` is set to the current logical time, `expires_at` is set to `verified_at` plus 1095 days (three years of validity), and `withholding_rate_bps` is re-resolved in full. On `REJECT`, `status` becomes `REJECTED`, the supplied `rejection_reason` is recorded, and `withholding_rate_bps` is set to the backup rate. A `REJECT` decision requires a non-blank `rejection_reason` (`ERR_REJECTION_REASON_REQUIRED`), and a `VERIFY` decision requires `rejection_reason` to be absent or null (`ERR_REJECTION_REASON_FORBIDDEN`). Either way the profile's `version` increments, and no earnings statement already created is recomputed. A rejected profile is corrected by the channel, which may overwrite `form_type`, `tin_provided`, `treaty_claimed` and `treaty_rate_bps`, clears `rejection_reason` to null, returns `status` to `DRAFT`, re-resolves `withholding_rate_bps` to the backup rate and increments the profile's `version`. Correction rewinds the status but not the audit trail: the profile's `submitted_at` is **not** cleared and keeps whatever instant the earlier submission stamped on it, so a corrected draft still reports a non-null `submitted_at` — only a freshly collected profile is born with `submitted_at` null.

**Resolving the effective withholding rate** happens in two stages. First the *current* profile is selected — the only place in the service where a record is chosen by description rather than by identifier. The candidate set is every profile of that channel whose `status` is anything other than `SUPERSEDED`, that is, `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `VERIFIED`, `REJECTED` or `EXPIRED`, ranked by `created_at` descending. No tie-break is required, because collecting a profile supersedes every prior non-superseded profile in the same request, so the candidate set holds at most one row by construction. If the candidate set is empty — the channel has no profile at all — the rate is the backup withholding rate of 2400 basis points, that is, 24 percent.

Second, the selected profile's rate is derived. If its `status` is anything other than `VERIFIED`, the rate is the backup rate of 2400 basis points. If its `status` is `VERIFIED` and its `form_type` is `W9`, meaning a US person, the rate is 0 basis points when `tin_provided` is true and the backup rate of 2400 basis points when it is false. If its `status` is `VERIFIED` and its `form_type` is `W8BEN`, meaning a non-US person, the rate is the non-resident default of 3000 basis points whenever `treaty_claimed` is false, or `tin_provided` is false, or the declared `country_code` has no published treaty floor — which is the case for `US` and for `BR`. Otherwise the rate is the larger of the claimed `treaty_rate_bps` and the country's published treaty floor, where the floors are 0 basis points for `GB`, 0 basis points for `DE`, 1500 basis points for `IN` and 1000 basis points for `JP`. A claim *below* the country's floor is silently lifted to the floor and is not an error; a claim *above* the floor is honoured as supplied, so a channel may choose to over-withhold. The channel's own `country_code` is not read here — the profile's declared `country_code` is, and the consistency rule above guarantees the two agree.

`withholding_rate_bps` is written on every status change of the profile it belongs to, and rates are integer basis points with no rounding anywhere in this resolution.

### 3.3. The Product Catalogue, Content Publication and Revenue Accrual

The catalogue of monetization surfaces is administrative configuration. Declaring a surface requires a `product_type` of `ADVERTISING`, `SUBSCRIPTIONS`, `TIPS` or `COMMERCE`, a human-readable `name` (1 to 120 characters, non-blank), and the two halves of the commercial split: `creator_share_bps`, the per-product creator share in basis points before any bonus or multiplier, from 0 to 9000 inclusive; and `platform_fee_bps`, the per-product platform fee, from 0 to 10000 inclusive. The two must add to exactly 10000 basis points on the resulting row, both at declaration and after any amendment; a split that does not close is rejected with `ERR_SHARE_SPLIT_NOT_WHOLE`. Two optional terms may be supplied: `min_payable_cents`, the minimum per-event creator share that accrues at all, defaulting to 0 and never negative; and `terms_version`, an integer of at least 1 defaulting to 1. A surface is created with `status` `ACTIVE` and `retired_at` null, and with `suitability_sensitive` computed rather than supplied: it is true if and only if `product_type` is `ADVERTISING`, and only suitability-sensitive surfaces apply the content suitability multiplier described below.

Terms may be amended by an administrator, overwriting any of `creator_share_bps`, `platform_fee_bps`, `min_payable_cents` and `terms_version` and incrementing the product's `version`. Amendment is purely forward-looking: no revenue event, no statement and no ledger entry is recomputed, and every already-accrued event keeps the `terms_version_at_accrual` and `creator_share_cents` it earned under the terms in force at the time. A surface may also be retired, which sets `status` to `RETIRED`, sets `retired_at` to the current logical time and increments the product's `version`; retirement closes the surface to new accruals but leaves existing events untouched, and those events are still swept into statements at the next period close. A `RETIRED` product's terms are frozen: both retiring an already-retired product and amending one are rejected with `ERR_PRODUCT_ALREADY_RETIRED`.

Content is published either by the channel itself or by an administrator, carrying the owning `creator_id`, a `title` (1 to 200 characters, non-blank) and a `published_at` instant that must be at or before the current logical time — a future publication instant is rejected with `ERR_PUBLISHED_IN_FUTURE`. A published item is born with `suitability_rating` `GREEN`, `monetization_state` `MONETIZED`, `rating_source` `DEFAULT`, `last_rated_by_user_id` null and `last_rated_at` null, so no appeal is admissible against it yet. `monetization_state` is never written directly: it is derived strictly from `suitability_rating`, with `GREEN` yielding `MONETIZED`, `YELLOW` yielding `LIMITED` and `RED` yielding `DEMONETIZED`. `open_appeal_count` is likewise derived, counting the item's appeals whose state is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED`.

**Ingesting revenue.** A `PlatformAdmin` or `FinanceOperator` ingests one gross revenue event at a time, supplying the earning `creator_id`, the `product_id` whose terms apply, an optional `content_item_id`, a `gross_amount_cents` strictly greater than zero, and an `occurred_at` attribution instant. Before anything is computed, five domain gates apply. The channel's `eligibility_status` must be exactly `ELIGIBLE`; `PENDING_REVIEW`, `INELIGIBLE` and `SUSPENDED` all reject with `ERR_CREATOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The product's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the ingestion is rejected with `ERR_PRODUCT_RETIRED`. No new revenue may accrue inside a window that is under active review: if the channel has an investigation in `OPEN` state whose closed window from `violation_window_start` to `violation_window_end` inclusive contains the supplied `occurred_at`, the ingestion is rejected with `ERR_ACCRUAL_UNDER_INVESTIGATION`. When a `content_item_id` is supplied, that item's owning creator must be the same creator as the supplied `creator_id`, or the ingestion is rejected with `ERR_CONTENT_CREATOR_MISMATCH`. And the content link is mandatory or forbidden according to the surface: an `ADVERTISING` product requires a non-null `content_item_id` (`ERR_CONTENT_LINK_REQUIRED`), while `SUBSCRIPTIONS`, `TIPS` and `COMMERCE` all require it to be null (`ERR_CONTENT_LINK_FORBIDDEN`).

The event's owning attribution window is then resolved from `occurred_at`: there must be exactly one earnings period in `OPEN` status whose half-open window from `period_start` inclusive to `period_end` exclusive contains `occurred_at`, and that period becomes the event's `period_id`. Zero matching open periods rejects the ingestion with `ERR_NO_OPEN_PERIOD_FOR_EVENT`; more than one is impossible, because the calendar rule in §3.5 forbids overlapping windows. Two snapshots are then frozen onto the event: `terms_version_at_accrual` takes the product's current `terms_version`, and `suitability_rating_at_accrual` takes the linked content item's current `suitability_rating`, or `GREEN` when `content_item_id` is null.

**The revenue share split** then runs, in this exact order. The base share is the product's `creator_share_bps`. A loyalty bonus in basis points is selected from the channel's `lifetime_paid_cents` as read *at this evaluation*: 0 basis points while `lifetime_paid_cents` is strictly below 100000 cents; 200 basis points from 100000 cents up to but not including 1000000 cents; 400 basis points from 1000000 cents up to but not including 10000000 cents; and 600 basis points at 10000000 cents and above. Because the bucket is read live, a re-score performed after a payout may legitimately select a higher bucket than the original accrual did. The base share plus the bonus is then capped at 9000 basis points — the cap is applied **before** the suitability multiplier, and applying the multiplier first and capping afterwards yields a different and wrong number for any event whose bonused share exceeds the cap. A suitability multiplier is chosen next: for a product whose `suitability_sensitive` is true, it is 10000 basis points when `suitability_rating_at_accrual` is `GREEN`, 6000 basis points when `YELLOW`, and 0 basis points when `RED`; for every other product it is 10000 basis points. The event's `effective_share_bps` is the capped share multiplied by the multiplier and divided by 10000, **truncated down** to a whole basis point — this is the single place in the service where a basis-point value is truncated, and it is truncated before touching money.

Money follows: the exact rational product of `gross_amount_cents` and `effective_share_bps` divided by 10000 is computed with no intermediate rounding, and the result is rounded half-up to a whole cent. That rounded share is then compared against the product's `min_payable_cents` with strictly-below semantics: if it is strictly below the minimum, `creator_share_cents` is set to 0 and the event's `status` becomes `BELOW_MINIMUM`; otherwise `creator_share_cents` takes the rounded share and `status` becomes `ACCRUED`. A share exactly equal to `min_payable_cents` accrues in full. `last_status_change_at` is set to the current logical time on every status write.

The ledger entry is keyed on the **delta** against the share the event carried before the run, not on the new value. Subtracting the previous `creator_share_cents` from the new one: a positive delta appends an entry whose `amount_cents` is that positive delta, with `entry_type` `ACCRUAL` on the initial accrual and `ACCRUAL_RESTORE` on a restore; a negative delta appends an `ACCRUAL_REVERSAL` entry carrying that negative amount; and a delta of exactly zero appends **no** entry at all. Each appended entry stamps `balance_after_cents` with the channel's `ledger_balance_cents` after the append, and carries `source_entity` `REVENUE_EVENT` with `source_id` naming the event. On an initial accrual the previous share is treated as zero, so an event that accrues a positive share appends an `ACCRUAL` entry and an event that lands `BELOW_MINIMUM` appends nothing.

A revenue event's `status` takes one of `ACCRUED`, `BELOW_MINIMUM`, `REVERSED` or `CLAWED_BACK`. Events in `CLAWED_BACK` status are **never** re-scored by any later rating change or appeal.

### 3.4. Advertiser Suitability, Demonetization and the Appeal Cycle

A `PolicyReviewer` changes a content item's advertiser suitability by supplying the new `suitability_rating`, a `rating_source` recording the provenance of the judgement, and an optional free-text `note`. The supplied rating must differ from the stored `suitability_rating`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_RATING_UNCHANGED`. The supplied `rating_source` must be either `AUTOMATED` or `MANUAL`: the values `DEFAULT` and `APPEAL` are reserved for system provenance and may not be asserted by a reviewer, and attempting to do so is rejected with `ERR_RATING_SOURCE_RESERVED`.

Re-rating is a three-hop cascade. The first hop writes the item: `suitability_rating` takes the supplied value, `monetization_state` is recomputed from it, `rating_source` takes the supplied value, `last_rated_by_user_id` takes the acting reviewer's `X-User-ID`, `last_rated_at` takes the current logical time, and the item's `version` increments by one. Recording the acting reviewer matters beyond provenance: `last_rated_by_user_id` is the comparand for the segregation-of-duties rule that governs the eventual appeal decision. The second hop re-scores money: every revenue event linked to this item whose `status` is `ACCRUED` or `BELOW_MINIMUM` and whose product is suitability-sensitive is re-scored by the revenue share split, processed in `occurred_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending order so that the appended ledger rows carry a deterministic `balance_after_cents` sequence. On a re-score the event's `suitability_rating_at_accrual` is **overwritten** with the new rating and the split is recomputed, while `terms_version_at_accrual` is *not* refreshed — a later amendment of the product's terms therefore never reaches an old event. A `RED` rating drives the multiplier to zero, so `effective_share_bps` and `creator_share_cents` both become 0 and the event's `status` becomes `REVERSED`, not `BELOW_MINIMUM`: the reversal status wins over the threshold status whenever the multiplier is zero. A `YELLOW` rating applies the 6000-basis-point multiplier and leaves the event `ACCRUED`, unless the recomputed share falls strictly below the product's `min_payable_cents`, in which case the event becomes `BELOW_MINIMUM`. The third hop is the ledger: because each re-scored event's share fell, each appends an `ACCRUAL_REVERSAL` entry for its negative delta, which itself lowers the channel's `ledger_balance_cents` and is stamped into that entry's `balance_after_cents`. Statements already created for a period that is `CLOSED` or `FINALIZED` are never retroactively recomputed.

The two downgrade edges are `GREEN` or `YELLOW` → `RED`, which lands the item in `DEMONETIZED`, and `GREEN` → `YELLOW`, which lands it in `LIMITED`. Both are driven by the same reviewer action and differ only in the multiplier applied and the resulting event status.

**Appealing a rating.** A channel may appeal an item whose `monetization_state` is `LIMITED` or `DEMONETIZED`; an item that is still `MONETIZED` is rejected with `ERR_CONTENT_NOT_APPEALABLE`. Only one appeal may be in flight per item: if the item's `open_appeal_count` is anything other than zero, the request is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_OPEN`. The appeal carries the `content_item_id` under appeal, the `requested_rating` the channel asks the platform to restore — either `GREEN` or `YELLOW` — and a `statement_text` justification of 1 to 2000 characters, non-blank. The requested rating must be strictly less restrictive than the item's current rating: a `RED` item admits a `requested_rating` of `GREEN` or `YELLOW`, and a `YELLOW` item admits only `GREEN`; anything else is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_RATING_NOT_IMPROVEMENT`. The appeal is created in `state` `SUBMITTED` with `revision_round` 0, `info_request` null, `response_statement` null, `decided_by_user_id` null, `decided_at` null and `rationale` null, and with its `creator_id` copied from the appealed item's owning creator. Creating it changes no rating and no event; it merely raises the item's derived `open_appeal_count` to 1.

An appeal's lifecycle is deliberately cyclic. A reviewer picks it up, moving `SUBMITTED` → `UNDER_REVIEW` and changing no field other than the state and the audit timestamps. From `UNDER_REVIEW` the reviewer may ask for more information, supplying `info_request` text and moving the appeal `UNDER_REVIEW` → `INFO_REQUESTED`. The channel then answers, supplying `response_statement`, which moves the appeal `INFO_REQUESTED` → `SUBMITTED`, increments `revision_round` by one and closes the cycle `SUBMITTED` → `UNDER_REVIEW` → `INFO_REQUESTED` → `SUBMITTED`. `revision_round` is the termination counter and the boundary is strict: a response is admissible only while `revision_round` is strictly below 2, so exactly two responses are accepted and a third is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. Each of these steps increments the appeal's `version`.

From `UNDER_REVIEW` the reviewer decides, supplying a `decision` of `UPHELD` or `DENIED` together with a `rationale`. Before either branch runs, segregation of duties applies: the deciding reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the appealed item's `last_rated_by_user_id`, or the decision is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the reviewer who set the disputed rating may not sit in judgement on it. When `last_rated_by_user_id` is null, the check passes. Both branches write `state` to `UPHELD` or `DENIED`, set `decided_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, set `decided_at` to the current logical time, record the `rationale` and increment the appeal's `version`. `UPHELD` and `DENIED` are both terminal.

The two branches then diverge completely. **`DENIED` mutates nothing but the appeal**: no rating change, no event re-scoring, no ledger entry — not one content item, revenue event or ledger row changes. **`UPHELD` runs a four-hop restoration.** Hop one writes the item: `suitability_rating` becomes the appeal's `requested_rating`, `rating_source` becomes `APPEAL`, `last_rated_by_user_id` is reset to null, `last_rated_at` becomes the current logical time and `monetization_state` is recomputed — so the item moves `YELLOW` or `RED` → `GREEN` when `GREEN` was requested, or `RED` → `YELLOW` when `YELLOW` was requested. Hop two re-scores: every revenue event on that item whose `status` is `REVERSED` or `BELOW_MINIMUM` and whose product is suitability-sensitive is re-scored in `occurred_at` ascending, `created_at` ascending order, with `suitability_rating_at_accrual` overwritten by the appeal's `requested_rating` and the loyalty bonus read from the channel's *current* `lifetime_paid_cents`; each such event returns to `ACCRUED`, or stays `BELOW_MINIMUM` when the restored share is strictly below the product's `min_payable_cents`. Hop three and hop four are the ledger and the balance: each restored event appends an `ACCRUAL_RESTORE` entry for its positive delta, and each such entry raises the channel's `ledger_balance_cents`. Statements belonging to a period that is already `CLOSED` or `FINALIZED` are never retroactively recomputed; the restoration reaches the channel through the ledger and the next disbursement.

### 3.5. The Attribution Calendar and the Period-Close Checkpoint

An administrator opens an attribution window by supplying a `label` (1 to 60 characters, non-blank), a `period_start` and a `period_end`. The window is half-open: `period_start` is the inclusive lower bound and `period_end` the exclusive upper bound, and `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start` — a zero-length window is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_WINDOW_INVALID`. The new half-open window must intersect no existing period's half-open window, in any status; an intersection is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Abutment, however, is legal and expected: a new `period_start` exactly equal to an existing `period_end` is accepted, and so is a new `period_end` exactly equal to an existing `period_start`. A period is created with `status` `OPEN`, `closed_at` null, and its two derived counters at zero: `statement_count`, which counts every statement of that period in any status, and `finalized_statement_count`, which counts those whose status is `FINALIZED`, `ADJUSTED` or `PAID`.

Closing a period is the ops-run checkpoint of the whole service and is reserved to a `FinanceOperator`. The period must be `OPEN`, or the close is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`, and periods must close oldest-first: if any other period in `OPEN` status has a strictly earlier `period_start`, the close is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSE_OUT_OF_ORDER`. The close then executes **six steps whose order is normative**, because step 1 changes the rate step 4 uses and step 2 changes the strike count step 3 reads.

*Step 1 — tax expiry.* Every tax profile in `VERIFIED` status whose `expires_at` is at or before the close instant transitions `VERIFIED` → `EXPIRED`, and its `withholding_rate_bps` is set to the backup rate of 2400 basis points, so statements created later in this same close use the backup rate.

*Step 2 — strike lapse.* Every channel whose `active_strike_count` is greater than zero and whose `last_strike_at` plus ninety days is at or before the close instant has `active_strike_count` reset to 0. All strikes lapse together.

*Step 3 — re-evaluation.* The eligibility evaluation runs once for every channel, in `created_at` ascending order, reading the strike counts that step 2 just adjusted. `evaluation_count` increments and `last_evaluated_at` is set to the close instant for every channel, even those whose `eligibility_status` does not move. This is the step that can carry a channel from `SUSPENDED` back to `ELIGIBLE`.

*Step 4 — statement creation.* One earnings statement is created for each channel holding at least one revenue event whose `period_id` is this period and whose `status` is `ACCRUED`, processed in channel `created_at` ascending order. The statement records its `period_id`, its `creator_id`, a `payout_id` of null, and three aggregates over that same event set as of close time: `gross_cents`, the sum of those events' `gross_amount_cents`; `creator_share_cents`, the sum of their `creator_share_cents`; and `event_count`, the number of events in the set. It is created with `withholding_variant` `PROVISIONAL`, `status` `PROVISIONAL`, `clawback_applied_cents` 0, `dispute_adjustment_cents` 0, `finalized_at` null, `dispute_reason` null, `dispute_claimed_amount_cents` null and `dispute_resolution` null. Its `withholding_rate_bps`, `withholding_cents` and `net_payable_cents` are written by the withholding computation below, and a `WITHHOLDING` ledger entry for the negative of `withholding_cents` is appended whenever `withholding_cents` is greater than zero.

*Step 5 — arrears offset.* For every channel whose `outstanding_clawback_cents` is greater than zero **and** which received a statement in step 4, in channel `created_at` ascending order, an offset equal to the lesser of `outstanding_clawback_cents` and that statement's `net_payable_cents` is computed. When the offset is greater than zero, it is added to the statement's `clawback_applied_cents`, subtracted from the channel's `outstanding_clawback_cents`, and recorded as a `CLAWBACK` ledger entry for the negative of the offset whose `source_entity` is `EARNINGS_STATEMENT` and whose `source_id` names the statement; `payout_state` is then recomputed, which returns the channel from `ARREARS` to `ACCRUING` precisely when the offset reduced `outstanding_clawback_cents` to exactly zero. In this variant the statement's `status` is **unchanged** — it is `PROVISIONAL` at this instant and stays `PROVISIONAL`. A channel carrying arrears but receiving no step-4 statement is skipped entirely and keeps its full arrears.

*Step 6 — closure.* The period's `status` becomes `CLOSED`, `closed_at` is set to the close instant, and the period's `version` increments by one. The period does **not** become `FINALIZED` here; that is a compound transition evaluated after each statement finalization.

**The statement withholding computation** has two variants that differ only in the *order* in which rounding is applied, and the difference is deliberately observable at the cent level. Both variants operate over the same frozen event set, fixed at the instant the period closed and never re-derived afterwards. For each revenue event, define its accrual basis as the sum of the `amount_cents` of that event's ledger entries whose `entry_type` is `ACCRUAL`, `ACCRUAL_REVERSAL` or `ACCRUAL_RESTORE` and whose `occurred_at` is at or before the period's `closed_at`. The statement's event set is every revenue event whose `period_id` and `creator_id` match the statement's and whose accrual basis is strictly positive, ordered by `occurred_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending. By construction the sum of those bases equals the statement's `creator_share_cents`, because an event that never cleared the minimum produced no accrual entry and a reversed event's accrual and reversal entries cancel to zero. A rating change made *after* `closed_at` appends entries with a later `occurred_at` and therefore cannot move the event set or any basis — this is the mechanism that makes a closed period's statement immutable.

Both variants begin by resolving the rate: the withholding resolution of §3.2 is evaluated for the statement's channel **at the moment the variant runs**, and the result is written to the statement's `withholding_rate_bps` — which is the backup rate of 2400 basis points when the channel has no non-superseded profile at all. The `PROVISIONAL` variant then aggregates and rounds once: `withholding_cents` is the product of `creator_share_cents` and the rate divided by 10000, rounded half-up to a whole cent — a single rounding operation. The `FINAL` variant rounds per event and then sums: for each member of the event set, the product of that event's accrual basis and the rate divided by 10000 is rounded half-up to a whole cent, and `withholding_cents` is the sum of those rounded amounts — as many rounding operations as there are events. Both variants close identically: `net_payable_cents` is `creator_share_cents` minus `withholding_cents`. Because the rounding order changed, `net_payable_cents` may move at finalization even when the rate did not.

A worked example makes the divergence concrete. Take a rate of 3000 basis points and three events whose accrual bases are 335, 335 and 335 cents, so that `creator_share_cents` is 1005. The `PROVISIONAL` variant rounds 301.5 half-up to 302 cents. The `FINAL` variant rounds 100.5 half-up to 101 cents three times and sums to 303 cents. The difference is one cent, and it is settled by the true-up entry described in §3.6.

### 3.6. Finalizing Statements, Disputes and Audit Support

Finalization is a `FinanceOperator` action that switches a statement from the aggregate-then-round variant to the per-event-then-sum variant. The statement must be `PROVISIONAL` (`ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_PROVISIONAL`) and its period must be `CLOSED` (`ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`) — the second check pins the dependency direction between the two records. Finalization sets `withholding_variant` to `FINAL`, re-resolves `withholding_rate_bps` from the channel's current non-superseded tax profile, and recomputes `withholding_cents` and `net_payable_cents` under the `FINAL` variant. Both effects are independent and both land in a single ledger entry: subtracting the final `withholding_cents` from the provisional `withholding_cents` gives a signed delta, and when that delta is non-zero a `WITHHOLDING_TRUE_UP` entry carrying exactly that signed delta is appended; when the delta is zero no entry is written. A positive delta means the `FINAL` variant withheld *less*, so the channel is credited; a negative delta debits the channel. A profile verified between close and finalization therefore changes both the rate and the rounding order in one step. Finalization sets `status` to `FINALIZED`, sets `finalized_at` to the current logical time and increments the statement's `version`; the statement now enters `statement_payable_cents`, so the channel's `available_balance_cents` rises by `net_payable_cents` minus `clawback_applied_cents` plus `dispute_adjustment_cents`.

After each finalization the parent period re-evaluates its **compound** state: the period moves `CLOSED` → `FINALIZED` when its `statement_count` is at least 1 and its `finalized_statement_count` equals `statement_count`. `FINALIZED` is terminal for a period, and the transition changes no field on any statement. Because `ADJUSTED` and `PAID` both count as finalized for that counter, a statement that later moves to `ADJUSTED` still satisfies the condition.

**Disputes** are the channel's recourse and the audit surface of the service. A channel disputes a statement whose `status` is `FINALIZED` or `ADJUSTED`; any other status is rejected with `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_DISPUTABLE`, and a statement already attached to a payout is rejected with `ERR_STATEMENT_ALREADY_ATTACHED`. The dispute carries a `dispute_reason` (1 to 2000 characters, non-blank) and a `dispute_claimed_amount_cents` naming the amount the channel believes is missing, which must be strictly greater than zero and at most the statement's `net_payable_cents`; a claim outside that range is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_STATEMENT`. Disputing moves the statement `FINALIZED` or `ADJUSTED` → `UNDER_DISPUTE`, records both fields and increments the statement's `version`. The money consequence is immediate: the statement leaves the payable set, so the channel's `statement_payable_cents` and therefore `available_balance_cents` drop by `net_payable_cents` minus `clawback_applied_cents` plus `dispute_adjustment_cents`. The statement also leaves the clawback waterfall's bucket list until the dispute is resolved.

A `FinanceOperator` resolves the dispute by supplying a `decision` of `REJECTED` or `UPHELD` together with an `adjustment_cents` amount of at least zero. The statement must be `UNDER_DISPUTE` (`ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_UNDER_DISPUTE`). The amount is bounded by the decision: a `REJECTED` decision requires `adjustment_cents` to be exactly 0, and an `UPHELD` decision requires it to be strictly greater than 0 and at most the recorded `dispute_claimed_amount_cents`; a violation of either bound is rejected with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_OUT_OF_BOUNDS`. On `REJECTED`, the statement returns `UNDER_DISPUTE` → `FINALIZED`, `dispute_resolution` is set to `REJECTED`, `dispute_adjustment_cents` is left exactly as it was, and **no ledger entry is appended** — the cycle `FINALIZED` → `UNDER_DISPUTE` → `FINALIZED` restores `statement_payable_cents` to its exact pre-dispute value. On `UPHELD`, the statement moves `UNDER_DISPUTE` → `ADJUSTED`, `dispute_resolution` is set to `UPHELD`, `dispute_adjustment_cents` takes the supplied `adjustment_cents`, and a `DISPUTE_ADJUSTMENT` ledger entry for that positive amount is appended, so `statement_payable_cents` returns raised by the adjustment. Either way the statement's `version` increments and the parent period re-evaluates its compound condition.

A statement's `status` therefore takes one of `PROVISIONAL`, `FINALIZED`, `UNDER_DISPUTE`, `ADJUSTED` or `PAID`, with `PAID` terminal. Two further routes into `ADJUSTED` exist and are described in §3.8: a substantiated investigation's waterfall pushes an absorbing statement from `FINALIZED` or `ADJUSTED` into `ADJUSTED` by raising its `clawback_applied_cents`, and it does so only for statements whose `payout_id` is null and to which the waterfall assigned a positive amount.

### 3.7. Disbursement: Creating, Settling, Retrying and Abandoning a Payout

A disbursement is created either by the channel itself or by a `FinanceOperator`, and the only input is the `creator_id` being paid. A payout always disburses the channel's *whole* available balance, so there is no amount parameter. Four gates guard creation, and their relative order matters. The channel's `payout_state` must be exactly `ACCRUING`; `HOLD`, `ARREARS` and `PAYOUT_IN_FLIGHT` are all rejected with `ERR_CREATOR_NOT_PAYABLE`. The channel must hold a tax profile whose `status` is `VERIFIED`; a `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED`, superseded or entirely absent profile blocks disbursement with `ERR_TAX_DOCUMENTATION_MISSING`, even though none of those states blocks accrual. The channel's `available_balance_cents` must be strictly greater than zero — both zero and negative reject with `ERR_NO_PAYABLE_BALANCE`. And that balance must be at least the channel's `payout_threshold_cents`, with an inclusive boundary: a balance exactly equal to the threshold is payable, and a balance below it is rejected with `ERR_BELOW_PAYOUT_THRESHOLD`.

**The fee and amount computation** runs exactly once, at creation, and the evaluation order is normative: the fee tier is keyed on the *pre-fee* payable, while the disbursed amount is the payable net of that fee. Re-deriving the tier from the disbursed amount is the canonical wrong answer, because it changes the tier for every payable within one fee of a boundary.

The payable is the channel's `available_balance_cents` snapshotted **before** any field of this request is written — that is, `statement_payable_cents` plus `fund_credit_balance_cents` minus `held_cents`, where `held_cents` is necessarily zero because creation requires `payout_state` `ACCRUING`. That snapshot is stored as `payable_before_fee_cents`, and the payout's `method` is set from a snapshot of the channel's `payout_method`, not from a live read, so a later change of rail never re-prices an existing payout. A volume tier is then selected from the payable with inclusive lower bounds: below 50000 cents the tier multiplier is 10000 basis points; from 50000 cents up to but not including 500000 cents it is 7500 basis points; and at 500000 cents and above it is 5000 basis points. A payable of exactly 50000 cents sits in the middle tier and 49999 cents in the lowest.

A gross fee is computed from the snapshotted `method`: a flat component of 200 cents for `BANK_TRANSFER`, 100 cents for `INSTANT_DEBIT` or 500 cents for `CHECK`, plus a percentage component equal to the payable multiplied by the rail's rate and divided by 10000, **rounded up** to the next whole cent, where that rate is 0 basis points for `BANK_TRANSFER`, 150 basis points for `INSTANT_DEBIT` and 0 basis points for `CHECK`. The gross fee is then multiplied by the tier multiplier and divided by 10000, again **rounded up** to the next whole cent. The result is capped at the rail's ceiling — 1500 cents for `BANK_TRANSFER`, 4000 cents for `INSTANT_DEBIT`, 500 cents for `CHECK` — and finally clamped so that it never exceeds the payable minus one cent. That last clamp is a floor guarantee: a payout always disburses at least one cent, so a payable of one cent yields a `fee_cents` of zero. It is unreachable in practice, because creation also requires the payable to be at least `payout_threshold_cents`, which is itself at least 1000 cents, but the clamp is part of the contract. Nothing in this computation uses half-up rounding.

`fee_cents` takes the clamped fee and `amount_cents` is the payable minus `fee_cents`. `fund_credit_cents` records how much of the payable came from creator-fund money: it is the lesser of the channel's `fund_credit_balance_cents` and the payable, and the channel's `fund_credit_balance_cents` is then set to 0, consumed into the payout. Two ledger entries are appended in this order: a `PAYOUT_DEBIT` entry for the negative of `amount_cents`, then a `PAYOUT_FEE` entry for the negative of `fee_cents`, the latter omitted entirely when `fee_cents` is zero.

Creation also attaches statements. Every statement of that channel whose `status` is `FINALIZED` or `ADJUSTED` and whose `payout_id` is null has its `payout_id` set to the new payout, in order of the owning period's `period_start` ascending and then statement `created_at` ascending; the payout's derived `statement_count` counts them. The payout is created in `state` `PENDING` with `failure_code` null, `retry_count` 0, `submitted_at` null and `settled_at` null, the channel's `payout_state` is recomputed to `PAYOUT_IN_FLIGHT`, and the channel's `version` increments by one.

Two worked boundaries pin the arithmetic. With `BANK_TRANSFER` — flat 200 cents, 0 basis points — a payable of 49999 cents sits in the lowest tier: the gross fee of 200 cents scaled by 10000 basis points and rounded up is 200 cents, so `fee_cents` is 200 and `amount_cents` is 49799. A payable of exactly 50000 cents sits in the middle tier: 200 cents scaled by 7500 basis points and rounded up is 150 cents, so `fee_cents` is 150 and `amount_cents` is 49850 — a *larger* disbursement from a *larger* payable, which is the intended effect of the volume tiers. With `INSTANT_DEBIT` — flat 100 cents, 150 basis points, cap 4000 cents — a payable of 500000 cents sits in the top tier: the percentage component of 7500 cents plus the flat 100 cents gives a gross fee of 7600 cents, scaled by 5000 basis points and rounded up gives 3800 cents, which is under the 4000-cent cap, so `fee_cents` is 3800 and `amount_cents` is 496200.

**Settlement.** A `FinanceOperator` hands a payout to the processing rail, moving it `PENDING` → `PROCESSING`, setting `submitted_at` to the current logical time and incrementing the payout's `version`; no ledger movement occurs, because the debit was taken at creation. The payout must be `PENDING` (`ERR_PAYOUT_NOT_PENDING`) and the channel's `payout_state` must not be `HOLD` (`ERR_PAYOUT_HELD`) — a condition reachable after creation precisely because `HOLD` outranks `PAYOUT_IN_FLIGHT` in the disbursement resolution.

The processor outcome is then recorded against a payout that must be `PROCESSING` (`ERR_PAYOUT_NOT_PROCESSING`), carrying an `outcome` of `SUCCEEDED` or `FAILED` and an optional `failure_code`. A `FAILED` outcome requires a `failure_code` of `INSUFFICIENT_DETAILS`, `BANK_REJECTED` or `ACCOUNT_CLOSED` (`ERR_FAILURE_CODE_REQUIRED`); a `SUCCEEDED` outcome requires `failure_code` to be absent or null (`ERR_FAILURE_CODE_FORBIDDEN`). Every branch increments both the payout's `version` and the channel's `version`.

On `SUCCEEDED` the payout moves `PROCESSING` → `PAID`: `settled_at` is set to the current logical time, the channel's `lifetime_paid_cents` increases by `amount_cents`, `consecutive_payment_failures` is reset to 0, every attached statement transitions from `FINALIZED` or `ADJUSTED` to `PAID` with no monetary field changing on it, and the channel's `payout_state` is recomputed — landing on `ACCRUING` when nothing else encumbers it. **No ledger entry is appended on success**: the debit was already taken at creation, and the money simply left.

On `FAILED` the outcome branches on `retry_count`, and the discriminator is the only difference between the two branches. While `retry_count` is strictly below 2, the payout moves `PROCESSING` → `FAILED`: the supplied `failure_code` is recorded, the channel's `consecutive_payment_failures` increases by one, a `PAYOUT_REVERSAL` ledger entry for the positive sum of `amount_cents` and `fee_cents` is appended, every attached statement is detached — its `payout_id` returns to null and it keeps its pre-payout status — the channel's `fund_credit_balance_cents` is increased by `fund_credit_cents`, and `payout_state` is recomputed to `ACCRUING`, or to `HOLD` or `ARREARS` when either of those outranks. When `retry_count` has already reached 2, the same trigger instead moves the payout `PROCESSING` → `ABANDONED`, which is terminal: `settled_at` is set to the current logical time and the supplied `failure_code` recorded, with exactly the same reversal entry, the same statement detachment, the same fund-credit restore and the same failure-counter increment as the retryable branch.

**Retry** re-submits a failed payout, moving it `FAILED` → `PROCESSING` and closing the retry cycle `PROCESSING` → `FAILED` → `PROCESSING`. The payout must be `FAILED` (`ERR_PAYOUT_NOT_FAILED`) — a payout that reached `ABANDONED` is terminal and is caught by that same check — and its `retry_count` must be strictly below 2, or the retry is rejected with `ERR_PAYOUT_RETRIES_EXHAUSTED`. The channel's `available_balance_cents` must also be at least the sum of the payout's `amount_cents` and `fee_cents`, proving that the reversal restored the money and nothing else consumed it; otherwise the retry is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE_FOR_RETRY`. A retry increments `retry_count` by one, sets `submitted_at` to the current logical time, clears `failure_code` to null and increments the payout's `version`. It re-appends a `PAYOUT_DEBIT` entry for the negative of `amount_cents` and, when `fee_cents` is greater than zero, a `PAYOUT_FEE` entry for the negative of `fee_cents`. It re-attaches statements by the same selection and the same ordering as creation — the channel's statements whose `status` is `FINALIZED` or `ADJUSTED` and whose `payout_id` is null, in period `period_start` ascending then `created_at` ascending order — consumes `fund_credit_balance_cents` to zero again up to `fund_credit_cents`, recomputes `payout_state` to `PAYOUT_IN_FLIGHT` and increments the channel's `version`. Crucially, **`fee_cents` and `amount_cents` are reused verbatim**: the fee tier is never re-selected, even if the channel's balance changed in between.

A payout's `state` therefore takes one of `PENDING`, `PROCESSING`, `PAID`, `FAILED`, `VOIDED` or `ABANDONED`, with `PAID`, `VOIDED` and `ABANDONED` terminal. The route to `VOIDED` belongs to the clawback cascade in §3.8.

### 3.8. Policy Investigations, Revenue Holds and Retroactive Clawback

A `PolicyReviewer` opens an investigation over a channel by supplying the `creator_id` under investigation, a `scope` describing the suspected violation (1 to 500 characters, non-blank), a `violation_window_start` and a `violation_window_end`, and an optional `hold_payouts` flag that defaults to true. Unlike the attribution calendar, an investigation window is **closed on both ends**: both `violation_window_start` and `violation_window_end` are inclusive. `violation_window_end` must be at or after `violation_window_start` — the bound is non-strict, so a single-instant window is legal — and a violation is rejected with `ERR_INVESTIGATION_WINDOW_INVALID`. The new closed window must intersect no other investigation of the same channel that is in `OPEN` state; because both endpoints are inclusive, **touching windows collide**: a new `violation_window_start` exactly equal to an existing `violation_window_end` is rejected with `ERR_INVESTIGATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, which is the opposite of the abutment rule that governs attribution windows.

The investigation is created in `state` `OPEN` with `opened_by_user_id` set to the acting reviewer's `X-User-ID`, `finding` null, `resolved_by_user_id` null, `resolved_at` null and all three clawback results at zero. When `hold_payouts` is true, `hold_amount_cents` is set to the greater of the channel's `available_balance_cents` and zero, snapshotted **immediately before** the hold applies; the channel's `held_cents` then rises by that amount and `available_balance_cents` drops correspondingly, moving the channel `ACCRUING` → `HOLD` unless arrears outrank the hold. When `hold_payouts` is false, `hold_amount_cents` is 0 and no balance moves. Either way the channel's `payout_state` is recomputed and its `version` increments. No revenue event is touched at open time: assessment happens only at substantiation.

Resolution is a fork with identical preconditions and disjoint effects, and both branches are reserved to a reviewer other than the one who opened the case. The investigation must be `OPEN`, or the resolution is rejected with `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_OPEN`; and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `opened_by_user_id`, or it is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Note the order: a resolution sent by the opening reviewer against an already-resolved case reports the state error, not the duties error. Both branches take a written `finding` (1 to 2000 characters, non-blank), record `resolved_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID` and `resolved_at` as the current logical time, and increment the investigation's `version`; `SUBSTANTIATED` and `CLEARED` are both terminal.

**Clearing** exonerates the channel, moving `OPEN` → `CLEARED`. Its effect set is deliberately empty of money movement: `clawback_base_cents`, `clawback_recovered_cents` and `clawback_unrecovered_cents` all stay 0, no revenue event, statement or ledger entry changes at all, and `active_strike_count` is **not** incremented. The channel's `held_cents` drops to zero because the investigation is no longer `OPEN`, and `payout_state` is recomputed by the precedence of §2.7: `ARREARS` if arrears remain, otherwise `HOLD` if another investigation of that channel is still `OPEN` with holds enabled, otherwise `PAYOUT_IN_FLIGHT` if a `PENDING` or `PROCESSING` payout exists, otherwise `ACCRUING`. The channel's `version` increments.

**Substantiating** confirms the violation, moving `OPEN` → `SUBSTANTIATED`, and runs a five-hop retroactive cascade.

*Hop one — assessment and the strike.* Every revenue event of that channel whose `status` is `ACCRUED` and whose `occurred_at` lies within the closed window, endpoints included, is collected; `clawback_base_cents` is the sum of those events' `creator_share_cents`, and each of those events has its `status` set to `CLAWED_BACK` with `last_status_change_at` set to the current logical time. Events already `REVERSED`, `BELOW_MINIMUM` or `CLAWED_BACK` are not re-assessed, and an event sitting exactly on either endpoint **is** in the window. In the same hop the channel's `active_strike_count` increases by one and `last_strike_at` is set to the current logical time, which itself forces the eligibility evaluation to write `eligibility_status` `SUSPENDED` with `last_evaluation_failed_criteria` containing only `POLICY_COMPLIANCE` and to increment `evaluation_count`.

*Hop two — the waterfall.* Because `clawback_base_cents` is greater than zero, the assessed amount is recovered from the channel's absorbing statements. The bucket list is every statement of that channel whose `status` is `FINALIZED` or `ADJUSTED` and whose `payout_id` is null, ordered by the owning period's `period_start` ascending and then statement `created_at` ascending — oldest period first. Statements that are `PROVISIONAL`, `UNDER_DISPUTE` or `PAID`, and statements already attached to a payout, are **not** buckets and cannot absorb anything. The waterfall is a single pass with no revisiting: starting with the full base as the remaining amount, each bucket in turn is skipped once the remaining amount reaches zero; otherwise its capacity is computed as its `net_payable_cents` minus its `clawback_applied_cents` plus its `dispute_adjustment_cents`, floored at zero, and the amount taken is the lesser of the remaining amount and that capacity. When the amount taken is greater than zero, the statement's `clawback_applied_cents` increases by it, the statement's `status` is set to `ADJUSTED`, and the remaining amount decreases by it. `clawback_recovered_cents` is the base minus whatever remains, and `clawback_unrecovered_cents` is exactly what remains.

*Hop three — the ledger and the receivable.* When `clawback_recovered_cents` is greater than zero, a `CLAWBACK` ledger entry for the negative of that amount is appended with `source_entity` `INVESTIGATION` and `source_id` naming the investigation. When `clawback_unrecovered_cents` is greater than zero, the channel's `outstanding_clawback_cents` increases by that amount and **no** ledger entry is written for it: an unrecovered clawback is a receivable, not a ledger movement, and it lands on the ledger only when a later period-close offset absorbs it.

*Hop four — the disbursement state.* Because `outstanding_clawback_cents` is now greater than zero, the disbursement resolution writes `payout_state` `ARREARS`, which by the precedence of §2.7 supersedes an open hold and an in-flight payout alike, from any of `ACCRUING`, `HOLD` or `PAYOUT_IN_FLIGHT`.

*Hop five — voiding pending disbursements.* Because the channel entered `ARREARS` in this same request, every payout of that channel whose `state` is `PENDING` moves `PENDING` → `VOIDED`. Each void appends a `PAYOUT_REVERSAL` ledger entry for the positive sum of that payout's `amount_cents` and `fee_cents`, detaches its statements by returning their `payout_id` to null, and restores the channel's `fund_credit_balance_cents` by that payout's `fund_credit_cents`. `consecutive_payment_failures` is **not** incremented by a void: no payment was ever attempted.

Two ordering facts about the cascade are normative. The channel's `held_cents` drops to zero because this investigation is no longer `OPEN`. And the waterfall of hop two runs exactly once, *before* the hop-five void, so statements freed by that void are **not** re-swept in the same request — they remain available for a later close-time offset instead.

The two clawback variants differ in more than their trigger. The substantiation sweep derives its base from revenue events in a closed window, computes bucket capacity as `net_payable_cents` minus `clawback_applied_cents` plus `dispute_adjustment_cents`, pushes each absorbing statement to `ADJUSTED`, consumes multiple buckets in period order, and turns any unabsorbed residue into `outstanding_clawback_cents`. The period-close arrears offset of §3.5 instead takes the stored `outstanding_clawback_cents` as its base, treats plain `net_payable_cents` as the capacity (because a freshly created statement has both `clawback_applied_cents` and `dispute_adjustment_cents` at zero), consults exactly one bucket, leaves that statement `PROVISIONAL`, and stamps its ledger entry with `source_entity` `EARNINGS_STATEMENT` rather than `INVESTIGATION`. Neither variant rounds anything: a waterfall only ever moves whole cents that already exist.

### 3.9. Creator Funds: Two Allocation Methodologies and Their Trade-off

A supplemental fund is declared by an administrator against a period, supplying a `name` (1 to 120 characters, non-blank), the `period_id` the fund supplements, a `total_pool_cents` strictly greater than zero, and a `methodology` of either `PRO_RATA_WATCH_TIME` or `GUARANTEED_FLOOR_WATERFALL`. Two optional parameters shape the pool: `min_watch_time_minutes`, the minimum trailing-year watch time required to enter the candidate pool, defaulting to 0 and never negative; and `max_allocation_cents`, the per-recipient cap, defaulting to 2000000 cents and always strictly greater than zero. The named period's `status` must be `OPEN` or `CLOSED`; a `FINALIZED` period admits no new fund and is rejected with `ERR_FUND_PERIOD_FINALIZED`. At most one live fund may exist per period: if any other fund with the same `period_id` has `status` `DRAFT` or `ALLOCATED`, the declaration is rejected with `ERR_FUND_ALREADY_EXISTS_FOR_PERIOD`, while a `DISBURSED` fund does not block a successor. When `methodology` is `GUARANTEED_FLOOR_WATERFALL`, `max_allocation_cents` must be at least the guaranteed floor of 5000 cents, so that the per-recipient cap cannot silently defeat the floor; a lower cap is rejected with `ERR_FUND_CAP_BELOW_FLOOR`. The fund is created with `status` `DRAFT`, `allocation_run_count` 0, `allocated_cents` 0, `unallocated_remainder_cents` equal to `total_pool_cents`, `recipient_count` 0 and `disbursed_at` null.

**The candidate pool.** An allocation run, performed by a `FinanceOperator`, first selects candidates: every channel whose `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE`, whose `watch_time_minutes_365d` is at least the fund's `min_watch_time_minutes`, and whose `watch_time_minutes_365d` is strictly greater than zero. Candidates are ordered by `watch_time_minutes_365d` descending, with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and each candidate's one-based position in that ordering is its `rank`. A channel's `payout_state` is deliberately **not** part of the filter: a channel in `ARREARS` or `HOLD` still receives an allocation. If the candidate pool is empty the run is rejected with `ERR_FUND_POOL_EMPTY`, no allocation row is created, `allocation_run_count` does not advance, and any existing `ACTIVE` allocation rows survive untouched.

Under **`PRO_RATA_WATCH_TIME`**, which maximizes transparency, the total watch time of the pool is summed, and each candidate's provisional amount is the `total_pool_cents` multiplied by that candidate's watch time and divided by the pool total, **truncated down** to a whole cent. Each provisional amount is then capped at `max_allocation_cents`, and capped excess is **not** redistributed. The remainder is `total_pool_cents` minus the sum of the capped amounts, and it is distributed by a **single** pass in `rank` ascending order: each recipient whose amount is still strictly below `max_allocation_cents` receives exactly one additional cent while remainder is left, and the pass stops when the remainder reaches zero. At most one cent per recipient is added and the pass is never repeated, so whatever remainder survives is not redistributed and surfaces as the fund's `unallocated_remainder_cents`.

Under **`GUARANTEED_FLOOR_WATERFALL`**, which maximizes predictability for small channels, the guaranteed floor is 5000 cents per recipient. If `total_pool_cents` is strictly less than the number of candidates multiplied by that floor, the fund is under-funded and the allocation becomes a strict rank waterfall: walking candidates in `rank` order, each receives the lesser of the floor and whatever is left of the pool, so the boundary rank may receive a **partial** floor and every later rank receives nothing at all. Otherwise the surplus is `total_pool_cents` minus the number of candidates multiplied by the floor; each candidate's proportional share is that surplus multiplied by the candidate's watch time and divided by the pool's total watch time, truncated down to a whole cent; each amount is the floor plus that share, capped at `max_allocation_cents`; and the leftover remainder is distributed by exactly the same single rank-ordered one-cent pass as the pro-rata methodology. Under either methodology a candidate whose computed amount is zero receives **no** allocation row at all — rows exist only for positive amounts — so a `recipient_count` lower than the candidate count is normal for an under-funded fund.

Each created allocation row records its `fund_id`, the recipient's `creator_id`, the computed `amount_cents` (never negative), the recipient's `rank` (at least 1), a `methodology_snapshot` of the fund's `methodology` at the run that produced the row, a `watch_time_minutes_at_allocation` snapshot of the recipient's `watch_time_minutes_365d` at that run, `status` `ACTIVE`, `voided_at` null and `void_reason` null.

The same trade-off is visible at the cent level. Consider a pool of 100000 cents with a `max_allocation_cents` of 1000000 and three eligible channels whose watch minutes are 500000, 300000 and 200001, summing to 1000001. Under `PRO_RATA_WATCH_TIME` the truncated provisional amounts are 49999, 29999 and 20000, summing to 99998, leaving a remainder of 2 which gives one cent each to ranks 1 and 2, for final amounts of 50000, 30000 and 20000 and an `unallocated_remainder_cents` of 0. Under `GUARANTEED_FLOOR_WATERFALL` on the same pool, three floors of 5000 cents total 15000 which the pool covers, leaving a surplus of 85000; the truncated proportional shares are 42499, 25499 and 17000, so the amounts before the remainder pass are 47499, 30499 and 22000, summing to 99998; the remainder of 2 again gives one cent each to ranks 1 and 2, for final amounts of 47500, 30500 and 22000. Identical inputs, materially different per-recipient results.

**Re-running, discarding and disbursing.** A fund's `status` must be `DRAFT` or `ALLOCATED` for an allocation run; a `DISBURSED` fund can never be re-allocated and is rejected with `ERR_FUND_NOT_ALLOCATABLE`. Every run requires the `expected_fund_version` described in §2.8. When the fund was already `ALLOCATED`, the run is a legitimate self-loop `ALLOCATED` → `ALLOCATED`: every existing allocation row whose `status` is `ACTIVE` is first set to `VOIDED`, with `voided_at` set to the current logical time and `void_reason` set to the literal string `SUPERSEDED_BY_REALLOCATION`, and a fresh set of rows is then created from the current candidate pool. In both the first-run and the supersede case `allocation_run_count` increases by one, `status` becomes `ALLOCATED`, the three derived aggregates are recomputed — `allocated_cents` as the sum of `amount_cents` over rows whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `unallocated_remainder_cents` as `total_pool_cents` minus `allocated_cents`, and `recipient_count` as the number of `ACTIVE` rows — and the fund's `version` increments by one.

An allocation set may be discarded, moving the fund `ALLOCATED` → `DRAFT`. This requires the fund to be `ALLOCATED` (`ERR_FUND_NOT_ALLOCATED`), takes both `expected_fund_version` and a written `reason`, and sets every `ACTIVE` allocation row to `VOIDED` with `voided_at` at the current logical time and `void_reason` set to the supplied `reason`. `allocated_cents` returns to 0, `unallocated_remainder_cents` returns to `total_pool_cents`, `recipient_count` returns to 0, and `allocation_run_count` is **not** decremented. The fund's `version` increments, and no channel's `fund_credit_balance_cents` is touched, because nothing was disbursed.

Disbursement moves the fund `ALLOCATED` → `DISBURSED`, which is terminal. The fund must be `ALLOCATED` (`ERR_FUND_NOT_ALLOCATED`) and must have at least one `ACTIVE` allocation row, that is, a `recipient_count` of at least 1, or the request is rejected with `ERR_FUND_NO_RECIPIENTS`. For each `ACTIVE` allocation row, in `rank` ascending order, a `FUND_CREDIT` ledger entry for the positive `amount_cents` is appended with `source_entity` `FUND_ALLOCATION`, and the recipient's `fund_credit_balance_cents` increases by that amount, raising that channel's `available_balance_cents`. The fund's `status` becomes `DISBURSED`, `disbursed_at` is set to the current logical time and the fund's `version` increments. The sum of the appended fund-credit amounts equals `allocated_cents` exactly. Fund money is purely additive to statement money and reaches a channel only through `fund_credit_balance_cents`: no revenue event, statement or payout is read by an allocation run.

### 3.10. Reporting, Filtering and Reconciliation for Creators and Auditors

Earnings reporting and audit support are served by filtered reads rather than by bespoke report objects. The channel collection may be filtered by `eligibility_status`, `payout_state` and `country_code`; tax profiles by `creator_id`, `status` and `form_type`; the product catalogue by `product_type` and `status`; content items by `creator_id`, `suitability_rating`, `monetization_state` and `rating_source`; revenue events by `creator_id`, `product_id`, `content_item_id`, `period_id` and `status`; appeals by `content_item_id`, `creator_id` and `state`; periods by `status`; statements by `creator_id`, `period_id`, `payout_id`, `status` and `withholding_variant`; payouts by `creator_id`, `state` and `method`; investigations by `creator_id`, `state` and `hold_payouts`; funds by `period_id`, `status` and `methodology`; a channel's allocation rows by `status` and `fund_id`; a fund's allocation rows by `status` and `creator_id`; and a channel's ledger by `entry_type`, `source_entity` and `source_id`. Every one of these reads also accepts `limit` and `offset` as described in §2.3.

Two reads exist specifically for reconciliation. The ledger read is the only way to observe rows created by a cascade — the reversal that followed a demonetization, the restore that followed an upheld appeal, the withholding true-up that followed a finalization — and it is returned in `occurred_at` then `created_at` order so that its `balance_after_cents` column reads as a running balance. The earnings-summary read returns the channel projection with every derived money field recomputed from source rows, so that a client can reconcile `available_balance_cents` against the ledger without trusting a cached value; as noted in §2.10 it is also the one read that repairs a stale stored `payout_state`. Similarly, a fund's allocation rows are the only channel through which cascade-created `amount_cents`, `rank` and `methodology_snapshot` values can be observed.

### 3.11. Lifecycle Reference: Every Legal Transition

The narrative above is normative; this inventory exists so that no legal transition is left implicit. A transition not listed here is illegal, and attempting it raises the state error named in §2.9 for the entity concerned.

- **Channel eligibility.** From `PENDING_REVIEW` the evaluation writes `PENDING_REVIEW` → `ELIGIBLE` when every criterion passes; from any of `PENDING_REVIEW`, `ELIGIBLE` or `SUSPENDED` it writes → `INELIGIBLE` when a metric criterion fails while no strike is active; from any of `PENDING_REVIEW`, `ELIGIBLE` or `INELIGIBLE` it writes → `SUSPENDED` whenever `active_strike_count` is greater than zero, and a policy strike forces that same write; and `SUSPENDED` → `ELIGIBLE` is legal only once strikes are back to zero and both metric criteria pass. Initial state is `PENDING_REVIEW`; no eligibility state is terminal.
- **Channel disbursement.** `ACCRUING` → `HOLD` on opening a holding investigation; `HOLD` → `ACCRUING` or `HOLD` → `PAYOUT_IN_FLIGHT` on resolving the last such investigation, decided by the precedence of §2.7; `ACCRUING` → `PAYOUT_IN_FLIGHT` on creating or retrying a payout; `PAYOUT_IN_FLIGHT` → `ACCRUING` once no `PENDING` or `PROCESSING` payout remains; any of `ACCRUING`, `HOLD` or `PAYOUT_IN_FLIGHT` → `ARREARS` the moment `outstanding_clawback_cents` becomes positive; and `ARREARS` → `ACCRUING` once an offset clears it to zero. Initial state is `ACCRUING`; no disbursement state is terminal.
- **Tax document.** Initial `DRAFT`; `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED` on submission; `SUBMITTED` → `VERIFIED` and `SUBMITTED` → `REJECTED` are the two verification outcomes; `REJECTED` → `DRAFT` on revision; and any of `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED` → `SUPERSEDED` when a newer document for the same channel is submitted. `VERIFIED` → `EXPIRED` occurs when the validity window lapses. `SUPERSEDED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal.
- **Revenue product.** Initial `ACTIVE`; `ACTIVE` → `RETIRED` on retirement, which is terminal.
- **Content suitability.** Initial rating `GREEN` with `monetization_state` `MONETIZED`; from `GREEN` or `YELLOW` a rating action may write `RED`, which sets `monetization_state` `DEMONETIZED`; from `GREEN` it may write `YELLOW`, which sets `LIMITED`; from `YELLOW` or `RED` it may write `GREEN`, restoring `MONETIZED`; and from `RED` it may write `YELLOW`, setting `LIMITED`. No rating is terminal, and an unchanged rating is refused.
- **Appeal.** Initial `SUBMITTED`; `SUBMITTED` → `UNDER_REVIEW` when a reviewer takes the case; `UNDER_REVIEW` → `INFO_REQUESTED` when more information is asked for; `INFO_REQUESTED` → `UNDER_REVIEW` when the channel responds; and `UNDER_REVIEW` → `UPHELD` or `UNDER_REVIEW` → `DENIED` at decision. `UPHELD` and `DENIED` are terminal.
- **Attribution period.** Initial `OPEN`; `OPEN` → `CLOSED` at the close checkpoint; `CLOSED` → `FINALIZED` when the last statement of the period is finalized. `FINALIZED` is terminal.
- **Statement.** Initial `PROVISIONAL`; `PROVISIONAL` → `FINALIZED` at finalization; from `FINALIZED` or `ADJUSTED`, → `UNDER_DISPUTE` on dispute, → `ADJUSTED` when a clawback waterfall absorbs against it, and → `PAID` on successful settlement; `UNDER_DISPUTE` → `ADJUSTED` when a dispute is upheld and `UNDER_DISPUTE` → `FINALIZED` when it is denied. `PAID` is terminal.
- **Payout.** Initial `PENDING`; `PENDING` → `PROCESSING` on submission; `PROCESSING` → `PAID`, `PROCESSING` → `FAILED` or `PROCESSING` → `ABANDONED` at settlement; `FAILED` → `PENDING` on retry; and `PENDING` → `VOIDED` when a clawback cascade puts the channel into arrears. `PAID`, `VOIDED` and `ABANDONED` are terminal.
- **Investigation.** Initial `OPEN`; `OPEN` → `SUBSTANTIATED` and `OPEN` → `CLEARED` are the two resolutions, and both are terminal.
- **Supplemental fund.** Initial `DRAFT`; `DRAFT` → `ALLOCATED` on the first allocation run; `ALLOCATED` → `ALLOCATED` on a re-run that supersedes prior rows; `ALLOCATED` → `DRAFT` when an allocation set is discarded; and `ALLOCATED` → `DISBURSED` on disbursement, which is terminal.
