# Resort Fee & Ancillary Charge Disclosure Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Lodging operators have spent a decade adding mandatory resort fees, destination fees, amenity fees and premium-connectivity charges on top of the advertised nightly rate, and regulators across the jurisdictions those properties sit in have responded with disclosure mandates that differ in almost every detail: some require the all-in total at the very first quote a shopper sees, some require every mandatory fee to be broken out as its own line on the guest's folio, a few forbid mandatory fees outright, and several insist that any fee bundling services a guest can decline must itself be declinable. The operator's exposure is not the fee — it is the gap between what the guest was shown on a booking path and what the guest was eventually billed. That gap is where guest complaints, refund liability and regulator dockets come from.

This document specifies the service that closes that gap. It maintains a versioned inventory of every mandatory and ancillary fee a property charges, records the inclusion analysis that justifies each fee, and determines per fee code and per jurisdiction whether the charge is taxable. It then tracks what each distribution channel — the operator's own web and app estate as well as third-party intermediaries — actually renders at first quote, scores each of those booking paths against the jurisdiction's mandates, and lets compliance staff audit, flag and remediate a path that falls short. Every booking taken through a scored path carries a pinned fee inventory, an attestation of what the channel displayed, and a computed disclosure gap; every folio itemises the mandatory fees with an explicit per-line taxability determination; every opt-out and every substantiated complaint is settled by appending a compensating ledger line rather than by editing history.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification. The first is that money is never mutated in place: the folio is an append-only ledger, and a credit, a rebill or a refund is always a new line whose sequence number continues the existing series, so that the guest's out-of-pocket position can always be reconciled against the lines themselves. The second is that compliance authority is separated from compliance authorship: the revenue manager who writes a fee program is not the compliance officer who attests it, the agent who investigates a complaint is not the officer who adjudicates it, and the liaison who drafts a regulator response is not the one who submits it. Both commitments are enforced by the API, not by convention.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the logical-clock read carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); a header whose value is only whitespace counts as empty for this purpose. If `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the seven role names defined below, the rejection is `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401); the comparison against the seven names is exact and case-sensitive, so `platformadmin` or `PLATFORMADMIN` is an unknown role rather than a `PlatformAdmin`, and a role name padded with whitespace is likewise unknown. `X-User-ID` is otherwise taken as sent and is recorded and compared by exact string equality. If the role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the addressed endpoint in the §4 table, the rejection is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). The single exception to all three rules is the logical-clock read (`GET /_clock`), which is deliberately unauthenticated so that a test harness can establish the current instant before assuming any identity.

The seven roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `RevenueManager`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `RegulatoryLiaison`, `ChannelOperator`, `GuestServicesAgent` and `Guest`.

A `PlatformAdmin` operates the multi-property platform. It owns the reference data every property depends on — jurisdictions, channels and properties — it drives the logical clock, and it stands in for the regulator's own moves on a docket: ordering remediation, rejecting a remediation submission, accepting one, and withdrawing the docket. A `RevenueManager` authors fee programs, adds fees and inclusion analysis to them, submits them for review, configures disclosure profiles, repairs flagged booking paths and runs roll-in revenue models; it can never attest a program. A `ComplianceOfficer` is the attesting authority: it publishes fee programs, audits deployed profiles, confirms remediations and adjudicates guest complaints, and it is also permitted to author programs and add fees — which is precisely what makes the segregation-of-duties check on publication reachable. A `RegulatoryLiaison` handles the operator's side of a regulator docket: recording the inquiry, drafting and submitting the response, and reporting completed remediation. A `ChannelOperator` is a distribution channel acting on its own behalf. A `GuestServicesAgent` runs the front desk and the folio: check-in, check-out, itemisation, posting, closing, opt-out decisions and refund payment; it never adjudicates a complaint and never attests a program. A `Guest` is the staying guest, able to read its own reservation documents and to raise an opt-out request or a complaint against its own folio.

Five of the seven roles are opaque operator identities. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `RevenueManager`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `RegulatoryLiaison` or `GuestServicesAgent`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition says so — as `authored_by_user_id` and `attested_by_user_id` on a fee program, as `investigated_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a complaint, and as `drafted_by_user_id` and `submitted_by_user_id` on a regulator inquiry — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

The remaining two roles are entity-backed and carry ownership scoping. When `X-User-Role` is `ChannelOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Channel`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Channel` compare the resource's `channel_id` field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `ChannelOperator` whose `X-User-ID` is not an existing `Channel` identifier, or which addresses a disclosure profile or booking whose `channel_id` differs from `X-User-ID`, is rejected with `ERR_CHANNEL_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403). That check guards exactly six operations: creating a channel disclosure profile, deploying one, remediating one, creating a booking, reading a single disclosure profile, and reading a single booking.

When `X-User-Role` is `Guest`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Booking`; ownership checks compare the addressed resource's owning booking to `X-User-ID`. A `Guest` whose `X-User-ID` is not an existing `Booking` identifier, or which addresses a booking, folio, folio line, opt-out request or complaint that does not resolve to that same booking, is rejected with `ERR_BOOKING_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403). That check guards exactly nine operations: reading a single booking, listing the folios of a booking, reading a single folio, reading a single folio line, listing folio lines, creating an opt-out request under a booking, creating a guest complaint under a booking, reading a single opt-out request, and reading a single complaint.

Beyond role membership and ownership, three duty separations are enforced on `X-User-ID` rather than on role, and all three carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The officer who publishes a fee program must not be the user recorded in that program's `authored_by_user_id`. The officer who substantiates or dismisses a complaint must not be the user recorded in that complaint's `investigated_by_user_id`. The liaison who submits an inquiry response must not be the user recorded in that inquiry's `drafted_by_user_id`. Those four operations — publish, substantiate, dismiss, submit-response — are the complete set guarded by this rule.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the only timestamp in the system taken from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, because it describes when the request was served rather than any modelled business instant; it is therefore never equal to the clock reading and never asserted against it.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- The same envelope is also used for the four transport-level failures that sit outside the §5 business catalogue: a request for a path this document does not define answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` at HTTP 404; a request using a method the addressed path does not serve answers `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` at HTTP 405; a request the web layer itself rejects as malformed before any handler runs answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 422 — a body that is not parseable JSON is reported this way rather than as HTTP 400, so 400 is a status this API never returns — while any other web-layer rejection answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at that layer's own status; and an unexpected server fault answers `ERR_INTERNAL` at HTTP 500. Any `error_id` outside the §5 catalogue that a handler raises without a catalogued status is answered at HTTP 422. Trailing slashes are not significant: `GET /jurisdictions/` and `GET /jurisdictions` address the same endpoint.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A `limit` or `offset` that is present but not a base-ten integer — an empty value, a decimal, or any non-numeric text — is rejected with the same `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, whereas a parameter omitted altogether simply takes its default. An `offset` at or beyond the end of the filtered result set is legal and yields an empty `data` array with `total_count` still reporting the full filtered size.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` object, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered result set before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true when further rows follow the current window. Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with `id` ascending as the tiebreaker, so pagination remains stable under insertion. The one endpoint that departs from this is the folio-line list, which is ordered by `folio_id` and then by `sequence_no` ascending, because a folio's ledger only makes sense read in append order.

Filter parameters on a list endpoint are ANDed together, and any filter naming a value that no row matches yields an empty array with a `total_count` of 0 rather than an error. Every identifier, code and enum filter is an exact, case-sensitive comparison of the raw query value against the stored field, so no prefix, substring or case-insensitive matching is available anywhere. A boolean filter accepts `true`, `false`, `1` and `0`, case-insensitively and ignoring surrounding whitespace; any other value for a boolean filter is not a malformed request but a value nothing matches, and therefore yields the same empty array with `total_count` 0. A filter parameter that is omitted is not applied at all; an unrecognised query parameter is ignored. Filtering and ordering are applied before the page window, so `total_count` always reflects the filtered set and never the whole table.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock as the Sole Time Source

Every temporal rule in this system reads a single server-side logical clock and nothing else. Fee-program effective windows, disclosure-profile cutover buffers, opt-out windows, regulator response deadlines, arrival and departure gating, and every `created_at`, `updated_at`, `published_at`, `posted_at`, `decided_at` and `last_audited_at` stamp are all derived from it. At startup the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must not fall back to real wall-clock time before it has been moved explicitly. A `PlatformAdmin` moves it by supplying a required `to_iso8601` instant, which may be earlier or later than the current reading; the response reports the new instant as `current_utc` at HTTP 200, and the clock read reports the same single field. Every instant this API accepts — `to_iso8601` and every body timestamp — is parsed the same way: a trailing `Z` in either case and an explicit numeric UTC offset are both accepted and the value is normalised to UTC, and a value carrying no zone at all is read as already being UTC. A value that is not a string, or a string no ISO 8601 reading can parse, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at rank 7. Instants are stored to microsecond precision but always rendered with exactly three fractional digits, so a supplied instant reads back truncated to milliseconds. Moving the clock recomputes nothing: no stored row is re-derived, and the new instant is simply what subsequent temporal checks read. Startup also leaves the system entirely empty alongside setting the clock: no jurisdiction, channel, property or any other row is seeded, so every list endpoint answers with an empty array until a caller creates something, and there is no route of any kind that empties or reloads the store. The whole API is built from `GET` reads and `POST` creates and transitions only — nothing is ever updated in place through `PUT` or `PATCH` and nothing is ever removed through `DELETE`, so a request using one of those methods against a defined path is the `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` of §2.2.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Version Counters and Immutability

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp, refreshed on every mutation, and an integer `version` that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `Jurisdiction`, `TaxabilityRule`, `Property`, `Channel`, `FeeProgram`, `Fee`, `FeeInclusion`, `ChannelDisclosureProfile`, `Booking`, `Folio`, `OptOutRequest`, `GuestComplaint` and `RegulatorInquiry` all carry the full quartet. On creation `updated_at` is stamped from the same clock reading as `created_at` and therefore reads identical to it until the first mutation. Every serialised resource opens with these system fields in a fixed order — `id`, `created_at`, then `updated_at` and `version` where the entity carries them — followed by the entity's own fields in the order §3 introduces them. Any timestamp field that is not yet set, and any optional scalar not yet supplied, is rendered as JSON `null` rather than omitted, so a resource's key set is the same at every point in its life. Nothing else is exposed: the insertion counter the server keeps for deterministic internal ordering is never serialised, and neither is any concurrency token supplied on input.

Two entities are deliberately leaner. A `FolioLine` carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, because a ledger line is written once and never edited — an adjustment appends a compensating line instead. A `RevenueImpactModel` likewise carries only `id` and `created_at`: it is an immutable projection with no update endpoint, and a changed assumption means a new row rather than a revised one.

Where a computed counter or aggregate lives on a parent row, a write that changes it counts as a mutation of that parent: the parent's `version` increments and its `updated_at` is refreshed. This is why publishing a fee program bumps the `version` of the owning `Property`, and why substantiating a complaint bumps the `version` of the complaint, its booking, the booking's disclosure profile and the property behind it.

### 2.6. Nested Path Resolution and the Global Validation Precedence Ladder

Identifiers embedded in a URL are resolved strictly left to right, and the leftmost missing segment determines the response. Adding a fee under a fee-program identifier that does not exist returns `ERR_FEE_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) and the request body is never inspected at all. The full set of not-found codes is `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TAXABILITY_RULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FEE_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FOLIO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FOLIO_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPT_OUT_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INQUIRY_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_REVENUE_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`, each at HTTP 404.

Validation precedence across the whole API is global and total: when one request violates several rules, the response carries the error of the highest-precedence violated rule and no lower-precedence check is evaluated at all. No two rules share a rank, so the outcome is never ambiguous. The ladder, from first evaluated to last, is:

1. Header presence — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role recognition — `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`.
3. Role authorisation for the endpoint — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
4. `ChannelOperator` channel ownership — `ERR_CHANNEL_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`.
5. `Guest` booking ownership — `ERR_BOOKING_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`.
6. Existence of every identifier in the path, left to right — the not-found family.
7. Request shape: required fields present and non-null, enum values among their declared literals, numeric ranges, date orderings, and the conditional field pairings described in §3 — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). Concretely this rank checks that every basis-points field lies between 0 and 10000 inclusive except where §3 narrows the range for a named field, as it does for a jurisdiction's `occupancy_tax_rate_bps` and a taxability rule's `tax_rate_bps` (0 to 5000 inclusive) and for a fee's `percent_bps` (1 to 10000 inclusive); that `amount_cents`, `retail_value_cents`, `base_room_rate_cents` and `room_rate_cents` are at least 0; that `nights`, `guests` and `units` are at least 1; that `opt_out_window_hours` is at least 0; that `effective_to` is strictly after `effective_from`; that `deployed_to` is strictly after `deployed_from`; that `response_due_at` is strictly after the current logical clock; that `amount_cents` is present and `percent_bps` absent for any basis other than `PERCENT_OF_ROOM` and the reverse for `PERCENT_OF_ROOM`; that `opt_out_window_hours` is supplied only when `avoidable` is true; that `disclosed_fee_amounts` is an object whose values are integers of at least 0; that `channel_scope` and `roll_in_fee_codes` are duplicate-free arrays of strings; and that `refund_amount_cents` is at least 1. Four conventions govern this rank uniformly. First, a field explicitly sent as JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted: for a required field that is the same `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` a missing field earns, and for an optional field the field's documented default applies rather than a stored null. Second, no type coercion is ever performed — a declared integer rejects `"5"` and rejects `true` and `false`, a declared boolean rejects `1` and `"true"`, a declared enum rejects any non-string and any string outside its literals, and a declared identifier rejects any non-string and any empty or whitespace-only string. Third, an absent request body, an empty body and a body of literal `null` all read as the empty JSON object, so the response is whatever the endpoint's first required-field check yields; a body that parses to anything other than an object — an array, a number, a string — is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Fourth, a body field this document does not declare for the endpoint is ignored rather than rejected.
8. Existence of every identifier supplied in the request body — `jurisdiction_id`, `property_id`, `channel_id`, `fee_program_id`, `supersedes_program_id`, `channel_disclosure_profile_id`, `fee_id` and `folio_line_id` — checked in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, again yielding the matching not-found code. This rank guards the creation of taxability rules, properties, fee programs, disclosure profiles, bookings, opt-out requests, guest complaints, regulator inquiries and revenue impact models.
9. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
10. Stale `Property` version on fee-program creation — `ERR_STALE_PROPERTY_VERSION`.
11. Stale `FeeProgram` version on disclosure-profile creation — `ERR_STALE_FEE_PROGRAM_VERSION`.
12. Target-state admissibility for the requested transition, including terminal-state lockdown — `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).
13. Folio adjustability for a compensating line — `ERR_FOLIO_NOT_ADJUSTABLE`.
14. Fee-program publication status where one is referenced — `ERR_FEE_PROGRAM_NOT_PUBLISHED`.
15. Disclosure-profile bookability — `ERR_PROFILE_NOT_BOOKABLE`.
16. Property operating status — `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_ACTIVE`.
17. Property disclosure posture freeze — `ERR_PROPERTY_DISCLOSURE_FROZEN`.
18. Regulator response window — `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.
19. Jurisdictional mandatory-fee ban — `ERR_MANDATORY_FEE_BANNED_IN_JURISDICTION`.
20. Jurisdictional total-price-display mandate — `ERR_TOTAL_PRICE_DISPLAY_REQUIRED`.
21. Channel capability mismatch — `ERR_CHANNEL_CAPABILITY_MISMATCH`.
22. Mandatory fee without a value-bearing inclusion — `ERR_MANDATORY_FEE_WITHOUT_INCLUSION`.
23. Missing taxability rule — `ERR_TAXABILITY_RULE_MISSING`.
24. Opt-out fee not on the booking's pinned program — `ERR_FEE_NOT_ON_BOOKING_PROGRAM`.
25. Complained-of folio line not on the booking — `ERR_FOLIO_LINE_NOT_ON_BOOKING`.
26. Uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_FEE_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_JURISDICTION_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_TAXABILITY_RULE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOCKET_NUMBER` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_INCLUSION_CODE`, all at HTTP 409.
27. Fee-program effective-interval overlap — `ERR_FEE_PROGRAM_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.
28. Disclosure-profile cutover-buffer collision — `ERR_CUTOVER_BUFFER_VIOLATION`.
29. Effective date in the past at publication — `ERR_EFFECTIVE_FROM_IN_PAST`.
30. Duplicate live opt-out for the same fee — `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPT_OUT`.
31. Fee not avoidable — `ERR_FEE_NOT_AVOIDABLE`.
32. Opt-out window closed — `ERR_OPT_OUT_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
33. Roll-in code that is not a mandatory fee — `ERR_ROLL_IN_FEE_NOT_MANDATORY`.
34. Roll-in set inconsistent with the declared scenario — `ERR_ROLL_IN_SCENARIO_MISMATCH`.
35. Empty or malformed inquiry channel scope — `ERR_INQUIRY_SCOPE_EMPTY`.
36. No refund entitlement — `ERR_NO_REFUND_ENTITLEMENT`.
37. Refund above entitlement — `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_ENTITLEMENT`.

40. Remediation incomplete at submission — `ERR_REMEDIATION_INCOMPLETE`.
41. Empty fee program at review submission — `ERR_FEE_PROGRAM_EMPTY`.
42. Arrival not reached at check-in — `ERR_ARRIVAL_NOT_REACHED`.
43. Departure not reached at check-out — `ERR_DEPARTURE_NOT_REACHED`.
44. Missing fee line on the folio for a credit or rebill — `ERR_FEE_LINE_NOT_ON_FOLIO`.

The ordering rationale is worth stating because it is observable: authentication precedes authorisation, authorisation precedes path existence, path existence precedes request shape, request shape precedes body-reference existence, duty separation and concurrency precede any state test, state tests precede business rules, and inside the business band the order runs safety and compliance, then referential completeness, then uniqueness and temporal conflicts, then fiscal arithmetic. A jurisdiction that forbids mandatory fees outright makes the inclusion question moot, which is why the ban outranks inclusion completeness; a refund can only be mis-sized once everything else about the complaint is well-formed, which is why refund arithmetic is last.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Configuration Parents

Two creation endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the `version` of a parent row, and they are the only two.

`POST /fee-programs` requires a non-optional `expected_property_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Property` named by `property_id`. If that value does not equal the property's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROPERTY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful create increments `Property.version` by exactly 1, so a second create replayed against the same read version is rejected. The rule exists because a writer holding a stale view of the property may believe a different fee inventory is in force, and may be about to author a program that supersedes something it has never seen.

`POST /channel-disclosure-profiles` requires a non-optional `expected_fee_program_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `FeeProgram` named by `fee_program_id`. If that value does not equal the program's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FEE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful create increments `FeeProgram.version` by exactly 1. The rule exists because deploying a profile legitimately retires the prior profile for the same property and channel, so a writer holding a stale view of the fee program may be about to retire a booking path it has never observed.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field. In particular, none of the state-transition endpoints, none of the fee, inclusion, booking, opt-out, complaint, inquiry or revenue-model creates, and none of the reads accept or require one.

### 2.8. Cents, Basis Points and Rounding Order Discipline

Every monetary quantity in this system is an integer number of cents; there are no fractional-cent fields anywhere. Every tax and commission rate is expressed in basis points, so a rate of 1338 basis points means 13.38 percent, and converting a rate to a multiplier means dividing the basis points by 10000. Wherever a rounding is required, the method is round half up: the value is rounded to the nearest whole cent, and a value exactly halfway between two cents rounds away from zero.

What matters far more than the method is the **order** of roundings, and this system deliberately uses two different orders in two different places so that the difference is observable. When the guest is quoted, each mandatory fee's per-night value is rounded to whole cents first and the rounded per-night values are then multiplied by the number of nights; when the guest is billed, each fee is expanded across the whole stay unrounded and rounded once at the stay level. Similarly, the tax on a quote is computed with a single rounding over the aggregated taxable base, whereas the tax on a folio is rounded independently on each line and the rounded line taxes are then summed. The two orders produce different integers whenever a percentage-based fee yields a fractional per-night value, and the difference between them is exactly what the booking's stored variance figure measures. Neither order is an approximation of the other and neither may be substituted for the other.

One ratio is not monetary: a fee program's inclusion coverage is a decimal with exactly four fractional digits, also rounded half up. One score is a plain bounded integer: a disclosure score is an integer between 0 and 100 inclusive, and the clamp to that range is applied exactly once, last, after every additive and subtractive term has been applied.

### 2.9. Cascade Propagation and Parent Recomputation

This system is aggressively denormalised: counts, sums, minima, scores and postures are stored on parent rows rather than recomputed on read, and a single write frequently has to walk several levels of the graph. The deepest chain runs four levels: a regulator ordering remediation flags the property's in-scope booking paths, each flagged path drags the property's minimum disclosure score down, the recomputed minimum re-derives the property's posture, and a posture that lands on non-compliant disputes the property's still-unaudited quotes. Every level of such a chain is mandatory, not best-effort: a write that performs the first cascade step and skips the third is wrong even though the immediate response looks correct.

Two of these chains have explicit compensating counterparts, and the compensation must undo exactly what the original did and nothing more. Withdrawing a regulator docket returns to service exactly the booking paths that docket itself flagged and that are still flagged, leaving any path flagged by another docket or by an audit untouched. Revoking a granted opt-out appends a mirror ledger line that restores the folio total to its exact pre-grant value with no drift. Where a compensating action fires, every counter the original action incremented must be decremented — it is not sufficient to merely stop incrementing.

### 2.10. Conservation Invariants

Two quantities are conserved at all times, and they are conserved over different entity sets.

The first is money on the folio. Reading the folio's own lines, the charge side is the sum of `amount_cents` plus `tax_cents` over every line whose `line_type` is `ROOM`, `MANDATORY_FEE`, `OPTIONAL_FEE` or `FEE_REBILL`, and the credit side is the sum of `amount_cents` minus `tax_cents` over every line whose `line_type` is `FEE_CREDIT` or `COMPLAINT_REFUND` — credit lines store a positive amount and a negative tax, so subtracting their tax adds it back into the credit side. The booking's `guest_out_of_pocket_cents` must always equal the charge side minus the credit side, and must always equal the booking's `folio_total_cents`. The folio's own `folio_total_cents` must always equal `charges_subtotal_cents` minus `credits_total_cents` plus `tax_total_cents`. And the folio's `line_count` must always equal the number of lines it holds, with every `sequence_no` from 1 up to `line_count` appearing exactly once. These four equalities are exact: no tolerance whatsoever is permitted on them, and they are asserted after itemisation, after posting, after an opt-out grant, after an opt-out revocation, after a complaint refund and after closing. Their practical effect is a prohibition: an implementation may not satisfy a total by editing the original charge line in place, because that would destroy the audit trail and break the contiguous sequence requirement.

The second conserved quantity is a cardinality rather than money — the count of open compliance obligations a property carries. A property's `open_disclosure_obligation_count` must always equal the number of its disclosure profiles in `FLAGGED` status, plus the number of its bookings whose `disclosure_status` is `DISPUTED` or `UNDISCLOSED_CONFIRMED`, plus the number of complaints on **any** of its bookings — not merely on the gap-bearing ones — whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` or `SUBSTANTIATED`, so a live complaint raised against a booking still standing at `PENDING_AUDIT` or `DISCLOSED` is itself an open obligation and is counted, plus the number of regulator inquiries on the property whose status is anything other than `ACCEPTED` or `WITHDRAWN`. Alongside it, a property's `aggregate_disclosure_gap_cents` must always equal the sum of `quote_disclosure_gap_cents` over its bookings whose `disclosure_status` is `DISPUTED` or `UNDISCLOSED_CONFIRMED`; a profile's `confirmed_undisclosed_count` must always equal the number of complaints in `SUBSTANTIATED` or `REFUNDED` status whose booking was taken through that profile **and which were substantiated since that path's most recent remediation**, because remediation deliberately resets the counter to 0 (§3.3) while leaving every one of those complaints exactly where it stands; a property's `published_fee_program_count` must always equal the number of its fee programs in `PUBLISHED` status, and that count is never greater than 1 for any single instant covered by the programs' effective windows; and every profile's stored `disclosure_score` must equal the score recomputed from scratch, so the denormalisation never drifts from the formula.

The compensating direction is where these invariants bite hardest. Withdrawing a regulator inquiry decrements the obligation count by one for the closed docket plus one for each restored booking path, and restores the property's posture to whatever the recomputed minimum score dictates. Refunding a complaint moves the booking to `CURED`, which removes that booking's gap from the property's aggregate gap, while deliberately leaving the profile's `confirmed_undisclosed_count` intact — a substantiated disclosure failure is never erased by paying for it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Jurisdictional Mandates, Taxability Determination and the Property Estate

Everything in this system hangs off a jurisdiction. Registering one is a `PlatformAdmin` operation and it captures, in a single payload, both the taxing posture and the disclosure posture of an authority. The `code` is the stable machine identifier — something like `US-NV-CLARK` — and it must be between 3 and 32 characters long, composed only of uppercase letters, digits and hyphens, and globally unique; a second jurisdiction offered the same `code` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_JURISDICTION_CODE`. The human-readable `name` is between 1 and 120 characters. The `occupancy_tax_rate_bps` is the default transient-lodging tax rate in basis points applied to taxable amounts, and it must lie between 0 and 5000 inclusive — that is, no jurisdiction may declare a default occupancy tax above 50 percent.

Four boolean mandate flags follow, and they are the levers that make one jurisdiction stricter than another. The first two, `first_quote_total_required` and `itemization_required_on_folio`, are required on input and have no default, so a payload omitting either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the second two are optional as noted below. When `first_quote_total_required` is true, a booking channel must display the all-in total — room plus mandatory fees plus tax — at the first quote the shopper sees. When `itemization_required_on_folio` is true, every mandatory fee must appear as its own line at itemisation. When `mandatory_fee_ban` is true, no fee program carrying any fee classified `MANDATORY` may be published for a property in that jurisdiction at all; this flag is optional on input and defaults to false. When `opt_out_mandate` is true, the jurisdiction declares that every `MANDATORY` fee bundling avoidable services must be marked as avoidable and must carry a stated opt-out window; this flag is likewise optional and defaults to false, and it is recorded as jurisdictional policy on the row rather than being re-derived anywhere. Jurisdictions can be listed with an optional exact-match `code` filter and an optional `mandatory_fee_ban` filter.

Taxability is decided per fee code, per jurisdiction, and never globally. A taxability rule names the `jurisdiction_id` it governs and the `fee_code` it applies to; matching against a fee's own code is exact and case-sensitive, the code is between 1 and 32 characters with no character-class restriction of its own — the rule may be declared for any code shape, and it is the fee's own stricter code format that decides what will ever join to it — and the pair of jurisdiction and fee code is unique across the whole rule set — a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TAXABILITY_RULE`. The `taxability` value is one of three literals with genuinely different downstream behaviour. `TAXABLE` means the charge is always taxed. `EXEMPT` means it is never taxed. `TAXABLE_IF_MANDATORY` means it is taxed only while the charge is a mandatory fee on that booking: once the guest is granted an opt-out the charge stops being mandatory and the tax collected on it is credited back in full. An optional `tax_rate_bps` overrides the jurisdiction's default for that fee code; it defaults to null, and when null the jurisdiction's `occupancy_tax_rate_bps` applies. When supplied it must itself lie between 0 and 5000 inclusive. An explicitly supplied `0` is a genuine zero-rate override and is not the same as null: it holds the rate at zero for that fee code no matter what the jurisdiction's default is, whereas an omitted or explicitly null value falls back to that default. Either a `PlatformAdmin` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may declare a rule, and rules are immutable and undeletable — there is no edit and no delete route — which is what guarantees that a published program can never later lose its taxability coverage. Rules are listable with optional `jurisdiction_id`, `fee_code` and `taxability` filters.

A property is created by a `PlatformAdmin` inside exactly one jurisdiction. Its payload carries the `jurisdiction_id` whose mandates and tax rates govern it, a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `brand_code` of 1 to 16 characters used on guest-facing documents, and a `base_room_rate_cents` reference nightly rate of at least 0 cents, which serves as the modelling baseline for fee-program rollups and revenue projections. An optional `status` is one of `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and defaults to `ACTIVE`; it is fixed at creation time, has no transition endpoint, and consequently a property that starts `ACTIVE` stays `ACTIVE` for its whole life.

Five further fields on a property are computed and never supplied by the caller, and they are the whole of the property row's denormalisation. A freshly created property reads with `min_channel_disclosure_score` null, `disclosure_posture` `COMPLIANT`, `open_disclosure_obligation_count` 0, `published_fee_program_count` 0 and `aggregate_disclosure_gap_cents` 0 — the posture is compliant precisely because the candidate set of booking paths is empty, and a null minimum score is the honest reading of an empty set. `disclosure_posture` is one of `COMPLIANT`, `AT_RISK` or `NON_COMPLIANT`, and a `NON_COMPLIANT` posture is what freezes fee-program publication. `published_fee_program_count` is the number of the property's fee programs in `PUBLISHED` status. Properties are listable with optional `jurisdiction_id`, `status` and `disclosure_posture` filters.

Alongside the plain property read, a computed disclosure-summary capability re-derives the property's live compliance position on read from its profiles, bookings, complaints and inquiries rather than reporting the stored denormalisation, and it is restricted to `PlatformAdmin`, `RevenueManager`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `RegulatoryLiaison`. It returns `property_id`, `disclosure_posture`, `min_channel_disclosure_score`, `open_disclosure_obligation_count`, `deployed_profile_count` (the number of the property's profiles currently in `DEPLOYED`), `flagged_profile_count` (the number currently in `FLAGGED`), `published_fee_program_count`, `total_mandatory_fee_cents_per_night` (the per-night mandatory burden carried by the property's `PUBLISHED` fee programs, summed across them, and 0 when the property has none) and `aggregate_disclosure_gap_cents`, in exactly that order and with no other keys. It is a computed report rather than a resource read, so it is not a paginated list and it carries none of the identity quartet of §2.5 — no `id`, no `created_at`, no `updated_at` and no `version`; the property it describes is named by `property_id` instead. Reading it recomputes and reports but never writes: the stored denormalisation on the property row is left exactly as it was. Every field it reports that has a counterpart on the property row — `disclosure_posture`, `min_channel_disclosure_score`, `open_disclosure_obligation_count`, `published_fee_program_count` and `aggregate_disclosure_gap_cents` — must equal that stored denormalisation; that identity is precisely how the obligation-conservation invariant of §2.10 becomes observable from outside the system. The remaining three figures — `deployed_profile_count`, `flagged_profile_count` and `total_mandatory_fee_cents_per_night` — are summary-only derivations with no counterpart field on the property row, the burden figure being summed from the `total_mandatory_fee_cents_per_night` rollups of the property's `PUBLISHED` fee programs.

Channels are the other axis of the graph and are registered by a `PlatformAdmin`. A channel carries a display `name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `channel_type` drawn from `OWNED_WEB`, `OWNED_APP`, `OTA`, `GDS`, `METASEARCH` and `VOICE` — the same vocabulary a regulator uses to scope a docket. Two booleans declare what the channel is technically able to render: `supports_total_price_display` for an all-in total at first quote, and `supports_itemization` for a per-fee breakdown at first quote. Both are required on input and have neither a default nor an inference from `channel_type`, so omitting either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. These are capability facts, not policy choices, and a disclosure profile may never claim a display behaviour its channel cannot perform. A channel is pure reference data: it carries no status, no denormalised counter and no transition endpoint, so its four declared fields plus the identity quartet are the whole of its read shape, and a channel's capabilities are fixed for its whole life. Channels are listable with optional `channel_type`, `supports_total_price_display` and `supports_itemization` filters.

### 3.2. Assembling, Reviewing and Attesting the Mandatory Fee Inventory

A fee inventory is versioned, not edited. A `RevenueManager` (or a `ComplianceOfficer`, who is also permitted to author) opens a new inventory for a property by supplying the `property_id`, an operator-chosen `version_label` of 1 to 32 characters that must be unique among that property's programs, and the half-open effective interval `[effective_from, effective_to)` — `effective_from` inclusive, `effective_to` exclusive and strictly later than `effective_from`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits. Where the new inventory is meant to replace one already in force, the optional `supersedes_program_id` names it; this is a self-referential edge onto another fee program, it is left null for a property's first program, and the replacement is not effected at creation but at publication. The create also carries the `expected_property_version` concurrency token described in §2.7, and requires the property to be `ACTIVE` — a program cannot be opened against a `SUSPENDED` property, which is rejected with `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_ACTIVE`. The `version_label` uniqueness test is the very last check this endpoint performs — after both the concurrency token and the property-status test — and a repeat is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) rather than with any dedicated duplicate code, so a stale token or a suspended property is reported in preference to a duplicate label.

The new row lands in `DRAFT` status, which is one of `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `PUBLISHED` and `SUPERSEDED`. Its `authored_by_user_id` is stamped from the acting `X-User-ID` and is the anchor for the segregation-of-duties check that fires later at attestation; `attested_by_user_id`, `attestation_note` and `published_at` are all null until then. All four rollup fields start at their defaults: `fee_count` 0, `mandatory_fee_count` 0, `total_mandatory_fee_cents_per_night` 0 and `inclusion_coverage_ratio` 0.0000. Programs are listable with optional `property_id`, `supersedes_program_id` and `status` filters.

Fees are appended to a draft program one at a time. Each fee carries a `code` of 1 to 32 characters composed of uppercase letters, digits and underscores, unique within its own program — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FEE_CODE` — and that code is the join key against both the taxability rules of the property's jurisdiction and the channel's disclosure attestation on a booking. The guest-facing `label` is 1 to 120 characters. The `disclosure_text` between 8 and 500 characters is the exact sentence a channel must render for this fee in booking flows, in confirmations and on check-in documents, and it is also the text copied onto the guest's folio line at itemisation. Each fee reads back with two computed inclusion rollups, `inclusion_count` and `inclusion_value_cents`, both starting at 0. Appending a fee immediately refreshes the parent program's `fee_count` and `mandatory_fee_count` from the program's current fee rows and counts as a mutation of the program per §2.5, so those two counters are live denormalisations on a `DRAFT` program rather than figures that wait for review. Fees are listable with optional `fee_program_id`, `classification`, `basis` and `avoidable` filters, and the fee read is one of the few configuration reads a `Guest` is permitted.

The `classification` of a fee is one of `MANDATORY`, `OPTIONAL` or `CONDITIONAL`, and the distinction drives almost every downstream calculation. A `MANDATORY` fee is charged on every stay and is therefore part of the all-in total that a total-price-display channel must show. An `OPTIONAL` fee is charged only when the guest elects the service. A `CONDITIONAL` fee is charged only when some stay attribute triggers it; for total-price display it is treated exactly as `OPTIONAL` is, and it is never itemised automatically. Only `MANDATORY` fees enter the quoted mandatory subtotal, the taxable base at quote time, the disclosure-gap computation, the itemised folio, the program's per-night burden, its inclusion coverage and the revenue roll-in model.

The `basis` of a fee is one of `PER_NIGHT`, `PER_STAY`, `PER_PERSON_PER_NIGHT` or `PERCENT_OF_ROOM`, and it decides how the fee's stored magnitude expands into a charge. For the first three bases the magnitude lives in `amount_cents`, which is required and must be at least 0, and `percent_bps` must be null. For `PERCENT_OF_ROOM` the magnitude lives in `percent_bps`, which is required and must lie between 1 and 10000 inclusive, and `amount_cents` must be null. Getting that pairing wrong is a shape error and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The expansion itself is used in four places and is always the same: against a given nightly rate and guest count, a `PER_NIGHT` fee's unrounded per-night value is its `amount_cents`; a `PER_PERSON_PER_NIGHT` fee's is its `amount_cents` multiplied by the guest count; a `PERCENT_OF_ROOM` fee's is the nightly rate multiplied by `percent_bps` and divided by 10000; and a `PER_STAY` fee has no per-night value at all. To get a whole-stay value, a `PER_STAY` fee contributes its `amount_cents` unchanged and every other fee contributes its per-night value multiplied by the number of nights. Both expansions are carried as exact rational quantities: no rounding happens inside the expansion, and every caller pins its own rounding order, which is why the quote and the folio can legitimately disagree by a cent or two.

Avoidability is declared per fee. The boolean `avoidable` defaults to false and states whether a guest may decline the fee through an opt-out request. When `avoidable` is true, `opt_out_window_hours` is required and states the minimum number of whole hours before the booking's arrival by which the request must be lodged; it must lie between 0 and 720 inclusive — that is, no window may reach further back than thirty days. When `avoidable` is false, `opt_out_window_hours` must be null, and supplying one anyway is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The inclusion analysis is the evidence base that justifies charging a fee at all. A `RevenueManager` or `ComplianceOfficer` records one bundled service at a time against a fee: a `service_code` of 1 to 32 characters, unique per fee, with a repeat rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_INCLUSION_CODE`; a guest-facing `service_label` of 1 to 120 characters; a `retail_value_cents` standalone value of at least 0 cents for one unit of the service; and an optional `units` count of bundled units per stay-night, which defaults to 1 and must lie between 1 and 100 inclusive. The row's `included_value_cents` is computed at creation as `retail_value_cents` multiplied by `units`, an exact integer product with no rounding. Recording an inclusion immediately refreshes the parent fee's `inclusion_count` to the number of its inclusions and its `inclusion_value_cents` to the sum of their `included_value_cents`; there is no status on an inclusion, so no state filter applies to that aggregation. Recording an inclusion requires the fee's owning program to still be in `DRAFT`: an inclusion offered against a fee whose program has already reached `UNDER_REVIEW`, `PUBLISHED` or `SUPERSEDED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and that state test is evaluated before the `service_code` uniqueness test. What recording an inclusion does *not* do is touch the program at all — neither its `inclusion_coverage_ratio` nor its `version` moves, because coverage is computed once, at publication, from the inclusions then in force. Listing a fee's inclusions is the only discovery route for inclusion rows.

Submitting the inventory for review moves it from `DRAFT` to `UNDER_REVIEW` and may be done by a `RevenueManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer`. It requires the program to be in `DRAFT`, the property to be `ACTIVE`, and the program to hold at least one fee of any classification; an empty program is rejected with `ERR_FEE_PROGRAM_EMPTY` (HTTP 409). The transition refreshes `fee_count` and `mandatory_fee_count` from the program's current fee rows and increments the program's `version`.

Attestation is the compliance event, and it is reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer` alone. The checks run in the global order of §2.6, which for this operation means: first the attesting `X-User-ID` must differ from `authored_by_user_id`, or the request fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; only then is the program's status examined, and a program not in `UNDER_REVIEW` fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the property must be `ACTIVE`, failing with `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_ACTIVE`; the property's `disclosure_posture` must not be `NON_COMPLIANT`, failing with `ERR_PROPERTY_DISCLOSURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409) — the operator must repair its flagged booking paths before it may publish anything new; if the property's jurisdiction has `mandatory_fee_ban` true and the program holds at least one `MANDATORY` fee, the attempt fails with `ERR_MANDATORY_FEE_BANNED_IN_JURISDICTION` (HTTP 422); every `MANDATORY` fee in the program must carry at least one inclusion whose `included_value_cents` is greater than 0, because a mandatory fee must buy the guest something, failing with `ERR_MANDATORY_FEE_WITHOUT_INCLUSION`; a taxability rule must exist for the pair of the property's `jurisdiction_id` and the fee's `code` for every `MANDATORY` fee of the program, failing with `ERR_TAXABILITY_RULE_MISSING`; the program's half-open effective interval must not overlap the interval of any other `PUBLISHED` program of the same property, excluding the program named by `supersedes_program_id`, failing with `ERR_FEE_PROGRAM_PERIOD_OVERLAP`; and `effective_from` must be at or after the current logical clock, since a program cannot be published into the past, failing with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_FROM_IN_PAST`. Where `supersedes_program_id` is non-null, the named program must exist (`ERR_FEE_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`), must belong to the same property (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`) and must itself be in `PUBLISHED` status (`ERR_FEE_PROGRAM_NOT_PUBLISHED`); despite being stated last here, all three of those tests sit together at rank 14 and are therefore evaluated immediately after the program's own status test and before every one of the property-status, posture-freeze, fee-ban, inclusion-completeness, taxability, overlap and effective-date tests enumerated above them.

The overlap test is worth stating exactly, because touching intervals are legal. Two programs collide when the first's `effective_from` is strictly before the second's `effective_to` **and** the second's `effective_from` is strictly before the first's `effective_to`. A program that ends at precisely the instant the next begins therefore does not collide, and leaves no gap in coverage.

Publication carries a required `attestation_note` string in the request body, which is stored verbatim on the program; it is bounded neither below nor above, so the empty string is accepted while a missing or null note is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On success the program moves `UNDER_REVIEW → PUBLISHED`, its `attested_by_user_id` is set from the acting `X-User-ID` and its `published_at` is set to the current logical clock. This is also the single moment at which the two heavier rollups leave their defaults. `mandatory_fee_count` is refreshed to the number of `MANDATORY` fees. `total_mandatory_fee_cents_per_night` becomes a reference per-night mandatory burden, computed against the *property's* `base_room_rate_cents` rather than any booking's rate, at a reference occupancy of two guests and over a reference stay of four nights, walking the mandatory fees in ascending `created_at` order and using the per-night rounding order: a `PER_STAY` fee contributes its `amount_cents` divided by the four reference nights and rounded half up to whole cents, and every other fee contributes its unrounded per-night value rounded half up to whole cents; those rounded contributions are then summed, and a program that holds no `MANDATORY` fee at all — legal, since review admits a program of purely optional fees — sums to 0. `inclusion_coverage_ratio` becomes the fraction of `MANDATORY` fees that carry at least one inclusion with `included_value_cents` greater than 0, expressed as a decimal with exactly four fractional digits and rounded half up; when `mandatory_fee_count` is 0 the ratio is 0.0000 by convention, never 1.0000 and never a division by zero. For illustration, a property with a `base_room_rate_cents` of 29900 and three mandatory fees — one `PERCENT_OF_ROOM` at 750 basis points, one `PER_NIGHT` of 1495 cents and one `PER_PERSON_PER_NIGHT` of 325 cents — yields per-night contributions of 2243, 1495 and 650 cents and therefore a `total_mandatory_fee_cents_per_night` of 4388, a `mandatory_fee_count` of 3, and, with one value-bearing inclusion on each, an `inclusion_coverage_ratio` of 1.0000.

Publication is also the head of the supersession cascade, and every link of it must fire. First, when `supersedes_program_id` is non-null, that predecessor program transitions `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED` and its `version` increments. Second, every disclosure profile bound to the superseded program whose status is `DEPLOYED`, `FLAGGED` or `REMEDIATED` transitions to `RETIRED`, each incrementing its own `version`. Third, because a retired profile leaves the candidate set that determines the property's minimum score, the property's `min_channel_disclosure_score` and `disclosure_posture` are recomputed. Fourth, because retired profiles no longer count as flagged, the property's `open_disclosure_obligation_count` is recomputed as well. Alongside the cascade, the property's `published_fee_program_count`, `open_disclosure_obligation_count` and `aggregate_disclosure_gap_cents` are recomputed, the property's `version` increments by 1, and the newly published program's own `version` increments by 1. That single property recomputation and that single property `version` increment happen on **every** successful publication, including one whose `supersedes_program_id` is null and which therefore retires nothing at all. Bookings already pinned to the superseded program keep pointing at it: supersession never re-points a live stay, so a guest who booked under last season's inventory is billed under last season's inventory.

### 3.3. Booking Paths: Disclosure Profiles, Scoring, Deployment and Remediation

A **disclosure profile** is the three-way junction that records what a specific booking path actually shows a shopper. It binds a `property_id`, a `channel_id` and a `fee_program_id` together, and it is the entity a regulator ultimately remediates. Either a `RevenueManager` or a `ChannelOperator` may create one; when a `ChannelOperator` does so, the `channel_id` in the payload must be that operator's own channel identity, as described in §2.1. The referenced fee program must be in `PUBLISHED` status, and a draft, under-review or superseded program is rejected with `ERR_FEE_PROGRAM_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 409). The create also carries the `expected_fee_program_version` concurrency token of §2.7. The property's own operating status is deliberately **not** tested at creation, so a `DRAFT` path may be authored against a `SUSPENDED` property; the `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_ACTIVE` test bites only at deployment. Creation likewise fires no property recomputation, because a `DRAFT` path is outside the candidate set.

Three switches describe the path's behaviour. `first_quote_shows_total` states whether the all-in total — room plus mandatory fees plus tax — is rendered at first quote. `itemized_at_first_quote` states whether each mandatory fee is broken out by label and amount at first quote. `disclosure_placement` names the earliest point in the path at which the mandatory-fee disclosure text appears, and is one of `FIRST_QUOTE`, `RATE_DETAILS`, `CHECKOUT`, `CONFIRMATION_ONLY` or `ABSENT`. A profile may not claim a behaviour its channel cannot perform: claiming `first_quote_shows_total` true on a channel whose `supports_total_price_display` is false, or `itemized_at_first_quote` true on a channel whose `supports_itemization` is false, is rejected with `ERR_CHANNEL_CAPABILITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) at creation and again at remediation.

The deployment window is the closed interval from `deployed_from` to `deployed_to`, with `deployed_to` strictly after `deployed_from`. Windows on the same property and channel must be separated by a cutover buffer of a full day. Formally, the window is inflated by 24 hours on each side, and it collides with another profile's window when the first's `deployed_from` is earlier than the second's `deployed_to` advanced by 24 hours **and** the second's `deployed_from` is earlier than the first's `deployed_to` advanced by 24 hours. The comparison set is every other profile on the same `property_id` and `channel_id` whose status is `DEPLOYED`, `FLAGGED`, `REMEDIATED` or `RETIRED` — retired paths still reserve their historical window. Touching windows are therefore forbidden, as is any gap shorter than 24 hours; a gap of exactly 24 hours is the smallest legal one. A collision is rejected with `ERR_CUTOVER_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409), and the check runs twice: once when the profile is created and again when it is taken live.

A new profile lands in `DRAFT`, one of `DRAFT`, `DEPLOYED`, `FLAGGED`, `REMEDIATED` and `RETIRED`. Its `confirmed_undisclosed_count` and `remediation_round` both start at 0, its `last_audited_at` is null, and its `disclosure_score` is computed immediately at creation. Profiles are listable with optional `property_id`, `channel_id`, `fee_program_id`, `status` and `disclosure_placement` filters.

**How a path is scored.** The `disclosure_score` is an integer between 0 and 100 driven by three independent mechanisms — a pair of flat weights, a piecewise placement lookup, a floor-capped coverage bonus, and two penalties that behave differently from one another. Start from zero. Award 40 points when `first_quote_shows_total` is true, and a further 25 points when `itemized_at_first_quote` is true. Add the placement points for the profile's `disclosure_placement`: 25 for `FIRST_QUOTE`, 15 for `RATE_DETAILS`, 8 for `CHECKOUT`, 3 for `CONFIRMATION_ONLY` and 0 for `ABSENT`. Add a coverage bonus computed from the bound fee program's `inclusion_coverage_ratio`: multiply that ratio by 10, take the floor of the product, and cap the result at 10 — so a program whose mandatory fees are fully covered contributes the full 10 points and one covered half the time contributes 5.

Then subtract the capability penalty, which is the **maximum** of the applicable penalties rather than their sum. A channel that cannot display a total in a jurisdiction whose `first_quote_total_required` is true incurs 30; a channel that cannot itemise in a jurisdiction whose `itemization_required_on_folio` is true incurs 20; when both conditions hold the penalty is 30, not 50. Finally subtract 5 points for each confirmed undisclosed charge — that is, 5 multiplied by the profile's `confirmed_undisclosed_count`. Only after every additive and subtractive term has been applied is the result clamped, once, into the range 0 to 100 inclusive. To make the mechanics concrete against a jurisdiction with both mandates true and a program at full coverage: a capable channel showing a total, itemising, and placing disclosure at `FIRST_QUOTE` with no confirmed failures scores 40 plus 25 plus 25 plus 10, that is 100; the same channel showing a total but not itemising, placing disclosure at `RATE_DETAILS`, with one confirmed failure scores 40 plus 15 plus 10 less 5, that is exactly 60; a second confirmed failure takes it to 55; and a channel that can do neither, shows no total, does not itemise and places disclosure `ABSENT` scores 10 less 30, that is negative 20, clamped up to 0.

**How a property's posture follows from its paths.** The candidate set for a property's posture is every one of its profiles whose status is `DEPLOYED`, `FLAGGED` or `REMEDIATED`. Profiles in `DRAFT` or `RETIRED` are excluded entirely, which is why a draft path scoring 0 does not drag a property down. Within the candidate set each profile contributes an effective score: 0 if the profile is `FLAGGED`, regardless of its stored `disclosure_score`, because being flagged is itself a total disclosure failure, and its stored score otherwise. The property's `min_channel_disclosure_score` is the minimum of those effective scores. When the candidate set is empty the minimum is null and the posture is `COMPLIANT`. Otherwise the posture follows from the minimum by two strict comparisons: a minimum below 60 gives `NON_COMPLIANT`, a minimum below 80 gives `AT_RISK`, and anything else gives `COMPLIANT`. The thresholds are half-open on the low side, so a minimum of exactly 60 is `AT_RISK` while 59 is `NON_COMPLIANT`, and exactly 80 is `COMPLIANT` while 79 is `AT_RISK`. The same recomputation pass also refreshes the property's `published_fee_program_count`, its `open_disclosure_obligation_count` and its `aggregate_disclosure_gap_cents` per the definitions in §2.10. Posture is re-derived on profile creation-to-deployment, on audit, on remediation, on remediation confirmation, on fee-program publication, on complaint substantiation, on a regulator's remediation order and on a docket withdrawal.

**Taking a path live.** Deployment moves a profile `DRAFT → DEPLOYED` and may be performed by a `ComplianceOfficer` or by the owning `ChannelOperator` — an officer may deploy on the channel's behalf, but an operator may only deploy a profile whose `channel_id` matches its own identity. The profile must be in `DRAFT`, the property must be `ACTIVE`, and the bound fee program must still be `PUBLISHED`. Where the property's jurisdiction has `first_quote_total_required` true, the profile must have `first_quote_shows_total` true; a profile that hides the total is rejected with `ERR_TOTAL_PRICE_DISPLAY_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). A profile may perfectly legitimately be *drafted* with `first_quote_shows_total` false, but in such a jurisdiction it can never reach `DEPLOYED` or `REMEDIATED` — and because only deployed and remediated paths can take bookings, the quoting path never needs to re-check the mandate. The cutover-buffer test is repeated here against the same status set. On success the profile's `disclosure_score` is recomputed and its `last_audited_at` is stamped with the current logical clock; any other profile on the same property and channel that was `DEPLOYED` transitions `DEPLOYED → RETIRED`, incrementing its own `version`; the property's `min_channel_disclosure_score` and `disclosure_posture` are recomputed; and both the property's `version` and the profile's `version` increment by 1.

**Auditing.** A `ComplianceOfficer` may audit a profile in `DRAFT`, `DEPLOYED` or `REMEDIATED` status; a `FLAGGED` path — already known to be failing, and repairable only through remediation — and a terminal `RETIRED` path are both rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Auditing always recomputes the `disclosure_score` from current inputs and stamps `last_audited_at` with the logical clock. When the audited profile is `DEPLOYED` and the recomputed score is strictly below 60, the profile transitions `DEPLOYED → FLAGGED`; the property's `min_channel_disclosure_score` and `disclosure_posture` are recomputed with the newly flagged profile contributing 0, the property's `open_disclosure_obligation_count` is recomputed, and the property's and profile's `version` fields each increment by 1. When the recomputed score is at or above 60, a deployed profile stays `DEPLOYED` with only its score and `last_audited_at` refreshed. Auditing is also legal from `DRAFT` and from `REMEDIATED`, where it likewise only recomputes the score and the audit stamp and never changes status. Because every audit stamps `last_audited_at`, every audit increments the profile's own `version`; the property, by contrast, is re-derived on every audit but is only *touched* when the pass actually moves one of its five denormalised values, so an audit that rescores a candidate path from 90 to 75 changes the property's minimum and posture and therefore increments the property's `version`, while an audit that lands on the same score the path already carried, or an audit of a `DRAFT` path outside the candidate set, leaves the property row untouched.

**Repairing a flagged path.** Remediation moves a profile `FLAGGED → REMEDIATED` and may be performed by the owning `ChannelOperator` or by a `RevenueManager` acting on the channel's behalf. The request body restates all three switches — `first_quote_shows_total`, `itemized_at_first_quote` and `disclosure_placement` — and those new values overwrite the stored ones. Both guard rails are re-evaluated against the *new* values, not the old ones: the channel-capability rule yields `ERR_CHANNEL_CAPABILITY_MISMATCH`, and the jurisdiction's total-price mandate yields `ERR_TOTAL_PRICE_DISPLAY_REQUIRED`. On success the profile's `confirmed_undisclosed_count` is reset to 0 — the repaired path is not made to carry the sins of the path it replaced — its `remediation_round` increments by 1, its `disclosure_score` is recomputed from the new switches, and its `last_audited_at` is stamped. The property's `min_channel_disclosure_score`, `disclosure_posture` and `open_disclosure_obligation_count` are then recomputed, and both the property's `version` and the profile's `version` increment by 1.

**Signing off a repair.** Confirmation is a `ComplianceOfficer` action and moves a profile `REMEDIATED → DEPLOYED`, closing the cycle `DEPLOYED → FLAGGED → REMEDIATED → DEPLOYED`. It requires the profile's recomputed `disclosure_score` to be at or above 80: exactly 80 passes, 79 does not and is rejected with `ERR_REMEDIATION_SCORE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 409), leaving the operator to remediate again — which is why a path may accumulate several remediation rounds. A rejected confirmation persists nothing whatsoever: the recomputed score is discarded rather than written back to `disclosure_score`, `last_audited_at` is left where it was, and neither the profile's nor the property's `version` moves. On success `last_audited_at` is stamped, the property's `min_channel_disclosure_score`, `disclosure_posture` and `open_disclosure_obligation_count` are recomputed, and the property's and profile's `version` fields each increment by 1. Confirmation fires no retirement cascade of its own: unlike deployment it leaves every sibling path on the same property and channel exactly as it found it, so a path confirmed back into service alongside a path that was deployed while it sat flagged legitimately leaves two `DEPLOYED` paths on one property and channel.

Three further transitions on a profile are driven by the system rather than requested directly, and are documented where they originate: a deployed path is flagged when a substantiated complaint pushes its recomputed score below 60 (§3.7); a deployed path is flagged when a regulator orders remediation on an in-scope docket, and a flagged path returns to `DEPLOYED` when that docket is withdrawn (§3.8). Retirement is likewise systemic: a `DEPLOYED` path retires when a successor path on the same property and channel is deployed or when its fee program is superseded, and a `FLAGGED` or `REMEDIATED` path retires when its fee program is superseded. Retirement from `FLAGGED` recomputes the property's `min_channel_disclosure_score`, `disclosure_posture` and `open_disclosure_obligation_count`; retirement from `DEPLOYED` or `REMEDIATED` recomputes the minimum score and the posture; in every case the profile's `version` increments by 1. `RETIRED` is terminal — a retired path never returns to service.

### 3.4. Quoting a Stay, Attesting What Was Shown, and Progressing the Guest

A booking is created by a `ChannelOperator` acting for itself or by a `GuestServicesAgent` taking the reservation on a channel's behalf. The payload names the `property_id` booked, the `channel_id` the quote was shown on, and the `channel_disclosure_profile_id` of the booking path that produced the first quote; the `channel_id` supplied must be the profile's own channel, and a mismatch is a malformed quote request rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. When a `ChannelOperator` books, the rank-4 ownership check of §2.1 reads the `channel_id` carried in this payload and not the profile's, so an operator that names its own channel while pointing at another channel's booking path clears ownership and is stopped by this shape mismatch instead. The mismatch can only be tested once the payload's `property_id`, `channel_id` and `channel_disclosure_profile_id` have each been resolved in that left-to-right order at rank 8, so a payload naming a channel that does not exist answers `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`, and one naming a profile that does not exist answers `ERR_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, in preference to the mismatch. It then carries the stay itself: an `arrival_date` as an ISO 8601 UTC instant, a `nights` count between 1 and 30 inclusive, a `guests` count between 1 and 8 inclusive, and the channel-quoted `room_rate_cents` of at least 0. Note that percentage-based fees are computed against this quoted `room_rate_cents` and never against the property's reference `base_room_rate_cents`; the property's figure is a modelling baseline only.

Only a live path can take a booking: the referenced profile must be in `DEPLOYED` or `REMEDIATED` status, and a `DRAFT`, `FLAGGED` or `RETIRED` path is rejected with `ERR_PROFILE_NOT_BOOKABLE` (HTTP 409). On success the profile's `fee_program_id` is **pinned** onto the booking as a system-written field. That pin is permanent: later supersession of the program never re-points the booking, so a stay is always priced and billed against the inventory that was in force when it was sold. Every fee code of the pinned program must have a taxability rule in the property's jurisdiction before the quote arithmetic can run; a gap there is reported as `ERR_TAXABILITY_RULE_MISSING`, though in practice publication already guaranteed the coverage and taxability rules can never be deleted.

The channel's own attestation of what it displayed arrives as `disclosed_fee_amounts`, an optional map from fee code to whole-stay cents that defaults to the empty object. Its keys are fee codes and its values are integers of at least 0. A code absent from the map is not a neutral omission — it counts as a charge that was not disclosed at all, with a disclosed amount of zero.

**The quote arithmetic runs in a pinned five-step order.** Step one: `quoted_room_total_cents` is the `room_rate_cents` multiplied by `nights`. Step two: `quoted_mandatory_fee_cents` is the mandatory-fee subtotal under the per-night rounding order. That subtotal walks the pinned program's `MANDATORY` fees in ascending `created_at` order; for every fee that is not `PER_STAY`, its unrounded per-night value against the booking's `room_rate_cents` and `guests` is rounded half up to whole cents and those rounded per-night values are accumulated; the accumulated per-night sum is then multiplied by `nights`; and finally each `PER_STAY` fee's `amount_cents` is added on top. `OPTIONAL` and `CONDITIONAL` fees are excluded entirely. Step three: the taxable base is assembled as an aggregate. The room amount always enters it. A mandatory fee enters it unless its taxability rule for the property's jurisdiction says `EXEMPT` — both `TAXABLE` and `TAXABLE_IF_MANDATORY` fees are in the base, because at quote time every fee in the subtotal is by definition a mandatory charge — and the amount each contributes is its per-night-rounded value multiplied by `nights`, or its `amount_cents` when the basis is `PER_STAY`. Step four: `quoted_tax_cents` is computed with exactly **one** rounding over the aggregate. The rate applied to the room portion is the jurisdiction's `occupancy_tax_rate_bps`, and the rate applied to each contributing fee is that fee's rule-level `tax_rate_bps` when it is non-null and the jurisdiction rate otherwise; when every contributing rate is the same, the whole base is multiplied by that single rate, divided by 10000, and rounded half up once. When the contributing rates differ, the base is split into one portion per distinct rate, each portion is multiplied by its own rate, divided by 10000 and rounded half up separately, and the rounded results are summed in ascending order of `tax_rate_bps`. Step five: `quoted_total_cents` is `quoted_room_total_cents` plus `quoted_mandatory_fee_cents` plus `quoted_tax_cents`, and that is the number a total-price-display path is obliged to render.

A worked illustration keeps the two rounding orders straight. Take a `room_rate_cents` of 29900, four `nights`, two `guests`, and three mandatory fees: a `PERCENT_OF_ROOM` fee at 750 basis points, whose unrounded per-night value is 2242.5 cents; a `PER_NIGHT` fee of 1495 cents; and a `PER_PERSON_PER_NIGHT` fee of 325 cents, giving 650 cents a night for two guests. Under the per-night rounding order the per-night values round to 2243, 1495 and 650, summing to 4388, which multiplied by four nights gives a `quoted_mandatory_fee_cents` of 17552. With a jurisdiction rate of 1338 basis points, the first fee `TAXABLE`, the second `TAXABLE_IF_MANDATORY` and the third `EXEMPT`, the room total is 119600, the taxable base is 119600 plus 8972 plus 5980, that is 134552, the single rounding of 18003.0576 gives a `quoted_tax_cents` of 18003, and `quoted_total_cents` is 155155.

**The disclosure gap** measures how much of the mandatory burden the channel failed to show. Walking the pinned program's `MANDATORY` fees in ascending `created_at` order, each fee's charged amount is its per-night-rounded whole-stay value — the same variant the quote used — and its shown amount is the value stored under its code in `disclosed_fee_amounts`, or 0 when the code is absent. The gap contribution is the charged amount minus the shown amount when that difference is positive, and 0 otherwise: over-disclosure never produces a negative offset that could mask a different fee's under-disclosure. `quote_disclosure_gap_cents` is the sum of those contributions. Continuing the illustration, a map declaring 6000 for the percentage fee and 5980 for the per-night fee, against charged amounts of 8972, 5980 and 2600, gives 2972 plus 0 plus 2600, that is 5572; a fully honest map declaring 8972, 5980 and 2600 gives a gap of 0.

Every folio-side figure on a new booking starts at 0: `folio_fee_total_cents`, `folio_total_cents`, `quote_folio_variance_cents` and `guest_out_of_pocket_cents`. The row also lands in its two initial states — `stay_status` `QUOTED` and `disclosure_status` `PENDING_AUDIT`.

**A booking carries two independent state dimensions on one row.** `stay_status` tracks stay progress through `QUOTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_HOUSE` and `CHECKED_OUT`, and advances only through operator stay actions. `disclosure_status` tracks disclosure standing through `PENDING_AUDIT`, `DISCLOSED`, `DISPUTED`, `UNDISCLOSED_CONFIRMED` and `CURED`, and advances only through posture cascades, itemisation variance and complaint adjudication. Neither dimension constrains the other, and every combination of the two is reachable — a booking may sit at `QUOTED` with `DISPUTED` standing, or at `CHECKED_OUT` with `UNDISCLOSED_CONFIRMED` standing. `CHECKED_OUT` is terminal on the stay dimension and `CURED` is terminal on the disclosure dimension. Bookings are listable with optional `property_id`, `channel_id`, `channel_disclosure_profile_id`, `fee_program_id`, `stay_status` and `disclosure_status` filters.

Confirmation advances `stay_status` from `QUOTED` to `CONFIRMED` and may be performed by the owning `ChannelOperator` or by a `GuestServicesAgent`. It requires the booking to be `QUOTED`, the booking path to still be `DEPLOYED` or `REMEDIATED` — otherwise `ERR_PROFILE_NOT_BOOKABLE` — and the pinned program to still be `PUBLISHED`, otherwise `ERR_FEE_PROGRAM_NOT_PUBLISHED`. Confirmation is where an honest booking path earns its clean standing: when the path's `first_quote_shows_total` is true, its `disclosure_placement` is either `FIRST_QUOTE` or `RATE_DETAILS`, and the booking's `quote_disclosure_gap_cents` is 0, then the same row's `disclosure_status` also transitions `PENDING_AUDIT → DISCLOSED`. If any of those three conditions fails, standing stays at `PENDING_AUDIT`. Either way the booking's `version` increments by 1.

Check-in is a `GuestServicesAgent` action and advances `stay_status` from `CONFIRMED` to `IN_HOUSE`. It requires the logical clock to be at or after the booking's `arrival_date`, with equality permitted; an early attempt is rejected with `ERR_ARRIVAL_NOT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). Check-in creates exactly one folio for the booking, in `OPEN` status with `line_count` 0 and every monetary aggregate at 0, and increments the booking's `version`. The new folio lands with `version` 1, `itemization_complete` false and `posted_at` null, and the response body reports the updated `Booking` rather than that folio, which is discovered through the folio list under the booking. There is one folio per booking and no route to create a second. Check-out advances `stay_status` from `IN_HOUSE` to `CHECKED_OUT`, again as a `GuestServicesAgent` action, and requires the logical clock to be at or after the `arrival_date` advanced by `nights` whole days, with equality permitted; an early attempt is rejected with `ERR_DEPARTURE_NOT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). It increments the booking's `version`.

The disclosure dimension moves only under system control, and each of its edges has a distinct trigger. A booking in `PENDING_AUDIT` whose `stay_status` is still `QUOTED` transitions to `DISPUTED` when its property's `disclosure_posture` becomes `NON_COMPLIANT`; only unconfirmed quotes are swept this way, and confirmed or in-house bookings are deliberately left alone. The sweep fires only on the **transition into** `NON_COMPLIANT`: a recomputation pass that finds the property already `NON_COMPLIANT` and leaves it there sweeps nothing, so a property is swept once per descent into non-compliance rather than on every subsequent write. Within the pass that does fire it, the sweep runs after the minimum and the posture have been written but before `open_disclosure_obligation_count` and `aggregate_disclosure_gap_cents` are recomputed, so the bookings it has just disputed are already reflected in both of those figures in the very same response. Substantiation of a complaint on the booking moves standing to `UNDISCLOSED_CONFIRMED` from `PENDING_AUDIT`, from `DISCLOSED` or from `DISPUTED`; each of those three edges recomputes the property's `aggregate_disclosure_gap_cents`, increments the booking path's `confirmed_undisclosed_count` by 1, recomputes that path's `disclosure_score`, and increments the booking's `version`. Dismissal of a complaint moves standing to `DISCLOSED` from `PENDING_AUDIT` or from `DISPUTED`, recomputes the property's `aggregate_disclosure_gap_cents` and increments the booking's `version` — and pointedly does **not** change the path's `confirmed_undisclosed_count` and does not rescore the path; the dismissal branch's side-effect set is disjoint from the substantiation branch's. Payment of a substantiated complaint moves standing from `UNDISCLOSED_CONFIRMED` to `CURED`, recomputes the property's `aggregate_disclosure_gap_cents`, recomputes the booking's `guest_out_of_pocket_cents` and `folio_total_cents`, and increments the booking's `version`. Itemisation supplies the last edge, described next.

### 3.5. Folio Itemisation, Per-Line Taxability and Variance Reconciliation

A folio is the guest bill for one booking. It is created by check-in, itemised exactly once, and re-postable after every adjustment. Its own fields are the `booking_id` it bills, a `status` that starts at `OPEN`, the computed `line_count`, `charges_subtotal_cents`, `credits_total_cents`, `tax_total_cents` and `folio_total_cents`, the computed `itemization_complete` flag that starts false, and a system `posted_at` that starts null and records the logical-clock instant of the most recent entry into `POSTED`. A folio moves through `OPEN`, `ITEMIZED`, `POSTED`, `ADJUSTED` and `CLOSED`, with `CLOSED` terminal, and its lifecycle is cyclic: `ITEMIZED → POSTED → ADJUSTED → POSTED` may repeat any number of times. Folios are readable individually, listable under their booking with only paging inputs, and listable globally with optional `booking_id` and `status` filters; the list under a booking returns an array of zero rows before check-in and one row after, the shape being uniform either way.

The line ledger beneath a folio is **append-only and immutable**. A folio line records the `folio_id` it belongs to, a `fee_id` naming the fee it charges, credits, rebills or refunds — null on `ROOM` lines, and otherwise carried over from the source line the new line answers, so a `FEE_CREDIT`, `FEE_REBILL` or `COMPLAINT_REFUND` line repeats the `fee_id` of the line it compensates — the `jurisdiction_id` whose taxability determination produced the line's rate and tax, a `line_type` from `ROOM`, `MANDATORY_FEE`, `OPTIONAL_FEE`, `FEE_CREDIT`, `FEE_REBILL` and `COMPLAINT_REFUND`, a denormalised `fee_code` that is null on `ROOM` lines, a guest-facing `description`, an `amount_cents`, a `taxability_determination`, the `tax_rate_bps` actually applied, a signed `tax_cents`, and a 1-based `sequence_no` giving append order within the folio. The `sequence_no` is the only ordering key for lines and is never derived from `id`. `amount_cents` is **always positive on every line type**: a credit line states the magnitude of the credit there and carries its sign in `tax_cents`, and the aggregation subtracts the credit bucket. `taxability_determination` is one of `TAXABLE`, `EXEMPT`, `TAXABLE_IF_MANDATORY_APPLIED` — the conditional rule taxed the line because the charge was mandatory — and `TAXABLE_IF_MANDATORY_WAIVED` — the same rule un-taxed a credit line because an opt-out made the charge non-mandatory. `tax_rate_bps` is the rule's override when non-null, the jurisdiction's `occupancy_tax_rate_bps` otherwise, and 0 for exempt outcomes. A line has no `updated_at`, no `version` and no status; nothing ever edits one. Lines are readable individually and listable with optional `folio_id`, `fee_id`, `jurisdiction_id` and `line_type` filters. A `Guest` listing lines is scoped rather than refused: the result set is restricted to the lines of its own booking's folios, so an unfiltered list is that booking's ledger alone; a `folio_id` filter naming another booking's folio is the `ERR_BOOKING_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` of §2.1, while a `folio_id` naming no folio at all is simply a filter nothing matches and yields an empty page.

Itemisation is a `GuestServicesAgent` action from `OPEN` to `ITEMIZED`. It requires the folio to be `OPEN`, the booking's `stay_status` to be `IN_HOUSE` or `CHECKED_OUT`, and every fee code of the pinned program to have a taxability rule for the property's jurisdiction, a gap being reported as `ERR_TAXABILITY_RULE_MISSING`. The lines are appended in a **pinned order**. First one `ROOM` line at `sequence_no` 1, whose `description` is the literal string `Room charge`, whose `amount_cents` is `room_rate_cents` multiplied by `nights`, whose determination is `TAXABLE`, whose `tax_rate_bps` is the jurisdiction's `occupancy_tax_rate_bps`, and whose `tax_cents` is that amount times that rate divided by 10000, rounded half up. Then one `MANDATORY_FEE` line per fee of the pinned program with classification `MANDATORY` **whose stay amount is strictly greater than 0**, taken in ascending `Fee.created_at` order and numbered from 2 upward. A mandatory fee that computes to 0 cents for this stay is still disclosed in the program but produces **no** folio line at all. `OPTIONAL` and `CONDITIONAL` fees produce no line at itemisation.

Each fee line's amount is the **stay-aggregation variant**: the fee's unrounded whole-stay value against the booking's `room_rate_cents`, `guests` and `nights` is computed first and rounded half up **once**, at the end. This is the opposite order from the quote, which rounded per night before multiplying, and the divergence between the two is the entire source of the booking's `quote_folio_variance_cents`. Each line's `description` is the `disclosure_text` of the fee as in force at itemisation. Each line's taxability is then determined from the rule for the property's jurisdiction and the line's own `fee_code`: an `EXEMPT` rule gives determination `EXEMPT` and `tax_rate_bps` 0; a `TAXABLE` rule gives determination `TAXABLE`; and a `TAXABLE_IF_MANDATORY` rule gives determination `TAXABLE_IF_MANDATORY_APPLIED`, because a line written at itemisation is by construction a mandatory charge. In both taxed cases the rate is the rule's `tax_rate_bps` when non-null and the jurisdiction's `occupancy_tax_rate_bps` otherwise. The line's `tax_cents` is then its own `amount_cents` times its own rate divided by 10000, **rounded half up per line**; the folio's `tax_total_cents` is the plain sum of those already-rounded per-line figures, never a single rounding over an aggregate base.

The reconciliation that follows is a straight aggregation over every line then present. `charges_subtotal_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over lines whose type is `ROOM`, `MANDATORY_FEE`, `OPTIONAL_FEE` or `FEE_REBILL`. `credits_total_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over lines whose type is `FEE_CREDIT` or `COMPLAINT_REFUND`, and is therefore positive or zero. `tax_total_cents` is the sum of `tax_cents` over **every** line, credits contributing their negative values. `folio_total_cents` is `charges_subtotal_cents` **minus** `credits_total_cents` **plus** `tax_total_cents`. The booking's `folio_fee_total_cents` receives the stay-aggregation mandatory-fee subtotal, its `folio_total_cents` and `guest_out_of_pocket_cents` receive the folio total, and its `quote_folio_variance_cents` becomes `folio_total_cents` minus `quoted_total_cents` — negative when the folio bills less than the quote promised. The folio's `line_count` is set to the number of lines and `itemization_complete` to true, and both the folio's and the booking's `version` increment by 1.

**The tolerance gate.** A variance inside the tolerance is bookkeeping noise from the two rounding orders and does not touch disclosure standing. Only when the **absolute value** of `quote_folio_variance_cents` is **strictly greater than 100** does the booking's `disclosure_status` move to `DISPUTED`, and only from `PENDING_AUDIT` or from `DISCLOSED`. A variance of exactly 100, in either direction, does **not** dispute the booking. Where the gate does dispute the booking, the same request then recomputes the property's five denormalised values — `min_channel_disclosure_score`, `disclosure_posture`, `published_fee_program_count`, `open_disclosure_obligation_count` and `aggregate_disclosure_gap_cents` — and increments the property's `version` only where that pass actually moves one of them; an itemisation inside the tolerance recomputes nothing and leaves the property row entirely untouched.

The canonical folio makes the contrast concrete. Continuing the fixture of §3.4, the `ROOM` line is 119600 with tax 16002; the percentage fee's stay amount rounds once from 8970.0 to 8970 with tax 1200; the per-night fee is 5980 with tax 800; and the exempt per-person fee is 2600 with tax 0. The mandatory-fee subtotal under stay aggregation is 17550 against the quote's 17552, `tax_total_cents` is 18002 against the quote's 18003, and `folio_total_cents` is 119600 plus 17550 plus 18002, that is 155152, giving a `quote_folio_variance_cents` of 155152 minus 155155, that is −3. Since 3 is not greater than 100, the booking's standing stays where it was.

Posting is a `GuestServicesAgent` action and has two entry edges. From `ITEMIZED` it requires the folio to be `ITEMIZED` with `itemization_complete` true and the booking to be in `stay_status` `CHECKED_OUT` — a booking still in house is rejected with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_CHECKED_OUT` (HTTP 409). From `ADJUSTED` it requires the folio to be `ADJUSTED`, the booking to be `CHECKED_OUT`, and **no** opt-out request on the booking to still be in `REQUESTED`; an undecided opt-out blocks the re-post as an invalid-state condition reported with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Either edge sets `posted_at` to the logical clock and increments the folio's `version`.

Adjustment is never a caller action. The folio moves from `POSTED` to `ADJUSTED` under system control when a granted opt-out or a paid complaint refund appends its compensating line at the next `sequence_no`; the folio's `line_count`, `charges_subtotal_cents`, `credits_total_cents`, `tax_total_cents` and `folio_total_cents` are recomputed, the booking's `folio_total_cents` and `guest_out_of_pocket_cents` are recomputed, and both `version` counters increment. A folio already in `ADJUSTED` stays there when a further compensating line lands.

Closing is a `GuestServicesAgent` action from `POSTED` to `CLOSED`. It requires the folio to be `POSTED` and no complaint on the booking to be in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` or `SUBSTANTIATED`; a live complaint blocks the close as an invalid-state condition reported with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Closing expires every opt-out request on the booking that is still `REQUESTED`, stamping each one's `decided_at` with the logical clock, and increments the folio's `version`. **There is no edge from `ADJUSTED` to `CLOSED`**: an adjusted folio must be re-posted first, so an unposted adjustment can never be locked away.

### 3.6. Opt-Out Requests: Crediting an Avoidable Fee and Reversing the Credit

An opt-out request is a guest's request to decline an avoidable fee. It is created under the booking by the owning `Guest` or by a `GuestServicesAgent`, and the only body field is the `fee_id` being declined. The row records its `booking_id` and `fee_id`, a `status` that starts at `REQUESTED`, the computed `requested_hours_before_arrival`, the computed `credit_cents` and `tax_credit_cents` that both default to 0, a system `credit_line_sequence_no` that defaults to null, a system `denial_reason_code` that defaults to null, and a system `decided_at` that defaults to null and records the instant of the grant, denial, expiry or revocation. `requested_hours_before_arrival` is the whole hours between the logical clock and the booking's `arrival_date`, **floored**, and is negative when the request lands after arrival. Statuses are `REQUESTED`, `GRANTED`, `DENIED`, `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED`, with `DENIED`, `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED` all terminal. Requests are readable individually and listable with optional `booking_id`, `fee_id` and `status` filters.

Four business rules gate creation, and they fire in the global precedence order. The `fee_id` must belong to the booking's pinned `fee_program_id`, and a fee drawn from any other program is rejected with `ERR_FEE_NOT_ON_BOOKING_PROGRAM` (HTTP 422). No other request for the same booking and fee pair may be in `REQUESTED` or `GRANTED`, a live duplicate being rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPT_OUT` (HTTP 409); a previously `DENIED`, `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` request does not block a fresh one. The fee must have `avoidable` true **and** classification `MANDATORY` or `CONDITIONAL`, since a non-avoidable fee cannot be opted out of at all, and a violation is rejected with `ERR_FEE_NOT_AVOIDABLE` (HTTP 422). Finally the request must land early enough: the floored `requested_hours_before_arrival` must be at least the fee's `opt_out_window_hours`, and the comparison is **inclusive**, so a request arriving at exactly `opt_out_window_hours` before arrival is accepted while one arriving an hour later is rejected with `ERR_OPT_OUT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422).

Granting is a `GuestServicesAgent` action from `REQUESTED` to `GRANTED`. The folio must be in `POSTED` or `ADJUSTED` so that the reversal has something to reverse — an `OPEN`, `ITEMIZED` or `CLOSED` folio cannot absorb a compensating line, and neither can a booking that carries no folio at all because it has never checked in, so all four cases are rejected with `ERR_FOLIO_NOT_ADJUSTABLE` (HTTP 409) — and the folio must carry a `MANDATORY_FEE` line for the request's `fee_id` with no un-rebilled credit already standing against it, the absence being rejected with `ERR_FEE_LINE_NOT_ON_FOLIO` (HTTP 422). That absence is genuinely reachable: a `MANDATORY` avoidable fee whose stay amount computes to 0 cents is a legitimate opt-out target under the fee-membership and avoidability rules, yet itemisation wrote no line for it, so there is nothing to credit.

The grant appends exactly one `FEE_CREDIT` line at the next `sequence_no`. Its `fee_id` is the request's fee. Its `amount_cents` is the source `MANDATORY_FEE` line's `amount_cents` — the stay-aggregation figure the folio actually billed, never the quote's per-night-rounded figure — carried positive. Its `tax_cents` is the **negation** of the source line's `tax_cents`, and its `tax_rate_bps` is the source line's rate. **No new rounding occurs**: the credit is a byte-for-byte reversal of the source line's amount and tax. Its `description` is the fee's own `disclosure_text`, and its `jurisdiction_id`, `fee_id` and `fee_code` are copied from that source line. Its `taxability_determination` is `TAXABLE_IF_MANDATORY_WAIVED` when the fee code's rule is `TAXABLE_IF_MANDATORY`, because a fee ceases to be mandatory once the guest opts out and the tax collected on the original line is credited back in full; for any other rule the credit line simply repeats the source line's determination. The request's `credit_cents` and `tax_credit_cents` are set to the absolute values of the credit line's amount and tax, `credit_line_sequence_no` to that line's `sequence_no`, and `decided_at` to the logical clock. The folio moves `POSTED → ADJUSTED`, or stays in `ADJUSTED` if it is already there, its subtotals and the booking's `folio_total_cents` and `guest_out_of_pocket_cents` are re-derived, and the request's `version` increments.

Denial is a `GuestServicesAgent` action from `REQUESTED` to `DENIED`, carrying a `denial_reason_code` of `NOT_AVOIDABLE`, `SERVICE_ALREADY_CONSUMED` or `POLICY_EXCEPTION_DENIED`. It sets that code and `decided_at`, appends no folio line, changes no money aggregate, and increments the request's `version`. Expiry is not a caller action at all: closing the booking's folio moves every still-`REQUESTED` request to `EXPIRED`, stamping `decided_at` with the logical clock and incrementing each `version`.

Revocation is the grant's compensating action, performed by a `GuestServicesAgent` from `GRANTED` to `REVOKED`, for instance because the guest consumed the service after all. It requires the request to be `GRANTED` and the booking's folio to be in `ADJUSTED`, other folio states being rejected with `ERR_FOLIO_NOT_ADJUSTABLE`, and it requires the `FEE_CREDIT` line named by `credit_line_sequence_no` to be present, its absence being rejected with `ERR_FEE_LINE_NOT_ON_FOLIO`. It appends one `FEE_REBILL` line with the same `fee_id`, an `amount_cents` equal to the recorded `credit_cents`, a **positive** `tax_cents` equal to the recorded `tax_credit_cents`, and both the `taxability_determination` and the `tax_rate_bps` of the original charged line — not the waived determination of the credit — while its `description`, `jurisdiction_id`, `fee_id` and `fee_code` are copied from the credit line it mirrors. It then resets `credit_cents` to 0, `tax_credit_cents` to 0 and `credit_line_sequence_no` to null, re-derives the folio and booking aggregates, and increments the request's `version`. Because the credit line stays on the ledger and the rebill is appended beside it, the arithmetic must land back exactly where it started: on the canonical folio, granting an opt-out on the per-night fee credits 5980 with a tax credit of 800 and drops `guest_out_of_pocket_cents` from 155152 to 148372, and revoking it restores exactly 155152 with no drift. Mutating the original `MANDATORY_FEE` line in place would reach the same total while destroying the audit trail and breaking the contiguity of `sequence_no`, and is forbidden.

### 3.7. Guest Complaints: Investigation, the Adjudication Fork and Refunds

A guest complaint is an allegation that a posted charge was not properly disclosed. It is created under the booking by the owning `Guest` or by a `GuestServicesAgent`, and carries the `folio_line_id` of the specific disputed line, a `complaint_reason` from `NOT_DISCLOSED`, `MISSTATED_AMOUNT`, `MANDATORY_NOT_AVOIDABLE` and `TOTAL_NOT_SHOWN`, and a `narrative` of 8 to 1000 characters giving the guest's account of what was and was not shown. The line must belong to a folio of the addressed booking **and** have `line_type` `MANDATORY_FEE` or `OPTIONAL_FEE`; a room line, a credit line, or a line from someone else's folio is rejected with `ERR_FOLIO_LINE_NOT_ON_BOOKING` (HTTP 422).

Alongside its `booking_id` the row carries a `status` starting at `SUBMITTED`, a computed `refund_variant` defaulting to null, computed `disputed_amount_cents`, `disclosed_amount_cents` and `refund_entitlement_cents` all defaulting to 0, a system `refunded_amount_cents` defaulting to 0, a system `dismissal_reason_code` defaulting to null and constrained to `DISCLOSURE_EVIDENCED`, `GUEST_ACKNOWLEDGED_AT_CHECK_IN` or `DUPLICATE_COMPLAINT`, and the two system attribution fields `investigated_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_by_user_id`, both null at creation. At creation the four entitlement fields deliberately stay at their defaults — the entitlement arithmetic runs **only** at substantiation — while the property's `open_disclosure_obligation_count` is recomputed immediately, because a submitted complaint is itself an open obligation. Statuses are `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, `SUBSTANTIATED`, `UNSUBSTANTIATED` and `REFUNDED`, with `UNSUBSTANTIATED` and `REFUNDED` terminal. Complaints are readable individually and listable with optional `booking_id`, `folio_line_id`, `status` and `refund_variant` filters.

Investigation, available to a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `GuestServicesAgent`, moves a `SUBMITTED` complaint to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, records `investigated_by_user_id` from the caller's identity header, and increments the complaint's `version`. That recorded identity is exactly what the segregation-of-duties check compares against at adjudication, so the investigation step must happen before either fork branch can be taken.

**The adjudication fork.** Both branches leave `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, both are `ComplianceOfficer` actions, both require the adjudicating identity to differ from `investigated_by_user_id` on pain of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and both set `adjudicated_by_user_id`. Everything else about them differs.

Substantiation moves the complaint to `SUBSTANTIATED` and drives a four-level cascade. Level one writes the complaint's own arithmetic: `refund_variant`, `disputed_amount_cents`, `disclosed_amount_cents` and `refund_entitlement_cents`. Level two moves the booking's `disclosure_status` to `UNDISCLOSED_CONFIRMED`, from `PENDING_AUDIT`, from `DISCLOSED` or from `DISPUTED`. Level three increments the booking path's `confirmed_undisclosed_count` by 1 and rescores that path. Level four fires only conditionally: when the rescored `disclosure_score` is below 60 **and** the path is in `DEPLOYED`, the path transitions `DEPLOYED → FLAGGED`, which in turn recomputes the property's `min_channel_disclosure_score`, `disclosure_posture` and `open_disclosure_obligation_count`. The property's `aggregate_disclosure_gap_cents` is recomputed as well, since the booking has entered a gap-contributing standing. All five of the property's denormalised values are in fact re-derived on every substantiation, but the property's `version` increments only where level four actually flagged the path or where one of those five values moved, so substantiating a complaint that moves none of them — an already-`DISPUTED` booking carrying a zero gap, on a path that stays `DEPLOYED` — legitimately leaves the property row untouched. The complaint's `version` increments by 1.

Dismissal moves the complaint to `UNSUBSTANTIATED` and its side-effect set is **deliberately disjoint** from substantiation's. It records the mandatory `dismissal_reason_code`, forces `refund_entitlement_cents` to 0 and leaves `refund_variant` null, moves the booking's `disclosure_status` to `DISCLOSED` when it is `PENDING_AUDIT` or `DISPUTED` and leaves it unchanged otherwise, recomputes the property's `open_disclosure_obligation_count` and `aggregate_disclosure_gap_cents` — touching the property row, and so incrementing its `version`, only where one of its five denormalised values actually moves, which the obligation count always does because the complaint has just left its live statuses — and increments the complaint's `version`. It does **not** touch the path's `confirmed_undisclosed_count`, does **not** rescore the path, and therefore causes no posture change and no flagging.

**The entitlement arithmetic has two variants, and the variant is selected by `complaint_reason` alone — never by any caller input.** In both variants `disputed_amount_cents` is the disputed line's `amount_cents` and `disclosed_amount_cents` is the value stored under that line's `fee_code` in the booking's `disclosed_fee_amounts`, or 0 when the code is absent or the line has no `fee_code`; the map is read as captured at quote time and is never back-filled.

Under `FULL_FEE_REFUND`, selected when `complaint_reason` is `NOT_DISCLOSED`, `TOTAL_NOT_SHOWN` or `MANDATORY_NOT_AVOIDABLE`, the guest was never told about the charge at all, so `refund_entitlement_cents` is the line's `amount_cents` plus the line's `tax_cents` — the tax already rounded on the line, so this variant introduces no rounding of its own — and `disclosed_fee_amounts` is ignored entirely.

Under `DISCLOSURE_GAP_DIFFERENTIAL`, selected when `complaint_reason` is `MISSTATED_AMOUNT`, the guest was told a smaller number, so only the difference is owed. The differential is the line's `amount_cents` minus `disclosed_amount_cents`, floored at 0, and `refund_entitlement_cents` is that differential plus the differential times the line's own `tax_rate_bps` divided by 10000, **rounded half up independently** of the tax already carried on the line. Because that tax is rounded afresh, the differential refund is **not** a proportional slice of the full refund.

The canonical folio shows both. A `FULL_FEE_REFUND` against the exempt per-person line, amount 2600 with `tax_cents` 0, yields an entitlement of 2600. A `DISCLOSURE_GAP_DIFFERENTIAL` against the percentage line, amount 8970 at a `tax_rate_bps` of 1338, with `disclosed_fee_amounts` declaring 6000 for that code, gives a differential of 2970 and a tax of 397.386 rounded to 397, so `refund_entitlement_cents` is 3367 — not the 10170 that 8970 plus 1200 would give, and not the 3368 that a proportional slice of 10170 would give.

Refunding is a `GuestServicesAgent` action from `SUBSTANTIATED` to `REFUNDED`, carrying a `refund_amount_cents`. It requires the complaint to be `SUBSTANTIATED`; it requires `refund_entitlement_cents` to be greater than 0, since a substantiated complaint whose differential computed to 0 cannot be refunded and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_NO_REFUND_ENTITLEMENT` (HTTP 422); it requires `refund_amount_cents` to be at least 1 and at most `refund_entitlement_cents`, equality being permitted and one cent over being rejected with `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_ENTITLEMENT` (HTTP 422); and it requires the booking's folio to be in `POSTED` or `ADJUSTED`, other folio states being rejected with `ERR_FOLIO_NOT_ADJUSTABLE`. It appends one `COMPLAINT_REFUND` line that is fee-specific — its `fee_id` and its denormalised `fee_code` are copied from the disputed `folio_line_id`, which by the creation rule always names a fee-bearing line — with `amount_cents` equal to `refund_amount_cents` carried **positive** under the credit sign convention, `tax_cents` of 0, a `tax_rate_bps` of 0 and `taxability_determination` `EXEMPT` — because the entitlement already includes whatever tax was collected, taxing the refund line again would double-count — with the `jurisdiction_id` of the disputed line and a `description` of the literal string `Complaint refund`. It records `refunded_amount_cents`, moves the folio `POSTED → ADJUSTED` or leaves it in `ADJUSTED`, re-derives the folio subtotals and the booking's `folio_total_cents` and `guest_out_of_pocket_cents`, moves the booking's `disclosure_status` from `UNDISCLOSED_CONFIRMED` to `CURED`, recomputes the property's `open_disclosure_obligation_count` and `aggregate_disclosure_gap_cents`, and increments the complaint's `version`. Curing the booking removes its gap from the property's aggregate while **leaving the path's `confirmed_undisclosed_count` intact**: a substantiated disclosure failure is never erased by paying for it.

### 3.8. Regulator Inquiries: Docket Scope, Exposure and the Remediation Loop

A regulator inquiry is a jurisdiction's enforcement inquiry into one property's disclosure practices, scoped to one or more channel families. It is recorded by a `PlatformAdmin` or a `RegulatoryLiaison` with the inquiring `jurisdiction_id`, the `property_id` under inquiry, the regulator's `docket_number` of 1 to 64 characters, a `channel_scope`, and a `response_due_at` deadline. The `jurisdiction_id` must equal the property's own `jurisdiction_id`, a mismatch being a malformed docket rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The pair of `jurisdiction_id` and `docket_number` is unique, a collision being rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOCKET_NUMBER` (HTTP 409). `channel_scope` is a non-empty array of `channel_type` literals with no duplicates; an empty array, or one containing any value that is not a `channel_type` member, is rejected with `ERR_INQUIRY_SCOPE_EMPTY` (HTTP 422). Only profiles on channels whose `channel_type` appears in that array are ever touched by this docket's directives.

The row also carries a `status` starting at `RECEIVED`; the system text fields `response_summary`, `remediation_directive`, `remediation_summary`, `rejection_reason` and `withdrawal_reason`, all null at creation and each supplied as a required string on the one transition that writes it, bounded neither below nor above exactly as `attestation_note` is, so the empty string is accepted while a missing, null or non-string value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the segregation-of-duties anchors `drafted_by_user_id` and `submitted_by_user_id`, both null at creation and required to differ from one another; a system `flagged_profile_ids` array defaulting to empty, holding the ids of the booking paths this docket flagged, in flagging order; a system `remediation_round` counting the number of times the docket has entered `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, defaulting to 0; and a computed `exposure_cents` defaulting to 0. Statuses are `RECEIVED`, `RESPONSE_DRAFTED`, `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED` and `WITHDRAWN`, with `ACCEPTED` and `WITHDRAWN` terminal, and the machine is cyclic through the remediation rounds. Creation computes `exposure_cents` and recomputes the property's `open_disclosure_obligation_count`, because a live docket is itself an open obligation. Inquiries are readable individually and listable with optional `jurisdiction_id`, `property_id`, `status` and `docket_number` filters, all reads being restricted to `PlatformAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `RegulatoryLiaison`.

**Exposure** is the money the docket reaches, restricted to the channel families it names. Walking the bookings of the inquiry's property, a booking is skipped when its channel's `channel_type` is not in `channel_scope` and skipped again when its `disclosure_status` is not `DISPUTED` or `UNDISCLOSED_CONFIRMED`; each surviving booking contributes its `quote_disclosure_gap_cents`, and `exposure_cents` is the plain integer sum of those already-rounded per-booking gaps with no re-rounding. Bookings in `PENDING_AUDIT`, `DISCLOSED` or `CURED` contribute nothing, and a booking on an out-of-scope channel contributes nothing even when its standing is `UNDISCLOSED_CONFIRMED`. A single booking on an OTA channel with a `quote_disclosure_gap_cents` of 5572 that has moved to `UNDISCLOSED_CONFIRMED` gives a docket scoped to `OTA` an `exposure_cents` of 5572, and gives a docket scoped to `GDS` an `exposure_cents` of 0. Exposure is recomputed at creation, when a response is submitted, when remediation is required, when completed remediation is reported and when a remediation submission is rejected; drafting a response, acceptance and withdrawal all leave the stored `exposure_cents` exactly as they found it.

Drafting is a `RegulatoryLiaison` action from `RECEIVED` to `RESPONSE_DRAFTED`, carrying the `response_summary` text. It requires the logical clock to be at or before `response_due_at`, a lapsed docket being rejected with `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409) on the reasoning that a lapsed docket must be escalated by the regulator, not answered late. It records `drafted_by_user_id` from the caller's identity header and increments the docket's `version`.

Submission is also a `RegulatoryLiaison` action, from `RESPONSE_DRAFTED` to `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`. It requires the submitting identity to differ from `drafted_by_user_id`, on pain of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and it requires the logical clock still to be at or before `response_due_at`, on pain of `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. It records `submitted_by_user_id`, recomputes `exposure_cents` and increments the docket's `version`.

**Requiring remediation drives the deepest cascade in this API.** It is a `PlatformAdmin` action carrying a `remediation_directive`, entered from `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` on a first round and from `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` on a rejected round. Link one records the directive and increments `remediation_round` by 1. Link two transitions every booking path of the inquiry's property that is in `DEPLOYED` and whose channel's `channel_type` is in `channel_scope` from `DEPLOYED` to `FLAGGED`, appending each id to `flagged_profile_ids` in ascending profile `created_at` order; paths on out-of-scope channels and paths not currently `DEPLOYED` are untouched. Link three recomputes the property's `min_channel_disclosure_score` and `disclosure_posture`, and because a `FLAGGED` path contributes 0 to the minimum, the posture becomes `NON_COMPLIANT` whenever at least one path was flagged. Link four then transitions every booking of that property whose `stay_status` is `QUOTED` and whose `disclosure_status` is `PENDING_AUDIT` to `DISPUTED`. This link is the one place where the sweep is **unconditional** rather than gated on the descent into `NON_COMPLIANT` described in §3.4: it runs on every remediation order, so a docket that flagged nothing at all — because no in-scope path was `DEPLOYED` — and a property that was already `NON_COMPLIANT` before the order both still have their unaudited quotes disputed. The property's `open_disclosure_obligation_count` and `aggregate_disclosure_gap_cents` are recomputed, `exposure_cents` is recomputed over the newly disputed bookings, and both the property's and the docket's `version` increment by 1.

Submitting remediation is a `RegulatoryLiaison` action from `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` to `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`, carrying a `remediation_summary`. Every profile id in `flagged_profile_ids` must have **left** `FLAGGED` — each must now be `REMEDIATED` or `DEPLOYED` — and a submission made while any of them is still `FLAGGED` is premature and rejected with `ERR_REMEDIATION_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409). It records the summary, recomputes `exposure_cents` and the property's `open_disclosure_obligation_count`, and increments the docket's `version`; the property row itself is touched only where that recomputation actually moves one of its five denormalised values, so a submission that changes none of them leaves the property's `version` alone.

Rejecting the remediation closes the cycle. It is a `PlatformAdmin` action from `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` back to `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, carrying a `rejection_reason`. It records that reason, increments `remediation_round` by 1, transitions every profile id already in `flagged_profile_ids` whose status is `DEPLOYED` or `REMEDIATED` back to `FLAGGED` — the list itself is **not** re-appended, so it never accumulates duplicates — recomputes the property's `min_channel_disclosure_score`, `disclosure_posture` and `open_disclosure_obligation_count`, recomputes `exposure_cents`, and increments both the property's and the docket's `version` by 1. Unlike the original order, rejection does not sweep on its own account; it is the property recomputation that carries the descent sweep of §3.4, so where the posture had recovered above `NON_COMPLIANT` in the meantime the re-flagging drives it back down and disputes every `QUOTED` booking of the property still standing at `PENDING_AUDIT`, whereas a property that never left `NON_COMPLIANT` has nothing swept. The docket may therefore loop `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED → REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED → REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` any number of times, `remediation_round` counting the rounds.

Acceptance is a `PlatformAdmin` action into the terminal `ACCEPTED`, taken from `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`, or directly from `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` when no remediation was ever ordered. It sets `flagged_profile_ids` to the empty array — acceptance **clears** the list without un-flagging anything, since remediation has already returned those paths to service — recomputes the property's `open_disclosure_obligation_count`, which falls by 1 for the closed docket, and increments both the property's and the docket's `version` by 1.

Withdrawal is the **compensating action** for the flagging cascade. It is a `PlatformAdmin` action carrying a `withdrawal_reason`, and it is reachable from `RECEIVED`, from `RESPONSE_DRAFTED`, from `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` and from `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`; `WITHDRAWN` is **not** reachable from `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` and not from `ACCEPTED`. It records the reason, transitions every profile id in `flagged_profile_ids` that is still `FLAGGED` from `FLAGGED` back to `DEPLOYED`, sets `flagged_profile_ids` to the empty array, recomputes the property's `min_channel_disclosure_score`, `disclosure_posture` and `open_disclosure_obligation_count`, and increments both the property's and the docket's `version` by 1. It restores **exactly** the paths this docket flagged and nothing else — a path flagged by a substantiated complaint or by a different docket stays flagged — and when nothing else has changed in the meantime the property's score, posture and obligation count must return to their exact pre-require-remediation values, with no drift. Withdrawal from `RESPONSE_DRAFTED` or `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` finds `flagged_profile_ids` already empty and therefore changes no profile at all; from `RECEIVED` the same is true.

### 3.9. Revenue-Impact Modelling of Rolling Fees Into the Room Rate

A revenue-impact model is an immutable projection comparing the status quo against rolling a subset of mandatory fees into the room rate. It is created by a `RevenueManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` with the `property_id` being modelled, the `fee_program_id` whose mandatory fees are candidates for roll-in, a `scenario`, a `variant` and a `roll_in_fee_codes` array. All five inputs are required and none has a default, so a `STATUS_QUO` model must still send `roll_in_fee_codes` explicitly as an empty array rather than omit it. It has **no update endpoint, no `updated_at` and no `version`** — a different assumption means a new row — and it is readable individually and listable with optional `property_id`, `fee_program_id`, `scenario` and `variant` filters.

The referenced program must be `PUBLISHED`, an unpublished program being rejected with `ERR_FEE_PROGRAM_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 409), and it must belong to the named `property_id`, a mismatch being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every code in `roll_in_fee_codes` must name a fee of that program whose classification is `MANDATORY`, with no duplicates — a repeated code is the rank-7 `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` of §2.6 rather than a roll-in violation; `OPTIONAL` and `CONDITIONAL` fees never enter the model and may not appear in the array, a violation being rejected with `ERR_ROLL_IN_FEE_NOT_MANDATORY` (HTTP 422). The array must then agree with the declared `scenario`: `STATUS_QUO` requires it to be empty, `FULL_ROLL_IN` requires it to name **every** mandatory code of the program, and `PARTIAL_ROLL_IN` requires a non-empty **proper** subset. A non-empty array under `STATUS_QUO`, an incomplete array under `FULL_ROLL_IN`, or an empty or complete array under `PARTIAL_ROLL_IN` is rejected with `ERR_ROLL_IN_SCENARIO_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). A program holding no `MANDATORY` fee at all therefore admits both `STATUS_QUO` and `FULL_ROLL_IN` with an empty array — the empty set does name every mandatory code — while `PARTIAL_ROLL_IN` is unreachable for such a program. The `variant` is `RATE_PARITY`, where the operator moves the fee into the rate and keeps the pre-tax rate whole so the guest's tax base rises and the guest pays more, or `TAX_ABSORBED`, where the operator holds the guest's tax-inclusive number constant and back-solves the rate.

The economics are stated per night and per room against two fixed reference constants — 2 guests and 4 nights — so that models are comparable across properties. Every step rounds half up to whole cents. Throughout, the jurisdiction's `occupancy_tax_rate_bps` is the tax rate.

**The baseline is always computed**, is identical under both variants, and is what the modelled side equals under `STATUS_QUO`. Step B1 sets `baseline_adr_cents` to the property's `base_room_rate_cents`. Step B2 selects the commission tier keyed on that ADR — 1500 basis points when the ADR is at most 19999, 1800 basis points when it is at most 34999, and 2100 basis points otherwise — and sets `baseline_commission_cents` to the ADR times those basis points divided by 10000, rounded half up. Step B3 retains **every** mandatory fee, each rounded per night against `baseline_adr_cents` under the per-night rounding order, with a `PER_STAY` fee contributing its `amount_cents` divided by the 4 reference nights and rounded half up. Step B4 sets `baseline_tax_cents` to the sum of `baseline_adr_cents` and the retained fees whose taxability rule is **not** `EXEMPT`, times the jurisdiction rate divided by 10000, rounded half up **once** over that aggregate. Step B5 sets `baseline_guest_total_cents` to `baseline_adr_cents` plus the retained taxable fees plus the retained exempt fees plus `baseline_tax_cents`. Step B6 sets `baseline_net_revenue_cents` to `baseline_adr_cents` plus the retained taxable fees plus the retained exempt fees minus `baseline_commission_cents`; equivalently it is `baseline_guest_total_cents` less `baseline_tax_cents` less `baseline_commission_cents`.

**The modelled side runs in a pinned six-step order that is not interchangeable.** The order matters because step M2 changes the ADR, step M3's commission **tier** is keyed on the ADR that M2 produced, and step M4 re-derives every `PERCENT_OF_ROOM` fee against that same new ADR; evaluating commission before the roll-in, or holding a percent fee at its baseline value, yields different integers. Step M1 sets the rolled amount to the sum, over the codes in `roll_in_fee_codes`, of each fee's per-night amount rounded against `baseline_adr_cents`, a `PER_STAY` fee contributing its `amount_cents` divided by 4 and rounded half up. Step M2 sets `modeled_adr_cents`: under `RATE_PARITY` it is `baseline_adr_cents` plus the rolled amount; under `TAX_ABSORBED` it is `baseline_adr_cents` plus the rolled amount, times 10000, divided by 10000 plus the jurisdiction rate, rounded half up — which lowers the ADR below the parity figure, drops the commission tier input and shrinks every percent-of-room fee. Step M3 sets `applied_commission_bps` to the tier selected by `modeled_adr_cents` under the same three thresholds, and `modeled_commission_cents` to `modeled_adr_cents` times `applied_commission_bps` divided by 10000, rounded half up. Step M4 identifies the surviving fees as the mandatory fees whose code is **not** in `roll_in_fee_codes`, each rounded per night against `modeled_adr_cents`, so a `PERCENT_OF_ROOM` fee grows under `RATE_PARITY` and shrinks under `TAX_ABSORBED`. Step M5 sets `modeled_tax_cents` to `modeled_adr_cents` plus the surviving fees whose rule is not `EXEMPT`, times the jurisdiction rate divided by 10000, rounded half up once over that aggregate. Step M6 sets `modeled_guest_total_cents` to `modeled_adr_cents` plus the surviving taxable fees plus the surviving exempt fees plus `modeled_tax_cents`, and `modeled_net_revenue_cents` to `modeled_adr_cents` plus the surviving taxable fees plus the surviving exempt fees minus `modeled_commission_cents`.

Five signed deltas are then written, each the modelled figure minus the baseline figure and each free to be negative: `modeled_adr_delta_cents`, `modeled_commission_delta_cents`, `modeled_tax_delta_cents`, `modeled_guest_total_delta_cents`, and `modeled_net_revenue_delta_cents`, the last being the headline number of the model.

A worked reference pins every figure. Take a `base_room_rate_cents` of 29900, a jurisdiction rate of 1338 basis points, and three mandatory fees: a percentage fee at 750 basis points, a per-night fee of 1495, and an exempt per-person-per-night fee of 325. With `roll_in_fee_codes` naming the per-night fee and the exempt fee, under `PARTIAL_ROLL_IN`, the baseline is an ADR of 29900, a tier of 1800 basis points, a commission of 5382, retained taxable fees of 3738 — that is 2243 plus 1495 — retained exempt fees of 650, a tax base of 33638, a tax of 4501, a guest total of 38789 and a net revenue of 28906. Under `RATE_PARITY` the rolled amount is 2145, `modeled_adr_cents` is 32045, `applied_commission_bps` is 1800, the commission is 5768, the surviving percentage fee is 2403, the tax base is 34448, the tax is 4609, the guest total is 39057 and the net revenue is 28680, giving deltas of plus 2145 on ADR, plus 386 on commission, plus 108 on tax, plus 268 on guest total and **minus 226** on net revenue. Under `TAX_ABSORBED` the same inputs give a `modeled_adr_cents` of 28263, `applied_commission_bps` of 1800, a commission of 5087, a surviving percentage fee of 2120, a tax base of 30383, a tax of 4065, a guest total of 34448 and a net revenue of 25296, giving deltas of minus 1637 on ADR, minus 295 on commission, minus 436 on tax, minus 4341 on guest total and **minus 3610** on net revenue.

The commission-tier boundary is exact and is keyed on the **modelled** ADR. With the same roll-in set under `RATE_PARITY`, a `base_room_rate_cents` of 32854 produces a `modeled_adr_cents` of 34999, an `applied_commission_bps` of 1800 and a `modeled_commission_cents` of 6300; a `base_room_rate_cents` of 32855 produces a `modeled_adr_cents` of 35000, an `applied_commission_bps` of 2100 and a `modeled_commission_cents` of 7350. One cent of rate moves the commission by 1050 cents.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is served under the root path prefix. Creation endpoints return HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`; every other endpoint, including all reads, all list endpoints and every lifecycle action, returns HTTP 200 with the affected or requested resource in `data`. Error responses use the status codes catalogued in §5 and always carry the envelope described in §2.2. Every endpoint except the clock read requires the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers, and role checks precede all other validation.
