# Wildfire Mitigation PSPS API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Western electric utilities operate distribution circuits through terrain where a single wind-driven conductor fault can start a catastrophic fire. The regulatory answer has two halves that pull against each other. The first half is *mitigation*: hardening the grid over multi-year programmes — covered conductor, undergrounding, enhanced powerline safety settings, and shortened inspection cycles — each booked against a plan year, reviewed by a regulator, and reported back with audited mileage. The second half is *de-energization*: when a fire-weather forecast crosses a threshold, the utility deliberately switches off circuits, accepting an outage in order to avoid an ignition. Both halves are adversarial to sloppy record-keeping, because both are litigated after the fact.

This document specifies the API for the system that runs both halves on one data model. It ingests weather telemetry from a station network, blends those observations into a per-circuit **fire potential index**, and turns the resulting tier into a **risk posture** that gates whether hardening crews may work on the circuit at all. When the forecast justifies a **Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS)**, the system determines the shutoff scope by walking the feeder topology downstream from every at-risk circuit, scores the trade-off between the ignition risk avoided and the customer harm inflicted, sequences multilingual and accessibility-aware customer notifications with hard lead-time floors, opens community resource centers for medical-baseline and critical-facility customers, and — after meteorology declares the weather over — drives the patrol, damage-assessment, and re-energization cycle that puts the circuits back in service.

Three design commitments run through everything that follows. **Segregation of duties**: the reviewer who sent a project back for revision may not approve it, the crew lead who cleared a circuit may not re-energize it, and one reviewer may not clear both tracks of a plan filing. **Conservation**: an arm-then-cancel pair must return the served-customer population to exactly its prior value, and a record-completion-then-withdraw pair must return the hardened-mileage ledger to exactly its prior value — no drift, no tolerance. **Determinism**: every ordering, tie-break, threshold, and rounding step in this document is normative, because the after-action report is a regulatory filing and not an estimate.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the human or service acting, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that authorises the action. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the eight roles below, the request is likewise rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) — an unrecognised role is treated as a failure to authenticate, not as a permission problem. If the role is recognised but is not listed among the authorised roles for the endpoint being called (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `GridPlanner`, `MeteorologyAnalyst`, `PSPSDutyOfficer`, `CommunityLiaison`, `RegulatoryReviewer`, `FieldPatrolSupervisor`, `AssetInspector`, and `TransmissionEngineer`.

- A `GridPlanner` owns the static grid model and the mitigation portfolio: registering circuits, booking and submitting work packages, and opening and filing wildfire mitigation plans.
- A `MeteorologyAnalyst` runs the weather desk: filing telemetry observations, computing fire potential indices, and — uniquely — declaring the damaging-weather window over.
- A `PSPSDutyOfficer` holds switching authority: opening events, determining scope, arming, de-energizing, activating resource centers, re-energizing cleared circuits, closing, and cancelling.
- A `CommunityLiaison` owns customer communication and resource-center inventory: notification profiles, batch planning, dispatch, and site registration.
- A `RegulatoryReviewer` is the regulator-side decision-maker on mitigation projects and plan-filing review tracks, and is subject to every segregation-of-duties gate in this specification.
- A `FieldPatrolSupervisor` runs post-event patrols and damage assessment.
- An `AssetInspector` records executed mitigation mileage and inspection completions against approved work packages.
- A `TransmissionEngineer` reviews determined scope and is the only role that may hold a circuit out of a scoped or armed event.

For all eight roles, `X-User-ID` is an **opaque operator identifier** that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any row's `id`. There is no entity-backed identity in this system: the server does not resolve `X-User-ID` to a `Customer`, a `Circuit`, or any other resource, and no ownership check anywhere compares a resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. The server does, however, *record* the acting identifier where the specification calls for it — `X-User-ID` is persisted as `last_revision_reviewer_user_id` on a mitigation project when a revision is requested, and as `safety_reviewer_user_id` or `fiscal_reviewer_user_id` on a plan filing when the corresponding review track is decided. Those recorded identifiers are then compared against the `X-User-ID` of a *later* request to enforce segregation of duties (§2.9). The same comparison shape applies to `patrol_lead_user_id`, which is supplied in a request body rather than taken from a header, and is compared against the `X-User-ID` of the operator attempting re-energization.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from the host's wall clock.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 when omitted, and its minimum accepted value is 0 (there is no upper bound). A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); so is a `limit` or `offset` whose text is not a plain optionally-signed integer.

Unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The one documented departure is the observation listing for a weather station, which is ordered by `observed_at` ascending. Because `created_at` is read from the logical clock and many rows therefore share the identical instant, every ordering and every "greatest `created_at`" selector in this specification breaks a `created_at` tie by the server's row-creation sequence — the row written later in wall-clock terms sorts later, and never by identifier. Filter values that name a foreign key are matched as plain strings and are *not* required to resolve: an unmatched `upstream_circuit_id`, `psps_event_id`, `circuit_id`, `trigger_fire_potential_index_id`, or `forecast_date` simply yields an empty page. The one exception is `GET /notification-records`, where a supplied `notification_batch_id` or `customer_id` must resolve to an existing row or the request is rejected with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_BATCH_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

Every list response populates four fields inside `meta`: `limit` (the effective page size actually applied), `offset` (the effective offset actually applied), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters *before* the page window is applied), and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page, false otherwise). Single-resource reads and action endpoints omit these four fields from `meta`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID. Identifiers never carry a type prefix, and clients never supply them on creation. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp: an ISO 8601 UTC string with three fractional digits, fixed at the logical-clock instant (§2.5) at which the row was written. `updated_at` is likewise read from the logical clock.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation. A mutation includes any change to a *computed* field, not only to operator-supplied input: when a customer registration raises a circuit's `customer_count`, when a completion record moves a circuit's `hardened_fraction`, or when a resource-center activation moves an event's `net_benefit_ratio`, the affected row's `version` increments and its `updated_at` refreshes. The resources carrying `updated_at` and `version` are `Circuit`, `WeatherStation`, `PSPSEvent`, `EventScope`, `Customer`, `NotificationBatch`, `MitigationProject`, `PatrolAssignment`, `CommunityResourceCenter`, and `WildfirePlanFiling`.

Four resource types are **append-only records** and carry only `id` and `created_at`: both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from their representations entirely. These are `WeatherObservation`, `FirePotentialIndex`, `NotificationRecord`, and `DamageFinding`. A fire potential index, in particular, is immutable once computed — a later forecast produces a new row rather than editing an existing one, which is why the risk posture selects among rows rather than reading one.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every deadline, lead time, inspection cycle, outage duration, and reset buffer in this specification is evaluated against a **logical clock** rather than the host's wall clock. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server never falls back to real time. A test-driving endpoint, `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, accepts a body field `to_iso8601` carrying an ISO 8601 UTC instant, positions the clock at that instant, and returns an object with a single field `current_utc` echoing the new clock reading back as the exact `to_iso8601` string the client supplied, character for character, rather than as a renormalised three-fractional-digit rendering. Setting the clock mutates no entity and increments no `version`. All eight roles may call it.

Wherever this document says a field is "stamped from the logical clock" — `actual_de_energized_at`, `actual_restored_at`, `dispatched_at`, `started_at`, `completed_at`, `found_at`, `activated_at`, `demobilized_at`, `restored_at` — the value written is the current logical clock reading at the moment the request is processed, and it is rendered on output as an ISO 8601 UTC string with three fractional digits. The same distinction as for `current_utc` applies to every datetime field whose value the *client* supplied — `last_inspection_completed_at`, `observed_at`, `last_observation_at`, `forecast_onset_at`, `forecast_all_clear_at`, `scheduled_send_at`, `window_start_at`, `window_end_at`, and the `last_served_all_clear_at` copied from an event's `forecast_all_clear_at`: each is parsed as an instant for every comparison this specification makes, but is echoed back verbatim in the exact textual form the client wrote it.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

All arithmetic in this system is decimal arithmetic; binary floating-point accumulation is not acceptable, because several rules turn on a single cent or a single ten-thousandth. Unless a rule states otherwise, rounding is **round half up** (a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero).

The scales are fixed per quantity and must be honoured exactly:

- `hardened_fraction` and `net_benefit_ratio` carry **4 decimal places**.
- `fpi_score`, `fpi_score_applied`, `latest_forecast_fpi_score`, `ignition_risk_term`, `ignition_risk_avoided_score`, `customer_impact_score`, `crc_mitigation_credit`, `adjusted_customer_impact_score`, `risk_reduction_score`, `avg_notification_lead_minutes`, and `compliance_score` carry **2 decimal places**.
- `covered_conductor_miles`, `underground_miles`, `covered_conductor_miles_reported`, and `underground_miles_reported` are each rounded half up to **1 decimal place** when they are derived. `completed_miles` and `miles_patrolled` are the two apparent members of this group that are *not* quantized: `completed_miles` is the exact running sum of the `completed_miles_delta` values supplied against the project and `miles_patrolled` is the value supplied at patrol completion, and neither input carries a decimal-place limit, so a delta of `0.05` is accepted and reads back as `0.05`.
- `budget_cents`, `duration_hours`, `lead_time_minutes`, `customer_minutes_interrupted`, every `*_count` field, `crc_capacity_required`, `crc_capacity_served`, `attempt_order`, `activation_order`, `review_round`, and `deficiency_round_count` are integers.
- `equipment_condition_index` and `elevation_ft` are **not** among the integer quantities. Each is accepted as any decimal number inside its declared range — no decimal-place limit is imposed on either, so a fractional value such as `55.7` is a valid input — and each is carried and echoed as a decimal value rather than as a JSON integer, so an `equipment_condition_index` supplied as `55` reads back as `55.0`.

Three roundings deviate from "round half up at the end" and are called out where they occur: `budget_cents` is **truncated** toward zero rather than rounded; `duration_hours` is rounded **up** to the next whole hour whenever any fractional hour remains; and `lead_time_minutes` is floored toward negative infinity, so a lead time of minus one and a half minutes becomes minus two.

Two calculations pin the *order* of rounding, and the alternative order produces a different published number. The ignition-risk score rounds **each per-circuit term** to two decimal places and then sums those already-rounded terms; it never sums at full precision and rounds once. The fire potential index, conversely, applies **exactly one** rounding to the score itself, at the very end, after the hardening discount has been applied — which means the four index components (`wind_component`, `dryness_component`, `terrain_component`, `equipment_component`) are themselves subject to no rounding at all and are published at whatever exact decimal precision their inputs produce.

### 2.7. Version Guards on Scope Determination and Mileage Recording

Two write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a *parent* resource's `version`. In both cases the guard exists because the write is legitimately repeatable — a second, strictly sequential call with a refreshed version would succeed on its own merits — so the only thing worth rejecting is a caller acting on a view of the parent that has already been superseded.

`POST /psps-events/{id}/scope` requires a non-optional body field `expected_psps_event_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `PSPSEvent`. Re-scoping hard-deletes the event's existing scope rows and rewrites them from the current forecast, so a caller whose `expected_psps_event_version` no longer equals the event's current `version` is holding a stale picture of the scope set and of every count and net-benefit score derived from it. Such a request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

`POST /mitigation-projects/{id}/record-completion` requires a non-optional body field `expected_circuit_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the project's `Circuit`. Recording mileage moves the circuit's `covered_conductor_miles` or `underground_miles`, its `hardened_fraction`, and through those its `risk_posture`, so a caller whose `expected_circuit_version` no longer equals the circuit's current `version` is computing against numbers that have already moved. Such a request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. In particular, arming, de-energizing, excluding a scope row, dispatching a batch, reviewing a project, completing a patrol, and re-energizing a circuit take no expected-version input, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.8. Validation Order

When more than one rule could reject a request, the rule with the lowest priority number below decides the response. This ordering is normative: a caller who violates two rules receives the error of the earlier one, and a test that provokes a late-priority failure must not be masked by an early-priority one.

1. **Headers present** — `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role recognised** — `X-User-Role` names one of the eight roles in §2.1. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Role authorised** — `X-User-Role` appears in the called endpoint's authorised-role list in §4. Otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
4. **Path identifiers resolve** — every id embedded in the request path resolves to an existing row, validated left to right, so that the leftmost unresolvable segment determines the error. The applicable code is `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WEATHER_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PSPS_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVENT_SCOPE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTIFICATION_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTIFICATION_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MITIGATION_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATROL_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESOURCE_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_PLAN_FILING_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint with a path id.
5. **Request shape** — every required body field is present; every enum value is a declared member; every numeric value sits inside its declared range (`overhead_miles` greater than 0; `equipment_condition_index` from 0 through 100 inclusive; `representativeness_weight` greater than 0 and at most 1; `site_capacity` at least 1; `planned_miles` greater than 0; `unit_cost_per_mile_cents` at least 0; `completed_miles_delta` greater than 0; `miles_patrolled` at least 0; `plan_year` from 2020 through 2100 inclusive; `limit` from 1 through 100 inclusive; `offset` at least 0); `notification_channels` is a non-empty, duplicate-free subset of `SMS`, `VOICE`, and `EMAIL`; `accessibility_needs` is a duplicate-free subset of `LARGE_PRINT`, `TTY`, and `AUDIO`; every datetime parses as an ISO 8601 UTC instant; and `forecast_date` matches the four-digit-year, two-digit-month, two-digit-day pattern `YYYY-MM-DD`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint with a request body or query parameters.
6. **Body foreign keys resolve** — every foreign-key identifier carried in the request body resolves to an existing row: `circuit_id`, `weather_station_id`, `psps_event_id`, `trigger_fire_potential_index_id`, `patrol_assignment_id`, and each member of `requested_resource_center_ids`. The applicable code is `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WEATHER_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PSPS_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FIRE_POTENTIAL_INDEX_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATROL_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_RESOURCE_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404. Applies to `POST /weather-stations`, `POST /weather-observations`, `POST /fire-potential-indices`, `POST /psps-events`, `POST /customers`, `POST /notification-batches`, `POST /mitigation-projects`, `POST /patrol-assignments`, `POST /damage-findings`, and `POST /psps-events/{id}/activate-resource-centers`.
7. **Parent version guard** — the OCC field equals the guarded parent's current `version`, as specified in §2.7. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Applies to exactly two endpoints: `POST /psps-events/{id}/scope` and `POST /mitigation-projects/{id}/record-completion`.
8. **Own-state guard** — the target resource itself is in a state that admits the requested transition. The applicable code is `ERR_EVENT_NOT_SCOPED`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_ARMED`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_IN_RESTORATION`, `ERR_EVENT_TERMINAL`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_CANCELLABLE`, `ERR_SCOPE_ALREADY_EXCLUDED`, `ERR_BATCH_ALREADY_DISPATCHED`, `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PATROL_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_FILING_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_REVIEW_TRACK_ALREADY_DECIDED`, `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_CLEARED_FOR_RESTORE`, or `ERR_RESOURCE_CENTER_ALREADY_ACTIVE`, each HTTP 409. Applies to every state-transition endpoint.
9. **Governing-entity state guard** — the *other* entity that governs the operation is in the required state: a circuit's `risk_posture` is anything other than `LOCKED` for mitigation writes; a circuit's `energization_state` is `DE_ENERGIZED`, `PATROL_IN_PROGRESS`, or `CLEARED_FOR_RESTORE` for patrol scheduling and starting; the governing event's `scope_status` is `RESTORING` for patrol start and re-energization; and the parent event's `scope_status` is `SCOPED` or `ARMED` for scope exclusion. The applicable code is `ERR_CIRCUIT_POSTURE_LOCKED`, `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_DE_ENERGIZED`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_IN_RESTORATION`, or `ERR_EVENT_NOT_SCOPED`, each HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /mitigation-projects`, `POST /mitigation-projects/{id}/submit`, `POST /patrol-assignments`, `POST /patrol-assignments/{id}/start`, `POST /circuits/{id}/re-energize`, and `POST /event-scopes/{id}/exclude`.
10. **Field-clearance gates** — re-energization requires that the circuit's latest `COMPLETED` patrol assignment carry an `ignition_risk_finding_count` of exactly 0, and closing an event requires that every `CONFIRMED` scope row of the event already carry a `restored_at`. The applicable code is `ERR_IGNITION_RISK_DAMAGE_UNRESOLVED` or `ERR_PATROL_NOT_COMPLETED`, each HTTP 422. Applies to `POST /circuits/{id}/re-energize` and `POST /psps-events/{id}/close`.
11. **Notification-completeness gates** — arming requires an `ADVANCE_48H` batch for the event whose `status` is `DISPATCHED` or `FAILED_PARTIAL`; de-energizing additionally requires an `IMMINENT_1H` batch in which every `TIER_A_CRITICAL` and `TIER_B_MEDICAL` customer on a `CONFIRMED`-scoped circuit was `DELIVERED`. The applicable code is `ERR_ADVANCE_NOTIFICATION_MISSING` or `ERR_CRITICAL_NOTIFICATION_INCOMPLETE`, each HTTP 422. Applies to `POST /psps-events/{id}/arm` and `POST /psps-events/{id}/de-energize`.
12. **Segregation of duties** — the `APPROVE` decision on a mitigation project must come from an `X-User-ID` other than `last_revision_reviewer_user_id`; re-energization must come from an `X-User-ID` other than the clearing patrol's `patrol_lead_user_id`; and the two review tracks of one plan-filing round must be decided by different `X-User-ID` values. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /mitigation-projects/{id}/review`, `POST /circuits/{id}/re-energize`, and `POST /plan-filings/{id}/reviews`.
13. **Interval and lead-time well-formedness** — `forecast_all_clear_at` is strictly later than `forecast_onset_at`; `window_end_at` is strictly later than `window_start_at`; an advance-stage or imminent-stage batch's `scheduled_send_at` is strictly earlier than the event's `forecast_onset_at`; and the realised dispatch lead time meets the stage minimum. The applicable code is `ERR_EVENT_WINDOW_INVALID`, `ERR_PATROL_WINDOW_INVALID`, `ERR_NOTIFICATION_WINDOW_INVALID`, or `ERR_NOTIFICATION_LEAD_TIME_SHORT`, each HTTP 422. Applies to `POST /psps-events`, `POST /patrol-assignments`, `POST /notification-batches`, and `POST /notification-batches/{id}/dispatch`.
14. **Required related rows** — the event has at least one `CONFIRMED` scope row; scope determination found at least one circuit with a qualifying forecast index; index computation found at least one in-window observation on an eligible station; and a filing has at least one qualifying mitigation project for its plan year. The applicable code is `ERR_NO_CIRCUITS_IN_SCOPE`, `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_CIRCUITS`, `ERR_OBSERVATION_WINDOW_EMPTY`, or `ERR_FILING_METRICS_INCOMPLETE`, each HTTP 422. Applies to `POST /psps-events/{id}/scope`, `POST /psps-events/{id}/arm`, `POST /notification-batches`, `POST /notification-batches/{id}/dispatch`, `POST /fire-potential-indices`, and `POST /plan-filings/{id}/submit`.
15. **Natural-key uniqueness** — `circuit_code`, `station_code`, `event_code`, `account_number`, `project_code`, `resource_center_code`, `filing_code`, and a plan filing's `plan_year` are each unique across all rows of their own entity. A collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (409). Applies to `POST /circuits`, `POST /weather-stations`, `POST /psps-events`, `POST /customers`, `POST /mitigation-projects`, `POST /community-resource-centers`, and `POST /plan-filings`.
16. **Interval-occupation conflicts** — a circuit may not sit in a `CONFIRMED` scope of two live events whose windows overlap; a resource center may not be activated when the new event's onset falls strictly inside its previous service window extended by the reset buffer; and a circuit may not hold two `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` patrol windows whose closed intervals intersect. The applicable code is `ERR_CIRCUIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_RESOURCE_CENTER_BUFFER_CONFLICT`, or `ERR_PATROL_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, each HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /psps-events/{id}/scope`, `POST /psps-events/{id}/activate-resource-centers`, and `POST /patrol-assignments`.
17. **Ceilings and thresholds** — `net_benefit_ratio` must be at least 0.25 to arm; cumulative `completed_miles` may not exceed `planned_miles`; `review_round` may not exceed two revision requests; the activation selector must find at least one eligible site whenever required capacity is positive; and an explicitly named site must sit in a scoped district. The applicable code is `ERR_NET_BENEFIT_BELOW_THRESHOLD`, `ERR_MITIGATION_MILES_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_REVIEW_ROUND_LIMIT`, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_RESOURCE_CENTER`, or `ERR_RESOURCE_CENTER_DISTRICT_MISMATCH`, each HTTP 422. Applies to `POST /psps-events/{id}/arm`, `POST /mitigation-projects/{id}/record-completion`, `POST /mitigation-projects/{id}/review`, and `POST /psps-events/{id}/activate-resource-centers`.

Any state-advancing call whose own target *is the event* — `POST /psps-events/{id}/scope`, `/arm`, `/de-energize`, `/all-clear`, `/close`, `/cancel`, and `/activate-resource-centers` — is rejected with `ERR_EVENT_TERMINAL` (409) at the own-state step when the event's `scope_status` is already `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`, regardless of which specific transition was requested. Calls whose target is some *other* resource never emit `ERR_EVENT_TERMINAL`; they answer with their own governing-entity code instead. So excluding a scope row of a closed or cancelled event yields `ERR_EVENT_NOT_SCOPED`, starting a patrol on one or re-energizing one of its circuits yields `ERR_EVENT_NOT_IN_RESTORATION`, and planning or dispatching a notification batch inspects the event's `scope_status` not at all.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Three gates enforce that no single operator can both create and bless the same risk. Each is evaluated as an exact string comparison between the acting `X-User-ID` and a previously recorded identifier, and each failure carries `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

The **project-approval gate** compares the acting `X-User-ID` against the project's `last_revision_reviewer_user_id`. If they match, the approval is refused; the reviewer who demanded the change may not certify that the change is adequate. When `last_revision_reviewer_user_id` is still null — that is, the project reached submission without ever being sent back — the gate cannot fire and any reviewer may approve.

The **re-energization gate** compares the acting `X-User-ID` against the `patrol_lead_user_id` of the patrol assignment whose clearance the re-energization relies on: the latest `COMPLETED` assignment on that circuit. The crew lead who declared the line clear may not also be the person who closes the breaker.

The **filing-review gate** compares the acting `X-User-ID` against the other track's recorded reviewer identifier. If the other track has already been decided in this round and its `safety_reviewer_user_id` or `fiscal_reviewer_user_id` equals the acting identifier, the second decision is refused. One reviewer may not clear both the safety and the fiscal track of the same round.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline

Several operations mutate more than one entity. Every cascade described in §3 is part of the same request and the same transaction: either all of it lands or none of it does, and every row that a cascade touches has its `version` incremented by exactly 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed, even when the row's visible enum value did not change. "Exactly 1" is a per-request ceiling as well as a floor: when several steps of one request touch the same row — as when scope determination writes a row and then rescores it, or when an event is both restamped and rescored — the row's `version` still advances by only 1 for that request, though its `updated_at` is refreshed on each touch. A row *created* by the request reads `version` 1 on the way out and is never pushed to 2 by a later step of the same request.

Cascades in this system run in a fixed direction and a fixed number of hops, and the hop count matters because intermediate states are observable. Executing a shutoff order stamps the event, then walks the confirmed circuits, then walks each circuit's in-flight work packages — three hops. Re-energizing a circuit recomputes that circuit's posture and resumes its suspended work, then — only if this was the last confirmed circuit awaiting restoration — stamps the event's realised restoration and rescores it, then demobilizes the event's activated resource centers. A cascade never runs backwards: demobilizing a site does not re-energize a circuit.

### 2.11. Conserved Quantities

Two quantities in this system are conserved across a forward action and its compensating action, and conservation is asserted exactly, with no tolerance.

The **energized-customer total** is the sum of `customer_count` over every circuit whose `energization_state` is `ENERGIZED`. Arming an event removes the confirmed circuits from that sum; cancelling the event from an armed state returns them. After such a pair, the energized-customer total must equal its value before the pair, to the integer. The same identity must hold across a complete restoration cycle — arm, de-energize, all-clear, patrol, re-energize — because customer rows are never mutated by any state transition, so any drift can only come from a missed or double-applied cascade.

The **hardened-mileage ledger** is, per circuit, the sum of `covered_conductor_miles` and `underground_miles`. At all times that sum must equal the sum of `completed_miles` over the circuit's mitigation projects whose `program_type` is `COVERED_CONDUCTOR` or `UNDERGROUNDING` and whose `status` is `IN_EXECUTION`, `SUSPENDED`, or `COMPLETED`. Projects of type `FAST_TRIP_SETTING` or `INSPECTION_CYCLE` are excluded from both sides of the identity. Recording completion is the forward action and withdrawal is the compensating action: after such a pair, the per-circuit ledger must equal its pre-completion value and `hardened_fraction` must return to exactly its pre-completion four-decimal value.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Grid Model: Circuits, Feeder Chains and Served Accounts

Everything in this system hangs off the circuit. A `GridPlanner` registers one by supplying a `circuit_code` — the operator-facing identifier, between 3 and 40 characters and unique across all circuits — together with the `district_code` of the operating district it belongs to (also between 3 and 40 characters, and later the matching key for resource-center eligibility), the `hftd_tier` describing its terrain, and two physical attributes: `overhead_miles`, the baseline overhead line mileage, strictly greater than zero and carrying at most one decimal place, and `equipment_condition_index`, an asset-health score from 0 through 100 inclusive where 100 means pristine. The `hftd_tier` is one of `TIER_1`, `TIER_2`, `TIER_3`, or `NON_HFTD`, mirroring the regulator's high fire threat district designations. `overhead_miles` is an immutable baseline — it is the denominator of every hardening ratio the circuit will ever report — and nothing in this specification changes it after registration.

Three further attributes are optional at registration. `inspection_cycle_months`, the nominal number of months between full inspections, must sit between 1 and 120 inclusive and **defaults to 36** — three years — when omitted. `last_inspection_completed_at` records the timestamp of the most recent completed inspection and **defaults to null**, which is read throughout this specification as "never inspected". `fast_trip_enabled` records whether enhanced powerline safety settings are armed on the circuit and **defaults to false**. A fourth optional field, `upstream_circuit_id`, is the system's only self-reference: it names the immediately upstream feeder circuit and **defaults to null**, and a null value means the circuit is fed directly from transmission rather than from another distribution circuit. Unlike every other foreign key in a request body, `upstream_circuit_id` is *not* resolved at registration — it is stored verbatim as whatever string the client supplied, so a value naming no existing circuit is accepted rather than refused with `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_FOUND`, and such a circuit simply behaves as an unreachable child during the feeder walk. Because a circuit stores only its parent, discovering a circuit's children means listing circuits filtered by `upstream_circuit_id` — that filter is the reverse-reachability channel for the feeder chain, alongside filters on `district_code`, `hftd_tier`, `energization_state`, and `risk_posture`.

Upon registration the server initialises the circuit's derived state: `energization_state` starts at `ENERGIZED`, `risk_posture` at `NORMAL`, `hardened_fraction` at zero carried to four decimal places, `covered_conductor_miles` and `underground_miles` at zero, the three population counters `customer_count`, `medical_baseline_count`, and `critical_facility_count` at zero, and both `latest_forecast_fpi_score` and `latest_forecast_fpi_tier` at null because no index has yet scored the circuit. A duplicate `circuit_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

Accounts served by a circuit are registered by either a `GridPlanner` or a `CommunityLiaison`. Each **Customer** carries an `account_number` — unique across all accounts, 3 to 40 characters — a `circuit_id` naming the circuit that serves it (an identifier that does not resolve yields `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_FOUND`, and reading an account that does not exist yields `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`), a `customer_class` of `RESIDENTIAL`, `COMMERCIAL`, or `CRITICAL_FACILITY`, a `preferred_language` chosen from `EN`, `ES`, `ZH`, `TL`, and `VI`, a `notification_channels` set that must be non-empty, duplicate-free, and drawn from `SMS`, `VOICE`, and `EMAIL`, and an `address_zone_code` of 1 to 20 characters that field crews use to locate the service address. Two fields are optional: `medical_baseline`, flagging enrolment in the medical baseline programme, **defaults to false**, and `accessibility_needs`, a duplicate-free set drawn from `LARGE_PRINT`, `TTY`, and `AUDIO`, **defaults to an empty set**. Both channel and accessibility sets are unordered — the order in which a client lists them is never a decision input anywhere in this specification.

Registration computes one derived field, the account's `notification_tier`, from a strict precedence: an account whose `customer_class` is `CRITICAL_FACILITY` is `TIER_A_CRITICAL`; otherwise an account whose `medical_baseline` is true is `TIER_B_MEDICAL`; otherwise the account is `TIER_C_STANDARD`. Service class dominates the medical flag, so a critical facility that also carries a medical baseline enrolment is `TIER_A_CRITICAL` and not `TIER_B_MEDICAL`. Registration then rolls the account up onto its circuit: `customer_count` increases by one always, `medical_baseline_count` increases by one when `medical_baseline` is true, and `critical_facility_count` increases by one when `customer_class` is `CRITICAL_FACILITY` — so a medical-baseline critical facility increments all three. The circuit's `version` increments and its `updated_at` refreshes as a result. Accounts on a circuit can be listed filtered by `customer_class` and `medical_baseline`; a duplicate `account_number` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

### 3.2. The Weather Desk: Station Networks, Telemetry and the Fire Potential Index

Monitoring stations are the sensory apparatus of the whole system. A station is added to a circuit's network by a `GridPlanner` or a `MeteorologyAnalyst` with a `station_code` unique across all stations and 3 to 40 characters long, the `circuit_id` of the circuit whose index it feeds (unresolvable identifiers yield `ERR_WEATHER_STATION_NOT_FOUND` when reading a station, and `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_FOUND` when the referenced circuit is missing), an `elevation_ft` between −300 and 15,000 feet above sea level inclusive, and a `representativeness_weight` strictly greater than 0 and at most 1 with at most two decimal places, expressing how well the station speaks for the circuit's conditions. `telemetry_status` is optional and **defaults to `ONLINE`**; the accepted values are `ONLINE`, `DEGRADED`, and `OFFLINE`. New stations start with `observation_count` at zero and `last_observation_at` at null. Stations feeding a circuit can be listed filtered by `telemetry_status`. A duplicate `station_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

Telemetry arrives as observations filed by a `MeteorologyAnalyst` against a station. Each observation names its `weather_station_id`, the `observed_at` instant it applies to, and four measurements: `wind_gust_mph` (peak gust, from 0 through 200 inclusive, at most one decimal place), `relative_humidity_pct` (from 0 through 100 inclusive, at most one decimal place), `temperature_f` (dry-bulb air temperature in degrees Fahrenheit, from −40 through 140 inclusive, at most one decimal place), and `dead_fuel_moisture_pct` (from 0 through 60 inclusive, at most one decimal place, where a lower number means drier fuel). `temperature_f` is retained for the regulatory record and for human interpretation of the telemetry, but it is not an input to any calculation in this specification. Filing an observation increments the station's `observation_count` by one, sets `last_observation_at` to the greatest `observed_at` among the station's observations, and increments the station's `version`. A station's observations are listed in `observed_at` ascending order.

A **fire potential index** is the blended, scored judgement about one circuit on one forecast day. Only a `MeteorologyAnalyst` may compute one, by supplying the `circuit_id`, the `forecast_date` as a `YYYY-MM-DD` UTC calendar day, and the `computation_variant` — either `FORECAST` or `VALIDATED`. The computation proceeds in six steps, and every step is normative.

**Eligible stations.** The candidate set is the circuit's stations whose `telemetry_status` is `ONLINE` or `DEGRADED`; `OFFLINE` stations are dropped entirely and appear in neither the numerator nor the denominator of any average. Each remaining station gets an effective weight equal to its `representativeness_weight`, halved for a `DEGRADED` station and taken as-is for an `ONLINE` one.

**Observation window.** Let the day anchor be `forecast_date` at midnight UTC. The selection window opens 24 hours before that anchor and closes 24 hours after it — a 48-hour span — and it is half-open: an observation whose `observed_at` falls exactly on the opening boundary is inside the window, and one falling exactly on the closing boundary is outside it. A station with no observation inside the window contributes nothing and is dropped from the blend. `contributing_station_count` is the number of eligible stations that supplied at least one in-window observation; when that number is zero the computation is refused with `ERR_OBSERVATION_WINDOW_EMPTY`.

**Per-station reduction — variant-dependent.** Under `FORECAST` the reduction is a worst-case envelope across the station's in-window observations: the greatest `wind_gust_mph`, the smallest `relative_humidity_pct`, and the smallest `dead_fuel_moisture_pct`, each taken independently and therefore possibly from three different observations. Under `VALIDATED` the reduction takes a single observation — the one with the greatest `observed_at`, breaking ties by the greatest `created_at` — and uses its three values exactly as recorded.

**Weight blend — variant-dependent rounding.** Let the total weight be the sum of the effective weights of the contributing stations. For each of the three measurements, the blended value is the sum over contributing stations of that station's effective weight multiplied by its reduced value for that measurement, divided by the total weight. Under `FORECAST`, each blended value is rounded half up to one decimal place *before* the components are computed, and the rounded numbers are what `effective_wind_gust_mph`, `effective_relative_humidity_pct`, and `effective_dead_fuel_moisture_pct` report. Under `VALIDATED`, the blended values are carried at full precision into the components with no intermediate rounding, and those same full-precision quotients — not a rounded rendering of them — are what the three `effective_*` fields report. Against identical observation rows the two variants therefore produce different published numbers — that is the point of keeping both.

**Components.** Four sub-scores, each confined to the range 0 through 100, are computed identically under both variants. `wind_component` is the effective wind gust minus a 20 mph base, multiplied by 2.5, then clamped so that a negative result becomes 0 and a result above 100 becomes 100. `dryness_component` is the effective relative humidity subtracted from 100, plus the quantity formed by subtracting the effective dead fuel moisture from a 12 percent reference and multiplying that difference by 4; the sum is then clamped to the range 0 through 100 (note that a fuel moisture above the reference contributes a negative amount, which can pull the pre-clamp sum down). `terrain_component` is fixed by the circuit's `hftd_tier`: 100 points for `TIER_3`, 70 for `TIER_2`, 40 for `TIER_1`, and 10 for `NON_HFTD`. `equipment_component` is the circuit's `equipment_condition_index` subtracted from 100, plus a 30-point penalty when the inspection cycle has lapsed, clamped to the range 0 through 100. The boolean `inspection_overdue` records whether that penalty applied: it is true when the circuit's `last_inspection_completed_at` is null, and also true when the logical clock has reached or passed `last_inspection_completed_at` advanced by the circuit's `inspection_cycle_months` where each cycle month is treated as exactly 30 days — so a circuit on the default three-year cycle is overdue 1,080 days after its last inspection, at the exact instant as well as after it.

**Score and tier.** The raw score is the weighted sum of the four components with fixed weights: 35 percent on the wind component, 30 percent on the dryness component, 20 percent on the terrain component, and 15 percent on the equipment component. `hardened_fraction_applied` snapshots the circuit's `hardened_fraction` at computation time, and the raw score is discounted by multiplying it by one minus 40 percent of that snapshot — so a fully hardened circuit keeps 60 percent of its raw score, and mitigation can never discount a circuit's index below that floor. `fpi_score` is the discounted result rounded half up to two decimal places, and that is the **only** rounding applied to the score in either variant. `fpi_tier` follows from `fpi_score` by four cut-points: below 20 is `R1`, from 20 up to but not including 40 is `R2`, from 40 up to but not including 60 is `R3`, from 60 up to but not including 75 is `R4`, and 75 or above is `R5`.

Index rows are immutable once written; a refreshed forecast produces a new row. A `FORECAST` row additionally re-runs the circuit's risk-posture evaluation and refreshes `latest_forecast_fpi_score` and `latest_forecast_fpi_tier`, incrementing the circuit's `version` when the posture or either of those two fields changes. A `VALIDATED` row never moves the posture — it exists purely as the after-action record for regulatory reporting. Indices for a circuit can be listed filtered by `computation_variant` and `forecast_date`.

### 3.3. Risk Posture and the Mitigation Programme Portfolio

A circuit's `risk_posture` is the single gate that decides whether hardening crews may work on it, and it is recomputed whenever a forecast index lands, whenever the circuit's `energization_state` moves, whenever completion mileage is recorded, and whenever a project is withdrawn. Its evaluation has two branches and the first dominates. Every recomputation begins by selecting the circuit's `FORECAST`-variant index with the greatest `forecast_date`, breaking ties by the greatest `created_at` and never by identifier, and mirroring it into `latest_forecast_fpi_score` and `latest_forecast_fpi_tier` — that mirroring happens unconditionally, so those two fields stay current even on a circuit whose posture the next step pins at `LOCKED`, and they are null exactly when no such index exists. Only the *posture* itself has two branches. If the circuit's `energization_state` is any of `DE_ENERGIZATION_PENDING`, `DE_ENERGIZED`, `PATROL_IN_PROGRESS`, or `CLEARED_FOR_RESTORE`, the posture is `LOCKED` no matter how benign the weather looks. Only when the circuit is `ENERGIZED` does the forecast decide. If no index exists the posture is `NORMAL`. If the selected index's `fpi_tier` is `R4` or `R5` the posture is `EXTREME`; if it is `R3` the posture is `ELEVATED`; and for `R1` or `R2` the posture is `NORMAL`. In every non-null case `latest_forecast_fpi_score` and `latest_forecast_fpi_tier` mirror that selected index.

Work packages are booked by a `GridPlanner`. A **MitigationProject** carries a `project_code` unique across all projects and 3 to 40 characters long, the `circuit_id` of the circuit the work hardens, a `program_type` of `COVERED_CONDUCTOR`, `UNDERGROUNDING`, `FAST_TRIP_SETTING`, or `INSPECTION_CYCLE`, the `plan_year` it is booked against (from 2020 through 2100 inclusive, and the join key for plan-filing roll-up), a `planned_miles` scope strictly greater than zero with at most one decimal place, and a `unit_cost_per_mile_cents` of at least zero. Booking is refused with `ERR_CIRCUIT_POSTURE_LOCKED` when the target circuit's posture is `LOCKED` — the utility does not open new hardening work on a circuit that is in the middle of a shutoff — and with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` on a colliding `project_code`.

Two figures are derived at booking. `budget_cents` is `planned_miles` multiplied by `unit_cost_per_mile_cents`, **truncated** to a whole cent rather than rounded. `risk_reduction_score` is `planned_miles` multiplied by a programme effectiveness factor and then by a terrain risk multiplier, rounded half up to two decimal places. The effectiveness factors are 0.62 for `COVERED_CONDUCTOR`, 1.00 for `UNDERGROUNDING`, 0.45 for `FAST_TRIP_SETTING`, and 0.18 for `INSPECTION_CYCLE`; the terrain multipliers, read from the circuit's `hftd_tier` live at the moment of computation, are 1.5 for `TIER_3`, 1.2 for `TIER_2`, 1.0 for `TIER_1`, and 0.5 for `NON_HFTD`. A new project starts with `status` at `DRAFT`, `completed_miles` at zero, `review_round` at zero, and both `last_revision_reviewer_user_id` and `last_review_note` at null.

The project lifecycle contains two loops. The first is the **revision loop**. A `GridPlanner` submits a project from either `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, advancing it to `SUBMITTED`; submission from any other status is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`, and submission is likewise refused with `ERR_CIRCUIT_POSTURE_LOCKED` when the circuit's posture has become `LOCKED` since booking. Every submission recomputes `risk_reduction_score` against the circuit's *current* `hftd_tier`, so a terrain reclassification between rounds moves the score. A `RegulatoryReviewer` then records a decision, choosing among `APPROVE`, `REQUEST_REVISION`, and `WITHDRAW`, with an optional `review_note`. A `REQUEST_REVISION` decision on a `SUBMITTED` project moves it to `REVISION_REQUESTED`, increments `review_round` by one, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `last_revision_reviewer_user_id`, and stores the supplied `review_note` as `last_review_note`; it is permitted only while `review_round` is still below 2, so a third revision request against a project that has already absorbed two is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_ROUND_LIMIT`. Re-submission closes the loop and — importantly — does **not** reset `review_round`, so the two-round ceiling survives across the whole life of the project. An `APPROVE` decision on a `SUBMITTED` project moves it to `APPROVED` and records the supplied `review_note` as `last_review_note`, subject to the project-approval segregation-of-duties gate described in §2.9. On both the `APPROVE` and the `REQUEST_REVISION` branch `last_review_note` is written unconditionally, so omitting `review_note` sets it to null and thereby erases whatever note an earlier round had left there. A `WITHDRAW` decision, by contrast, ignores `review_note` entirely and leaves `last_review_note` at whatever it already held.

Executed work is recorded by an `AssetInspector` against an approved project, supplying `completed_miles_delta` (strictly greater than zero, and carrying no decimal-place limit), a `completion_date`, and `expected_circuit_version` matching the circuit's current `version`. `completion_date` is accepted in either of two textual forms: a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar day, which is read as that day at midnight UTC and stored in the canonical three-fractional-digit rendering, or a full ISO 8601 UTC instant, which is stored and echoed back verbatim as supplied. Each record adds `completed_miles_delta` to the project's `completed_miles`; the call is refused with `ERR_MITIGATION_MILES_EXCEEDED` when the resulting cumulative total would exceed `planned_miles`, and with `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE` when the project's `status` is neither `APPROVED` nor `IN_EXECUTION`. The first record advances the project from `APPROVED` to `IN_EXECUTION`; further records that leave the cumulative total strictly below `planned_miles` keep it in `IN_EXECUTION`; and the record whose cumulative total lands exactly on `planned_miles` advances it to `COMPLETED`.

The programme type decides what each record does to the circuit. A `COVERED_CONDUCTOR` record adds the delta to the circuit's `covered_conductor_miles`; an `UNDERGROUNDING` record adds it to `underground_miles`; an `INSPECTION_CYCLE` record sets the circuit's `last_inspection_completed_at` to the supplied `completion_date`, which is how an overdue circuit clears its 30-days-per-cycle-month inspection penalty; and a `FAST_TRIP_SETTING` project sets the circuit's `fast_trip_enabled` to true at the moment it reaches `COMPLETED`. Neither `FAST_TRIP_SETTING` nor `INSPECTION_CYCLE` ever contributes mileage to the hardening ledger. Every record then recomputes the circuit's `hardened_fraction` and, through it, the circuit's `risk_posture`, and increments both the circuit's `version` and the project's `version` by one each.

`hardened_fraction` is a credited ratio, not a raw one. Covered conductor earns partial credit and undergrounding earns full credit: the numerator is `covered_conductor_miles` multiplied by 0.6 plus `underground_miles` multiplied by 1.0, and the denominator is the circuit's immutable `overhead_miles`. That quotient is rounded half up to four decimal places and then capped at 1.0000, so an over-built circuit reports full hardening rather than a ratio above one. The two mileage accumulators themselves count only work that has actually begun: a project contributes its `completed_miles` only while its `status` is `IN_EXECUTION`, `SUSPENDED`, or `COMPLETED`. Projects in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, or `WITHDRAWN` contribute nothing.

The second loop is the **suspend/resume loop**, and it is driven entirely by circuit posture rather than by any operator action on the project. When a shutoff order locks a circuit's posture, every project on that circuit whose `status` is `IN_EXECUTION` moves to `SUSPENDED` and has its `version` incremented. When re-energization takes the posture back out of `LOCKED`, every project on the circuit whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` returns to `IN_EXECUTION`, again incrementing `version`. Suspension is not a terminal condition and it preserves `completed_miles`, which is why a suspended project keeps contributing to the hardening ledger.

`WITHDRAW` is the compensating action and the only route to the terminal `WITHDRAWN` status. A `RegulatoryReviewer` may withdraw a project whose `status` is `IN_EXECUTION`, `SUSPENDED`, or `COMPLETED`; from any other status the decision is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`, and no decision of any kind is admitted once a project is `WITHDRAWN`. Withdrawal subtracts the project's `completed_miles` from the circuit's `covered_conductor_miles` when the `program_type` is `COVERED_CONDUCTOR` or from `underground_miles` when it is `UNDERGROUNDING`, then sets the project's `completed_miles` back to zero, then recomputes the circuit's `hardened_fraction` and `risk_posture`, and increments both the circuit's `version` and the project's `version` by one each. The three withdrawal edges — out of `IN_EXECUTION`, out of `SUSPENDED`, and out of `COMPLETED` — perform exactly this same reversal; none of them is a lighter-weight variant of the others. The result must restore the per-circuit hardened-mileage ledger and `hardened_fraction` to precisely their pre-completion values, as §2.11 requires. A circuit's projects can be listed filtered by `program_type` and `status`.

### 3.4. Opening a Shutoff Event and Determining Its Scope

A `PSPSDutyOfficer` opens a **PSPSEvent** by naming an `event_code` unique across all events and 3 to 40 characters long, the `trigger_fire_potential_index_id` of the index whose tier justified opening the event (an identifier that does not resolve yields `ERR_FIRE_POTENTIAL_INDEX_NOT_FOUND`), and the two bounds of the damaging-weather window: `forecast_onset_at`, the forecast start, and `forecast_all_clear_at`, the forecast end, which must be strictly later than the onset or the request is refused with `ERR_EVENT_WINDOW_INVALID`. The onset instant is the reference point for every notification lead time in §3.5. A new event starts with `scope_status` at `DRAFT`, `notification_status` at `NOT_STARTED`, `benefit_variant` at `PRE_EVENT`, `cancellation_reason`, `actual_de_energized_at`, and `actual_restored_at` all null, `duration_hours` at zero, and every computed count and score at zero. A duplicate `event_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`; reading an event that does not exist yields `ERR_PSPS_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`.

A single event row carries **two independent state dimensions**. `scope_status` tracks the operational phase through `DRAFT`, `SCOPED`, `ARMED`, `ACTIVE`, `RESTORING`, and terminally either `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`. `notification_status` tracks customer communication through `NOT_STARTED`, `ADVANCE_SENT`, `IMMINENT_SENT`, `ACTIVATION_SENT`, and terminally `RESTORATION_SENT`. Neither dimension gates the other's edges: an event may be sitting at `SCOPED` while its notification dimension has already reached `ADVANCE_SENT`, and the notification dimension may still read `NOT_STARTED` while the operational dimension is being scoped and re-scoped. Events can be listed filtered by `scope_status`, `notification_status`, and `trigger_fire_potential_index_id` — the last of these being the reverse-reachability channel from an index back to the events it justified.

Scope determination is the officer's decision about which circuits actually go dark, and it is guarded by `expected_psps_event_version` as described in §2.7. The call runs from `DRAFT` to `SCOPED` the first time and as a self-loop from `SCOPED` back to `SCOPED` on every re-scope after a forecast refresh; from `ARMED`, `ACTIVE`, or `RESTORING` it is refused with `ERR_EVENT_NOT_SCOPED`, and from `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` with `ERR_EVENT_TERMINAL` — but in every case only *after* the version guard has cleared, since the guard sits at priority 7 and the own-state check at priority 8. A re-scope first **hard-deletes** every existing scope row of the event and then rewrites the set from scratch, and it reverts any circuit that was staged for de-energization but is no longer confirmed back to `ENERGIZED`. This is the only operation in the system that legitimately supersedes rows it did not itself create, which is precisely why it carries the version guard.

The determination itself proceeds in five steps against a target date equal to the UTC calendar date of the event's `forecast_onset_at`. **First**, the primary candidates: for each circuit, the qualifying index is that circuit's `FORECAST`-variant index whose `forecast_date` equals the target date exactly, choosing the one with the greatest `created_at` when several exist. A circuit is a primary candidate when its `energization_state` is `ENERGIZED`, a qualifying index exists for it, and that index's `fpi_tier` is `R4` or `R5`. Primary candidates are processed in circuit `created_at` ascending order. If no circuit qualifies, the call is refused with `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_CIRCUITS`.

**Second**, the fast-trip exemption, which is deliberately boundary-sensitive. A primary candidate whose `fast_trip_enabled` is true **and** whose qualifying index sits at tier `R4` exactly is written as a scope row with `inclusion_basis` of `PRIMARY_RISK` but `scope_status` of `EXCLUDED`, with `exclusion_justification` set to the literal string `FAST_TRIP_MITIGATED_R4`, with `fpi_score_applied` carrying the index's `fpi_score`, and with `ignition_risk_term` pinned at 0.00 — and its downstream subtree is **not** walked, so an exempted circuit shields its dependants from the shutoff too. A tier-`R5` candidate is never exempted regardless of `fast_trip_enabled`, and neither is an `R4` candidate whose fast-trip settings are not armed. Every non-exempted primary candidate is written with `inclusion_basis` of `PRIMARY_RISK`, `scope_status` of `CONFIRMED`, and `fpi_score_applied` equal to its own index's `fpi_score`, and is queued for the downstream walk.

**Third**, the transitive downstream closure. Starting from the confirmed primary circuits in `created_at` ascending order, the walk proceeds breadth-first along the feeder self-reference: for each circuit taken off the front of the queue, its children are the circuits whose `upstream_circuit_id` names it, whose `energization_state` is `ENERGIZED`, and which are not already in this event's scope set. Children are processed in `created_at` ascending order, each written as a scope row with `inclusion_basis` of `DOWNSTREAM_DEPENDENCY` and `scope_status` of `CONFIRMED`, and each is appended to the back of the queue so its own children are reached in turn. A downstream row does not have its own qualifying index — it **inherits** `fpi_score_applied` from the nearest ancestor scope row whose `inclusion_basis` is `PRIMARY_RISK`, found by walking the feeder chain upward.

**Fourth**, the window-conflict guard, applied across the entire candidate set before any row is written. A candidate conflicts when some other event already holds it in a `CONFIRMED` scope row, that other event's `scope_status` is `SCOPED`, `ARMED`, `ACTIVE`, or `RESTORING`, and the two events' damaging-weather windows overlap — meaning the other event's onset is strictly earlier than this event's all-clear **and** this event's onset is strictly earlier than the other event's all-clear. Touching windows, where one event's all-clear falls exactly on the other's onset, do not conflict. Any conflict refuses the whole call with `ERR_CIRCUIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` and leaves no scope rows behind.

**Fifth**, the snapshots. Each written row captures `customer_count_snapshot` from its circuit's `customer_count` at write time — a frozen figure, so later account registrations do not retroactively change what the event reports. The event's `confirmed_scope_count` and `excluded_scope_count` are the counts of its scope rows in each `scope_status`; `impacted_customer_count` is the sum of `customer_count_snapshot` over the `CONFIRMED` rows; `medical_baseline_customer_count` and the event's own `critical_facility_count` are the sums of `medical_baseline_count` and `critical_facility_count` over the circuits of those `CONFIRMED` rows; and `crc_capacity_required` is `medical_baseline_customer_count` plus `critical_facility_count`. Scope rows for an event, or every scope row that has ever named a given circuit, can be listed filtered by `scope_status`; an unresolvable scope row identifier yields `ERR_EVENT_SCOPE_NOT_FOUND`.

Scoping then scores the trade-off. **Ignition risk avoided** is accumulated one scope row at a time, in scope-row `created_at` ascending order. For each `CONFIRMED` row, `ignition_risk_term` is the row's `fpi_score_applied` multiplied by its circuit's `overhead_miles`, multiplied by one minus that circuit's `hardened_fraction` read live at recomputation time, divided by 100, and then rounded half up to two decimal places. Every `EXCLUDED` row carries `ignition_risk_term` pinned at 0.00 rather than being omitted from the set, which keeps the sum stable as circuits are held out. The event's `ignition_risk_avoided_score` is the exact sum of the `ignition_risk_term` values of **all** the event's scope rows — the per-row rounding has already happened, and no further rounding is applied to the total. Rounding once at the end over full-precision terms is a different number and is not what this system publishes.

A `TransmissionEngineer` — and only a `TransmissionEngineer` — may hold a confirmed circuit out of the event by supplying a written `exclusion_justification`, which overwrites whatever the determination had placed there. The row moves from `CONFIRMED` to `EXCLUDED` and its `ignition_risk_term` is set to 0.00; a row that is already `EXCLUDED` yields `ERR_SCOPE_ALREADY_EXCLUDED`, and the operation is admitted only while the parent event's `scope_status` is `SCOPED` or `ARMED`, otherwise `ERR_EVENT_NOT_SCOPED` — including when the parent has reached `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`, because the event is the governing entity here and not the target, so `ERR_EVENT_TERMINAL` never arises on this endpoint. When the circuit had already been staged for shutoff, the exclusion reverts it from `DE_ENERGIZATION_PENDING` back to `ENERGIZED` and recomputes its `risk_posture` out of `LOCKED` from its latest forecast index. The event's counts, `ignition_risk_avoided_score`, and net-benefit figures are all recomputed under the `PRE_EVENT` variant, and both the scope row's `version` and the event's `version` increment by one each.

### 3.5. Sequencing Customer Notifications

Notification is a staged sequence, planned in advance and dispatched against the clock. A `CommunityLiaison` plans one stage at a time by naming the `psps_event_id`, the `stage`, and a `scheduled_send_at` instant. The five stages are `ADVANCE_48H`, `ADVANCE_24H`, `IMMINENT_1H`, `ACTIVATION`, and `RESTORATION`. Planning is refused with `ERR_NO_CIRCUITS_IN_SCOPE` when the event's `confirmed_scope_count` is zero — there is nobody to notify — and, for the three pre-onset stages `ADVANCE_48H`, `ADVANCE_24H`, and `IMMINENT_1H`, with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_WINDOW_INVALID` when `scheduled_send_at` falls at or after the event's `forecast_onset_at`. Neither planning nor dispatch inspects the event's `scope_status` at all: the only population gate is the `confirmed_scope_count` check, so a batch may legitimately be planned for and dispatched against an event that has already reached `ACTIVE`, `RESTORING`, or even `CLOSED`, and a `CANCELLED` event is refused only incidentally, because cancellation has driven its `confirmed_scope_count` to zero. A planned batch starts with `status` at `PENDING`, `dispatched_at` and `lead_time_minutes` at null, and `target_count`, `delivered_count`, and `unreachable_count` all at zero. Batches for an event can be listed filtered by `stage` and `status`; an unresolvable batch identifier yields `ERR_NOTIFICATION_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`.

Dispatch is where the operator's plan meets reality. The `scheduled_send_at` the operator wrote down is a plan, not the measurement: `dispatched_at` is stamped from the logical clock at the moment dispatch runs, and `lead_time_minutes` is computed as the number of whole minutes from `dispatched_at` to the event's `forecast_onset_at`, floored toward negative infinity — so a dispatch that happens after the onset yields a negative lead time. Each stage carries a minimum: `ADVANCE_48H` requires at least 48 hours of lead time, `ADVANCE_24H` at least 24 hours, `IMMINENT_1H` at least 60 minutes, and both `ACTIVATION` and `RESTORATION` require at least zero minutes, which is to say they may be sent right up to the onset instant but not after it. A lead time strictly below the stage minimum is refused with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_LEAD_TIME_SHORT`; a lead time exactly equal to the minimum is accepted. Dispatch also re-checks that the event still has at least one `CONFIRMED` scope row, refusing with `ERR_NO_CIRCUITS_IN_SCOPE` otherwise, and refuses a batch whose `status` is already `DISPATCHED` or `FAILED_PARTIAL` with `ERR_BATCH_ALREADY_DISPATCHED`.

The targets of a dispatch are exactly the accounts whose `circuit_id` appears in a `CONFIRMED` scope row of the batch's event; accounts on `EXCLUDED`-scoped circuits are never contacted. Targets are ordered deterministically, first by notification tier — `TIER_A_CRITICAL` before `TIER_B_MEDICAL` before `TIER_C_STANDARD` — and then by account `created_at` ascending. Each target receives one **NotificationRecord** naming its `notification_batch_id` and `customer_id`, with `attempt_order` recording its 1-based position in that order. The resource identifier is never a decision input to the ordering.

Language selection restricts by stage. The two time-critical stages, `IMMINENT_1H` and `ACTIVATION`, are rendered only in `EN` and `ES`; the other three stages support the full set `EN`, `ES`, `ZH`, `TL`, and `VI`. `language_used` is the account's `preferred_language` when that language is available for the stage, and falls back to `EN` otherwise — so a `VI`-speaking household receives its 48-hour advance warning in Vietnamese but its one-hour imminent warning in English.

Channel selection is an accessibility-filtered waterfall over a **fixed** priority order: `SMS` first, then `VOICE`, then `EMAIL`. The order in which the account listed its `notification_channels` is irrelevant. Accessibility needs remove channels before the waterfall runs: a `TTY` need forbids `VOICE`, and an `AUDIO` need forbids both `SMS` and `EMAIL`. `channel_used` is the first channel in the fixed priority order that the account has consented to and that is not forbidden; when no channel survives the filter, `channel_used` is null. `delivery_status` is `DELIVERED` when a channel was found and `UNREACHABLE` when `channel_used` is null. Independently, `accessibility_format` is `LARGE_PRINT` when the account's `accessibility_needs` include `LARGE_PRINT`, and `STANDARD` otherwise. Consider an account that consented only to `VOICE` and carries an `AUDIO` need: `VOICE` is not forbidden by `AUDIO`, so the account is reachable. An account that consented only to `VOICE` and carries a `TTY` need, by contrast, has no permitted channel and comes out `UNREACHABLE`.

The dispatch then rolls up: `target_count` is the number of records written, `delivered_count` the number whose `delivery_status` is `DELIVERED`, and `unreachable_count` the number whose `delivery_status` is `UNREACHABLE`, which is `target_count` minus `delivered_count`. The batch settles at `DISPATCHED` when `unreachable_count` is zero and at `FAILED_PARTIAL` when it is greater than zero. Both outcomes are terminal for the batch and both are treated identically by the event's notification dimension — the divergence appears later, at the shutoff gates in §3.6, where a `FAILED_PARTIAL` batch still satisfies the advance-notification requirement but can fail the critical-customer requirement. Records can be listed filtered by `notification_batch_id`, `customer_id`, and `delivery_status`; an unresolvable record identifier yields `ERR_NOTIFICATION_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`.

Dispatch also advances the event's `notification_status` along a fixed stage map, and increments both the batch's `version` and the event's `version` by one each. Dispatching an `ADVANCE_48H` or `ADVANCE_24H` batch while the dimension reads `NOT_STARTED` advances it to `ADVANCE_SENT`; dispatching an `IMMINENT_1H` batch while it reads `ADVANCE_SENT` advances it to `IMMINENT_SENT`; dispatching an `ACTIVATION` batch while it reads `IMMINENT_SENT` advances it to `ACTIVATION_SENT`; and dispatching a `RESTORATION` batch while it reads `ACTIVATION_SENT` advances it to the terminal `RESTORATION_SENT`. Because the 48-hour and 24-hour batches both map to `ADVANCE_SENT`, dispatching the second of them when the dimension already reads `ADVANCE_SENT` is a self-loop: records are written, the batch settles, but the dimension neither regresses nor skips ahead.

### 3.6. Arming the Shutoff, Executing It, and Opening Resource Centers

Arming stages the shutoff without opening a single breaker — customers are still being served the whole time. A `PSPSDutyOfficer` arms an event from `SCOPED` to `ARMED`, and three gates must clear first — evaluated not in the order they are narrated below but in the §2.8 priority order, so that an event which simultaneously lacks both an advance batch and any confirmed scope row answers `ERR_ADVANCE_NOTIFICATION_MISSING` (priority 11) rather than `ERR_NO_CIRCUITS_IN_SCOPE` (priority 14), and the net-benefit threshold (priority 17) decides last of the three. The event must have at least one `CONFIRMED` scope row, or the request is refused with `ERR_NO_CIRCUITS_IN_SCOPE`. There must be an `ADVANCE_48H` batch for the event whose `status` is `DISPATCHED` or `FAILED_PARTIAL`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ADVANCE_NOTIFICATION_MISSING` — a partial delivery failure does **not** block arming, because the 48-hour warning has demonstrably gone out. And the event's `net_benefit_ratio` must be at least 0.25, or the request is refused with `ERR_NET_BENEFIT_BELOW_THRESHOLD`; a ratio of exactly 0.2500 clears the gate. Arming then cascades onto every `CONFIRMED`-scoped circuit whose `energization_state` is `ENERGIZED`, moving each to `DE_ENERGIZATION_PENDING`, setting each circuit's `risk_posture` to `LOCKED`, and incrementing each circuit's `version` by one along with the event's own.

Executing the order is a separate, second decision. De-energization is admitted only while the event's `scope_status` is `ARMED`, otherwise `ERR_EVENT_NOT_ARMED`, and it carries the strictest gate in the system: there must be an `IMMINENT_1H` batch for the event whose `status` is `DISPATCHED` or `FAILED_PARTIAL`, **and** every account on a `CONFIRMED`-scoped circuit whose `notification_tier` is `TIER_A_CRITICAL` or `TIER_B_MEDICAL` must have a record in that batch whose `delivery_status` is `DELIVERED`. A missing batch, or a single unreachable critical-facility or medical-baseline account, refuses the order with `ERR_CRITICAL_NOTIFICATION_INCOMPLETE`. This is exactly where a `FAILED_PARTIAL` batch diverges from a `DISPATCHED` one: it satisfies the advance gate but fails this one whenever the unreachable account was a tier-A or tier-B account, while an unreachable `TIER_C_STANDARD` account leaves the order clear to proceed.

When the gate clears, the event advances from `ARMED` to `ACTIVE` and the cascade runs in three hops. The event's `actual_de_energized_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Every `CONFIRMED`-scoped circuit moves from `DE_ENERGIZATION_PENDING` to `DE_ENERGIZED`, keeping its `risk_posture` at `LOCKED`. And because each posture is `LOCKED`, every mitigation project on those circuits whose `status` is `IN_EXECUTION` moves to `SUSPENDED`. Each touched circuit's `version`, each touched project's `version`, and the event's `version` all increment by one.

Community resource centers are the offsetting humanitarian measure, and they are registered independently of any event. A `CommunityLiaison` registers a site with a `resource_center_code` unique across all sites and 3 to 40 characters long, the `district_code` of the operating district it sits in (3 to 40 characters), a `site_capacity` of at least one customer, and an optional `accessibility_certified` flag that **defaults to false**. A new site starts with `status` at `STANDBY` and with `psps_event_id`, `activated_at`, `demobilized_at`, `last_served_all_clear_at`, and `activation_order` all null. A duplicate `resource_center_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`; an unresolvable site identifier yields `ERR_RESOURCE_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`.

Activation runs in one of two modes, chosen by whether the duty officer supplies the optional `requested_resource_center_ids` array; supplying it as an *empty* array still selects explicit mode, which then validates nothing, activates nothing, and returns a summary whose `activated_count` is 0. The operation is admitted from any non-terminal `scope_status` — `DRAFT` and `RESTORING` as readily as `SCOPED` or `ACTIVE` — and only a `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` event refuses it, with `ERR_EVENT_TERMINAL`. Before either mode runs, the server establishes two facts: the required capacity is the event's `crc_capacity_required`, and the scoped districts are the set of `district_code` values of the circuits named by the event's `CONFIRMED` scope rows. It also establishes what a **reset-buffer collision** means: a site collides when its `last_served_all_clear_at` is non-null and the new event's `forecast_onset_at` falls strictly earlier than that instant advanced by a 240-minute — four-hour — reset buffer. An onset falling exactly on the buffer edge is accepted; the buffer is closed at its trailing end.

In **explicit mode**, every breach is reported loudly rather than silently skipped: a site whose `status` is already `ACTIVATED` refuses the call with `ERR_RESOURCE_CENTER_ALREADY_ACTIVE`, a site with a reset-buffer collision refuses it with `ERR_RESOURCE_CENTER_BUFFER_CONFLICT`, and a site whose `district_code` is not among the scoped districts refuses it with `ERR_RESOURCE_CENTER_DISTRICT_MISMATCH`. The three checks are applied as three successive sweeps of the whole named list, taken in exactly that order — the §2.8 order of own-state (8), interval occupation (16), then ceilings (17) — rather than as one per-site pass; so when the first-listed site is merely in the wrong district while a later-listed one is already active, the call answers `ERR_RESOURCE_CENTER_ALREADY_ACTIVE`. Within a single sweep the request array's order decides which of two equal breaches is reported. All named sites are then activated together, and each site's `activation_order` is its 1-based position in the request array.

In **selector mode**, the server builds the candidate pool itself: sites whose `status` is `STANDBY` or `DEMOBILIZED`, whose `district_code` is among the scoped districts, and which have no reset-buffer collision. Sites already `ACTIVATED` are never candidates. Candidates are ranked by three keys in order: `accessibility_certified` descending, so certified sites come before uncertified ones; then `site_capacity` descending, so the largest site of equal certification comes first; then `created_at` ascending as the final tie-break — the site identifier is never used to rank. If the ranked pool is empty while the required capacity is greater than zero, the call is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_RESOURCE_CENTER`. Otherwise the server walks the ranking, stopping as soon as the capacity accumulated so far has reached or exceeded the required capacity, and activates each site it passes with an `activation_order` equal to its 1-based position among the sites activated by this call. Because the stop test runs *before* each activation, the last site activated is the one that carries the running total over the requirement.

Either mode returns a summary object with four fields: `activated_count`, the number of sites activated by this call; `activated_capacity`, the sum of `site_capacity` over those sites; `unmet_capacity`, the required capacity less the event's post-activation `crc_capacity_served`, floored at zero so it never goes negative; and `activated_resource_center_ids`, the identifiers of the sites activated. Each activated site records the `psps_event_id` it is serving, stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock, records its `activation_order`, and sets `last_served_all_clear_at` to the event's `forecast_all_clear_at`. A site being activated for a later event moves from `DEMOBILIZED` back to `ACTIVATED`, overwriting `psps_event_id`, `activated_at`, `activation_order`, and `last_served_all_clear_at` while deliberately leaving the stale `demobilized_at` stamp of the previous cycle in place — `DEMOBILIZED` is therefore not a terminal state, but nor is a site freely reusable, because the four-hour reset buffer measured from the previous event's all-clear must have elapsed by the new event's onset. Activation recomputes the event's `crc_capacity_served` and re-runs the net-benefit calculation, which moves `crc_mitigation_credit`, `adjusted_customer_impact_score`, and `net_benefit_ratio`, and increments both each site's `version` and the event's `version` by one. Sites attached to an event can be listed filtered by `status`.

Demobilization is always a cascade, never a direct operator action. A site whose `status` is `ACTIVATED` moves to `DEMOBILIZED`, with `demobilized_at` stamped from the logical clock, when the event it served reaches a non-null `actual_restored_at`, or is closed, or is cancelled. Its `last_served_all_clear_at` is deliberately left in place so the reset buffer still applies to the next event. Demobilization also recomputes the served event's `crc_capacity_served` and its `net_benefit_ratio` — the credit shrinks as sites stand down, which raises the ratio's denominator again — and increments the site's `version`.

### 3.7. All-Clear, Patrol, Damage Assessment and Re-energization

The weather judgement is deliberately separated from the switching authority: only a `MeteorologyAnalyst` may declare the damaging-weather window over, advancing the event from `ACTIVE` to `RESTORING` and incrementing its `version`. An event that is not `ACTIVE` refuses the declaration with `ERR_EVENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Nothing about restoration can begin before this declaration lands.

Patrols are reserved by a `FieldPatrolSupervisor`. A **PatrolAssignment** names the `psps_event_id` whose restoration it serves and the `circuit_id` to be walked or flown — the one entity in this system that reaches across both aggregates — together with a `patrol_mode` of `GROUND`, `AERIAL`, or `DRONE`, an inclusive `window_start_at`, an inclusive `window_end_at` that must be strictly later than the start or the reservation is refused with `ERR_PATROL_WINDOW_INVALID`, and a `patrol_lead_user_id` of 1 to 64 characters naming the crew lead accountable for the patrol. That crew lead identifier is the segregation-of-duties counterparty for re-energization, so it is worth recording accurately. A reservation is admitted only when the circuit's `energization_state` is `DE_ENERGIZED`, `PATROL_IN_PROGRESS`, or `CLEARED_FOR_RESTORE`, otherwise `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_DE_ENERGIZED`, and it is refused with `ERR_PATROL_WINDOW_OVERLAP` when its closed window from `window_start_at` through `window_end_at` intersects the closed window of another assignment on the same circuit whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` — shared endpoints count as an intersection. A new assignment starts at `SCHEDULED` with `started_at` and `completed_at` null and `miles_patrolled`, `damage_finding_count`, and `ignition_risk_finding_count` all at zero. Assignments can be listed filtered by `psps_event_id`, `circuit_id`, and `status`, which is the reverse-reachability channel for both of the assignment's foreign keys; an unresolvable assignment identifier yields `ERR_PATROL_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

Starting a patrol moves the assignment from `SCHEDULED` to `IN_PROGRESS` and stamps `started_at` from the logical clock; an assignment in any other status refuses the start with `ERR_PATROL_INVALID_STATE`. Two cross-entity conditions also apply: the governing event's `scope_status` must be `RESTORING`, otherwise `ERR_EVENT_NOT_IN_RESTORATION`, and the circuit must be in one of the three post-shutoff energization phases, otherwise `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_DE_ENERGIZED`. The start drives the circuit too: a circuit at `DE_ENERGIZED` advances to `PATROL_IN_PROGRESS`, and a circuit already at `CLEARED_FOR_RESTORE` also advances to `PATROL_IN_PROGRESS` — that second edge is the repair back-edge described below. A circuit already sitting at `PATROL_IN_PROGRESS`, as happens when a second concurrent assignment on the same circuit is started, simply stays there. Both the assignment's `version` and the circuit's `version` increment by one each, the circuit's even on that no-op third edge.

While a patrol is `IN_PROGRESS`, its supervisor files damage assessment findings. Each **DamageFinding** names the `patrol_assignment_id` it belongs to, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR`, or `IGNITION_RISK`, a `description` field note of 1 to 500 characters, and a `requires_repair` boolean stating whether a repair order is needed before the conductor is safe to energize. `found_at` is stamped from the logical clock by the server rather than supplied. Filing a finding increments the assignment's `damage_finding_count` and, when the `severity` is `IGNITION_RISK`, also its `ignition_risk_finding_count`, and increments the assignment's `version`. A finding filed against an assignment that is not `IN_PROGRESS` is refused with `ERR_PATROL_INVALID_STATE`. Findings on an assignment can be listed filtered by `severity` — the only discovery channel for finding rows.

Completing a patrol requires `miles_patrolled`, a value of at least zero carrying no decimal-place limit, and moves the assignment from `IN_PROGRESS` to `COMPLETED`, stamping `completed_at` from the logical clock and recording the reported mileage; an assignment in any other status refuses the completion with `ERR_PATROL_INVALID_STATE`. Completion drives the circuit from `PATROL_IN_PROGRESS` to `CLEARED_FOR_RESTORE` **unconditionally** — even when the patrol filed ignition-risk damage. This is intentional: `CLEARED_FOR_RESTORE` means "the patrol is finished", not "the line is safe". A circuit that is in any other energization state when the patrol completes is left in it. Both versions increment by one each, the circuit's whether or not its state moved.

The safety gate lives on re-energization, which belongs to the `PSPSDutyOfficer`. Four conditions must hold. The circuit's `energization_state` must be `CLEARED_FOR_RESTORE`, otherwise `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_CLEARED_FOR_RESTORE`. The governing event's `scope_status` must be `RESTORING`, otherwise `ERR_EVENT_NOT_IN_RESTORATION` — and the governing event is identified from the circuit's own `CONFIRMED` scope rows that do not yet carry a `restored_at`, taking the newest such row by `created_at` whose event is `RESTORING`, falling back to the newest whose event is otherwise live, and failing with the same code when the circuit holds no unrestored confirmed row at all. The latest `COMPLETED` assignment on that circuit — latest by `completed_at`, ties broken by `created_at` and then by creation sequence — must have an `ignition_risk_finding_count` of exactly zero, otherwise `ERR_IGNITION_RISK_DAMAGE_UNRESOLVED`, and the same code is returned when the circuit has no `COMPLETED` assignment at all — and note that the rule reads **only** the latest completed patrol, which is what makes the repair loop work: after the crew repairs the damage, a follow-up assignment is started on the `CLEARED_FOR_RESTORE` circuit, taking it back to `PATROL_IN_PROGRESS`, and its clean completion becomes the latest completed patrol and clears the gate. Finally, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from that clearing assignment's `patrol_lead_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Re-energization is the largest cascade in the system, and it runs in a fixed order. The circuit moves from `CLEARED_FOR_RESTORE` to `ENERGIZED` and its `risk_posture` is recomputed out of `LOCKED` against its latest `FORECAST` index. Because the posture left `LOCKED`, every mitigation project on the circuit whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` returns to `IN_EXECUTION`. The circuit's scope row for the event has its `restored_at` stamped from the logical clock. Then — and only when **every** `CONFIRMED` scope row of the event now carries a `restored_at` — the event's `actual_restored_at` is stamped, its `duration_hours` is recomputed, and its net-benefit figures are re-run under the `POST_EVENT` variant with `benefit_variant` set accordingly. Because `actual_restored_at` has become non-null, every resource center whose `psps_event_id` is that event and whose `status` is `ACTIVATED` moves to `DEMOBILIZED` with `demobilized_at` stamped. Finally, any patrol assignment still left on the circuit at `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` is moved to the terminal `ABORTED` status with its `version` incremented, because there is nothing left to patrol. The circuit's own `version` increments by one, as does the `version` of every other row the cascade touched.

### 3.8. Closing, Cancelling and the Net-Benefit Accounting

Closing is the duty officer's final act on an event. It is admitted only while the event's `scope_status` is `RESTORING`, otherwise `ERR_EVENT_NOT_IN_RESTORATION`, and only when every `CONFIRMED` scope row of the event already carries a `restored_at` — equivalently, when `actual_restored_at` is non-null — otherwise `ERR_PATROL_NOT_COMPLETED`. Closing moves the event to the terminal `CLOSED` status, demobilizes any resource center still `ACTIVATED` for the event, makes the event eligible for plan-filing metric roll-up, and increments the event's `version`. The `POST_EVENT` scores are frozen only in the sense that no later request re-runs them: the close-time demobilization itself is a coverage change, so whenever it demobilizes at least one center the event's `crc_capacity_served` falls to zero and the `POST_EVENT` credit and ratio are recomputed one last time from that zero coverage before the event goes terminal, leaving `crc_mitigation_credit` at 0.00 and `adjusted_customer_impact_score` equal to `customer_impact_score`.

Cancellation is the compensating action, and it is available only before the breakers open: an event whose `scope_status` is `DRAFT`, `SCOPED`, or `ARMED` may be cancelled with a supplied `cancellation_reason`, while an event that is `ACTIVE` or `RESTORING` refuses the request with `ERR_EVENT_NOT_CANCELLABLE` and an event that is already `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` is caught first by the terminal-event rule of §2.8 and refused with `ERR_EVENT_TERMINAL`. What cancellation *does* depends on how far the event had progressed, and the three edges are not interchangeable. Cancelling from `DRAFT` records the `cancellation_reason` and increments the event's `version` — there are no scope rows and no staged circuits to undo. Cancelling from `SCOPED` additionally sets every `CONFIRMED` scope row of the event to `EXCLUDED` with `exclusion_justification` set to the literal string `EVENT_CANCELLED` and that row's `ignition_risk_term` zeroed to 0.00, and then — because every roll-up must keep matching the rows it is defined over — re-runs the counts, the ignition-risk total, the coverage figure and the whole net-benefit chain over the now entirely `EXCLUDED` scope set, so `confirmed_scope_count`, `impacted_customer_count`, `medical_baseline_customer_count`, the event's `critical_facility_count` and `crc_capacity_required` all fall to zero, `ignition_risk_avoided_score` and `customer_impact_score` to 0.00, and `net_benefit_ratio` to 0.0000, while `excluded_scope_count` rises to the whole scope set. Cancelling from `ARMED` does all of that **and** performs the compensation proper: every circuit sitting at `DE_ENERGIZATION_PENDING` for this event reverts to `ENERGIZED` with its `risk_posture` recomputed out of `LOCKED`, which must restore the energized-customer total of §2.11 to exactly its pre-arm value. The last two side effects are not conditioned on how far the event had progressed and run on all three edges, `DRAFT` included: every resource center already `ACTIVATED` for the event is demobilized, and any patrol assignment on the event still at `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` moves to `ABORTED`. In all three cases `CANCELLED` is terminal.

The **net-benefit ratio** is the number that authorises or forbids a shutoff, and its five steps must be evaluated in the order given, because the credit rate is keyed on the very score the credit then reduces. Evaluating the credit against the already-adjusted score is wrong.

*Step one — raw customer impact.* `duration_hours` is the length of the outage window rounded **up** to the next whole hour whenever any fraction of an hour remains. The impact base is `impacted_customer_count`, plus four times `medical_baseline_customer_count`, plus ten times the event's `critical_facility_count` — a medical-baseline household counts as four ordinary accounts and a critical facility as ten. `customer_impact_score` is that base multiplied by `duration_hours`, divided by 10, and rounded half up to two decimal places.

*Step two — the credit rate.* The rate is read from `customer_impact_score` as computed in step one, on four bands: a score below 20 earns a rate of 0.00, a score from 20 up to but not including 60 earns 0.05, a score from 60 up to but not including 200 earns 0.12, and a score of 200 or above earns 0.20. Larger outages earn a proportionally larger credit for sheltering people.

*Step three — coverage.* `crc_capacity_required` is `medical_baseline_customer_count` plus the event's `critical_facility_count`. `crc_capacity_served` is the sum of `site_capacity` over the resource centers whose `psps_event_id` is this event and whose `status` is `ACTIVATED`; sites that are `DEMOBILIZED` or `STANDBY` contribute nothing. The coverage ratio is `crc_capacity_served` divided by `crc_capacity_required`, where a required capacity of zero is treated as one to keep the division defined, and the quotient is capped at 1 so over-provisioning earns no extra credit.

*Step four — credit and adjustment.* `crc_mitigation_credit` is `customer_impact_score` multiplied by the step-two rate and then by the step-three coverage ratio, rounded half up to two decimal places. `adjusted_customer_impact_score` is `customer_impact_score` less `crc_mitigation_credit`, rounded half up to two decimal places.

*Step five — the ratio.* `net_benefit_ratio` is `ignition_risk_avoided_score` divided by `adjusted_customer_impact_score`, where a denominator below one cent is treated as exactly 0.01 to keep the division defined, and the quotient is rounded half up to four decimal places.

The whole calculation runs under one of two variants, recorded in `benefit_variant`, and they differ only in which pair of instants bounds the outage window. Under `PRE_EVENT` — the default from scope determination until `actual_restored_at` is stamped, and the variant the arming threshold reads — the window runs from `forecast_onset_at` to `forecast_all_clear_at`, so `duration_hours` is the *forecast* outage length and the counts are the scope-time snapshots. Under `POST_EVENT` — computed once `actual_restored_at` is stamped, and frozen at close — the window runs from `actual_de_energized_at` to `actual_restored_at`, so `duration_hours` is the *realised* outage length including all the patrol and restoration time. Identical scope rows and identical resource centers therefore yield a different `customer_impact_score`, possibly a different credit band, and a different `net_benefit_ratio` between the two variants. The calculation is re-run on scope determination, on scope exclusion, on resource-center activation, on resource-center demobilization, and when `actual_restored_at` is stamped.

### 3.9. Regulatory Plan Filings and Performance Reporting

The annual wildfire mitigation plan is what the regulator actually reads, and it is assembled entirely from what the rest of the system recorded. A `GridPlanner` opens a filing with a `filing_code` unique across all filings and 3 to 40 characters long, and a `plan_year` from 2020 through 2100 inclusive that must **also** be unique across all filings — one filing per plan year, and a collision on either key is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. Opening the filing immediately snapshots its metrics and then its compliance score. A new filing starts at `DRAFT` with `safety_review_state` and `fiscal_review_state` both `PENDING`, `safety_reviewer_user_id` and `fiscal_reviewer_user_id` both null, `deficiency_round_count` at zero, and `last_deficiency_note` null. An unresolvable filing identifier yields `ERR_PLAN_FILING_NOT_FOUND`.

The **performance metrics** are computed in a fixed order. The qualifying projects are the mitigation projects whose `plan_year` equals the filing's `plan_year` and whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `IN_EXECUTION`, `SUSPENDED`, or `COMPLETED`; projects in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, or `WITHDRAWN` are excluded entirely. `qualifying_project_count` is how many there are. `covered_conductor_miles_reported` is the sum of `completed_miles` over the qualifying projects whose `program_type` is `COVERED_CONDUCTOR`, rounded half up to one decimal place, and `underground_miles_reported` is the same sum over those whose `program_type` is `UNDERGROUNDING`, also rounded half up to one decimal place.

The event side of the report counts only finished outages. The closed events of a plan year are the events whose `scope_status` is `CLOSED` and whose `actual_de_energized_at` falls in that UTC calendar year; cancelled and in-flight events are excluded. `psps_event_count` is how many there are. `customer_minutes_interrupted` accumulates over those events, taken in `actual_de_energized_at` ascending order with ties broken by event `created_at` ascending, and within each event over its `CONFIRMED` scope rows in scope-row `created_at` ascending order: for each such row, the number of whole minutes from the event's `actual_de_energized_at` to the row's `restored_at`, floored, is multiplied by that row's `customer_count_snapshot`, and the products are summed as integers. Because each row's contribution is a whole-minute integer before multiplication, there is no rounding ambiguity in the total. `avg_notification_lead_minutes` looks only at the 48-hour advance batches of those closed events — batches whose `stage` is `ADVANCE_48H` and whose `status` is `DISPATCHED` or `FAILED_PARTIAL` — and is the sum of their `lead_time_minutes` divided by how many there are, rounded half up to two decimal places; when there are none, it is 0.00.

The **compliance score** is computed immediately after the metrics, in the same request, and reads the three figures the metrics just wrote. It is a weighted attainment score out of 100 built from three ratios, each capped at 1 before weighting: `underground_miles_reported` against a 20.0-mile annual target carries 40 points, `covered_conductor_miles_reported` against a 50.0-mile annual target carries 30 points, and `avg_notification_lead_minutes` against a 48-hour target — 2,880 minutes — carries the remaining 30 points. The three weighted contributions are added, the sum is clamped to the range 0 through 100, and exactly one rounding half up to two decimal places is applied after both the per-ratio caps and the final clamp. A utility that meets or exceeds all three targets scores 100.00; one that undergrounded 10.0 miles, covered 25.0 miles, and averaged 1,440 minutes of advance warning scores exactly half of each component's weight.

Filing with the regulator advances a filing from `DRAFT`, or from `DEFICIENCY_ISSUED` on a cure round, to `SUBMITTED`; a filing in any other status refuses the submission with `ERR_FILING_INVALID_STATE`, and a filing whose `qualifying_project_count` is zero refuses it with `ERR_FILING_METRICS_INCOMPLETE` — there is nothing to report. That count is the one the submission itself just recomputed rather than the value the filing was carrying, and when the refusal fires none of the freshly computed metrics is persisted: the filing is left exactly as it was, `version` included. Each submission that succeeds re-snapshots every reported metric and re-computes `compliance_score`, resets both `safety_review_state` and `fiscal_review_state` to `PENDING`, clears both `safety_reviewer_user_id` and `fiscal_reviewer_user_id`, and increments the filing's `version`. What submission does **not** reset is `deficiency_round_count`, which accumulates across the whole life of the filing and is what eventually exhausts the regulator's patience, nor `last_deficiency_note`, which keeps showing the note from the round being cured.

Review is a **fork-join over two independent tracks**. A `RegulatoryReviewer` records one decision on one track at a time, naming the `review_track` — either `SAFETY`, which writes `safety_review_state` and `safety_reviewer_user_id`, or `FISCAL`, which writes `fiscal_review_state` and `fiscal_reviewer_user_id` — a `decision` of either `CLEAR` or `DEFICIENT`, and an optional `deficiency_note`. A decision is admitted only while the filing's `status` is `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_FILING_INVALID_STATE`, and only while the named track's own state is still `PENDING`; a track already `CLEARED` or `DEFICIENT` in the current round refuses the decision with `ERR_REVIEW_TRACK_ALREADY_DECIDED`. Once those two gates pass, the filing-review segregation-of-duties gate applies to the second decision of a round whatever that decision is: if the other track already records a reviewer identifier and it equals the acting `X-User-ID`, the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` whether the incoming `decision` is `CLEAR` or `DEFICIENT`.

A `CLEAR` decision on the **first** of the two tracks sets that track's state to `CLEARED` and its reviewer identifier to the acting `X-User-ID`, and leaves the filing at `SUBMITTED` waiting for its sibling — one leg of the join has landed. A `CLEAR` decision on the **second** track does the same to its own track and then completes the join: because both tracks now read `CLEARED`, the filing advances to the terminal `APPROVED` status. That second clearance is the usual place the segregation-of-duties gate described above bites — the other track's recorded reviewer identifier must differ from the acting `X-User-ID`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

A `DEFICIENT` decision short-circuits the join instead, and its side-effect set differs from the clear branch in three ways. It sets the named track's state to `DEFICIENT` and its reviewer identifier to the acting `X-User-ID`, it stores the supplied `deficiency_note` as `last_deficiency_note`, and it increments `deficiency_round_count` by one. It also deliberately leaves the sibling track in whatever state it happens to hold — the join simply never completes for this round. While `deficiency_round_count` was below 2 before the decision, the filing moves to `DEFICIENCY_ISSUED`, from which a cure round can re-submit it. When `deficiency_round_count` was already exactly 2, the third deficiency is fatal: the filing moves to the terminal `REJECTED` status with `deficiency_round_count` set to 3. Both `APPROVED` and `REJECTED` are terminal — no further submission or review is admitted.
