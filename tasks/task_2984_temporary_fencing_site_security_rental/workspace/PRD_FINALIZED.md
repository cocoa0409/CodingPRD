# Temporary Fencing & Site Security Rental API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

National Construction Rentals supplies temporary fence panels, gates, barricades and water barriers to construction sites, festivals, film sets and street fairs across dozens of municipal jurisdictions. The commercial reality of that business is that almost nothing about a fence line is priced in isolation: a quote depends on how many linear feet of run the customer wants, whether a mesh or solid wind-load screen is stretched across it, whether the contract is a three-day street festival or a fourteen-month highway job, and whether the posts are being driven into soil, cut into asphalt or bolted to concrete. Once the configuration is priced, an entirely separate set of constraints takes over — a municipality will only allow so much sidewalk closed at once, a crew can only be in one place at a time, a yard only owns so many panels, and an account with an overdue balance should not be receiving new inventory.

Today those constraints live in spreadsheets, permit binders and dispatchers' heads, and the failures they produce are expensive: fence goes out on a job whose sidewalk-closure permit was never approved, two crews are booked into the same afternoon, a partial install is billed as though it were complete, or fifty panels come back from a jobsite with nobody able to say how many of them were stolen. This API is the single system of record intended to eliminate that class of failure. It owns the quote, the contract, the permit, the labor visit, the invoice and the yard count, and it enforces the relationships between them synchronously — every derived figure a request touches is recomputed inside that same request, so the response body a caller receives is already consistent with the change it just made.

The service is mounted under the `/fence-rentals` path prefix, with a single additional test-driving hook mounted at `/_clock/set_current_utc`. It is a pricing engine first and a scheduling engine second: the majority of the rules below are arithmetic whose evaluation order is load-bearing, and the remainder are temporal window comparisons whose boundary inclusivity is load-bearing. Both are specified exactly.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the logical-clock hook carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the function that principal is performing. Both must be present and non-empty; a header supplied as an empty string counts as absent and the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. The role string is then matched exactly and case-sensitively against the set of roles the target endpoint admits, and a request whose role is not in that set — including a request whose role value is not a recognised role at all — is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. An unrecognised role value is a role failure, never a request-shape failure.

Six roles exist, and no role is a superset of another. **`PlatformAdmin`** owns reference data — permitting jurisdictions, the product catalogue and its fleet, and crews — and is deliberately barred from every commercial and operational action; it cannot quote, convert, dispatch, permit, bill or certify. **`AccountManager`** is the commercial owner: accounts, jobsites, quoting, quote issuance, conversion to contract, contract cancellation, billing runs, payments and invoice voids. **`CustomerContact`** is the customer themselves, and holds exactly one write capability — accepting an issued quote. **`DispatchCoordinator`** owns field operations: planning work orders, ordering phase moves, dispatching crews, and reporting orders complete, aborted or cancelled. **`MunicipalLiaison`** owns permitting: drafting applications, submitting and resubmitting them, and recording the municipality's decision; it explicitly cannot dispatch against the permit it obtained. **`YardAuditor`** owns returns: certifying what came back from a removal and what of it was damaged or stolen; it explicitly cannot have completed the removal visit it certifies.

Identity semantics differ between the customer role and the five staff roles. When `X-User-Role` is `CustomerContact`, `X-User-ID` **is** the `id` of the acting `CustomerAccount`; ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` against the account reached from the resource — `customer_account_id` on the jobsite for jobsites and quotes, and `customer_account_id` on the rental contract for contracts, work orders, invoices and reconciliations. A `CustomerContact` that reads or acts on a row resolving to a different account is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `AccountManager`, `DispatchCoordinator`, `MunicipalLiaison` or `YardAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow demands attribution — as `issued_by_user_id` on an invoice, `completed_by_user_id` on a work order, and `finalized_by_user_id` on a reconciliation — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. Those five staff roles are unscoped with respect to account ownership: they may read and act across all accounts.

Recording the acting identifier is not merely audit decoration. Two operations compare a stored identifier against the caller's `X-User-ID` and refuse a match with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`: an invoice may not be voided by the same `X-User-ID` that issued it, and a removal reconciliation may not be finalized by the same `X-User-ID` that reported the removal visit complete. Holding the correct role is necessary but not sufficient — the acting person must be a different person from the one who created the artefact being reversed or certified.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, and a supplied value must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 when omitted, and a supplied value must be zero or greater. A `limit` below 1, a `limit` above 100, or a negative `offset` is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Unless a subsection below says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest row first. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the values actually applied, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` set to true exactly when rows matching the filters remain beyond the current page.

Collection endpoints also accept domain filters. Jurisdictions filter by `jurisdiction_code`; accounts by `credit_hold_flag`; products by `code` and by `category`; crews by `depot_code` and by `certified_for_solid_screen`; jobsites by `customer_account_id` and by `municipality_id`; quotes by `jobsite_id` and by `status`; permit applications by `jobsite_id` and by `status`; contracts by `customer_account_id`, `quote_id`, `jobsite_id`, `lifecycle_status` and `billing_status`; work orders by `contract_id`, `crew_id`, `status`, `work_order_type` and `predecessor_work_order_id`; invoices by `contract_id`, `status` and `invoice_kind`; and reconciliations by `contract_id`, `work_order_id` and `status`. Filters combine conjunctively — a row must match every supplied filter to appear. Any filter naming an identifier of another resource must resolve to a stored row of that type; a filter value that does not resolve is answered with the not-found error of the referenced type rather than with an empty page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Version Counter

Every resource in this system — jurisdictions, accounts, jobsites, products, crews, quotes, permit applications, rental contracts, work orders, invoices and removal reconciliations — carries the same four housekeeping properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp of creation, rendered with three fractional digits. `updated_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp of the most recent mutation, rendered the same way, and equal to `created_at` on a freshly created row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row.

No entity in this system is exempt: all eleven resource types carry `updated_at` and `version`. Any transition, cascade or recomputation described anywhere below that mutates a row both refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments its `version` by 1. This matters most when one request touches several rows — completing an install visit, for example, mutates the work order, the rental contract, one or more products, an invoice and the customer account, and every one of those rows advances its own `version` independently.

### 2.5. Money in Cents, Hours in Quarters, and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integer numbers of cents. There are no decimal currency fields, no currency codes, and no float accumulation anywhere in a money path. Wherever a calculation step below says a figure is rounded, the rounding is **half up**: a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero, and the result is an integer number of cents. Wherever a step does not say a figure is rounded, that step is exact integer arithmetic and no intermediate rounding may be introduced.

Rounding position is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. Each named step in the calculations below is evaluated in the order stated; where a step rounds, the **rounded integer** is the value the next step consumes. Rounding once at the end of a chain of steps produces a different number from rounding at each declared position, and the declared positions are the ones this specification requires.

Labor time is the one non-money quantity with its own precision rule. Estimated labor is expressed in hours as a decimal with two fractional digits and is always a multiple of a quarter hour. The quantisation is a ceiling, never a rounding: an estimate of three and one third hours becomes 3.50, not 3.25. Crew daily capacity is likewise a decimal number of hours carried to one decimal place.

Linear footage, unit counts, day counts, basis points and phase numbers are all integers, and none of them is ever rounded — the corridor-utilisation aggregate, for instance, is exact integer linear feet.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and Half-Open Windows

Every temporal rule in this system reads a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. The clock is settable through an administrative test-driving capability that takes a single `current_utc` instant, and until that capability is first invoked the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Setting the clock recomputes nothing on its own: aggregates such as a jurisdiction's live committed closure footage are re-evaluated whenever the next request reads them. Because this hook exists to drive tests, it is exempt from both the authentication check and the role check — it must succeed whether or not `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are supplied.

Every instant crossing the API boundary — in a request body, in a response body, or in a filter — is a valid ISO 8601 UTC timestamp ending in `Z`. A malformed instant, or one carrying a non-UTC offset, is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Every time window in this system is **half-open**: it includes its start instant and excludes its end instant. A permit's closure window runs from `closure_start_utc` inclusive to `closure_end_utc` exclusive; a billing period runs from `period_start_utc` inclusive to `period_end_utc` exclusive; a crew's committed interval runs from a work order's `scheduled_start_utc` inclusive to its buffered end exclusive. Two half-open windows intersect exactly when the first starts strictly before the second ends and the second starts strictly before the first ends — which means a window beginning at the precise instant another ends does **not** intersect it. Every window submitted to this API must satisfy end strictly after start; an inverted or zero-length window is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

### 2.7. The Validation Ladder

Every endpoint evaluates the same ordered ladder of checks, and the ladder is walked in strict ascending priority order from the first rung. **The first rule whose condition is violated determines the response, and no later rule is evaluated.** This is what makes the error a request receives predictable: when a request breaks three rules at once, the caller learns about the lowest-numbered one.

The ladder in full is: (1) authentication of the two identity headers, answered with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`; (2) role authorization against the endpoint's admitted roles, answered with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`; (3) request-shape integrity, answered with `ERR_VALIDATION`; (4) resolution of every `{id}` path segment to a stored row of the expected type, answered with that type's not-found error; (5) resolution of every foreign key supplied in a body or query string, likewise answered with the referenced type's not-found error; (6) customer account scoping, answered with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`; (7) the two four-eyes rules, answered with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; (8) the stale-version check where an endpoint declares one, answered with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`; (9) the state check confirming the path entity is in the from-set of the transition being triggered, answered with that entity's state error; and then rungs 10 through 26, the domain rules, whose contents and relative order are spelled out in the workflow subsections that own them.

Rung 3, request-shape integrity, is worth stating in full because it absorbs a large family of failures. A request fails shape validation when a required input field is absent; when a field is not of its declared JSON type — money and counts arrive as JSON integers, never as strings, and `daily_capacity_hours` arrives as a JSON number; when an enum-typed field carries a value outside its declared members; when `linear_feet`, `addon_lines[].quantity`, `term_months`, `closure_linear_feet`, `amount_cents`, `replacement_cost_cents_per_unit` or `daily_capacity_hours` is not strictly positive; when `credit_limit_cents`, `unit_monthly_rate_cents`, `permit_lead_time_days` or `permit_review_rounds_allowed` is negative; when an instant is malformed or a window is inverted; when `limit` or `offset` falls outside its permitted range; when the body carries a field the endpoint does not define; or when a completion report supplies a `completed_linear_feet` that is not greater than zero or is greater than the `ordered_linear_feet` of the order named in the path. All of these carry `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Rungs 4 and 5 share a family of not-found errors, one per resource type: `ERR_MUNICIPALITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JOBSITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_RECONCILIATION_NOT_FOUND`. Rung 5 governs `customer_account_id`, `municipality_id`, `jobsite_id`, `quote_id`, `contract_id`, `crew_id`, `panel_product_code`, every `addon_lines[].product_code`, every `unit_returns[].product_code`, and every identifier appearing as a list filter — a product code that names no catalogue row yields `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, a `crew_id` that names no crew yields `ERR_CREW_NOT_FOUND`, and so on.

Rung 9 covers terminal-state lockdown and second-attempt idempotency alike. Attempting a transition against a row already in a terminal state, or repeating a transition that has already been applied, fails here with the owning entity's state error: `ERR_QUOTE_STATE` for quotes, `ERR_PERMIT_STATE` for permit applications, `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE` for rental contracts, `ERR_WORK_ORDER_STATE` for work orders, `ERR_INVOICE_STATE` for invoices, and `ERR_RECONCILIATION_STATE` for removal reconciliations.

The seventeen domain rungs above the state check are ordered as follows, and this ordering decides which failure a caller sees when several apply at once. Rung 10 is permit coverage at dispatch (`ERR_PERMIT_NOT_APPROVED` or `ERR_PERMIT_WINDOW_ELAPSED`). Rung 11 is the jurisdictional wind-load question at quoting (`ERR_WIND_LOAD_NOT_PERMITTED`). Rung 12 is crew screen certification at dispatch (`ERR_CREW_NOT_CERTIFIED`). Rung 13 is permit lead time at submission (`ERR_PERMIT_LEAD_TIME_NOT_MET`). Rung 14 is the crew's calendar collision at dispatch (`ERR_CREW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`). Rung 15 is the crew's daily hour cap at dispatch (`ERR_CREW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`). Rung 16 is billing-window validity (`ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_INVALID`). Rung 17 is the municipal closure-corridor cap at submission (`ERR_CLOSURE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`). Rung 18 is the municipal review-round bound at resubmission (`ERR_PERMIT_REVISION_LIMIT`). Rung 19 is fleet availability at dispatch (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FLEET`). Rung 20 is reference-data code uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`). Rung 21 is product-category integrity at quoting (`ERR_INVALID_ADDON_CATEGORY`). Rung 22 is phase-move integrity on a re-set order (`ERR_INVALID_PHASE_MOVE`). Rung 23 is the yard count reconciling against the deployed snapshot (`ERR_PANEL_COUNT_MISMATCH`). Rung 24 is the minimum committed term at quoting (`ERR_BELOW_MINIMUM_TERM`). Rung 25 is the account's credit hold at dispatch (`ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD`). Rung 26 is payment size against the open ledger (`ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`).

Two consequences of that ordering deserve to be called out. On a dispatch request, six of these rungs can apply, and they fire in the order permit coverage, crew certification, crew calendar collision, crew daily capacity, fleet availability, credit hold — so an uncertified crew is refused even when its calendar is wide open, a certified crew with a colliding window fails on the calendar rather than the certification, and the credit hold is reported last, meaning an order in the wrong state, on an unpermitted site, with an uncertified crew, a colliding window, an over-committed day or a short yard reports that failure instead of the credit hold. On a quoting request, the wind-load jurisdiction question is settled at rung 11 — before any pricing arithmetic runs — while the product-category and minimum-term questions are settled later at rungs 21 and 24.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Protection on Jobsite and Contract Writes

Two write operations in this system destroy work a client may not have seen, and both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check on the **parent** row's `version`. The check sits at rung 8 of the ladder — above the state check and above every domain rule — so a client holding a stale view of the parent is turned away before it can learn why its write would otherwise have failed.

Creating a quote on a jobsite requires the non-optional field `expected_jobsite_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of the target `Jobsite` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the jobsite's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing is written. The protection exists because a new quote legitimately supersedes the jobsite's `DRAFT` and still-valid `ISSUED` quotes and expires its elapsed ones — a writer working from a stale jobsite would silently discard a configuration it never saw.

Creating a re-set order on a rental contract requires the non-optional field `expected_contract_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of the target `RentalContract` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the contract's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing is written. The protection exists because a new re-set order legitimately supersedes the contract's open `RESET` order — a writer working from a stale contract would cancel a phase move it never saw and re-plan against an out-of-date `current_phase`.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field. Planning an ordinary work order, dispatching, completing, aborting, cancelling, issuing, accepting, converting, submitting a permit, recording a municipal decision, running a billing period, paying, voiding and finalizing a reconciliation all carry no expected-version input, and supplying one is an unknown field that fails request-shape validation.

### 2.9. Four-Eyes Controls and Customer Scoping

Two independent controls narrow who may act on a row beyond the role check, and both are keyed on `X-User-ID` rather than on the role.

The customer scope control applies to `CustomerContact` only, and it covers **every** read endpoint plus the one customer write — quote acceptance. On a jobsite or a quote, the account is reached through the jobsite's `customer_account_id`; on a contract, work order, invoice or reconciliation, it is reached through the contract's `customer_account_id`; on an account itself, it is that account's own `id`. A mismatch is `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The five staff roles are unscoped and this control never fires for them.

The four-eyes control applies to two operations regardless of role. Voiding an invoice is refused when `X-User-ID` equals the invoice's `issued_by_user_id`. Finalizing a removal reconciliation is refused when `X-User-ID` equals the `completed_by_user_id` recorded on the removal work order the reconciliation was opened against. Both refusals carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and both are evaluated at rung 7 — before the stale-version check, before the state check and before any business rule, so a second attempt by the wrong person never reveals the state of the artefact.

### 2.10. Fleet Conservation and Ledger Conservation

Two quantities in this system are conserved, and every cascade described in §3 is designed to keep them conserved.

The first is the fleet. Each catalogue product owns a fixed `owned_count`, and that total is always distributed across exactly five buckets: `available_count` for units sitting in the yard and assignable, `reserved_count` for units held by a `SCHEDULED` delivery-and-install visit but not yet standing on a site, `deployed_count` for units standing on a jobsite under an `ACTIVE` or `DEMOBILIZING` contract, `damaged_count` for units taken out of service by a finalized reconciliation, and `lost_count` for units never returned. Units move between buckets; they are never created or destroyed. Every reservation therefore has a matching compensating release: cancelling or aborting a dispatched visit returns exactly the units it reserved from `reserved_count` back to `available_count`, so that a dispatch followed by its cancellation leaves `available_count` precisely where it started.

The second is the ledger. A customer account's `open_balance_cents` is at all times the sum, over that account's invoices whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, of the invoice's `total_cents` less its `amount_paid_cents`. Its `lifetime_credited_cents` is the running total of amounts written off by voids. Its `credit_hold_flag` is true exactly when `open_balance_cents` is **strictly** greater than `credit_limit_cents` — an account sitting exactly on its limit is not held. Issuance raises the open balance; payment lowers it; a void lowers it by exactly the amount that was still open at the moment of the void, and raises `lifetime_credited_cents` by that same amount, so that an issuance followed by its void leaves `open_balance_cents` precisely where it started. Cash already collected is never refunded by a void, so a void never moves `amount_paid_cents`.

Both quantities are recomputed inside the request that disturbs them. There is no deferred settlement, no background sweep and no eventual consistency: the response body of the request that issues an invoice already shows the account's new `open_balance_cents` and `credit_hold_flag`, and the response body of the request that dispatches a crew already shows the product's new `available_count` and `reserved_count`.

### 2.11. Reference-Data Uniqueness and Catalogue Integrity

A jurisdiction's `jurisdiction_code` must be unique across all jurisdictions, and a product's `code` must be unique across all products; both comparisons are case-sensitive, and a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. A `jurisdiction_code` is between 3 and 12 characters and uses only uppercase letters, digits and hyphens; a product `code` is between 3 and 16 characters.

The catalogue is split by `category` into one panel family and three add-on families, and the split governs how a product may be priced and used. A `FENCE_PANEL` product is priced by the linear foot, must carry a positive `panel_length_ft`, and must carry a `unit_monthly_rate_cents` of exactly 0 — its rent comes from the footage tier, not from a per-unit rate. A `GATE`, `BARRICADE` or `WATER_BARRIER` product is priced per unit as an add-on, must carry a null `panel_length_ft`, and carries the per-unit monthly rent in `unit_monthly_rate_cents`. Every product, of whatever category, carries a strictly positive `replacement_cost_cents_per_unit`, a non-negative `fleet_age_months`, a strictly positive `owned_count`, a free-text `description`, and an optional `wind_load_rating_mph` that is either a positive integer or null for products with no certified rating.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Reference Layer: Jurisdictions, Accounts, Catalogue and Crews

Before any fence can be quoted, four kinds of reference row must exist, and three of the four are owned exclusively by `PlatformAdmin`.

A **permitting jurisdiction** is registered with a display `name` and a short `jurisdiction_code`. It carries four numbers and one flag that shape everything downstream. `permit_lead_time_days` is the minimum number of whole days that must separate a permit submission from the start of the closure window it requests, and it may be zero. `max_concurrent_closure_linear_feet` is the aggregate sidewalk footage the jurisdiction will allow overlapping permits to hold at once, and it must be strictly positive. `permit_review_rounds_allowed` bounds how many revision rounds a permit may accumulate before resubmission is refused; it defaults to 2 when omitted and may be zero. `rental_tax_rate_bp` is the rental tax rate expressed in basis points, applied to every invoice subtotal raised against a jobsite in that jurisdiction, and it lies between 0 and 2000 inclusive — a rate of 890 basis points is eight and nine tenths of one percent. `allows_solid_wind_screen` decides whether the 130 mph solid screen may be specified at all in that jurisdiction, and it defaults to true. A freshly registered jurisdiction has `committed_closure_linear_feet` of 0.

That last figure is not stored input but a live aggregate, and it answers the question "how much sidewalk is this jurisdiction closing right now". It is the sum of `closure_linear_feet` over every permit application whose jobsite resolves to this jurisdiction, whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`, and whose closure window contains the logical clock — that is, whose `closure_start_utc` is at or before the clock and whose `closure_end_utc` is strictly after it. Applications in `DRAFT`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `DENIED` and `CLOSED` hold no footage and contribute nothing.

A **billing account** is opened with a `legal_name`, a `billing_email` for invoice delivery, and a `credit_limit_cents` ceiling that may be zero but never negative. Both `PlatformAdmin` and `AccountManager` may open one. A new account starts with `open_balance_cents` of 0, `lifetime_credited_cents` of 0 and `credit_hold_flag` false.

A **catalogue product** is registered with its `code`, `category`, `description`, `unit_monthly_rate_cents`, `replacement_cost_cents_per_unit`, `fleet_age_months` and `owned_count`, plus the optional `panel_length_ft` and `wind_load_rating_mph`. Registration seats the whole fleet in the yard: `available_count` is set equal to `owned_count`, and `reserved_count`, `deployed_count`, `damaged_count` and `lost_count` all start at 0.

An **install crew** is registered by `PlatformAdmin` or `DispatchCoordinator` with a `crew_code`, its home yard's `depot_code`, a strictly positive `daily_capacity_hours` giving the labor hours it may hold across all scheduled visits on any single UTC calendar date, and a `certified_for_solid_screen` flag that defaults to false.

A **jobsite** is registered under both an account and a jurisdiction by `PlatformAdmin` or `AccountManager`, naming its owning `customer_account_id`, its `municipality_id`, an operator-facing `site_label` and the `address_line` printed on delivery tickets. Its `site_condition` — one of `SOIL`, `ASPHALT` or `CONCRETE` — describes the ground the posts are set into and directly drives the install labor rate. Its `phase_count` bounds how many construction phases the fence line may later be re-set through and defaults to 1, never below 1. Its `requires_sidewalk_closure` flag defaults to false and, when true, means fence work on this site occupies public sidewalk and cannot be dispatched without an approved permit covering the visit.

### 3.2. Pricing a Fence Line on a Jobsite

An `AccountManager` prices a configuration by submitting it against a jobsite. The commercial body of that request names the `contract_type` (`EVENT` or `CONSTRUCTION`), the total `linear_feet` of fence line in whole feet, the `panel_product_code` identifying which `FENCE_PANEL` catalogue product supplies the run, and the committed `term_months`. Two further inputs are optional: `wind_screen`, which is `NONE`, `MESH_70` or `SOLID_130` and defaults to `NONE`; and `addon_lines`, which defaults to an empty list and otherwise holds one entry per add-on request, each entry carrying a `product_code` and a strictly positive `quantity`. The concurrency field `expected_jobsite_version` described in §2.8 accompanies every such request.

Three domain gates guard the request before and around the arithmetic, and their relative order is the ladder order of §2.7. First, at rung 11, a `SOLID_130` screen is refused with `ERR_WIND_LOAD_NOT_PERMITTED` when the jobsite's jurisdiction has `allows_solid_wind_screen` false — a 130 mph solid screen is a jurisdictional wind-load question, decided before any pricing arithmetic runs. Later, at rung 21, `panel_product_code` must reference a product whose `category` is `FENCE_PANEL` and every `addon_lines[].product_code` must reference a product whose `category` is `GATE`, `BARRICADE` or `WATER_BARRIER`; a panel listed as an add-on, or a gate nominated as the panel product, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ADDON_CATEGORY`. Finally, at rung 24, the committed term is checked against the contract family: a `CONSTRUCTION` configuration requires `term_months` of at least 3, and an `EVENT` configuration requires `term_months` of exactly 1, because an event fence is quoted for a single billing month and prorated by the day. A violation of either is `ERR_BELOW_MINIMUM_TERM`.

The arithmetic then runs as a fixed sequence of steps, and five independent inputs each select a different factor before all five land in one figure. **Step one** selects the linear-foot tier rate and stores it as `tier_rate_cents_per_ft`. The rate is **flat on the total footage, not marginal**: the entire run is priced at the rate of the band the footage falls into. A run of at most 250 feet is priced at 62 cents per foot; a run above 250 feet and at most 500 feet at 54 cents per foot; a run above 500 feet and at most 1000 feet at 47 cents per foot; and a run above 1000 feet at 41 cents per foot. **Step two** multiplies `linear_feet` by that tier rate to give the panel base amount, exactly, with no rounding. **Step three** multiplies the panel base amount by the screen factor and rounds the product half up to whole cents: the factor is 1.00 for `NONE`, 1.15 for `MESH_70` and 1.35 for `SOLID_130`. **Step four** computes the screen adder by multiplying `linear_feet` by the per-foot screen adder, exactly: 0 cents per foot for `NONE`, 12 cents per foot for `MESH_70` and 26 cents per foot for `SOLID_130`. Note that the screen option is doing two things at once — it scales the panel rent multiplicatively and it adds a separate per-foot charge. **Step five** computes the add-on monthly total as the sum, over every entry of `addon_lines`, of that entry's `quantity` multiplied by the `unit_monthly_rate_cents` of the product its `product_code` resolves to; this sum is exact. **Step six** adds the three results of steps three, four and five together to give the pre-adjustment monthly subtotal, exactly.

**Step seven** selects the recurring adjustment factor from the contract family. An `EVENT` configuration takes a surge factor of 1.25, unconditionally — `term_months` never discounts an event quote. A `CONSTRUCTION` configuration takes one minus a term discount rate, where the rate is 0.14 when `term_months` is at least 12, 0.08 when `term_months` is at least 6 but below 12, and 0.00 otherwise; the resulting factors are therefore 0.86, 0.92 and 1.00. **Step eight** multiplies the pre-adjustment monthly subtotal by that factor, rounds the product half up, and stores the result as `monthly_recurring_cents`.

**Step nine** computes the one-time post-setting labor and stores it as `install_labor_cents`. It is the greater of a mobilization minimum of 35,000 cents and the sum of two terms: `linear_feet` multiplied by the per-foot condition labor rate — 95 cents per foot on `SOIL`, 140 cents per foot on `ASPHALT`, 210 cents per foot on `CONCRETE` — plus 2,500 cents for each add-on unit, where the add-on unit total is the sum of `quantity` across all of `addon_lines`. This step is exact integer arithmetic; the mobilization minimum acts as a floor, not a replacement.

**Step ten** decides the permit coordination fee and stores it as `permit_handling_fee_cents`. The fee is 0 when `monthly_recurring_cents` is at least 150,000 cents, and 12,500 cents otherwise. **The evaluation order here is load-bearing:** the waiver test reads the post-adjustment figure produced by step eight, never the pre-adjustment subtotal from step six. A configuration whose pre-adjustment subtotal clears 150,000 cents but whose discounted `monthly_recurring_cents` falls back below it still pays the handling fee. **Step eleven** sums `install_labor_cents`, a flat delivery fee of 18,500 cents and `permit_handling_fee_cents` into `one_time_cents`, exactly.

Two counts are stored alongside the money. `panel_unit_count` is `linear_feet` divided by the panel product's `panel_length_ft`, rounded **up** to the next whole panel — 500 feet of ten-foot panels is 50 panels, and 505 feet of ten-foot panels is 51. The add-on unit total described in step nine is not stored on the quote but is carried into the labor figure and, later, into the contract's fleet commitments.

The quote also stores `priced_lines`, a per-line breakdown whose entries each carry a `line_type`, a `product_code`, a `quantity` and an `amount_cents`. Exactly one `PANEL_RENTAL` line is present, naming the panel product's code, with `quantity` equal to `panel_unit_count` and `amount_cents` equal to the step-three screened panel amount. A `WIND_SCREEN` line is present only when `wind_screen` is not `NONE`; it carries a null `product_code`, a `quantity` equal to `linear_feet`, and an `amount_cents` equal to the step-four screen adder. One `ADDON_RENTAL` line is present per entry of `addon_lines`, in the order the entries were supplied, carrying that entry's `product_code` and `quantity` and an `amount_cents` equal to the entry's `quantity` multiplied by its product's `unit_monthly_rate_cents`. The three families of line therefore sum to the pre-adjustment monthly subtotal of step six, before the surge or discount of step eight is applied.

A newly priced configuration is created with `status` `DRAFT`, with `accepted_by_name` null, and with both `issued_at_utc` and `valid_through_utc` null — validity is not established at pricing time. Creating it also advances the jobsite's own `version` by 1.

Pricing a new configuration on a jobsite is not additive: it closes out the jobsite's open configurations, and it does so conditionally. Every quote on that same jobsite whose `status` is `DRAFT` moves to `SUPERSEDED`. Every quote on that jobsite whose `status` is `ISSUED` is examined against the logical clock: if the clock is at or before its `valid_through_utc` it moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and if the clock is strictly past its `valid_through_utc` it moves to `EXPIRED` instead. Quotes already in `CONVERTED`, `EXPIRED` or `SUPERSEDED` are left entirely alone. Each quote actually moved has its own `version` incremented by 1. Validity is therefore evaluated at the moment a newer configuration displaces the older ones, and an elapsed configuration lands in `EXPIRED` rather than `SUPERSEDED`.

### 3.3. From Issued Quote to Signed Contract

A quote reaches the customer only when an `AccountManager` issues it, moving it `DRAFT → ISSUED`. Issuance takes no request body. It stamps `issued_at_utc` with the logical clock and sets `valid_through_utc` to exactly fourteen days — two weeks — after that instant, then advances the quote's `version` by 1. Attempting to issue a quote that is not in `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_STATE`.

Acceptance is the customer's one write. A `CustomerContact` accepts, moving the quote `ISSUED → ACCEPTED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `customer_account_id` of the jobsite the quote was priced against — a mismatch is `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The quote must be in `ISSUED`, and the logical clock must be at or before `valid_through_utc`; the boundary instant itself is still acceptable. An acceptance attempted against a quote in any other status, or against an `ISSUED` quote whose `valid_through_utc` the clock has already passed strictly, is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_STATE`. The optional `accepted_by_name` field records the name the customer contact signed under; when it is omitted the field stays null. Acceptance advances the quote's `version` by 1.

Conversion is where commercial terms freeze. An `AccountManager` converts by naming the `quote_id` of an `ACCEPTED` quote together with a `terms_acknowledged_by` naming the person who acknowledged the event or construction terms. The quote moves `ACCEPTED → CONVERTED` and its `version` advances by 1; a conversion attempted against a quote in any other status is `ERR_QUOTE_STATE`. The new rental contract is created with `lifecycle_status` `PENDING_MOBILIZATION`, `billing_status` `NOT_BILLED` and `current_phase` 1. Its `customer_account_id` is copied from the quote's jobsite and its `jobsite_id` from the quote itself, so the contract knows both who pays and where the fence stands without either being supplied in the request. Its `contract_type`, `monthly_recurring_cents`, `install_labor_cents` and `one_time_cents` are copied verbatim from the quote, and its `ordered_linear_feet` is copied from the quote's `linear_feet`.

Freezing is made explicit by `pinned_quote_version`, which records the quote's `version` as observed at the moment of conversion — the value read before this conversion's own increment. The contract's commercial terms are frozen against that version, and nothing later re-derives them from the quote.

The contract also derives `fleet_commitments`, the list of units it owes the yard. Each entry carries a `product_code` and a `unit_count`. There is exactly one panel entry, whose `product_code` is the quote's `panel_product_code` and whose `unit_count` is the quote's `panel_unit_count`, and one further entry per entry of the quote's `addon_lines`, carrying that add-on's `product_code` and its `quantity` as the `unit_count`. The remaining contract figures start at their neutral values: `deployed_linear_feet` 0, `addons_deployed` false, `cancellation_fee_cents` 0, and `billing_anchor_utc` and `billed_through_utc` both null.

### 3.4. Sidewalk-Closure Permitting and the Municipal Review Cycle

Where a fence line occupies public sidewalk, a `MunicipalLiaison` opens a permit application against the jobsite, naming the `jobsite_id`, the `closure_linear_feet` of sidewalk the closure occupies, the half-open window bounded by `closure_start_utc` and `closure_end_utc`, and the `sidewalk_closure_type` requested from the municipality — `FULL`, `PARTIAL` or `LANE_SHIFT`. The application is created in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` 0, and it holds no corridor footage yet; `submitted_at_utc`, `decided_at_utc`, `reviewer_note` and `required_change_summary` all start null.

Submission moves the application `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and the same operation also serves as the resubmission edge `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` that closes the municipal review cycle. All three of `closure_linear_feet`, `closure_start_utc` and `closure_end_utc` may optionally be supplied on a submission to override what the application currently holds, and **the overrides are applied before the lead-time and corridor checks evaluate** — the checks judge the revised footage and the revised window, not the stale ones. A submission attempted from any status other than `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_STATE`.

Three domain gates then apply, in ladder order. At rung 13, the lead-time rule requires `closure_start_utc` to be at least `permit_lead_time_days` whole days after the logical clock — that is, at or after the clock advanced by `permit_lead_time_days` multiplied by 86,400 seconds. Sitting exactly on that boundary is admitted; falling short of it is `ERR_PERMIT_LEAD_TIME_NOT_MET`. At rung 17, the municipal corridor cap applies. At rung 18, and only on a resubmission out of `REVISION_REQUESTED`, the review cycle is bounded: the resubmission is admitted while `revision_round` is at or below the jurisdiction's `permit_review_rounds_allowed`, and refused with `ERR_PERMIT_REVISION_LIMIT` once `revision_round` exceeds it. With the default allowance of 2, an application may be resubmitted while its `revision_round` reads 1 and while it reads 2, and is refused once it reads 3. Because lead time sits below the revision bound on the ladder, an out-of-time resubmission on an exhausted permit reports the lead-time failure.

The corridor cap is the interesting constraint, because every individual permit is legal on its own and only the overlapping collective can breach it. The candidate's window is the half-open interval from its `closure_start_utc` to its `closure_end_utc`. The contending set is every **other** permit application — excluding the candidate itself — whose jobsite resolves to the same jurisdiction as the candidate's, whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`, and whose own half-open closure window intersects the candidate's. Intersection has the meaning fixed in §2.6: the other application's `closure_start_utc` must be strictly before the candidate's `closure_end_utc` **and** the candidate's `closure_start_utc` must be strictly before the other's `closure_end_utc`. A window that begins at the precise instant another ends does not contend. The committed footage is the exact integer sum of `closure_linear_feet` over that contending set; the projected footage is that sum plus the candidate's own `closure_linear_feet`. The submission is admitted when the projected footage is at or below the jurisdiction's `max_concurrent_closure_linear_feet` — equality is admitted — and refused with `ERR_CLOSURE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when it strictly exceeds it.

A successful submission stamps `submitted_at_utc` with the logical clock, clears `required_change_summary` back to null, recomputes the jurisdiction's `committed_closure_linear_feet`, and advances the application's `version` by 1. On a resubmission it also persists whichever of the three window and footage overrides were supplied.

The municipality's answer is recorded by the same `MunicipalLiaison` as a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `DENY` or `REQUEST_REVISION`, optionally accompanied by a free-text `reviewer_note` and, for a revision request, a `required_change_summary` describing the change the municipality demands. All three decisions may only be recorded against an application in `SUBMITTED`; anything else is `ERR_PERMIT_STATE`. All three stamp `decided_at_utc` with the logical clock, recompute the jurisdiction's `committed_closure_linear_feet`, and advance the application's `version` by 1. `APPROVE` moves the application `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and stores `reviewer_note` when supplied. `DENY` moves it `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, and because a denied application holds no footage, the recomputation releases what it had been holding. `REQUEST_REVISION` moves it `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED`, increments `revision_round` by exactly 1, stores both `required_change_summary` and `reviewer_note` when they are supplied, and likewise releases the footage it had been holding — a `REVISION_REQUESTED` application no longer contends for the corridor, which is what lets the corridor free up between review rounds.

An approved permit is retired by the fence coming down rather than by anyone acting on the permit directly: when a rental contract on the same `jobsite_id` enters `DEMOBILIZING`, every permit application on that jobsite whose `status` is `APPROVED` moves `APPROVED → CLOSED`, releasing its `closure_linear_feet` from the jurisdiction's committed corridor and advancing each such application's `version` by 1.

### 3.5. Planning Labor and Dispatching a Crew

Labor on a contract is planned by a `DispatchCoordinator` as a sequence of visits. Planning a visit names its `work_order_type` and the `ordered_linear_feet` of fence line the visit handles, and may optionally name a `scheduled_start_utc` at which the crew is expected to arrive. The footage must be strictly positive and must not exceed the contract's own `ordered_linear_feet`; a violation of either bound is `ERR_VALIDATION`. A planned visit is created in `PLANNED` with `completed_linear_feet` 0, with no `crew_id`, with an empty `reserved_unit_counts`, and with `abort_reason_code` and `cancel_reason_code` null. **Planning reserves nothing** — fleet is only ever committed at dispatch.

The planning capability creates the three ordinary visit types: `DELIVERY_INSTALL`, which brings panels out and sets posts; `RELOCATION`, which moves an already-standing run; and `REMOVAL`, which takes the fence down. On all three, `phase_from` and `phase_to` are left null, and supplying either is `ERR_VALIDATION`. The fourth type, `RESET`, is never created here — it is created only through the dedicated phase-move capability described at the end of this subsection — and requesting `work_order_type` `RESET` on the planning capability is likewise `ERR_VALIDATION`. Planning is admitted while the contract's `lifecycle_status` is `PENDING_MOBILIZATION` or `ACTIVE`; a contract in `DEMOBILIZING`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` refuses it with `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE`.

Dispatch is where a visit acquires a crew, a clock and an inventory reservation, moving it `PLANNED → SCHEDULED`. The dispatch request names the `crew_id` and the `scheduled_start_utc`; both are required, and a `crew_id` naming no crew fails with `ERR_CREW_NOT_FOUND`. A dispatch attempted against a visit that is not in `PLANNED` is `ERR_WORK_ORDER_STATE`.

Before any gate is judged, the labor estimate is computed, because four of the six dispatch gates depend on it. The estimate starts from a productivity rate keyed on the visit type: a `DELIVERY_INSTALL` crew sets 120 feet of fence per hour, a `RELOCATION` crew moves 80 feet per hour, a `REMOVAL` crew pulls 200 feet per hour, and a `RESET` crew re-sets 90 feet per hour. **The base hours** are the visit's `ordered_linear_feet` divided by that rate, kept as an exact fraction with no rounding at this point. **The add-on burden** is a quarter of an hour — fifteen minutes — for each add-on unit the contract commits, where the add-on unit total is the sum of `unit_count` over every entry of the contract's `fleet_commitments` other than the panel entry. That burden applies **only** to `DELIVERY_INSTALL` and `REMOVAL` visits, the two types that physically move inventory between the yard and the site; `RELOCATION` and `RESET` visits move already-deployed add-ons in place and take an add-on burden of zero even when the contract commits many add-on units. **The raw estimate** is the base hours plus the add-on burden. That raw estimate is then quantised **upward** to the smallest multiple of 0.25 hours that is at or above it — a ceiling, never a rounding. Finally, `estimated_labor_hours` is the greater of a two-hour minimum billable figure and the quantised estimate. As a worked illustration: a 500-foot install carrying six add-on units has base hours of five hundred over one hundred and twenty, a one-and-a-half-hour add-on burden, a raw estimate between 5.5 and 5.75 hours, and therefore an `estimated_labor_hours` of 5.75; a 250-foot removal with no add-ons has a raw estimate of 1.25 hours and an `estimated_labor_hours` of 2.00 after the minimum applies.

`scheduled_end_utc` is then `scheduled_start_utc` advanced by `estimated_labor_hours`. Separately — and this figure is not stored on the visit — the crew is treated as blocked until an hour and a half after `scheduled_end_utc`, a travel buffer that extends **only the trailing edge** of the crew's committed interval and never the leading edge.

Six gates then run, in the ladder order of §2.7.

**Permit coverage** comes first. When the contract's jobsite has `requires_sidewalk_closure` true and the visit type is `DELIVERY_INSTALL`, `RELOCATION` or `RESET`, some permit application on that jobsite must have `status` `APPROVED`, and failing that the dispatch is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_APPROVED`. When such a permit exists, its half-open closure window must contain **both** `scheduled_start_utc` and `scheduled_end_utc` — each of the two instants must be at or after `closure_start_utc` and strictly before `closure_end_utc` — and a visit whose interval is not entirely inside the window is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_WINDOW_ELAPSED`. `REMOVAL` visits never require a closure permit at all, and a jobsite with `requires_sidewalk_closure` false requires none for any type.

**Screen certification** comes second. When the configuration behind the contract was quoted with `wind_screen` of `SOLID_130`, the nominated crew must have `certified_for_solid_screen` true; otherwise the dispatch is refused with `ERR_CREW_NOT_CERTIFIED`. Certification is deliberately checked before the crew's calendar, so an uncertified crew is refused even when its diary is completely empty.

**Calendar collision** comes third. A crew's committed intervals may not overlap. The interval a `SCHEDULED` visit occupies runs from its `scheduled_start_utc` inclusive to its buffered end — `scheduled_end_utc` plus ninety minutes — exclusive. The candidate collides when its own such interval intersects that of any other `SCHEDULED` visit of the same crew, using the intersection rule of §2.6; a candidate starting at the precise instant an existing visit's buffered interval ends does **not** collide. A collision is `ERR_CREW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

**Daily capacity** comes fourth. The crew's committed hours for a UTC calendar date are the sum of `estimated_labor_hours` over its `SCHEDULED` visits whose `scheduled_start_utc` falls on that same UTC calendar date. That sum, plus the candidate's own `estimated_labor_hours`, must be at or below the crew's `daily_capacity_hours`; equality is admitted, and exceeding it is `ERR_CREW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Each visit fits individually — only the day's collective can breach the cap.

**Fleet availability** comes fifth, and it is judged against the yard's `available_count` alone; units already held in `reserved_count` by another `SCHEDULED` visit are not available. Dispatching a `DELIVERY_INSTALL` visit requires the panel product's `available_count` to be at least the visit's `ordered_linear_feet` divided by that product's `panel_length_ft` and rounded up to the next whole panel, and additionally requires each add-on product named in the contract's `fleet_commitments` to have an `available_count` at or above that entry's `unit_count`. A shortfall on either is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FLEET`.

**The credit hold** comes sixth and last. Dispatch is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD`, carrying HTTP 402, while the contract's owning account has `credit_hold_flag` true — that is, while its `open_balance_cents` is strictly greater than its `credit_limit_cents`. Because this gate sits at the top of the ladder, a visit that is simultaneously in the wrong state, on an unpermitted site, staffed by an uncertified crew, colliding with another booking, over the day's cap or short of fleet reports that other failure instead.

A dispatch that clears all six gates stores `crew_id`, `estimated_labor_hours` and `scheduled_end_utc`, and advances the visit's `version` by 1. For a `DELIVERY_INSTALL` visit it also sets `reserved_unit_counts` to a single entry naming the panel product's code with a `unit_count` equal to the whole-panel figure computed above, and moves exactly that many units of that product from `available_count` into `reserved_count`. For `RELOCATION`, `RESET` and `REMOVAL` visits `reserved_unit_counts` stays empty and no fleet counts move, because the panels those visits handle are already standing on the jobsite.

A jobsite phase move is ordered separately, through the contract's phase-move capability, and it is the second of the two stale-version-protected writes. Alongside `expected_contract_version` the request names the `ordered_linear_feet` the move handles, the destination `phase_to`, and optionally a `scheduled_start_utc`. At rung 22 the destination is checked: `phase_to` must lie between 1 and the jobsite's `phase_count` inclusive, and must differ from the contract's `current_phase`; re-setting the fence to the phase it already occupies, or to a phase the jobsite does not have, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PHASE_MOVE`. A phase move is admitted while the contract's `lifecycle_status` is `PENDING_MOBILIZATION` or `ACTIVE` and refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE` otherwise. The capability creates a `RESET` visit in `PLANNED` whose `phase_from` is taken from the contract's `current_phase` — never from the request — and whose `phase_to` is the supplied destination, and it advances the contract's `version` by 1.

Ordering a phase move also displaces any phase move already open on that contract. Every `RESET` visit on the same contract whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `SCHEDULED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and each such visit's `version` advances by 1; a displaced visit that had been `SCHEDULED` additionally has its `crew_id` cleared to null, freeing the crew's window and its share of that day's hours. Visits of other types, and `RESET` visits already in a terminal status, are untouched. When at least one visit was displaced, the new visit records `predecessor_work_order_id` pointing at the most recently created displaced visit and `predecessor_relation` `SUPERSEDES`; when nothing was displaced, both fields stay null.

### 3.6. Reporting Field Work: Full, Partial, Aborted and Abandoned Visits

A `DispatchCoordinator` reports a visit finished by supplying the `completed_linear_feet` actually handled. That figure must be strictly greater than zero and at most the visit's `ordered_linear_feet`, and anything outside that range is `ERR_VALIDATION`. Only a `SCHEDULED` visit may be reported; anything else is `ERR_WORK_ORDER_STATE`. When the reported footage equals `ordered_linear_feet` the visit moves `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`; when it is strictly less, the visit moves `SCHEDULED → PARTIAL`. Either way the visit stores the reported `completed_linear_feet`, stores the acting `X-User-ID` as `completed_by_user_id`, and advances its `version` by 1. A visit carries no completion-timestamp field of its own — the report stamps no such instant, and the only instant it refreshes on the visit is the housekeeping `updated_at` of §2.4. That stored identifier is what the four-eyes rule on reconciliation later reads.

What happens beyond the visit itself depends entirely on the visit type, and the branches diverge sharply.

**A delivery-and-install visit reported complete** is the trigger for the mobilization cascade, four hops deep. Hop one: every entry of the visit's `reserved_unit_counts` moves its `unit_count` of that product from `reserved_count` into `deployed_count` — the panels have left the yard and are standing on the site. Hop two: because the visit reached `COMPLETED`, the contract advances `PENDING_MOBILIZATION → ACTIVE`, which is described in full below. Hop three, inside that contract transition, issues the mobilization invoice. Hop four, inside that issuance, lifts the account's `open_balance_cents` and may raise its `credit_hold_flag`. All four hops complete inside the single request that reported the visit.

**A delivery-and-install visit reported partial** does two things the complete branch does not, and one thing identically. Identically: it drives the contract `PENDING_MOBILIZATION → ACTIVE` exactly as the complete branch does, with the same three downstream hops. Differently: only the panels actually erected move out of reserve — the reported `completed_linear_feet` divided by the panel product's `panel_length_ft`, rounded up to the next whole panel, moves from `reserved_count` into `deployed_count`, and the untouched remainder of the reservation is returned from `reserved_count` to `available_count`, so no panel is left stranded in reserve. And additionally: a follow-up visit is created in `PLANNED`, carrying the same `work_order_type`, an `ordered_linear_feet` equal to the original `ordered_linear_feet` less the reported `completed_linear_feet`, a `predecessor_work_order_id` pointing at the visit just reported, and a `predecessor_relation` of `REMAINDER_OF`. The follow-up visit has no crew and no reservation of its own until it is dispatched in turn.

**The contract's mobilization transition** fires only when the contract's `lifecycle_status` is `PENDING_MOBILIZATION`; a later install visit on an already-`ACTIVE` contract — the remainder visit, for instance — moves fleet counts but does not re-fire it. When it does fire, it stamps `billing_anchor_utc` with the logical clock, marking the start of the first billable period. It recomputes `deployed_linear_feet`, which is at all times the sum of `completed_linear_feet` over the contract's `DELIVERY_INSTALL` visits whose `status` is `COMPLETED` or `PARTIAL`. It sets `addons_deployed` to true and moves every add-on entry of `fleet_commitments` — every entry other than the panel entry — directly from that product's `available_count` into its `deployed_count`; note that add-on units are never held in `reserved_count`, they are checked for availability at dispatch and moved at activation. It then issues a `MOBILIZATION` invoice against the contract, carrying an `INSTALL_LABOR` line for the contract's `install_labor_cents`, a `DELIVERY` line for the flat delivery fee of 18,500 cents, and — only when the contract's permit coordination fee is non-zero — a `PERMIT_HANDLING` line for it. Finally the contract's `version` advances by 1.

**A removal visit reported complete** requires the contract's `lifecycle_status` to be `ACTIVE`, and is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE` otherwise. It triggers the removal cascade, three hops deep. Hop one: because the visit reached `COMPLETED`, the contract advances `ACTIVE → DEMOBILIZING`. Hop two, inside that contract transition, opens a removal reconciliation in `OPEN`. Hop three, also inside that contract transition, closes every `APPROVED` permit application on the contract's jobsite, which in turn releases that footage from the jurisdiction's committed corridor. Crucially, the fleet does **not** move on a removal: the units stay counted in `deployed_count` until the reconciliation is finalized, because until the yard has physically counted them nobody knows how many came back.

**A re-set or relocation visit reported complete** likewise requires the contract's `lifecycle_status` to be `ACTIVE`, and is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE` otherwise. Fleet counts are entirely unchanged, because the panels are moving within the jobsite rather than between the yard and the site. A `RESET` visit additionally writes its `phase_to` onto the contract as the new `current_phase`; a `RELOCATION` visit changes no phase.

**A visit of any type reported partial other than a delivery-and-install** records its `completed_linear_feet` and `completed_by_user_id` and lands in `PARTIAL`, and produces none of the install branch's consequences: no remainder visit is created, no fleet counts move, no contract transition fires and no invoice is issued. A removal reported partial in particular does not demobilize the contract and does not open a reconciliation.

Two compensating operations exist for a visit that will not happen as planned, and they differ in when they may be used and in what they leave behind.

**Aborting** abandons a visit the crew has already been dispatched to, moving it `SCHEDULED → ABORTED`. The request carries an `abort_reason_code`, which is stored; on any visit that was never aborted the field stays null. Aborting releases every entry of `reserved_unit_counts` from `reserved_count` back to `available_count` and empties `reserved_unit_counts`, and it clears `crew_id` to null, freeing both the crew's window and its share of that day's hours. It deliberately does **not** create a remainder visit, does **not** issue any invoice, and leaves both the contract's `lifecycle_status` and its `billing_status` untouched — a contract whose only install visit was aborted remains in `PENDING_MOBILIZATION`. The visit's `version` advances by 1.

**Cancelling** kills a visit before it runs, and unlike aborting it is available to `AccountManager` as well as `DispatchCoordinator`. It accepts a visit in `PLANNED` or in `SCHEDULED`, moving it to `CANCELLED`, and stores the supplied `cancel_reason_code`; the field stays null on any visit that was never cancelled. A visit cancelled out of `SCHEDULED` releases every entry of `reserved_unit_counts` from `reserved_count` back to `available_count`, empties `reserved_unit_counts`, and clears `crew_id` to null; a visit cancelled out of `PLANNED` had neither a reservation nor a crew and so releases nothing. Either way the `version` advances by 1. Cancellation is the exact compensating action for dispatch: a dispatch followed by a cancellation must leave the panel product's `available_count` precisely where it started.

### 3.7. Demobilization, Yard Reconciliation and Loss Assessment

When a contract enters `DEMOBILIZING`, the system opens a removal reconciliation in `OPEN` on the contract's behalf. Neither the contract reference nor the visit reference is client-supplied: `contract_id` is the demobilizing contract and `work_order_id` is the removal visit whose completion caused the demobilization. Its `deployed_snapshot` records what the yard expects back, as entries each carrying a `product_code` and a `unit_count`. There is always a panel entry, whose `unit_count` is the contract's `deployed_linear_feet` divided by the panel product's `panel_length_ft` and rounded up to the next whole panel. Add-on entries are present only when the contract's `addons_deployed` is true, one per add-on entry of the contract's `fleet_commitments`, carrying that entry's `unit_count`. While the reconciliation is `OPEN`, its `assessment_variant` and `finalized_by_user_id` are null, its `loss_charge_cents` and `damage_charge_cents` are 0, its `unit_returns` is an empty list, and its `theft_police_report_number` and `auditor_note` are null.

Only a `YardAuditor` may certify the count, and — by the four-eyes rule of §2.9 — never the same `X-User-ID` that reported the removal visit complete. Certification supplies `unit_returns`, one entry per product carrying a `product_code`, a `returned` count, a `damaged` count and a `missing` count. Two optional fields accompany it: `theft_police_report_number`, the police report reference filed for units that never came back, which stays null when no report was filed and which — as the next paragraph shows — is worth real money; and `auditor_note`, a free-text yard note that likewise stays null when not supplied. Certification moves the reconciliation `OPEN → FINALIZED`, stores the acting `X-User-ID` as `finalized_by_user_id`, and advances the reconciliation's `version` by 1. A certification attempted against a reconciliation already `FINALIZED` is `ERR_RECONCILIATION_STATE`.

At rung 23 the count must reconcile against the snapshot exactly. `unit_returns` must cover precisely the product codes present in `deployed_snapshot` — no code missing, no code extra, no code repeated — and for every entry the sum of `returned`, `damaged` and `missing` must equal that product's `unit_count` in the snapshot. Every deployed unit must be accounted for as one of the three. Any deviation is refused with `ERR_PANEL_COUNT_MISMATCH`.

The money is then assessed, and the theft term is variant-selected while the damage term is not. **The `DEPRECIATED_VALUE` variant** applies when `theft_police_report_number` is present **and** the contract's `contract_type` is `CONSTRUCTION`; both conditions must hold. **The `REPLACEMENT_COST` variant** applies otherwise — that is, when `theft_police_report_number` is null, or when the contract's `contract_type` is `EVENT`, or both. The reasoning is commercial: without a police report the operator carries no insurance recovery, and event contracts are excluded from the depreciation concession by their terms. Whichever variant applies is stored on the reconciliation as `assessment_variant`.

Under `REPLACEMENT_COST`, `loss_charge_cents` is the exact sum, over every entry of `unit_returns`, of that entry's `missing` count multiplied by its product's `replacement_cost_cents_per_unit`. No depreciation is applied and no rounding is needed.

Under `DEPRECIATED_VALUE`, each product first gets its own depreciation factor: the greater of a floor of 0.40 and the quantity one minus two hundredths multiplied by that product's `fleet_age_months`. The floor is **per product**, so a batch of panels aged fifty months has stopped depreciating at 0.40 while a batch aged eighteen months in the same reconciliation is still on the curve at 0.64. Then, for each entry of `unit_returns`, the entry's `missing` count is multiplied by its product's `replacement_cost_cents_per_unit` and by that product's depreciation factor, and **that per-product product is rounded half up to whole cents**. `loss_charge_cents` is the exact integer sum of those per-product rounded amounts.

`damage_charge_cents` is computed the same way in both variants and never depends on the police report. For each entry of `unit_returns`, the entry's `damaged` count is multiplied by its product's `replacement_cost_cents_per_unit` and by thirty-five hundredths, and that per-product product is rounded half up to whole cents; `damage_charge_cents` is the exact integer sum of those rounded amounts. Damage is never depreciated: a damaged panel is charged against undepreciated replacement cost no matter how old the batch is.

Finalization then runs its own three-hop cascade. Hop one moves the fleet: for each product, its `returned` count moves from `deployed_count` back into `available_count`, its `damaged` count moves from `deployed_count` into `damaged_count`, and its `missing` count moves from `deployed_count` into `lost_count`. Hop two issues money, but conditionally: when `loss_charge_cents` plus `damage_charge_cents` is strictly greater than zero, a `FINAL` invoice is issued against the contract carrying a `LOSS` line for `loss_charge_cents` and a `DAMAGE` line for `damage_charge_cents`; when the sum is exactly zero, **no invoice is created at all**. Hop three, when an invoice was created, lifts the account's `open_balance_cents` by that invoice's `total_cents` and re-evaluates its `credit_hold_flag`.

### 3.8. The Rental Ledger: Billing Runs, Payments and Voids

Four kinds of invoice exist, distinguished by `invoice_kind`, and they arise from four different places in the workflow. A `MOBILIZATION` invoice is raised by the contract's activation. A `PERIODIC` invoice is raised by an `AccountManager` running a billing period. A `CANCELLATION` invoice is raised by a pre-mobilization cancellation that carried a fee. A `FINAL` invoice is raised by a reconciliation that found loss or damage. Whatever raised it, every invoice is created with `status` `ISSUED`, with `amount_paid_cents` 0, with `void_reason_code` null, with `issued_at_utc` stamped from the logical clock, with `due_at_utc` set to exactly thirty days after `issued_at_utc`, and with `issued_by_user_id` recording the `X-User-ID` of the request that caused the issuance — including for the three kinds a system cascade produces. Only a `PERIODIC` invoice carries a billed window: `period_start_utc`, `period_end_utc` and `billable_days` are populated on `PERIODIC` invoices and are respectively null, null and 0 on `MOBILIZATION`, `CANCELLATION` and `FINAL` invoices.

Every invoice carries `lines`, each line holding a `line_type`, a `product_code`, a `quantity` and an `amount_cents`. Seven line types exist, and each kind of invoice draws from its own subset. A `MOBILIZATION` invoice carries an `INSTALL_LABOR` line, a `DELIVERY` line, and a `PERMIT_HANDLING` line when that fee is non-zero. A `PERIODIC` invoice carries exactly one `RENTAL` line. A `CANCELLATION` invoice carries exactly one `CANCELLATION_FEE` line. A `FINAL` invoice carries one `LOSS` line and one `DAMAGE` line, and carries both whenever it exists at all — even if one of the two amounts is zero. On the `INSTALL_LABOR`, `DELIVERY`, `PERMIT_HANDLING` and `CANCELLATION_FEE` lines, `product_code` is null and `quantity` is 1. On the `RENTAL` line, `product_code` is null and `quantity` is the invoice's `billable_days`. On the `LOSS` and `DAMAGE` lines, `product_code` is null and `quantity` is respectively the total `missing` count and the total `damaged` count summed across the reconciliation's `unit_returns`.

The money on the face of every invoice, of every kind, is rolled up by one fixed sequence. Each line's `amount_cents` is already an integer produced by whichever calculation owns it — the quote pricing, the period charge, the loss assessment or the cancellation fee — and the rollup never re-rounds a line. **First**, `subtotal_cents` is the exact integer sum of the line amounts. **Second**, `tax_cents` is `subtotal_cents` multiplied by the jurisdiction's `rental_tax_rate_bp` and divided by ten thousand, rounded half up; the jurisdiction is the one reached through the contract's jobsite. Tax is computed once on the rounded subtotal, never per line. **Third**, `late_fee_cents` is computed, and it is non-zero only when two conditions hold together: the contract's `billing_status` observed **at the start of the operation** is `PAST_DUE`, and the invoice being issued is a `PERIODIC` invoice. When both hold, the fee is the past-due open amount multiplied by fifteen thousandths — one and a half percent — rounded half up, where the past-due open amount is the sum, over the contract's invoices whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` and whose `due_at_utc` is at or before the logical clock, of `total_cents` less `amount_paid_cents`, measured **before the invoice being issued exists**. In every other case `late_fee_cents` is 0. **Fourth**, `total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` plus `tax_cents` plus `late_fee_cents` — note carefully that the late fee is added **after** the tax step and is therefore **not part of the taxable base**.

Billing a rental period is an `AccountManager` operation naming a `period_start_utc` and a `period_end_utc`. It is admitted while the contract's `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE` or `DEMOBILIZING`, and refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE` while it is `PENDING_MOBILIZATION`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`. The orientation of the submitted window is settled earlier, at rung 3: a `period_end_utc` that is not strictly after `period_start_utc` is the inverted-or-zero-length window of §2.6 and is answered with `ERR_VALIDATION`, judged straight after body-shape integrity and before the path contract is resolved — so on this endpoint an inverted window outranks the contract not-found, the contract-state and the billing-window failures alike, and no inverted window ever reaches rung 16. At rung 16 the window is then checked on three further counts: `period_start_utc` must be at or after the contract's `billing_anchor_utc`; `period_end_utc` must be at or before the logical clock, so no future period may be billed; and `period_start_utc` must be at or after the `period_end_utc` of the contract's most recent `PERIODIC` invoice, so no window is billed twice and none is billed retroactively into a window already invoiced. Any of the three failing is `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_INVALID`.

The rental amount is then prorated, and the two contract families prorate by genuinely different arithmetic rather than by a different constant. The variant is selected by the contract's own `contract_type`, copied from the converted quote, and it is **never overridable on the billing-run request**. Either way, the raw day count is the number of whole days in the half-open window from `period_start_utc` to `period_end_utc`, and that raw count is what is stored on the invoice as `billable_days`.

For an `EVENT` contract, the day count used for money is the greater of a three-day minimum and the raw day count. A daily rate is then obtained by dividing the contract's `monthly_recurring_cents` by thirty and **rounding that quotient half up to whole cents first**, and the `RENTAL` line amount is that rounded daily rate multiplied by the day count. Rounding the rate first and multiplying second is deliberate — multiplying first and rounding once at the end yields a different number, and this specification requires the former.

For a `CONSTRUCTION` contract there is neither a minimum day floor nor a daily rate. The raw day count is divided by a twenty-eight-day billing cycle and floored to give the number of whole cycles; the remainder days are the raw day count less the whole cycles multiplied by twenty-eight. The `RENTAL` line amount is then the number of whole cycles multiplied by the full `monthly_recurring_cents`, plus a prorated remainder term obtained by multiplying `monthly_recurring_cents` by the remainder days, dividing by twenty-eight, and rounding half up **once**. Whole cycles bill at the full monthly figure; only the remainder prorates. As an illustration of how far apart the two families land, a contract with a `monthly_recurring_cents` of 60,000 billed over a seventeen-day window yields 34,000 cents under the event variant — a daily rate of 2,000 multiplied by seventeen days — and 36,429 cents under the construction variant, which has no whole cycle and prorates seventeen twenty-eighths of the month.

A completed billing run issues the `PERIODIC` invoice, sets the contract's `billed_through_utc` to the window's `period_end_utc`, lifts the account's `open_balance_cents`, re-evaluates its `credit_hold_flag`, and advances the contract's `version`.

The contract's money dimension, `billing_status`, evolves independently of its `lifecycle_status`; the two are parallel dimensions on the same row, and a contract may perfectly well be `ACTIVE` and `PAST_DUE`, or `DEMOBILIZING` and `CURRENT`. The money dimension moves on five edges, and each of them advances the contract's `version` by 1. From `NOT_BILLED` it moves to `CURRENT` when the contract's first invoice of any kind — `MOBILIZATION`, `PERIODIC`, `CANCELLATION` or `FINAL` — is issued, and that issuance raises the account's `open_balance_cents` by the invoice's `total_cents`. From `CURRENT` it moves to `PAST_DUE`, re-evaluated at the start of a billing run and again on a payment, when at least one invoice of the contract has `status` `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` and a `due_at_utc` at or before the logical clock. From `PAST_DUE` it moves back to `CURRENT` on a payment when no invoice of the contract remains with `status` `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` and a `due_at_utc` at or before the clock, while at least one invoice of the contract is still `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`. From either `CURRENT` or `PAST_DUE` it moves to `SETTLED`, on a payment or on a void, once every invoice of the contract has `status` `PAID` or `VOID`. And `SETTLED` is **not** terminal in the money dimension: when a new invoice is issued for a contract whose ledger had been fully settled or written off, and whose `lifecycle_status` is not `CLOSED`, the money dimension moves `SETTLED → CURRENT` and the account's `open_balance_cents` rises by the new invoice's `total_cents`. A contract whose only invoice was voided therefore settles and then re-opens when the next period is billed, with `lifecycle_status` untouched throughout.

A payment is applied by an `AccountManager` at the **account** level, naming a single `amount_cents`, and it is allocated across that account's open invoices oldest-due first. The allocation order is the account's invoices whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, sorted by `due_at_utc` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending, then by `id` ascending. The system then walks that list carrying a remaining amount that starts at `amount_cents`. Each invoice in turn absorbs the lesser of the remaining amount and its own open amount — its `total_cents` less its current `amount_paid_cents` — that absorbed figure is added to its `amount_paid_cents`, and the remaining amount is reduced by the same figure. The first bucket fills completely before the second receives anything; there is no proportional spreading. An invoice whose `amount_paid_cents` reaches its `total_cents` moves to `PAID`; an invoice that absorbed a non-zero amount but is not yet full moves to `PARTIALLY_PAID`. Concretely, an invoice in `ISSUED` that absorbs strictly less than its full open amount moves `ISSUED → PARTIALLY_PAID`; one that absorbs exactly its full open amount moves `ISSUED → PAID`; and one already in `PARTIALLY_PAID` that absorbs exactly its remaining open amount moves `PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID`. Every invoice actually touched advances its own `version` by 1.

At rung 26, a payment larger than the account's total open amount across its `ISSUED` and `PARTIALLY_PAID` invoices is **refused in full** with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`: if any amount would remain after walking the whole list, no invoice is mutated at all and no partial allocation is retained. Within the same request, a successful payment recomputes `open_balance_cents` and `credit_hold_flag`, re-evaluates `billing_status` for every contract whose invoices were touched, and may cascade a contract from `DEMOBILIZING` to `CLOSED`.

Voiding is the compensating action for issuance, and it is available to `AccountManager` subject to the four-eyes rule of §2.9 — never by the `X-User-ID` recorded as the invoice's `issued_by_user_id`. A void accepts an invoice whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, moving it to `VOID`; an invoice already `PAID` or already `VOID` is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_STATE`. The request carries a `void_reason_code`, which is stored. The void reduces the account's `open_balance_cents` by exactly the invoice's `total_cents` less its `amount_paid_cents` as measured at the moment of the void, and raises the account's `lifetime_credited_cents` by that same amount. Cash already collected is not refunded, so the void never moves `amount_paid_cents` — voiding a partly paid invoice writes off only the unpaid remainder. The account's `credit_hold_flag` and the contract's `billing_status` are both re-evaluated inside the same request, and the invoice's `version` advances by 1.

### 3.9. Cancelling Before Mobilization, and Closing the Contract

A contract may be walked away from, but only before the fence goes up. An `AccountManager` cancels by supplying a `cancel_reason_code`, and the contract moves `PENDING_MOBILIZATION → CANCELLED`; a contract in `ACTIVE`, `DEMOBILIZING`, `CLOSED` or already `CANCELLED` refuses the request with `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE`. The penalty is assessed by the notice the customer gave, and the notice clock runs from the logical clock forward to the scheduled start of the contract's **first** install visit.

Identifying that reference visit is deterministic. Among the contract's visits whose `work_order_type` is `DELIVERY_INSTALL` and whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `SCHEDULED`, the candidates are sorted by `scheduled_start_utc` ascending with rows carrying a null `scheduled_start_utc` sorted last, then by `created_at` ascending, then by `id` ascending, and the first row of that ordering is the reference visit. When no such visit exists at all, or when the row so chosen has a null `scheduled_start_utc`, the `cancellation_fee_cents` is 0 — nothing was ever committed to a crew, so nothing is forfeited.

Otherwise the notice is the interval from the logical clock to the reference visit's `scheduled_start_utc`, and it may be negative if the install was already due. The free-notice allowance differs by family: an `EVENT` contract is allowed 72 hours — three days — of free notice, and a `CONSTRUCTION` contract is allowed 168 hours, a full week. When the notice is at or above the allowance the `cancellation_fee_cents` is 0; the boundary itself is free, so notice of exactly 72 hours on an event contract costs nothing while notice of 71 hours does not. When the notice falls short, the two families forfeit different things. An `EVENT` contract owes half of its `monthly_recurring_cents`, rounded half up, plus the whole of its `install_labor_cents` — it forfeits the actual labor it had committed. A `CONSTRUCTION` contract owes a quarter of its `monthly_recurring_cents`, rounded half up, plus a flat mobilization minimum of 35,000 cents, regardless of what its own labor figure happened to be.

Cancellation then does three things in order. The fee is computed first, while the contract's `PLANNED` and `SCHEDULED` install visits are still in those statuses, and stored as `cancellation_fee_cents`. Next, every visit on the contract whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `SCHEDULED` — of any type, not just installs — moves to `CANCELLED`, releasing every entry of its `reserved_unit_counts` from `reserved_count` back to `available_count`, clearing its `crew_id` and advancing its own `version` by 1. Finally, when and only when `cancellation_fee_cents` is strictly greater than zero, a `CANCELLATION` invoice is issued against the contract carrying a single `CANCELLATION_FEE` line for that amount, which lifts the account's `open_balance_cents` by the invoice's `total_cents` and moves the contract's `billing_status` off `NOT_BILLED`. When the fee is exactly zero **no invoice is created at all** and the money dimension does not move. The contract's `version` advances by 1.

The other way a contract leaves the system is by being closed out properly, and closure is the one edge that reads both parallel dimensions at once. A contract moves `DEMOBILIZING → CLOSED` when three conditions hold together: its `lifecycle_status` is `DEMOBILIZING`, its `billing_status` is `SETTLED`, and its removal reconciliation has reached `FINALIZED`. Those three conditions are evaluated at the end of any request that could newly satisfy them — a payment, an invoice void, or a reconciliation finalization — and the contract closes as soon as all three hold, advancing its `version` by 1. Both `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal in the mobilization dimension: no further visit may be planned or dispatched, no phase move ordered, and no billing period run against a contract in either.

A billing run returns 201, because it brings a new invoice into existence.
