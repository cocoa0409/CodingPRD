# Junior Golf Tour Ranking Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A regional junior golf association runs a competitive season on a shoestring staff, and almost every complaint it receives traces back to the same root cause: the tour's records live in spreadsheets that nobody can reconcile. A family is told their child is exempt into the state invitational and then finds they were waitlisted. A coach receives a ranking sheet that disagrees with the one the tournament director printed the same morning. An assistance cheque is written twice because two administrators each believed the other had not committed the money. A card is certified while a rules incident is still open, and the finish order has to be republished. None of these failures is a golf problem — they are all bookkeeping problems, and they are what this system exists to eliminate.

This document specifies the HTTP API for **Junior Golf Tour & Ranking Administration**: a single authoritative service that registers juniors into age divisions, verifies their eligibility and handicap provenance, publishes a tournament schedule with entry windows and finite field sizes, allocates those scarce playing slots by exemption category and certified ranking, moves competitors on and off a strictly ordered waitlist, tracks pace of play and peer-verified scoring, adjudicates rules and conduct cases under segregation-of-duties rules, computes ranking points from event strength and field quality, administers a season-long financial-assistance purse down to the cent, and exposes a consented recruiting view to college coaches. Every collection is a peer resource mounted at the service root — `/seasons`, `/members`, `/tournaments`, `/exemption-grants`, `/entries`, `/rounds`, `/rules-cases`, `/conduct-cases`, `/aid-applications`, `/coach-access-grants` — and every relationship between them travels as a foreign-key field in a request body rather than as a nested URL segment.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **scarcity is accounted for, never estimated**: a playing slot, a cent of assistance money and a ranking position are each conserved quantities, and the specification below pins the exact order in which they move. Second, **nobody decides their own case**: the official who reports a rules breach may not rule on it, the staffer who files a conduct complaint may not decide it, and the peer who attests a scorecard may not certify it. Both commitments are enforced on the acting user's identity, not merely on their role.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries its caller identity in two headers: `X-User-ID`, the identifier of the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, the name of the role that user is acting in. Seven roles exist, and their names are spelled exactly as follows: `TourAdmin`, `RulesOfficial`, `ChiefReferee`, `ConductCommittee`, `Member`, `Coach`, `Anonymous`.

A request whose `X-User-Role` header is absent, or whose value is not one of those seven names, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Two endpoints are exempt from that requirement because they are deliberately public: self-service junior registration (`POST /members`) and the test-only clock setter (`POST /_clock/set_current_utc`). A request whose role is recognised but is not listed among the target endpoint's authorised roles is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403); that authorisation check applies to every endpoint without exception, including the two public ones.

The roles divide the tour's authority deliberately. A `TourAdmin` is association staff: it owns the season, the schedule, the entry-selector run, the ranking run and the assistance purse, and it is the only role that can open a season, run the rankings, schedule or cancel a tournament, open or close an entry window, set a field, mint an exemption grant, decide an assistance application, or verify a junior's eligibility. It deliberately cannot officiate: a `TourAdmin` may neither adjudicate a rules case nor decide a conduct case. A `RulesOfficial` works on the course — it alone records pace-of-play observations, and it shares score submission, peer verification, round certification and rules-case reporting with others; it may not decide the cases it reports. A `ChiefReferee` is the sole adjudicator of rules cases, and additionally starts play and builds the tee sheet; it may not decide conduct cases, open a season or run the rankings. A `ConductCommittee` is the sole decider of parent and caddie conduct cases and therefore the only role that can suspend a junior; it holds no power over the schedule, the field, scoring or money. A `Member` is the junior competitor. A `Coach` is a college recruiter. `Anonymous` is the unauthenticated public, which may register a junior, read the public schedule and drive the test clock.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they matter for every ownership check in this specification. When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Member` compare that resource's member foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Member` caller may read or write only rows belonging to its own membership: its own member record, ranking view and published-results view, its own entries, its own assistance applications, its own rounds and grants. A `Member` caller addressing another junior's row is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE` (HTTP 403). For all six other roles — `TourAdmin`, `RulesOfficial`, `ChiefReferee`, `ConductCommittee`, `Coach` and `Anonymous` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to any entity's `id`. The server records it where the specification says to: `certified_by_user_id` on a round, `reported_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a rules case, `filed_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a conduct case, `decided_by_user_id` on an assistance application, and `coach_user_id` on a recruiting access grant. In particular, a `Coach`'s `X-User-ID` is stamped onto the grants it requests and never equals any entity's `id`; a `Coach` reaches a junior's data only through a live recruiting grant naming that same opaque identifier, and is otherwise rejected with `ERR_COACH_ACCESS_DENIED` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds, or one that is not an integer, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific endpoint narrative in section 3 states otherwise. The response envelope's `meta` object carries four pagination fields on list responses — `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied), and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window). Single-resource responses omit all four.

Where a list endpoint also accepts filter parameters, those filters are combined conjunctively: a request narrowing `/entries` by both `member_id` and `entry_status` returns only rows matching both. Omitted filters impose no restriction.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Row Versions

Every resource in this API — a `Season`, `Member`, `Tournament`, `ExemptionGrant`, `Entry`, `Round`, `RulesCase`, `ConductCase`, `AidApplication` or `CoachAccessGrant` — carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and is never accepted from a client. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped once when the row is written and never changed thereafter. `updated_at` carries the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. All ten entity types in this system carry all four properties; none of them is exempt.

Because `version` advances on every mutation, it is also the currency of the concurrency mechanism described in section 2.7, and clients that intend to write against a junior's record must read it first.

Timestamps that record business events — `submitted_at`, `withdrawn_at`, `certified_at`, `decided_at`, `requested_at`, `completed_at`, `expires_at`, `last_ranking_run_at` — are all ISO 8601 UTC instants and are all read from the logical clock described next, never from the host's wall clock. The three-fractional-digit rendering belongs to `created_at`, `updated_at` and `api_request_timestamp` alone; every other instant this API stores or returns — those business-event stamps, a tournament's `entry_open_at`, `entry_close_at` and `withdrawal_deadline_at`, a round's `tee_time`, and the `current_utc` and `previous_utc` reported by the clock capability — is rendered at whole-second precision, as `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ`. An instant supplied by a client is normalised into that same canonical rendering before it is stored or echoed back, so a zone offset it carried is converted to UTC and any fractional-second part it carried is discarded.

### 2.5. The Logical Tour Clock

Every temporal rule in this API reads a single process-wide logical UTC instant rather than real time. Entry windows, withdrawal deadlines, handicap freshness, the rules-case reopen window, appeal windows, the conduct-points decay window and recruiting-grant expiry are all evaluated against that one instant. At process start the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and every temporal expectation in this specification is expressed relative to that default.

An administrative test capability replaces the clock with a supplied `current_utc` instant and answers with both the new `current_utc` and the `previous_utc` value it displaced. Setting the clock modifies no domain row and recomputes no derived field: it is purely a change of the reference instant that subsequent requests will read. A malformed `current_utc` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.6. The Order of Refusal

A request that violates several rules at once must fail with a single, predictable error. The rules of this API are therefore strictly ordered, and the **first** violated rule wins and short-circuits every rule after it. Implementations must evaluate in exactly this sequence.

**Plumbing first.** Authentication (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`) is checked before authorisation (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`), and authorisation before request-schema validity (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`). The schema check covers every `POST` endpoint and requires that the body parses, that every required input field is present, that every enum-typed value belongs to its declared set, that integers are integers, that dates are ISO 8601, and that no numeric field is negative where the entity declares a non-negative constraint.

**Existence next, outermost referent first.** A `season_id` in a path or body that does not resolve yields `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`, and that check applies to the season ranking run, junior registration, tournament scheduling, assistance application creation and the single-season read. Then a `member_id` or `memberId` that does not resolve yields `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, on every endpoint that carries either. Then an unresolvable `tournament_id`, `tournamentId`, `source_tournament_id` or `qualifies_into_tournament_id` yields `ERR_TOURNAMENT_NOT_FOUND`, on every endpoint carrying any of the four. Then an unresolvable `entry_id` or `entryId` yields `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND` — applicable to the single-entry read, withdrawal, payment and round creation. Then an unresolvable `round_id` or `roundId` yields `ERR_ROUND_NOT_FOUND` — applicable to the single-round read, pace observations, score submission, peer verification, certification and rules-case reporting. Then a `caseId` that resolves to no `RulesCase` on a rules-case route, or to no `ConductCase` on a conduct-case route, yields `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND` on every read and write under either route family. Then an `applicationId` in a path or an `aid_application_id` in a body that does not resolve yields `ERR_AID_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND` — applicable to the single-application read, the assistance decision, and entry submission. Finally an unresolvable `grantId`, `accessGrantId` or `exemption_grant_id` yields `ERR_GRANT_NOT_FOUND` — applicable to the single exemption-grant read, every read and write under the recruiting-grant routes, and entry submission.

**Who is asking, third.** Ownership is checked next: a `Member` caller reaching a row that is not its own, or a `Coach` caller reaching a junior without a `GRANTED`, unexpired recruiting grant naming its own `X-User-ID`, is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE`. That check guards the single-member read, the ranking view, the published-results view, the single-entry read, entry submission, withdrawal, payment and assistance-application creation. Immediately after ownership comes segregation of duties, `ERR_SELF_ADJUDICATION` (HTTP 409): the `X-User-ID` adjudicating a rules case must differ from that case's `reported_by_user_id`, the `X-User-ID` deciding a conduct case must differ from that case's `filed_by_user_id`, and the `X-User-ID` certifying a round must differ from that round's `marker_member_id`. Those three operations — rules-case adjudication, conduct-case decision and round certification — are the only ones the rule guards.

**Concurrency, fourth.** The `expected_member_version` check described in section 2.7 runs after segregation of duties and before any state gate.

**State gates, fifth.** Each aggregate is checked for admissibility in its own right, and the aggregates are checked in a fixed order: tournament status (`ERR_INVALID_TOURNAMENT_STATE`), then entry status (`ERR_INVALID_ENTRY_STATE`), then round status (`ERR_INVALID_ROUND_STATE`), then rules-case status (`ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`), then conduct-case status (`ERR_INVALID_CONDUCT_STATE`), then assistance-application and recruiting-grant status (`ERR_INVALID_GRANT_STATE`). The admissible statuses for each operation are given in the workflow narratives of section 3, and all six errors carry HTTP 409.

**Safety gates, sixth — these outrank every business constraint.** A junior whose `eligibility_status` is `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_SUSPENDED` on five operations: entry submission, assistance-application creation, exemption-grant minting, recruiting-access requests, and handicap revisions. Immediately after that, a junior whose `eligibility_status` is anything other than `VERIFIED` is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_VERIFIED` on the two operations that put a junior into competition: entry submission and exemption-grant minting. (Because the suspension check runs first, a suspended junior always sees `ERR_MEMBER_SUSPENDED` and never `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_VERIFIED`.) Last of the safety gates, round creation validates its peer marker and refuses with `ERR_INVALID_MARKER`.

**Business and referential constraints, seventh.** In ascending order: division match (`ERR_DIVISION_MISMATCH`, on entry submission and exemption-grant minting), the handicap ceiling (`ERR_HANDICAP_ABOVE_LIMIT`, on entry submission), handicap freshness and provenance (`ERR_HANDICAP_STALE`, on entry submission and eligibility verification), exemption applicability (`ERR_EXEMPTION_NOT_APPLICABLE`, on entry submission), assistance approval (`ERR_AID_NOT_APPROVED`, on entry submission), and duplication (`ERR_DUPLICATE_ENTRY`, on entry submission and assistance-application creation).

**Clock windows, eighth.** The entry window (`ERR_ENTRY_WINDOW_CLOSED`, on entry submission), the close-entries precondition (`ERR_ENTRY_WINDOW_OPEN`, on closing an entry window), the 48-hour certified-round reopen window (`ERR_REOPEN_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, on both rules-case reporting and rules-case adjudication), and the appeal windows (`ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, on both rules-case appeals and conduct-case appeals).

**Capacity, overlap and consent, ninth.** The schedule-overlap rule (`ERR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`, on entry submission), the tee-slot capacity rule (`ERR_TEE_SLOT_FULL`, on round creation), the non-empty-field rule (`ERR_EMPTY_FIELD`, on starting play), the open-rules-case bar on certification (`ERR_OPEN_RULES_CASE`, on certification), publication consent (`ERR_PUBLICATION_NOT_CONSENTED`, on the published-results view and on recruiting-access requests), and live coach access (`ERR_COACH_ACCESS_DENIED`, on the published-results view and on the ranking view).

**Money, last.** Income eligibility (`ERR_INCOME_INELIGIBLE`, on the assistance decision), the per-junior allocation cap (`ERR_AID_ALLOCATION_EXHAUSTED`, on entry submission), the season budget (`ERR_AID_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`, on entry submission), payment exactness (`ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_INVALID`, on payment), and outstanding fees at the start of play (`ERR_ENTRY_FEE_OUTSTANDING`, on starting play).

The practical consequence is worth stating plainly: the money checks are the *weakest* rules in the system, and the safety gates are among the strongest. An entry submitted for a suspended junior against an exhausted assistance pool reports `ERR_MEMBER_SUSPENDED`, never `ERR_AID_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`. An entry submitted outside the entry window by a junior in the wrong age division reports `ERR_DIVISION_MISMATCH`, because the division check outranks the window check.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Junior Record

Three write operations in this API supersede something the junior already holds, and all three are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the `Member` row as the parent entity. Each of them requires a non-optional input field named `expected_member_version`, carrying the `version` value the client last observed on that junior's record. If `expected_member_version` does not equal the junior's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MEMBER_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written.

The three guarded operations are `POST /aid-applications`, `POST /coach-access-grants`, and `POST /members/{memberId}/handicap-revisions`. Each one has a concrete reason to be guarded. Creating an assistance application legitimately supersedes the junior's prior `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` application for the same season, so a writer holding an out-of-date view of the junior would silently discard a decision it never saw. Requesting recruiting access supersedes that same coach's prior `REQUESTED` or `GRANTED` grant on the junior, so a stale view can revoke access the coach still believes is live. Posting a handicap revision overwrites the stored `handicap_index` outright, so a writer working from a stale view would erase a revision it never read.

This is a version-comparison rule, not a timing rule: it does not matter how far apart in time the two writes are, only whether the version the client presents still matches the version the server holds. Every one of the three operations increments `Member.version` when it succeeds, which is precisely why a second write presenting the same `expected_member_version` will fail. No other endpoint in this API takes a version field of any kind.

### 2.8. Precision, Rounding and Money

All money in this system is stored as an integer number of cents and is never held fractionally. Every field whose name ends in `_cents` — `assistance_budget_cents`, `assistance_available_cents`, `assistance_committed_cents`, `assistance_disbursed_cents`, `entry_fee_cents`, `gross_fee_cents`, `late_entry_surcharge_cents`, `aid_award_cents`, `net_fee_due_cents`, `amount_paid_cents`, `refunded_cents`, `household_income_cents`, `income_per_dependent_cents`, `estimated_award_cents`, `per_member_cap_cents`, `consumed_cents` and the `amount_cents` supplied with a payment — is an integer.

Wherever this document says an amount or a factor is rounded, the rounding is **half up on the magnitude of the value**: an exact half moves away from zero, so 2.15 rounds to 2.2 and −2.15 rounds to −2.2. Three precisions appear in the specification and each is applied where stated: monetary results are rounded to a whole number of cents; ranking and field-quality figures are rounded to 2 decimal places; scoring differentials are rounded to 1 decimal place. Ages, stroke counts, positions, counters and conduct points are integers and involve no rounding at all.

Two calculations round more than once, and the order is load-bearing rather than incidental. Scoring differentials are rounded per round first and then the per-round rounded values are averaged and rounded again — never averaged from unrounded values. Event points round the finish factor and the differential bonus each to 2 decimal places *before* combining them, and then round the combined result to 2 decimal places again. Section 3 states both sequences step by step; implementations that collapse them into a single rounding at the end will produce different numbers.

### 2.9. Ledger Discipline: the Season Assistance Purse

A season's financial-assistance money lives in three buckets on the `Season` row and moves between them only in matched pairs. `assistance_budget_cents` is set once at season creation and is immutable thereafter. `assistance_available_cents` holds money not yet promised to anybody. `assistance_committed_cents` holds money reserved against entries that are still live. `assistance_disbursed_cents` holds money spent irreversibly.

Two invariants must hold after **every** request, without exception. The first: `assistance_budget_cents` always equals the sum of `assistance_available_cents`, `assistance_committed_cents` and `assistance_disbursed_cents`. The second: `assistance_committed_cents` always equals the sum of `aid_award_cents` over exactly those entries whose `entry_status` is `APPLIED`, `ACCEPTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `COMPETING` — the four live statuses, no more and no fewer.

There are exactly five movements, and each is a paired debit and credit of the same amount rather than a bare write to one bucket. **Settlement**, at entry submission, subtracts the award from `assistance_available_cents`, adds it to `assistance_committed_cents`, and adds it to the funding application's `consumed_cents`. **Withdrawal** before the deadline reverses that exactly: it adds the award back to `assistance_available_cents`, subtracts it from `assistance_committed_cents`, and subtracts it from `consumed_cents`. **Tournament cancellation** performs the identical reversal. **Forfeiture** subtracts the award from `assistance_committed_cents` and adds it to `assistance_disbursed_cents`, and deliberately does *not* release `consumed_cents` — a forfeited junior has spent that share of their personal cap. **Tournament completion** performs the same committed-to-disbursed movement, again without releasing `consumed_cents`. Settlement followed by a pre-deadline withdrawal is therefore an exactly compensating pair: `assistance_available_cents` returns to its precise pre-settlement value.

One movement notably does not exist. Promoting a junior off the waitlist onto the playing field moves **no money at all**. The award was reserved at submission time while the entry was still `APPLIED`, and the committed bucket already carries it, because the committed bucket spans `APPLIED`, `ACCEPTED`, `WAITLISTED` and `COMPETING` alike. An implementation that re-reserves money at promotion time will break the first invariant.

### 2.10. Slot Census Discipline

Each tournament maintains a live census of its entries, recomputed on every transition that changes any of its entries' `entry_status`. `accepted_count` counts entries whose `entry_status` is `ACCEPTED`, `COMPETING`, `FINISHED` or `DISQUALIFIED` — that is, every entry that holds or has consumed a playing slot. `waitlist_count` counts entries whose `entry_status` is `WAITLISTED`. `applied_count` counts entries whose `entry_status` is `APPLIED`. `closed_count` counts entries whose `entry_status` is `WITHDRAWN`, `FORFEITED`, `REJECTED` or `CANCELLED`. `open_slot_count` is `field_size_limit` less `accepted_count`, floored at zero so that it is never negative. `entry_count` is the total number of entries referencing the tournament in any status whatsoever, and `finished_count` counts only those whose `entry_status` is `FINISHED`.

Three invariants follow. `accepted_count` never exceeds `field_size_limit`. `entry_count` always equals `applied_count` plus `accepted_count` plus `waitlist_count` plus `closed_count`. And — the invariant that makes waitlist movement auditable — the three-bucket total of `accepted_count` plus `waitlist_count` plus `closed_count` is unchanged by a withdrawal that is answered by a waitlist promotion: the withdrawal moves one row from accepted to closed and the promotion moves one row from waitlisted to accepted, so both `accepted_count` and `open_slot_count` return to their exact pre-withdrawal values while the total holds steady.

Note that a `DISQUALIFIED` entry still occupies its slot. Disqualification never frees capacity and never triggers a promotion.

### 2.11. Cascade Depth and Compensation Asymmetry

Several operations in this system reach far beyond the row they name. A single conduct decision can suspend a junior, which rejects their pending entries, forfeits their accepted ones, revokes their exemptions, promotes other juniors off waitlists, renumbers those waitlists, and moves assistance money from committed to disbursed — five levels deep, all inside one request. Section 3 spells out each cascade in the order its hops must fire. Two general rules govern them all.

First, **every row a cascade touches has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented**, not merely the row the request named. A cascade that suspends a junior and forfeits three of their entries advances four `version` counters.

Second, **compensation is deliberately asymmetric, and the asymmetries are intentional rather than oversights**. A pre-deadline withdrawal returns a consumed exemption grant to `ACTIVE`; a forfeiture leaves it `CONSUMED` forever. A cleared conduct appeal can lift a suspension and restore a junior to `VERIFIED`, but it does **not** reinstate the entries that the suspension already rejected or forfeited. A dismissed rules appeal reverses the penalty strokes the case had applied, but if that case had disqualified a round, the round stays `DISQUALIFIED` — disqualification is final. Implementations must not "helpfully" undo these.

### 2.12. Path Resolution and Read Projections

Path parameters are validated left to right, and the outermost referent is resolved before anything nested inside it. A request naming a season and then a sub-resource of that season resolves the season first, so an unresolvable season yields `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND` regardless of what else in the request might also be invalid. The same principle applies to `{memberId}`, `{tournamentId}`, `{entryId}`, `{roundId}`, `{caseId}`, `{applicationId}`, `{grantId}` and `{accessGrantId}`.

Because every collection is a peer at the service root, each entity is reachable two ways: by its own single-resource read, and through a filtered list that accepts each of its foreign keys as an optional query parameter. Members can be narrowed by `season_id`, `age_division` and `eligibility_status`. Tournaments can be narrowed by `season_id`, `division`, `status`, `qualifies_into_tournament_id` and `winner_entry_id`. Exemption grants can be narrowed by `member_id`, `tournament_id`, `source_tournament_id`, `consumed_by_entry_id` and `status`. Entries can be narrowed by `member_id`, `tournament_id`, `exemption_grant_id`, `aid_application_id`, `entry_status` and `fee_status`. Rounds can be narrowed by `entry_id`, `tournament_id`, `marker_member_id`, `status` and `round_number`. Rules cases can be narrowed by `round_id` and `status`. Conduct cases can be narrowed by `member_id`, `tournament_id`, `status` and `subject_type`. Assistance applications can be narrowed by `member_id`, `season_id` and `status`. Recruiting grants can be narrowed by `member_id`, `coach_user_id` and `status`.

Two reads are computed projections rather than stored rows, and they exist because two different numbers must be observable side by side. The ranking projection returns `member_id`, `age_division`, `provisional_ranking_points`, `provisional_counted_events`, `certified_ranking_points`, `certified_counted_events`, `certified_rank_position`, `last_ranking_epoch` and the season's current `ranking_epoch`, so that the divergence between the live figure and the last certified figure — and the staleness of the latter — is directly visible. The recruiting projection returns `member_id`, `full_name`, `age_division`, `handicap_index`, `publication_opt_in`, `certified_ranking_points`, `certified_rank_position`, `finished_event_count` (the number of the junior's entries whose `entry_status` is `FINISHED`), `best_finish_position` (the numerically lowest `finish_position` across those entries, or null when there are none) and `avg_scoring_differential` (the mean of those entries' own `avg_scoring_differential` values, rounded half up to 1 decimal place, or null when there are none).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a Season and Registering Juniors

Everything in the tour hangs off a **season**, and a `TourAdmin` opens one by supplying its human label `name` (between 1 and 120 characters), its `start_date` and `end_date` as calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, an `age_cutoff_date`, a `ranking_window_start_date`, and the size of the participation-assistance purse as `assistance_budget_cents`. The `end_date` must fall strictly after the `start_date`, and `assistance_budget_cents` must be zero or greater; a season that fails either constraint is refused as an invalid request. The `age_cutoff_date` is the single reference date against which every junior's competitive age is measured for the whole season, and the `ranking_window_start_date` fences the ranking: only tournaments whose `start_date` falls on or after that date will ever feed a junior's ranking figures.

A freshly opened season starts fully funded and completely unspent. Its `assistance_available_cents` equals its `assistance_budget_cents`, while `assistance_committed_cents` and `assistance_disbursed_cents` both start at zero. The budget figure itself is immutable once written — no operation in this API changes it. The season's ranking watermarks likewise start empty: `ranking_epoch` is zero, `ranked_member_count` is zero, and both `last_ranking_run_at` and `last_ranking_as_of_date` are null until a ranking run has actually been executed.

Junior registration is deliberately self-service and publicly callable, because families sign up long before any staffer is available to look at them. A registration supplies the `season_id` of the season being joined, the golfer's `full_name` (1 to 120 characters), their `birth_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters recording residency, a `guardian_email` used for conduct notices, the current `handicap_index`, the `handicap_effective_date` on which that index was revised, and the `handicap_source` recording its provenance — `WHS_CLUB` for a club computed under the World Handicap System, `STATE_ASSOC` for a state association figure, or `SELF_ATTESTED` for one the family supplied itself. A `handicap_index` must lie between −5.0 and 54.0 inclusive and is carried to one decimal place. The optional `publication_opt_in` flag records the guardian's consent to publish results to college coaches and defaults to false when omitted.

Newly registered juniors land in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` and can do nothing competitive until a staffer looks at them. Registration sets `conduct_points` to zero, `late_withdrawal_count` to zero, `provisional_ranking_points` to 0.00 and `provisional_counted_events` to zero; the certified ranking fields start empty, with `certified_ranking_points` null, `certified_counted_events` zero, `certified_rank_position` null and `last_ranking_epoch` null.

Registration also derives the junior's age and division immediately. `age_at_cutoff` is the number of full elapsed years from the junior's `birth_date` to the season's `age_cutoff_date`, where the birthday counts as reached when the month-and-day of the `birth_date` falls on or before the month-and-day of the cutoff. From that integer the `age_division` follows by threshold: an age of 10 or below yields no division at all and `age_division` is null; an age of 11, 12 or 13 yields `U13`; 14 or 15 yields `U15`; 16 or 17 yields `U17`; 18 or 19 yields `U19`; and an age of 20 or above again yields a null `age_division`. Only the junior's own row and their season's `age_cutoff_date` participate in this derivation — no other junior and no tournament influences it. The practical effect is that the tour is open to golfers whose cutoff age is at least 11 and at most 19; anybody outside that band carries a null division and, as the next paragraph explains, can never be verified.

The eligibility decision is a `TourAdmin` prerogative. It carries a `decision` that must be either `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED`, and an optional `note`. Deciding `VERIFIED` re-derives `age_at_cutoff` and `age_division` from current data and then advances `eligibility_status` from `PENDING_VERIFICATION` to `VERIFIED`, refreshing `updated_at` and incrementing `version`. Verification has two preconditions. The junior's `age_division` must not be null — a golfer outside the 11-to-19 band cannot be verified. And the junior's handicap must be fresh: the `handicap_effective_date` must be no more than 180 days before the logical clock, or the decision is refused with `ERR_HANDICAP_STALE`. Deciding `REJECTED` advances `eligibility_status` from `PENDING_VERIFICATION` to `REJECTED` and requires a `note` recording the reason; a decision of `REJECTED` without a `note` is an invalid request. `REJECTED` is terminal — no operation in this API transitions a junior out of it, ever.

Beyond `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `VERIFIED` and the terminal `REJECTED`, a fourth eligibility state exists: `SUSPENDED`. A junior moves `VERIFIED → SUSPENDED` only as a consequence of a conduct decision, and back `SUSPENDED → VERIFIED` only as a consequence of a successful conduct appeal; both hops are described in section 3.9. No endpoint drives either one directly.

Handicaps change during a season, and a **handicap revision** records the change. A revision is posted by a `TourAdmin` or a `RulesOfficial` and carries a fresh `handicap_index`, a fresh `handicap_effective_date`, a fresh `handicap_source`, and the `expected_member_version` described in section 2.7. Because a revision supersedes the stored index outright rather than appending to a history, it overwrites all three fields, refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` by one. A revision against a `SUSPENDED` junior is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_SUSPENDED`. Critically, a revision does **not** retroactively alter any existing entry: entry-time eligibility was settled when the entry was submitted, and a junior whose index rises above a tournament's ceiling after acceptance keeps their slot.

### 3.2. Scheduling Events, Entry Windows and Exemption Categories

Scheduling a tournament is the act of declaring a finite, contested resource. A `TourAdmin` supplies the owning `season_id`, the event `name` (1 to 120 characters), the `division` it is played in — one of `U13`, `U15`, `U17` or `U19` — and its `tier`, which is the event-strength band and is one of `LOCAL`, `REGIONAL`, `NATIONAL` or `INVITATIONAL`. The dates of play are `start_date` and `end_date`, both `YYYY-MM-DD`, with `end_date` on or after `start_date`. Three instants govern the competitive calendar around it: `entry_open_at`, the moment entries begin to be accepted; `entry_close_at`, which must fall strictly after `entry_open_at` and at which they stop; and `withdrawal_deadline_at`, after which walking away stops being a withdrawal and becomes a forfeiture. All three are ISO 8601 UTC instants.

The scarcity parameters come next. `field_size_limit` is the maximum number of entries that may hold a playing slot and must be at least 1. `max_handicap_index` is an inclusive ceiling: a junior whose index sits exactly on it may still enter. `entry_fee_cents` is the gross fee and must be zero or greater. `round_count` is the number of stroke-play rounds and must be between 1 and 4 inclusive. Two course parameters feed the scoring differential: `course_rating`, carried to one decimal place, and `slope_rating`, an integer between 55 and 155 inclusive. Two tee-sheet parameters shape the starting grid: `tee_group_size`, the aggregate capacity of a single tee slot, which must be between 1 and 4 and defaults to 3 when omitted; and `tee_interval_minutes`, the width in minutes of a tee slot, which must be between 1 and 30 and defaults to 10 when omitted. Finally, `qualifies_into_tournament_id` optionally names another tournament that this one feeds; it defaults to null when the event feeds nothing, and when it is supplied it must resolve to an existing tournament.

A freshly scheduled event begins at `SCHEDULED` with an empty ledger of participation: `entry_count`, `applied_count`, `accepted_count`, `waitlist_count`, `closed_count` and `finished_count` are all zero, `open_slot_count` equals `field_size_limit`, and `field_quality_factor` is null because no field exists to have quality yet. `winner_entry_id`, `completed_at` and `cancellation_reason` are all null.

The event then walks a fixed lifecycle whose states are `SCHEDULED`, `ENTRY_OPEN`, `ENTRY_CLOSED`, `FIELD_SET`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED`. Opening the entry window advances a tournament `SCHEDULED → ENTRY_OPEN` and is permitted only while the logical clock is still strictly before `entry_close_at` — there is no point opening a window that has already elapsed. Closing it advances the tournament `ENTRY_OPEN → ENTRY_CLOSED` and is permitted only once the logical clock has reached `entry_close_at`; an attempt to close early is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_WINDOW_OPEN`. Both operations are `TourAdmin` work, and both increment the tournament's `version`. Attempting either from a status that does not admit it — opening a window on anything but a `SCHEDULED` event, or closing one on anything but an `ENTRY_OPEN` event — yields `ERR_INVALID_TOURNAMENT_STATE`. The remaining hops, `ENTRY_CLOSED → FIELD_SET`, `FIELD_SET → IN_PROGRESS` and the automatic `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, are the subjects of sections 3.5, 3.6 and 3.8 respectively.

**Exemptions** are how the tour honours a golfer's standing without making them queue. An exemption grant is minted by a `TourAdmin` against a specific pairing of junior and tournament: the payload names the `member_id` of the exempt golfer, the `tournament_id` of the event the exemption grants priority into, the `category`, and an `expires_at` instant after which the grant no longer confers priority. The `category` is one of four: `DEFENDING_CHAMPION` for last year's winner, `QUALIFIER_WINNER` for a golfer who won a feeder event, `NATIONAL_TEAM` for a national squad selection, or `SPONSOR_INVITE` for a discretionary invitation. An optional `source_tournament_id` cites the event that produced the exemption and must resolve to a real tournament when supplied; it is null for a `SPONSOR_INVITE`, which by definition has no feeder event behind it. A grant may only be minted while the target tournament is `SCHEDULED` or `ENTRY_OPEN`; later than that the field is already being decided and `ERR_INVALID_TOURNAMENT_STATE` applies. Minting also requires that the junior is not `SUSPENDED` (`ERR_MEMBER_SUSPENDED`), that their `eligibility_status` is `VERIFIED` (`ERR_MEMBER_NOT_VERIFIED`), and that their `age_division` equals the tournament's `division` (`ERR_DIVISION_MISMATCH`). A new grant is written with `status` set to `ACTIVE` and `consumed_by_entry_id` null.

A grant's own lifecycle has four states: `ACTIVE`, `CONSUMED`, `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED`, of which the last two are terminal. The `ACTIVE → CONSUMED` hop fires automatically at the moment an entry citing the grant actually takes a playing slot — never at submission — and it requires that the logical clock is at or before the grant's `expires_at`; consumption stamps `consumed_by_entry_id` with the accepting entry's identifier. The reverse hop `CONSUMED → ACTIVE` is compensation: when the consuming entry leaves the field through withdrawal or through a tournament cancellation, the grant returns to `ACTIVE` and `consumed_by_entry_id` is cleared. That hop deliberately does not fire on forfeiture — a forfeited junior loses the exemption along with the slot. A grant that is still `ACTIVE` when an entry tries to cite it after `expires_at` has passed is lazily marked `EXPIRED` at that moment, and the entry submission itself is refused with `ERR_EXEMPTION_NOT_APPLICABLE`. And a grant that is `ACTIVE` when its junior is suspended moves to `REVOKED`.

One grant is never minted by hand. When a tournament that names a `qualifies_into_tournament_id` completes and produced a winner, the system mints a fresh grant on the winner's behalf into the named target event: the new grant's `member_id` is the winning junior, its `tournament_id` is the target, its `source_tournament_id` is the completing tournament, its `category` is `QUALIFIER_WINNER`, its `expires_at` is the target tournament's `entry_close_at`, and its `status` is `ACTIVE`. That is the qualifier chain, and section 3.8 explains where in the completion sequence it fires.

### 3.3. Financial Assistance for Participation

Entry fees keep families out of the game, so the association runs a means-tested assistance programme funded from the season purse. A junior — or a `TourAdmin` acting for them — applies by supplying the `member_id`, the `season_id` whose budget is being drawn on, the annual `household_income_cents` (zero or greater), the number of `dependents` in the household (at least 1), and the `expected_member_version` required by section 2.7. A `SUSPENDED` junior may not apply, and such a request is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_SUSPENDED`.

The application immediately derives its means-test key. `income_per_dependent_cents` is `household_income_cents` divided by `dependents` and truncated downward to a whole number of cents. From that figure the `covered_pct` — the share of a fee the association will cover — follows as a five-band lookup: an income per dependent at or below 1,500,000 cents (fifteen thousand dollars) covers the whole fee, giving a `covered_pct` of 1.00; at or below 2,500,000 cents it covers 0.90; at or below 4,500,000 cents it covers 0.65; at or below 7,000,000 cents it covers 0.40; and anything above 7,000,000 cents covers nothing at all, giving a `covered_pct` of 0.00, which blocks approval outright.

A new application is written with `status` `SUBMITTED`, `consumed_cents` zero, `estimated_award_cents` null, and `per_member_cap_cents` snapshotted at 60,000 cents — a six-hundred-dollar ceiling on the total assistance any one junior may draw across a whole season. That snapshot is taken at creation and is thereafter the application's own private cap, unaffected by any later policy change.

Because a household's circumstances change mid-season, a junior may re-apply, and a new application supersedes the old. When a fresh application is created for a `member_id` and `season_id` pairing that already has one in `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` status, the old row moves to `SUPERSEDED` and the new one starts at `SUBMITTED` with `consumed_cents` zero. That supersession is only permitted while the old application's `consumed_cents` is zero: once assistance money has actually been settled onto an entry through that application, the row is load-bearing accounting and cannot be quietly retired, so the new submission is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENTRY` instead. Creating an application always increments the junior's `version` by one, which is exactly what makes a second submission carrying the same `expected_member_version` fail as stale. The application's own lifecycle is `SUBMITTED` at the start, branching to `APPROVED`, `DENIED` or `SUPERSEDED`, of which the latter two are terminal.

A `TourAdmin` decides an application by supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVED` or `DENIED`, and only an application still in `SUBMITTED` status may be decided — anything else yields `ERR_INVALID_GRANT_STATE`. Approval is permitted only when the computed `covered_pct` is strictly greater than 0.00; an application from a household above the top income band is refused with `ERR_INCOME_INELIGIBLE`. On approval the row moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, `decided_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `decided_by_user_id` is stamped from the deciding staffer's `X-User-ID`, and `estimated_award_cents` is published as the **estimate variant** of the award: the association's standard entry fee of 25,000 cents multiplied by `covered_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent. On denial the row moves `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, `covered_pct` is written as 0.00, `estimated_award_cents` as zero, and `decided_at` is stamped.

The estimate is a published notice, not a reservation. **No money moves on the season at approval time**: `assistance_available_cents`, `assistance_committed_cents` and `assistance_disbursed_cents` are all untouched. Money moves only when a concrete entry is submitted, and the amount that moves is computed by a *different* variant of the same award rule, described in section 3.4. The two variants deliberately disagree whenever a tournament's `entry_fee_cents` differs from the 25,000-cent standard figure: the estimate multiplies `covered_pct` by that association-wide standard fee and ignores both the per-junior cap and the season ledger, never moving money; the settlement multiplies `covered_pct` by the specific entry's own gross fee, then clamps the result against the junior's remaining cap and refuses outright if the season pool cannot fund it whole. A household covered at 0.90 entering an event whose fee is 32,000 cents therefore sees an `estimated_award_cents` of 22,500 on its application and an `aid_award_cents` of 28,800 on its entry. Only `APPROVED` applications are ever read at settlement time; `DENIED` and `SUPERSEDED` rows are invisible to it.

### 3.4. Submitting an Entry: Gates, Fee Settlement and the Priority Snapshot

Submitting an entry is the busiest gauntlet in the system. A `Member` submitting for themselves, or a `TourAdmin` submitting on their behalf, supplies the `member_id`, the `tournament_id`, and optionally an `exemption_grant_id` to invoke a standing exemption, a `request_assistance` flag (defaulting to false) asking the association to settle an award, and an `aid_application_id` naming the approved application to draw on.

Before anything is written, the entry must clear a long line of gates, in the order section 2.6 fixes. The tournament must be `ENTRY_OPEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TOURNAMENT_STATE`. The junior must not be `SUSPENDED` (`ERR_MEMBER_SUSPENDED`) and must be `VERIFIED` (`ERR_MEMBER_NOT_VERIFIED`). The junior's `age_division` must equal the tournament's `division`, or `ERR_DIVISION_MISMATCH`. The junior's `handicap_index` must be less than or equal to the tournament's `max_handicap_index` — equality passes — or `ERR_HANDICAP_ABOVE_LIMIT`. The handicap must be both fresh and adequately sourced: its `handicap_effective_date` must be no more than 180 days before the logical clock, **and** its `handicap_source` must not be `SELF_ATTESTED` when the tournament's `tier` is `NATIONAL` or `INVITATIONAL`; either failure yields `ERR_HANDICAP_STALE`. A junior may bring a self-attested figure to a `LOCAL` or `REGIONAL` event, but the two strongest tiers demand a `WHS_CLUB` or `STATE_ASSOC` index.

An invoked exemption is then checked on four counts at once: the referenced grant must be `ACTIVE`, must belong to the entering junior, must name this very tournament, and must have an `expires_at` strictly after the logical clock. Any of the four failing yields `ERR_EXEMPTION_NOT_APPLICABLE` — and, as section 3.2 notes, an expiry failure also flips the grant itself to `EXPIRED` on the way out. When `request_assistance` is true, the junior must hold exactly one `APPROVED` assistance application for the tournament's season and the supplied `aid_application_id` must be that row; otherwise `ERR_AID_NOT_APPROVED`. Duplication is then barred: the junior must have no existing entry for this tournament in any status other than `WITHDRAWN`, `REJECTED` or `CANCELLED`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENTRY`. A junior who withdrew may therefore re-enter; a junior who forfeited may not.

The entry window is checked next: the logical clock must be at or after `entry_open_at` and strictly before `entry_close_at`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Then the calendar is checked for double-booking. A junior may not hold an `ACCEPTED`, `WAITLISTED`, `COMPETING` or `FINISHED` entry in another tournament whose travel window intersects this tournament's dates. The other tournament's travel window runs from one day before its `start_date` to one day after its `end_date` inclusive, and this tournament's dates are its closed interval from `start_date` to `end_date` inclusive; if those two ranges share even a single day, the submission is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. The one-day padding on either side of the existing commitment is deliberate: juniors travel to these events, and a Sunday finish two states away does not permit a Monday first tee.

Once every gate clears, the entry is written. Its `entry_status` starts at `APPLIED` and its `submitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock — that instant is the primary tie-break inside a priority band, so it must be recorded exactly. `rank_at_entry` snapshots the junior's current `certified_rank_position`, and stays null when the junior is unranked. `waitlist_position` is null, `rejection_reason` is null, `withdrawn_at` is null, `finish_position` is null, `event_points` is null, `avg_scoring_differential` is null, `certified_round_count` is zero, `total_adjusted_strokes` is zero, `amount_paid_cents` is zero, and `refunded_cents` is zero. The tournament's `entry_count` and `applied_count` each increase by one.

`priority_band` is frozen here, at submission, and never recomputed afterwards. It is an integer taking one of three values — 0 for an exempt entrant, 1 for a ranked entrant, 2 for an open entrant — and it is resolved by evaluating three clauses top to bottom, where the first clause that holds wins. First: if the junior's `late_withdrawal_count` is 2 or more, the band is 2, regardless of anything else. Second: otherwise, if the entry cites an `exemption_grant_id` whose grant is `ACTIVE`, the band is 0. Third: otherwise, if the junior's `certified_rank_position` is not null, the band is 1. Failing all three, the band is 2. The ordering of those clauses is the whole point: **the demotion for repeat late withdrawals outranks the exemption**, so a junior who has walked away from two events after the withdrawal deadline is pushed to the back of the open queue even holding a `SPONSOR_INVITE`.

The fee is then settled, and the settlement runs in a **pinned four-step order** that must be executed exactly as written, because the three quantities involved look mutually dependent and a different order produces different money. Step one: `gross_fee_cents` is snapshotted from the tournament's `entry_fee_cents`. Step two: the late-entry surcharge. The entry counts as late when the logical clock is strictly after the instant 72 hours before `entry_close_at`; when it is late, `late_entry_surcharge_cents` is ten percent of `gross_fee_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent, and when it is not late the surcharge is zero. The surcharge is a share of the **gross fee only** — it never compounds onto the award and never onto the net. Step three: the assistance award. When `request_assistance` is false, `aid_award_cents` is zero. When it is true, the award is computed as the settlement variant: `covered_pct` from the approved application multiplied by this entry's own `gross_fee_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent, then clamped downward to the application's remaining personal allowance, which is `per_member_cap_cents` less the application's current `consumed_cents`. Note carefully what step three multiplies: the **gross fee**, never the gross fee plus surcharge. The association does not subsidise a family's lateness. Step four: `net_fee_due_cents` is `gross_fee_cents` plus `late_entry_surcharge_cents` minus `aid_award_cents`, floored at zero so it is never negative.

Two refusals guard the award. If the clamped award is not strictly positive — because the application's `consumed_cents` has already reached or exceeded its `per_member_cap_cents` — the submission is refused with `ERR_AID_ALLOCATION_EXHAUSTED`. If the clamped award exceeds the season's current `assistance_available_cents`, the submission is refused with `ERR_AID_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`: the association never part-funds an entry, so an award that cannot be granted whole is not granted at all.

To make the pinned order concrete: a 32,000-cent fee, submitted inside the late window, by a household covered at 0.90 yields a surcharge of 3,200 cents, an award of 28,800 cents and a net of 6,400 cents. Folding the surcharge into the subsidised base first would instead give an award of 31,680 cents and a net of 3,520 cents. The first result is the correct one.

An entry carries **two independent status dimensions on the same row**, and keeping them independent is a deliberate design choice. `entry_status` tracks the competitive lifecycle and takes one of ten values: `APPLIED`, `ACCEPTED`, `WAITLISTED`, `REJECTED`, `COMPETING`, `FINISHED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `FORFEITED`, `DISQUALIFIED` or `CANCELLED`, of which `REJECTED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `FORFEITED`, `DISQUALIFIED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. `fee_status` tracks the money lifecycle and takes one of six values: `UNPAID`, `AID_PARTIAL`, `AID_COVERED`, `PAID`, `REFUNDED` or `FORFEITED_FEE`, of which `REFUNDED` and `FORFEITED_FEE` are terminal. The two advance through disjoint sets of triggers, so an entry may perfectly legitimately be `WAITLISTED` and `PAID`, or `ACCEPTED` and `UNPAID`. There is exactly **one** coupling from the money dimension back into the competitive dimension, and it appears in section 3.6: a score may not be submitted unless the entry's `fee_status` is `PAID` or `AID_COVERED`.

At submission the money dimension is set in one step from the settlement result. When no award was granted, `fee_status` starts at `UNPAID`. When an award was granted and it covered the whole charge so that `net_fee_due_cents` came out at zero, `fee_status` moves `UNPAID → AID_COVERED`. When an award was granted but a remainder is still owed, `fee_status` moves `UNPAID → AID_PARTIAL` and the family still owes `net_fee_due_cents`. In both award cases the ledger moves in the same way: the award is subtracted from the season's `assistance_available_cents`, added to its `assistance_committed_cents`, and added to the funding application's `consumed_cents`. Entry submission is the **sole** point in this entire API at which an assistance award is reserved.

Paying is a single exact settlement, not a running tab. A `Member` or a `TourAdmin` supplies an `amount_cents` that must be strictly positive and must equal exactly the entry's outstanding remainder, which is `net_fee_due_cents` less `amount_paid_cents`; any other figure, including a partial instalment or an overpayment, is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_INVALID`. Payment further requires an entry in a non-terminal `entry_status` whose `net_fee_due_cents` is greater than zero, or `ERR_INVALID_ENTRY_STATE` applies. A successful payment adds `amount_cents` to `amount_paid_cents` and advances `fee_status` — `UNPAID → PAID` for an unassisted entry, `AID_PARTIAL → PAID` for a part-assisted one. `net_fee_due_cents` is deliberately **left** at the settled charge rather than being decremented to zero, so that the full fee breakdown of gross, surcharge, award and net stays auditable long after the money arrives. When a part-assisted entry is paid off, the award already committed against the season stays committed: only the family's remainder has moved.

### 3.5. Setting the Field, Working the Waitlist, and Leaving It

Once the entry window has elapsed and the tournament sits at `ENTRY_CLOSED`, a `TourAdmin` runs the **selector**: a single, once-per-tournament operation that turns a pile of applications into a field and a waitlist, and advances the tournament `ENTRY_CLOSED → FIELD_SET`. Everything about it is deterministic.

The selector first draws its candidate pool. A candidate is an entry belonging to this tournament whose `entry_status` is `APPLIED` and whose junior's `eligibility_status` is `VERIFIED`. Every `APPLIED` entry that fails that predicate is moved `APPLIED → REJECTED` on the spot, with `rejection_reason` set to `MEMBER_SUSPENDED` when the junior is `SUSPENDED` and to `MEMBER_NOT_VERIFIED` otherwise, and each such entry adds one to `closed_count`. Entries belonging to other tournaments never participate.

The surviving candidates are then sorted, ascending, on a four-part key applied lexicographically. The first component is the entry's frozen `priority_band`, so band 0 sorts ahead of band 1, which sorts ahead of band 2. The second component is `rank_at_entry`, with a lower certified rank sorting first; an entry whose `rank_at_entry` is null sorts as though its rank were 999999, which places every unranked entrant behind every ranked one inside the same band. The third component is `submitted_at` ascending, so the earlier application wins a tie. The fourth and final component is `created_at` ascending. No entity's `id` is ever consulted as a tie-break, here or anywhere else in this specification.

The first `field_size_limit` entries in that order move `APPLIED → ACCEPTED`, each with `waitlist_position` set to null. Every entry after the cut moves `APPLIED → WAITLISTED` and receives a `waitlist_position` numbered 1, 2, 3 and upward in the very same order, so that position 1 is the next golfer in. Each accepted entry that carries an `exemption_grant_id` consumes that grant: the grant moves `ACTIVE → CONSUMED` and its `consumed_by_entry_id` is stamped with the accepting entry's identifier. **No money moves during the selector run**, because every award was already reserved at submission. The tournament then recomputes `applied_count`, `accepted_count`, `waitlist_count`, `closed_count` and `open_slot_count`, recomputes its `field_quality_factor`, sets `status` to `FIELD_SET` and increments `version`.

An empty candidate pool is a legitimate outcome, not an error. A tournament whose every applicant turned out to be unverified still lands at `FIELD_SET`, with `accepted_count` zero, `waitlist_count` zero and `field_quality_factor` 1.00, and no error is raised.

The **field quality factor** measures how strong the field actually is, and it feeds directly into the ranking points the event will pay out. It is recomputed at the selector run and again on every waitlist promotion and on every transition that takes an entry out of `ACCEPTED` into `WITHDRAWN`, `FORFEITED` or `CANCELLED`. Its input set is exactly the entries holding a playing slot — those whose `entry_status` is `ACCEPTED`, `COMPETING`, `FINISHED` or `DISQUALIFIED`. Waitlisted, applied, rejected, withdrawn, forfeited and cancelled entries are all excluded. Two counts are taken over that set: the total number of entries in it, and the number of them whose `rank_at_entry` is not null and is at or below 50 — a `rank_at_entry` of exactly 50 counts, 51 does not. The factor then starts at a baseline of 1.00, adds 0.02 for each top-fifty entrant, and adds a further 0.01 for each entrant in the field beyond the first twelve (so a field of twelve or fewer earns no depth credit at all, and a field of twenty earns eight increments). The result is capped at 1.60 and then rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

Beginning play is a `TourAdmin` or `ChiefReferee` action that advances the tournament `FIELD_SET → IN_PROGRESS`. It has three preconditions. The logical clock must have reached midnight UTC on the tournament's `start_date`. The tournament's `accepted_count` must be at least 1 — a tournament whose selector produced an empty field cannot be started, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_FIELD`. And every `ACCEPTED` entry must have `amount_paid_cents` at or above its `net_fee_due_cents`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_FEE_OUTSTANDING`. Starting play leaves every `ACCEPTED` entry `ACCEPTED` and every `WAITLISTED` entry `WAITLISTED`; each accepted entry advances to `COMPETING` on its own first score submission, not here. Starting also has one durable consequence: **waitlist promotion is switched off from this moment on**. A slot freed while the tournament is `IN_PROGRESS` simply stays open.

**Withdrawing** is where the two branches diverge, and the deadline decides which one fires. A `Member` withdrawing their own entry, or a `TourAdmin` withdrawing on their behalf, may supply an optional `reason`. Withdrawal is admissible only from `APPLIED`, `ACCEPTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `COMPETING`; anything else yields `ERR_INVALID_ENTRY_STATE`.

Withdrawing an `APPLIED` entry, which is only possible while the tournament is `ENTRY_OPEN` or `ENTRY_CLOSED`, moves the entry `APPLIED → WITHDRAWN`, stamps `withdrawn_at` from the logical clock, decrements `applied_count` and increments `closed_count`. Withdrawing before the field is set is never a forfeiture, whatever the clock says relative to `withdrawal_deadline_at`. Withdrawing a `WAITLISTED` entry, possible while the tournament is `ENTRY_CLOSED` or `FIELD_SET`, moves it `WAITLISTED → WITHDRAWN`, stamps `withdrawn_at`, clears its `waitlist_position`, renumbers the surviving waitlist positions to run 1 upward again while preserving their relative order, decrements `waitlist_count` and increments `closed_count`. No promotion fires, because a waitlisted departure frees no playing slot. Both branches perform the same fee-and-aid compensation as the accepted branch described next.

Withdrawing an `ACCEPTED` entry while the tournament is `FIELD_SET` and the logical clock is at or before `withdrawal_deadline_at` moves the entry `ACCEPTED → WITHDRAWN` and unwinds every reservation it held, in four hops. Hop one: `entry_status` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, `withdrawn_at` is stamped, and if `fee_status` was `PAID`, `AID_COVERED` or `AID_PARTIAL` it moves to `REFUNDED` with `refunded_cents` set to the former `amount_paid_cents`, `amount_paid_cents` reset to zero and `net_fee_due_cents` reset to zero. Hop two: if `aid_award_cents` was greater than zero, that amount is added back to the season's `assistance_available_cents`, subtracted from its `assistance_committed_cents`, subtracted from the funding application's `consumed_cents`, and the entry's own `aid_award_cents` is cleared to zero; if the entry cited an `exemption_grant_id` whose grant is `CONSUMED`, that grant returns to `ACTIVE` with `consumed_by_entry_id` cleared; and the tournament's `accepted_count` falls by one while `open_slot_count` and `closed_count` each rise by one. Hop three: because `open_slot_count` has become at least 1 while the tournament is still `FIELD_SET`, the waitlisted entry holding the lowest `waitlist_position` is promoted. Hop four, performed by that promotion, renumbers the surviving waitlist positions and restores `accepted_count` and `open_slot_count` to their exact pre-withdrawal values. The tournament's `field_quality_factor` is recomputed at the end.

Withdrawing an `ACCEPTED` entry while the tournament is `FIELD_SET` but the logical clock has passed `withdrawal_deadline_at` is a **forfeiture** instead, and the compensation is markedly less generous. The entry moves `ACCEPTED → FORFEITED` and `withdrawn_at` is stamped. A `fee_status` of `PAID`, `AID_COVERED` or `AID_PARTIAL` moves to `FORFEITED_FEE`; `refunded_cents` stays at zero and `net_fee_due_cents` is set to zero, so the association keeps the money. An entry that was still `UNPAID` also moves `UNPAID → FORFEITED_FEE`, writing off the unpaid balance. Any `aid_award_cents` greater than zero is moved out of the season's `assistance_committed_cents` and into its `assistance_disbursed_cents` — irreversibly, and without releasing the funding application's `consumed_cents`. A consumed exemption grant stays `CONSUMED`: forfeiture never restores an exemption. The junior's `late_withdrawal_count` rises by one, but **only** on this junior-initiated branch; neither the suspension cascade nor the one-event venue ban of section 3.9 charges a late withdrawal even though both also produce forfeitures. The tournament's `accepted_count` falls by one while `open_slot_count` and `closed_count` each rise by one, and then waitlist promotion fires exactly as in the withdrawal branch.

**Promotion** is the mirror of departure and has four preconditions of its own: the tournament must be `FIELD_SET`, `open_slot_count` must be at least 1 after the vacating entry has left, the candidate must hold the lowest `waitlist_position` among the tournament's `WAITLISTED` entries, and the candidate's junior must be `VERIFIED`. Because waitlist positions are unique, no tie is ever possible. The promoted entry moves `WAITLISTED → ACCEPTED`, its `waitlist_position` is cleared, the remaining waitlist positions renumber from 1 upward preserving their relative order, `accepted_count` rises by one, `open_slot_count` falls by one, `waitlist_count` falls by one, any `exemption_grant_id` the promoted entry carries is consumed, and `field_quality_factor` is recomputed. The money dimension is **not touched and no money moves**, for the reason section 2.9 gives: the award was reserved at submission while the entry was `APPLIED`, and the committed bucket already carries it across all four live statuses.

**Cancelling a tournament** is available to a `TourAdmin` from `SCHEDULED`, `ENTRY_OPEN`, `ENTRY_CLOSED` or `FIELD_SET`, and requires a `cancellation_reason` recorded on the tournament. An `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED` or already-`CANCELLED` tournament cannot be cancelled and the attempt yields `ERR_INVALID_TOURNAMENT_STATE`. Cancelling a `SCHEDULED` event has nothing to unwind: the status becomes `CANCELLED`, the `cancellation_reason` is recorded, and `version` increments. Cancelling from any of the other three statuses fires an identical compensation cascade over every entry whose `entry_status` is `APPLIED`, `WAITLISTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `COMPETING`, applied in this order. First, `entry_status` becomes `CANCELLED`. Second, a `fee_status` of `PAID`, `AID_COVERED` or `AID_PARTIAL` becomes `REFUNDED`, with `refunded_cents` set to the former `amount_paid_cents` and `amount_paid_cents` reset to zero. Third, any `aid_award_cents` greater than zero is added back to `assistance_available_cents`, subtracted from `assistance_committed_cents`, subtracted from the funding application's `consumed_cents`, and cleared to zero on the entry. Fourth, any cited `exemption_grant_id` whose grant is `CONSUMED` returns that grant to `ACTIVE` with `consumed_by_entry_id` cleared. Every `SCHEDULED` round of the tournament is then set to `NO_CARD`. Finally the census is recomputed with `accepted_count` and `waitlist_count` at zero and `open_slot_count` back at `field_size_limit`, the `status` becomes `CANCELLED`, the `cancellation_reason` is recorded, and `version` increments. Because a `COMPETING` entry can only exist while a tournament is `IN_PROGRESS`, and an `IN_PROGRESS` tournament cannot be cancelled, the `COMPETING → CANCELLED` pairing is unreachable in practice; it is specified for completeness rather than because it can occur.

### 3.6. The Tee Sheet, Pace of Play, Scoring and Peer Verification

A **round** is one entry's card for one day of stroke play, and it exists as a row before a single shot is struck because the tee sheet is built in advance. A `TourAdmin` or a `ChiefReferee` creates one by naming the `entry_id` whose card it is, the `round_number` as a 1-based ordinal that must not exceed the tournament's `round_count`, the `tee_time` as an ISO 8601 UTC start instant, and the `marker_member_id` of the peer who will attest the card. The tournament's identity is not client-supplied: `tournament_id` is denormalised onto the round from its parent entry at creation and is never accepted from a request body.

Round creation is permitted only while the tournament is `FIELD_SET` or `IN_PROGRESS`, and only against an entry whose `entry_status` is `ACCEPTED` or `COMPETING`. The peer marker is then validated on two counts: the `marker_member_id` must name a junior other than the round's own entrant, and that marker must themselves hold an `ACCEPTED` or `COMPETING` entry in the same tournament. A marker who is the entrant, or who is not in the field, yields `ERR_INVALID_MARKER`.

The tee sheet enforces an aggregate group capacity over a half-open time slot, and the half-openness is exact. For the requested `round_number`, the number of existing rounds of that tournament whose `tee_time` falls inside the interval beginning at the requested `tee_time` and extending forward by `tee_interval_minutes` — including the starting instant and excluding the ending instant — must be fewer than the tournament's `tee_group_size`. A round whose `tee_time` is exactly `tee_interval_minutes` later than an existing group is **adjacent, not overlapping**, and is allowed. A request that would push a slot past capacity is refused with `ERR_TEE_SLOT_FULL`.

A newly created round starts at `SCHEDULED` with every counter at zero: `pace_warning_count`, `max_minutes_behind`, `pace_penalty_strokes`, `rules_penalty_strokes`, `dispute_count` and `open_rules_case_count`. Its `gross_strokes`, `adjusted_gross`, `scoring_differential`, `certified_at` and `certified_by_user_id` are all null. The round's own lifecycle spans `SCHEDULED`, `SUBMITTED`, `DISPUTED`, `PEER_VERIFIED`, `CERTIFIED`, `NO_CARD` and `DISQUALIFIED`, of which `NO_CARD` and `DISQUALIFIED` are terminal.

**Pace of play** is policed by observations, recorded by a `RulesOfficial` — the only role with the exclusive right to record them — or by a `ChiefReferee`. An observation carries an `observation_type` and a `minutes_behind` reading, and it may be recorded while the round is `SCHEDULED` or `SUBMITTED`; from any other status the attempt yields `ERR_INVALID_ROUND_STATE`. An observation whose `observation_type` is `WARNING` increases the round's `pace_warning_count` by one. Every observation, whatever its type, raises the round's `max_minutes_behind` to its own `minutes_behind` reading when that reading is larger than the stored maximum.

Both counters then feed the round's pace penalty, which is re-derived from scratch at the end of every observation. The first two warnings of a round are free; each warning beyond the second costs one stroke, so the warning component is the `pace_warning_count` less two, floored at zero. Separately, a group that ever fell a quarter of an hour or more behind pays a flat two strokes: the timing component is 2 when `max_minutes_behind` is at least 15 and 0 otherwise. The boundary is exact — a reading of 15 breaches, a reading of 14 does not. `pace_penalty_strokes` is the sum of the two components, capped at 3 strokes no matter how badly the group behaved. The second warning of a round therefore still costs nothing and the third costs one stroke. When the round already has a recorded `gross_strokes`, the observation also re-derives `adjusted_gross` and `scoring_differential` in the same request.

**Submitting a score** is the golfer's own act, though a `RulesOfficial` or `ChiefReferee` may also submit. The payload carries a single `gross_strokes` figure, which must lie between 50 and 130 inclusive. When the caller acts as `Member`, their `X-User-ID` must match the `member_id` of the round's entry. Four cross-entity conditions guard the submission: the tournament must be `IN_PROGRESS`, the entry's `entry_status` must be `ACCEPTED` or `COMPETING`, the entry's `fee_status` must be `PAID` or `AID_COVERED` — this is the single point at which the money dimension gates the competitive one — and the round itself must be `SCHEDULED` or `DISPUTED`.

From `SCHEDULED` the submission records `gross_strokes`, advances the round `SCHEDULED → SUBMITTED`, and, when this is the entry's first submission, also advances the entry `ACCEPTED → COMPETING`. From `DISPUTED` it is a resubmission after a marker's challenge: it overwrites `gross_strokes` with the new payload value, advances the round `DISPUTED → SUBMITTED`, and is permitted only while the round's `dispute_count` is still below 3. Either way `adjusted_gross` is re-derived as `gross_strokes` plus `pace_penalty_strokes` plus `rules_penalty_strokes`.

**Peer verification** is the tour's substitute for a paid scorer, and only the nominated marker may perform it: the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the round's `marker_member_id`, and any other caller is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MARKER`. Verification is admissible only while the round is `SUBMITTED`. The payload carries a `decision` that is either `VERIFY` or `DISPUTE`, plus an optional `note`.

A `decision` of `VERIFY` advances the round `SUBMITTED → PEER_VERIFIED` and nothing else changes. A `decision` of `DISPUTE` requires a `note` explaining the challenge — a dispute without one is an invalid request — and its effect depends on how many disputes the card has already survived, because the cycle is bounded at three. When the round's `dispute_count` is 0 or 1 before the increment, the dispute raises `dispute_count` by one and advances the round `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED`, returning the card to the golfer for resubmission and closing the loop `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED → SUBMITTED`. When the round's `dispute_count` is already 2 before the increment, the third dispute ends the loop instead of continuing it: `dispute_count` is set to 3, the round moves `SUBMITTED → NO_CARD`, and both `adjusted_gross` and `scoring_differential` stay null. A `NO_CARD` round can never be certified, so its entry can never reach `FINISHED`, and the consequence is that the entry's `event_points` will be 0.00 when the tournament completes. A voided card contributes nothing to the entry's `certified_round_count` and is excluded from its `avg_scoring_differential`.

### 3.7. Officiating: Certification, Rules Cases, Appeals and Disqualification

**Certification** is the moment a card becomes a result, and it is a `RulesOfficial` or `ChiefReferee` act. A round may be certified only from `PEER_VERIFIED`, only while the tournament is `IN_PROGRESS`, and only when the round's `open_rules_case_count` is zero — that is, when no rules case on the round sits in `REPORTED` or `APPEAL_OPEN` status; a card with unresolved officiating is refused with `ERR_OPEN_RULES_CASE`. Segregation of duties applies here too: the certifying official's `X-User-ID` must differ from the round's `marker_member_id`, and a self-certifying marker is refused with `ERR_SELF_ADJUDICATION`.

Certification freezes two numbers onto the round. `adjusted_gross` becomes `gross_strokes` plus `pace_penalty_strokes` plus `rules_penalty_strokes`. `scoring_differential` is then derived from that adjusted total by subtracting the tournament's `course_rating`, multiplying the remainder by the standard slope of 113, dividing by the tournament's `slope_rating`, and rounding the result half up to one decimal place. The round then moves `PEER_VERIFIED → CERTIFIED`, `certified_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `certified_by_user_id` records the certifying official's `X-User-ID`.

The entry roll-up follows in the same request. The entry's `certified_round_count` rises by one, its `total_adjusted_strokes` grows by the round's `adjusted_gross`, and its `avg_scoring_differential` is recomputed. That average is the second rounding of a two-stage sequence, and the order is load-bearing: the per-round `scoring_differential` values were each **already rounded** to one decimal place, and the average is the mean of those rounded values, itself rounded half up to one decimal place again. Only the entry's rounds whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` participate; `SUBMITTED`, `DISPUTED`, `PEER_VERIFIED`, `NO_CARD` and `DISQUALIFIED` rounds are all excluded from the average. To see why the order matters, consider a course with a `course_rating` of 71.4 and a `slope_rating` of 128 and two rounds of 66 and 72 adjusted strokes: rounding each round first gives differentials of −4.8 and 0.5, whose mean is −2.15 and rounds to **−2.2**. Averaging the unrounded values would instead give −2.11875, which rounds to −2.1. The correct answer is −2.2. When `certified_round_count` reaches the tournament's `round_count`, the entry advances `COMPETING → FINISHED`, the tournament's `finished_count` rises by one, and the compound completion check of section 3.8 runs immediately afterwards.

**Rules cases** are how an on-course incident is escalated. A `RulesOfficial`, a `ChiefReferee` or a `Member` reports one against a round by supplying the `round_id`, a `rule_reference` citation of between 1 and 40 characters such as `Rule 4.1b(1)`, a `proposed_disposition` that is either `PENALTY_STROKES` or `DISQUALIFICATION`, and, when the proposal is `PENALTY_STROKES`, a `penalty_strokes_proposed` figure between 0 and 8 inclusive; a proposal of `DISQUALIFICATION` must omit that figure or supply zero. A case may be reported against a round whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `DISPUTED`, `PEER_VERIFIED` or `CERTIFIED`; a `CERTIFIED` round additionally requires that the logical clock is within 48 hours of the round's `certified_at`, and a later attempt is refused with `ERR_REOPEN_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. That 48-hour reopen window is what makes retroactive disqualification possible without leaving results permanently provisional. A new case is written with `status` `REPORTED`, `appeal_count` zero, `penalty_strokes_applied` null, `decision` null, `disposition` null and `decided_at` null; `reported_by_user_id` is stamped from the reporting caller's `X-User-ID`, and the round's `open_rules_case_count` rises by one.

**Adjudication** belongs exclusively to the `ChiefReferee`. The payload carries a `decision` that is either `UPHELD` or `DISMISSED`, a `disposition` that is either `PENALTY_STROKES` or `DISQUALIFICATION`, and optionally a `penalty_strokes_applied` figure. A case may be adjudicated only from `REPORTED` or `APPEAL_OPEN` status, and the adjudicating referee's `X-User-ID` must differ from the case's `reported_by_user_id` — a referee who reported the incident may never rule on it, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SELF_ADJUDICATION`. Where the case targets a `CERTIFIED` round, adjudication is itself subject to the same 48-hour reopen window as reporting.

Upholding a `REPORTED` case advances it `REPORTED → UPHELD`, records the `disposition`, stamps `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `decided_at`, and requires a `penalty_strokes_applied` value between 1 and 8 inclusive when the `disposition` is `PENALTY_STROKES`. From there the effect branches. In the **penalty-strokes branch**, the payload's stroke figure is added to the round's `rules_penalty_strokes` and recorded on the case as `penalty_strokes_applied`, the round's `open_rules_case_count` falls by one, and the round's own `status` is left untouched; because the strokes changed, `adjusted_gross`, `scoring_differential`, the entry's `total_adjusted_strokes` and the entry's `avg_scoring_differential` are all re-derived. In the **disqualification branch**, the round's `open_rules_case_count` falls by one and the round moves to `DISQUALIFIED` — from `CERTIFIED`, `PEER_VERIFIED`, `SUBMITTED` or `DISPUTED`, whichever it was in. When the round was `CERTIFIED`, its `adjusted_gross` and `scoring_differential` are deliberately left frozen at their certified values for audit rather than being cleared. That hop then drives the entry to `DISQUALIFIED`, which is where the cascade continues.

Dismissing a `REPORTED` case advances it `REPORTED → DISMISSED`, decrements the round's `open_rules_case_count`, writes `penalty_strokes_applied` as zero, stamps `decided_at` and `adjudicated_by_user_id`, and changes neither the round's strokes nor its status. `DISMISSED` is terminal for the case.

An entry's disqualification is a multi-hop cascade in its own right, and it runs differently depending on where the entry stood. A `COMPETING` entry moves `COMPETING → DISQUALIFIED` with its `event_points` set to 0.00, and the junior's `provisional_ranking_points` and `provisional_counted_events` are recomputed. A `FINISHED` entry — the retroactive case, reachable only inside the 48-hour reopen window — moves `FINISHED → DISQUALIFIED` with `event_points` set to 0.00 and `finish_position` cleared to null; the tournament's `finished_count` falls by one while its `accepted_count` is left unchanged, because a `DISQUALIFIED` entry still occupies its slot; and the junior's `provisional_ranking_points` and `provisional_counted_events` are recomputed from their remaining qualifying results. What is emphatically **not** touched is the junior's certified ranking: `certified_ranking_points`, `certified_counted_events` and `certified_rank_position` all keep their previous values until the next ranking run is executed. That lag between the live figure and the certified figure is intended, and the ranking projection of section 2.12 exists precisely so it can be seen.

**Appealing** a rules case is the golfer's remedy. The junior whose entry owns the round — their `X-User-ID` must match the entry's `member_id` — or a `RulesOfficial` may appeal by supplying `grounds`, and the appeal must be filed within 72 hours of the case's `decided_at` or it is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. An appeal is admissible against a case that has actually been decided, meaning `UPHELD` or `DISMISSED`; anything else yields `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. Filing advances the case `UPHELD → APPEAL_OPEN`, raises its `appeal_count` by one, and raises the round's `open_rules_case_count` by one — which is why an open appeal blocks certification just as an open report does. Any penalty strokes already applied **stay applied** while the appeal is pending.

The referee then decides the appeal, and the two outcomes are asymmetric. Granting the appeal is a `DISMISSED` decision, which advances the case `APPEAL_OPEN → DISMISSED` and compensates: the case's `penalty_strokes_applied` figure is subtracted back out of the round's `rules_penalty_strokes` and the case's own `penalty_strokes_applied` is reset to zero, the round's `open_rules_case_count` falls by one, and `decided_at` is re-stamped. But if that case had disqualified the round, **the round is not restored**: a `DISQUALIFIED` status is final, and this asymmetry is deliberate. Denying the appeal is an `UPHELD` decision, which advances the case `APPEAL_OPEN → UPHELD`, closing the cycle `UPHELD → APPEAL_OPEN → UPHELD`; it requires a `disposition` in the payload equal to the disposition already recorded on the case, leaves the round's `rules_penalty_strokes` alone because the penalty was never lifted, decrements the round's `open_rules_case_count`, and re-stamps `decided_at`.

### 3.8. Completion, Finish Order, Event Points and the Ranking Run

No operator declares a tournament finished. Completion is a **compound, bubble-up transition**: at the end of every round certification and at the end of every rules-case disqualification, the system asks whether every entry that took the field has reached a terminal competitive outcome. Formally, the tournament advances `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` when its `accepted_count` is at least 1 and no entry of the tournament remains in `entry_status` `ACCEPTED` or `COMPETING` — in other words, when every entry whose `entry_status` is `ACCEPTED`, `COMPETING`, `FINISHED` or `DISQUALIFIED` has settled into `FINISHED` or `DISQUALIFIED`. A tournament that never had an accepted entry never completes this way.

When it does fire, the completion sequence runs in a fixed order. First, **finish positions** are assigned to every entry whose `entry_status` is `FINISHED`. The finishers are sorted ascending on four components in turn: `total_adjusted_strokes` ascending, so the lowest aggregate score wins; then the sum of `rules_penalty_strokes` across that entry's `CERTIFIED` rounds ascending, so that between two golfers on the same total the one who took fewer penalty strokes places higher; then `submitted_at` ascending; then `created_at` ascending. `finish_position` is the 1-based index in that order. Entries that are `DISQUALIFIED`, that carry a `NO_CARD` round, or that are `WITHDRAWN`, `FORFEITED` or `CANCELLED` receive a null `finish_position` and never occupy an index at all — the placings run 1, 2, 3 across the finishers with no gaps for the fallen.

Second, **event points** are computed for every `FINISHED` entry, while every `DISQUALIFIED` entry receives an `event_points` of 0.00. Four inputs interact, and each intermediate is rounded before it is combined. The base is set by the tournament's `tier`: a `LOCAL` event is worth 150 points at the base, a `REGIONAL` event 300, a `NATIONAL` event 600, and an `INVITATIONAL` event 800. The finish factor decays linearly from the win: it is 1.00 less five hundredths for each place below first — so first place scores a factor of 1.00, second 0.95, third 0.90 and so on — with a floor of 0.20 that no amount of decay can breach, and the resulting factor is rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The differential bonus rewards scoring quality against a par reference of 2.0 strokes: the entry's `avg_scoring_differential` is subtracted from 2.0, the remainder is multiplied by 25 points per stroke, that product is rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and the result is floored at 0.00 so a poor differential subtracts nothing. `event_points` is finally the base multiplied by the tournament's `field_quality_factor` multiplied by the finish factor, plus the differential bonus, with the whole sum rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Worked through: a `REGIONAL` event with a `field_quality_factor` of 1.00, won outright so that `finish_position` is 1, by an entry whose `avg_scoring_differential` is −2.2, yields a finish factor of 1.00, a differential bonus of 105.00, and `event_points` of 405.00.

Third, every affected junior's **live provisional ranking** is recomputed, as described later in this section. Fourth, the money is finalised: every `FINISHED` entry whose `aid_award_cents` is greater than zero has that amount moved out of the season's `assistance_committed_cents` and into its `assistance_disbursed_cents`, and the funding application's `consumed_cents` is not released. Fifth, `winner_entry_id` is stamped with the entry whose `finish_position` is 1, or left null when no entry finished at all. Sixth, when the tournament names a `qualifies_into_tournament_id` and a winner exists, the qualifier exemption described in section 3.2 is minted for that winner. Seventh and last, `status` becomes `COMPLETED`, `completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `version` increments.

**Two rankings coexist in this system, and they are meant to disagree.** Both draw from the same qualifying pool: a junior's entries whose `entry_status` is `FINISHED`, whose `event_points` is not null, whose tournament's `status` is `COMPLETED`, and whose tournament's `start_date` falls on or after the season's `ranking_window_start_date`. Disqualified entries are outside the pool by construction — they carry `event_points` of 0.00 but their `entry_status` is not `FINISHED`.

The **provisional** figures are live. `provisional_counted_events` is the number of entries in the pool, and `provisional_ranking_points` is the sum of their `event_points` divided by that count — or by 1 when the count is zero — rounded half up to 2 decimal places. These two fields are recomputed and persisted on every write to any of the junior's `event_points` values, whether that write comes from a tournament completing or from a disqualification, and they are also what the ranking projection serves. Because the divisor is the actual event count, a single strong result reads at its full value.

The **certified** figures are written only when a `TourAdmin` executes a **ranking run** against a season, and they are computed quite differently. The pool is ordered by `event_points` descending, then by the tournament's `start_date` ascending, then by the entry's `created_at` ascending, and only the best 4 entries are kept. `certified_counted_events` is the number of entries actually kept, which is fewer than 4 when the junior has played fewer than 4 qualifying events. `certified_ranking_points` is the sum of the kept entries' `event_points` divided by the **larger of `certified_counted_events` and 4**, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — so a junior with fewer than 4 counted events is penalised by the empty divisor slots. `certified_rank_position` is then assigned as a 1-based index over the season's juniors ordered by `certified_ranking_points` descending, then `certified_counted_events` descending, then the junior's own `created_at` ascending; only juniors whose `eligibility_status` is `VERIFIED` or `SUSPENDED` and whose `certified_counted_events` is at least 1 are ranked, and every junior with zero counted events receives a null `certified_rank_position`. The divergence is stark by design: a junior with exactly one 405.00-point event reads a `provisional_ranking_points` of 405.00 and a `certified_ranking_points` of 101.25.

The ranking run is an ops-run checkpoint and it accepts a single optional `as_of_date`. Executing it recomputes `certified_ranking_points` and `certified_counted_events` for every junior of the season holding at least one qualifying finished entry, assigns `certified_rank_position` across those juniors and leaves it null for juniors with none, increments the season's `ranking_epoch` by one, stamps every ranked junior's `last_ranking_epoch` with that new epoch value, sets the season's `last_ranking_run_at` to the logical clock, sets `last_ranking_as_of_date` to the supplied `as_of_date` or, when it is omitted, to the calendar date of the logical clock, and sets `ranked_member_count` to the number of juniors who received a non-null position. It does **not** alter `provisional_ranking_points` or `provisional_counted_events` — that separation is the whole point of running two figures. It matters operationally because `certified_rank_position` is exactly what the entry selector of section 3.5 reads when it snapshots `rank_at_entry`: a season whose rankings have not been run since the last event will allocate the next field on stale standings, and the gap between a junior's `last_ranking_epoch` and the season's current `ranking_epoch` is how an administrator detects it.

### 3.9. Conduct Policy, Sanctions, and College-Recruiting Exposure

Junior golf's discipline problems are rarely the juniors'. A **conduct case** is therefore filed against a member's entourage rather than the golfer: a `RulesOfficial`, `ChiefReferee` or `TourAdmin` files one naming the `member_id` of the junior whose entourage is implicated, the `tournament_id` of the event at which the incident occurred, a `subject_type` of either `PARENT` or `CADDIE`, the `subject_name` of the person concerned (1 to 120 characters), an `incident_code` classifying what happened, and a `narrative` giving a free-text account. The `incident_code` is one of six: `SPECTATOR_INTERFERENCE`, `COACHING_VIOLATION`, `ABUSIVE_LANGUAGE`, `CADDIE_ADVICE_BREACH`, `PHYSICAL_ALTERCATION` or `SCORE_TAMPERING`. A new case is written at `UNDER_REVIEW` with `points_assessed` zero, `cumulative_points_at_decision` null, `sanction_tier` null, `appeal_count` zero, `decided_at` null and `decided_by_user_id` null, and `filed_by_user_id` is stamped from the filing caller's `X-User-ID`. The case lifecycle spans `UNDER_REVIEW`, `SANCTIONED`, `CLEARED` and `APPEAL_OPEN`, with `CLEARED` terminal.

Only the `ConductCommittee` decides a conduct case, and no committee member may decide a case they themselves filed: the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from the case's `filed_by_user_id`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SELF_ADJUDICATION`. A decision is admissible only from `UNDER_REVIEW` or `APPEAL_OPEN`; anything else yields `ERR_INVALID_CONDUCT_STATE`. The payload carries a `decision` that is either `SANCTIONED` or `CLEARED`.

A `CLEARED` decision advances the case `UNDER_REVIEW → CLEARED`, writes `points_assessed` as zero, leaves `sanction_tier` null, stamps `decided_at` and `decided_by_user_id`, and leaves the junior's `conduct_points` entirely unchanged.

A `SANCTIONED` decision charges points and then acts on the cumulative total. The points for this case, `points_assessed`, are fixed by the `incident_code`: `SPECTATOR_INTERFERENCE` costs 2 points, `COACHING_VIOLATION` costs 2, `ABUSIVE_LANGUAGE` costs 3, `CADDIE_ADVICE_BREACH` costs 2, `PHYSICAL_ALTERCATION` costs 6, and `SCORE_TAMPERING` costs 6. Prior history is then added, but it decays: the prior total is the sum of `points_assessed` across the junior's *other* conduct cases whose `status` is `SANCTIONED` and whose `decided_at` falls within the 365 days before the logical clock. Cases that are `CLEARED`, still `UNDER_REVIEW`, or sitting in `APPEAL_OPEN` contribute nothing. `cumulative_points_at_decision` is the prior total plus this case's own `points_assessed`, and the junior's `conduct_points` is written to that same figure. `status` becomes `SANCTIONED`, and `decided_at` and `decided_by_user_id` are stamped.

The `sanction_tier` follows from the cumulative total by threshold, and both thresholds are **inclusive on the lower edge**. A cumulative total below 3 yields `WARNING`. A total of at least 3 but below 6 yields `VENUE_BAN_ONE_EVENT`. A total of 6 or more yields `MEMBER_SUSPENSION`. Two `ABUSIVE_LANGUAGE` cases at 3 points each therefore suspend, while an `ABUSIVE_LANGUAGE` case followed by a `COACHING_VIOLATION` — 3 plus 2 — only bans.

The three tiers have sharply different consequences, and each must be implemented at exactly its stated breadth. In the `WARNING` branch the junior's `conduct_points` is updated and **no entry anywhere is touched**. In the `VENUE_BAN_ONE_EVENT` branch the junior's `conduct_points` is updated and the ban bites at one venue only: the junior's entry on the very tournament named by the case's `tournament_id`, and only if that entry's `entry_status` is `ACCEPTED`, moves to `FORFEITED` with its `fee_status` moving to `FORFEITED_FEE`, its aid moved from `assistance_committed_cents` to `assistance_disbursed_cents`, its slot freed, and the resulting waitlist promotion fired. Entries on every other tournament, and entries in any other status on this one, are left completely alone — and `late_withdrawal_count` is **not** charged, because the junior did not choose to walk away.

The `MEMBER_SUSPENSION` branch is the deepest cascade in the system, and it runs five levels. Hop one is the decision itself, as described above. Hop two moves the junior `VERIFIED → SUSPENDED`, writing `eligibility_status` to `SUSPENDED`, setting `conduct_points` to the case's `cumulative_points_at_decision`, refreshing `updated_at` and incrementing `version`. Hop three is the reservation sweep: every entry of that junior whose `entry_status` is `APPLIED` or `WAITLISTED` moves to `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` set to `MEMBER_SUSPENDED`; every entry of that junior whose `entry_status` is `ACCEPTED` and whose tournament is `FIELD_SET` moves to `FORFEITED`; and every exemption grant of that junior whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `REVOKED`. Hop four is the backfill: each slot vacated by one of those forfeitures promotes the waitlisted entry holding the lowest `waitlist_position`, and the surviving waitlist positions renumber, exactly as in an ordinary withdrawal. Hop five is the money: each forfeited entry whose `aid_award_cents` is greater than zero has that amount moved from `assistance_committed_cents` to `assistance_disbursed_cents`. Every affected tournament's census and `field_quality_factor` are recomputed. A `WAITLISTED` entry that is rejected by this cascade also decrements its tournament's `waitlist_count`, increments its `closed_count`, and leaves the remaining waitlist positions renumbered from 1 upward in their original relative order. As section 2.6 records, a `SUSPENDED` junior is thereafter barred from entering, from applying for assistance, from being granted an exemption, from being the subject of a recruiting request, and from having a handicap revision recorded — all with `ERR_MEMBER_SUSPENDED`.

**Appealing** a conduct decision is how a suspension is undone. The junior themselves — their `X-User-ID` must equal the case's `member_id` — or a `TourAdmin` may appeal by supplying `grounds`, and the appeal must be filed within 14 days of the case's `decided_at` or it is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. An appeal is admissible against a decided case, meaning one whose `status` is `SANCTIONED` or `CLEARED`; anything else yields `ERR_INVALID_CONDUCT_STATE`. Filing advances the case `SANCTIONED → APPEAL_OPEN` and raises `appeal_count` by one, and the sanction stays in force for the duration of the appeal.

The committee then decides the appeal. A `SANCTIONED` decision denies it, advancing the case `APPEAL_OPEN → SANCTIONED` and closing the cycle `SANCTIONED → APPEAL_OPEN → SANCTIONED`; `decided_at` is re-stamped while `points_assessed` and `sanction_tier` are left unchanged, and **no new cascade fires** — a denied appeal does not re-forfeit anything. A `CLEARED` decision grants it, advancing the case `APPEAL_OPEN → CLEARED` and compensating: the case's `points_assessed` is reset to zero, its `sanction_tier` is cleared to null, `decided_at` is re-stamped, and the junior's `conduct_points` is recomputed over the remaining `SANCTIONED` cases still inside the 365-day decay window. When that recomputed total falls below 6 and the junior is currently `SUSPENDED`, the junior moves `SUSPENDED → VERIFIED`, with `conduct_points` set to the recomputed total, `updated_at` refreshed and `version` incremented. Reinstatement restores **eligibility only**: entries that the original suspension cascade already rejected or forfeited stay closed, and that asymmetry is deliberate.

**College-recruiting exposure** is the last piece of the tour's purpose, and it is gated entirely on consent. A junior's results are visible to recruiters only while their `publication_opt_in` is true; a request to read a junior's published results, or a coach's request for access to them, for a junior whose flag is false is refused with `ERR_PUBLICATION_NOT_CONSENTED`.

A `Coach` — and only a `Coach` — requests access, supplying the `member_id` of the recruited junior, the `coach_school` they recruit for (1 to 120 characters), a `scope` that is either `RESULTS_ONLY` or `FULL_PROFILE`, and the `expected_member_version` required by section 2.7. The request stamps `coach_user_id` from the requesting coach's own opaque `X-User-ID`, sets `status` to `REQUESTED`, stamps `requested_at` from the logical clock, leaves `expires_at` null, and increments the junior's `version` by one. Any prior grant of the same junior and the same coach whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `GRANTED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and the new grant starts at `REQUESTED` — which means a coach who re-requests **loses** the access they already had until the junior decides again. A request against a `SUSPENDED` junior is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_SUSPENDED`. The grant lifecycle spans `REQUESTED`, `GRANTED`, `DENIED`, `REVOKED` and `SUPERSEDED`, with the last three terminal.

The junior decides, or a `TourAdmin` decides on their behalf; when the caller acts as `Member`, their `X-User-ID` must equal the grant's `member_id`. Only a grant still in `REQUESTED` status may be decided, and the payload carries a `decision` of either `GRANTED` or `DENIED`. A `GRANTED` decision advances the grant `REQUESTED → GRANTED`, stamps `decided_at` from the logical clock, and sets `expires_at` to 180 days after that instant — a recruiting grant runs for half a year and then lapses on its own. Granting further requires that the junior's `publication_opt_in` is true. A `DENIED` decision advances the grant `REQUESTED → DENIED`, stamps `decided_at`, and leaves `expires_at` null. Either the junior or a `TourAdmin` may later revoke a live grant, supplying an optional `reason`; revocation is admissible only from `GRANTED`, advances the grant `GRANTED → REVOKED` and clears `expires_at`. An attempt to decide a grant that is not `REQUESTED`, or to revoke one that is not `GRANTED`, yields `ERR_INVALID_GRANT_STATE`.

A `Coach` reading a junior's recruiting data must hold a grant whose `status` is `GRANTED`, whose `expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock, and whose `scope` covers the requested view. A coach whose grant is absent, still `REQUESTED`, `DENIED`, `REVOKED`, `SUPERSEDED` or expired, or whose `scope` is too narrow, is refused with `ERR_COACH_ACCESS_DENIED`, and that same rule guards both the published-results view and the ranking view.

## 4. API Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is rooted at the API base path `/`. Endpoints that create a new resource return HTTP `201` on success and place the newly created resource object in the `data` field. Endpoints that modify an existing resource — including every state-transition action, every decision, every payment, and the clock and ranking-run controls — return HTTP `200` and place the updated resource object in the `data` field. Read endpoints return HTTP `200`, with a `response_type` of `"object"` for single-resource reads and `"array"` for list reads. Failures return the status code paired with the raised error identifier in section 5.
