# Packaging Artwork Development & Approval API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Consumer brand packaging is where regulation, marketing and manufacturing meet on a single printed surface, and where a mistake is unusually expensive: an allergen statement that never made it onto a carton cannot be patched after the press run, it has to be reprinted, recovered from the vendor and explained to a regulator. Today that work is coordinated in spreadsheets and email threads — a brief lives in one place, the legal clearance for an on-pack claim lives in another, the printer's proof measurements arrive as a PDF, and nobody can answer with confidence whether the plates currently being cut correspond to the artwork that legal actually cleared. This service replaces that coordination layer with a single system of record for the entire artwork lifecycle, from the first brief through to the recovery invoice raised against a printer whose press let a defect escape into the market.

The platform models a dozen linked record types. A **brief** fixes the regulatory regime, the market and language coverage, the mandatory on-pack elements and the authoritative net contents declaration for one SKU. Under it sit **artwork assets** — one per market-and-component variant, because a French label and an English carton are separately printed, separately proofed and separately obsoleted. Each asset accumulates a chain of **artwork versions**, every one carrying documented reason-for-change, a machine-checked mandatory element payload, and a set of library **marketing claims** whose legal clearance is time-bounded. A version enters review through a frozen three-stage **review route** whose **review tasks** bind each discipline to exactly one reviewer role and enforce segregation of duties; approval pins the claim clearances so that a claim withdrawn a week later cannot be silently printed. Downstream, **printer proofs** carry three independent verdict dimensions, **plate orders** draw on a per-asset plate budget, **inventory runouts** coordinate the depletion of superseded stock, and **error escapes** apportion the cost of a defect between the brand and its print vendor.

Two properties of the design deserve emphasis up front, because most of the specification below is a consequence of them. First, the system is a **conserved ledger**: committed plate cost, scrapped units, changeover write-off and vendor recovery balance are all sums over subordinate rows, and every operation that unwinds a commitment does so by a compensating debit of exactly the amount previously credited — the original amount is never rewritten. Second, the system is **cascading**: approving one artwork version can, in a single request, complete a review route, approve a version, repoint an asset, obsolete a predecessor, auto-cancel that predecessor's plate orders and debit the asset's plate ledger. Every reader of this document should assume that a state change is rarely local.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set that principal is acting under. If either header is absent — and an `X-User-ID` that is present but empty or whitespace-only counts as absent — or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` and HTTP 401. When both headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` with HTTP 403. The same `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` code covers the finer-grained discipline binding on review decisions: a reviewer role that does not match the reviewing discipline recorded on the task it is trying to decide is refused with that code, because the endpoint is shared across four reviewer roles while each individual task is not.

The seven roles are `PackagingCoordinator`, `BrandReviewer`, `LegalReviewer`, `RegulatoryReviewer`, `QualityReviewer`, `PrinterRep` and `PlatformAdmin`.

A `PackagingCoordinator` owns the artwork pipeline end to end: registering briefs and editing them while they are still in draft, registering claims into the claim library, creating market/component assets, creating and editing artwork versions, running mandatory element verification, submitting versions into review, re-opening rejected versions, releasing approved versions to print, ordering and cancelling plate sets, and opening and driving inventory runouts. A `BrandReviewer` decides review tasks bound to the `BRAND` discipline; a `LegalReviewer` records clearance decisions on marketing claims and decides `LEGAL` tasks; a `RegulatoryReviewer` approves or rejects briefs, waives failed mandatory-element verdicts, and decides `REGULATORY` tasks; a `QualityReviewer` owns proof verdicts across all three proof dimensions, opens error escapes, assigns root cause and raises recovery claims, and decides `QUALITY` tasks. A `PrinterRep` acts on behalf of exactly one print vendor: it submits proofs and disputes recovery claims. A `PlatformAdmin` is an operational role that onboards print vendors, settles or writes off escapes, reverses scrap dispositions and drives the logical clock. `PlatformAdmin` is deliberately **not** a hierarchical override — it cannot create briefs, decide review tasks, waive element checks or submit proofs, and attempting any of those yields `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Every read endpoint in the service is open to all seven roles.

Six of the seven roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `PackagingCoordinator`, `BrandReviewer`, `LegalReviewer`, `RegulatoryReviewer`, `QualityReviewer` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it on the rows those operators touch — as `author_user_id` on a version the coordinator created, as `approved_by_user_id` on a brief the regulatory reviewer decided, as `cleared_by_user_id` on a claim the legal reviewer cleared, as `waived_by_user_id` on an element check the regulatory reviewer waived, as `decided_by_user_id` on a review task — but it never compares that value against any entity's `id`.

`PrinterRep` is the one exception and is entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `PrinterRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Printer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Printer` compare the resource's `printer_id` field against `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a proof submission whose `printer_id` differs from the caller's `X-User-ID`, and a dispute raised on an escape whose `printer_id` differs from the caller's `X-User-ID`, are both rejected with `ERR_PRINTER_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` and HTTP 403.

Segregation of duties is a third, independent identity rule and applies only to review decisions. The `X-User-ID` recorded as the author of an artwork version may never decide any review task on any routing pass of that version, and no single `X-User-ID` may decide more than one task on the same routing pass. Both violations surface as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` and continues with exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`; it is minted afresh for every response, error responses included.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one timestamp in the service read from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, so it does not move when an operator sets that clock and it bears no relation to the `created_at` / `updated_at` values in the same response.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query inputs, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds, or one that is not an integer at all, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated exactly as if it had been omitted and takes its default.

Unless a specific collection states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Four collections deviate, and their ordering is part of the contract: briefs are returned newest first, by `created_at` descending; artwork versions are ordered by `version_no` ascending; routing passes are ordered by `route_no` ascending; review tasks are ordered by `stage_no` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending within a stage; printer proofs are ordered by `proof_no` ascending; printers are ordered by `name` ascending; and mandatory-element verdict rows are returned in the fixed element order described in §3.4 rather than in insertion order. Every one of these orderings is made total the same way: rows that tie on the stated key — routine here, because `created_at` only advances when an operator moves the logical clock, and because a secondary key such as an element code or a `stage_no` repeats freely across rows — are ordered by the sequence in which the service created them, and the descending brief list reverses that tie-break along with everything else, so the most recently created of two briefs sharing a `created_at` comes first.

For every list response the `meta` object carries the four pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before the window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows matching the filter remain beyond the current window. The filters a collection accepts are validated to different depths, and the difference is part of the contract: a filter naming a foreign key is existence-checked exactly as §2.5 describes, a filter whose value is a whole number — a check run number, for instance — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when it does not parse as an integer, but a filter naming a value from one of the closed vocabularies of §2.10 is **not** checked against that vocabulary at all, so an undeclared value simply matches no row and yields an empty page rather than an error. Any filter supplied as an empty string is treated exactly as if it had been omitted.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every persisted row carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp. Timestamps are rendered as ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits, and that rendering is applied to every `*_at` and `*_utc` instant the service returns — including the instants an operator supplied in a request body, such as a claim clearance bound, a press-check window endpoint, a runout start or a depletion record's week start, which are normalised to UTC — an instant carrying a non-zero UTC offset is converted, and one carrying no zone designator at all is read as though it were already UTC — and re-rendered in this form rather than echoed back verbatim. (Fields that carry a calendar date rather than an instant are unaffected and keep the `YYYY-MM-DD` form described in §3.8.) Every entity in this service also carries an `updated_at` timestamp and an integer `version`: `version` starts at 1 when the row is created, and every mutation of the row — whether requested directly by an operator or applied by one of the cascades in §2.9 — increments it by exactly 1 and refreshes `updated_at`. That increment is counted once per request rather than once per write: a row that several links of a single cascade all write to still advances by exactly 1 — a scrap disposition that both moves an asset's ledgers and completes that asset's obsolescence lifts the asset's `version` by 1, not by 2 — and a row created and then further mutated inside the very same request, as an inventory runout opened with an `on_hand_qty` of 0 is, is returned at `version` 1. There are no entities in this system that omit `updated_at` or `version`; briefs, marketing claims, artwork assets, artwork versions, element checks, review routes, review tasks, printers, printer proofs, plate orders, inventory runouts and error escapes all carry the full set.

The service does not read wall-clock time for business decisions. All the `*_at` and `*_utc` comparisons in this document — clearance windows, press-check windows, runout dates, settlement timestamps — read a **logical clock** that a `PlatformAdmin` sets explicitly by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant. Before the first such call the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real time. Setting the clock mutates no entity; it may move the clock forwards or backwards.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and Reference Existence

Nested and referenced resources are resolved strictly from left to right along the URL, and only afterwards from the request body. Each collection has its own not-found code and each is HTTP 404: a brief id that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_BRIEF_NOT_FOUND`; a marketing claim id, whether supplied in the path or as an entry of a claim array in a body, yields `ERR_MARKETING_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`; an artwork asset id yields `ERR_ARTWORK_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`; an artwork version id — in a path, or as `artwork_version_id`, `supersedes_version_id`, `obsolete_version_id` or `current_approved_version_id` — yields `ERR_ARTWORK_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`; an element check id yields `ERR_ELEMENT_CHECK_NOT_FOUND`; a review route id, in the path or as a `review_route_id` filter, yields `ERR_REVIEW_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND`; a review task id yields `ERR_REVIEW_TASK_NOT_FOUND`; a printer id, in the path or as a `printer_id` body or query field, yields `ERR_PRINTER_NOT_FOUND`; a printer proof id, in the path or as a `printer_proof_id` body or query field, yields `ERR_PRINTER_PROOF_NOT_FOUND`; a plate order id yields `ERR_PLATE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`; an inventory runout id yields `ERR_INVENTORY_RUNOUT_NOT_FOUND`; and an error escape id yields `ERR_ERROR_ESCAPE_NOT_FOUND`. Because resolution is ordered, a request that names both a missing printer and a missing proof reports whichever appears first in the left-to-right walk.

Filtered list endpoints are the only discovery channel for rows the system creates on its own behalf. Review tasks are created by the submission cascade and never by a direct call, so a client finds them by filtering tasks on their owning routing pass; plate orders cancelled by the obsolescence cascade are found by filtering plate orders on `auto_cancelled`.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Two write operations in this service are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the `version` of a **parent** record, because in both cases a write computed from a stale view of the parent would silently overwrite work the client never observed. Concurrency control here is a stale-version comparison and nothing else; it does not depend on requests overlapping in time.

A `POST /artwork-assets/{id}/versions` request must carry `expected_artwork_asset_version` as a required input field, holding the `version` of the `ArtworkAsset` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the asset's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ASSET_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The reason is structural: a new version legitimately becomes the asset's newest revision — it consumes the asset's revision counter and may point at a predecessor through `supersedes_version_id` — so a create issued from a stale view of the asset would chain onto a revision its author never saw.

A `POST /error-escapes/{id}/recovery-claim` request must carry `expected_error_escape_version` as a required input field, holding the `version` of the `ErrorEscape` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the escape's current `version`, the claim is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ESCAPE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Again the reason is structural: a revised root-cause apportionment legitimately supersedes the prior claim basis, so a claim computed from a withdrawn apportionment would bill the printer on shares that no longer apply.

No other endpoint in this service takes a version field. Requests to any endpoint other than those two must not supply one, and doing so is an unknown field and therefore `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.7. Rejection Precedence: The Order Checks Are Evaluated

When a request violates more than one rule, the response is determined by the **first** rule violated in the following fixed order, not by whichever check the implementation happens to run first. The ladder has six bands.

First, authentication and coarse authorisation: header presence and role validity (`ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`), then endpoint role membership (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`). Second, existence, resolved left to right as described in §2.5 — briefs, then marketing claims, then artwork assets, then artwork versions, then element checks, then review routes, then review tasks, then printers, then printer proofs, then plate orders, then inventory runouts, then error escapes. Third, identity-level authorisation that needs the resource loaded: the print-vendor identity match (`ERR_PRINTER_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`), then segregation of duties on a review decision (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`). Fourth, request schema and uniqueness: field-level validation (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), then duplicate SKU code (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU_CODE`), then duplicate printer name (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PRINTER_NAME`), then duplicate asset variant (`ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSET_VARIANT`). Fifth, the two optimistic-concurrency checks of §2.6, asset staleness (`ERR_STALE_ASSET_VERSION`) before escape staleness (`ERR_STALE_ESCAPE_VERSION`).

Sixth and last comes the business band, itself ordered. State preconditions come first, in this sequence: brief state (`ERR_BRIEF_STATE_INVALID`), artwork version state (`ERR_VERSION_STATE_INVALID`), claim state (`ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`), element-check-not-failed (`ERR_ELEMENT_CHECK_NOT_FAILED`), routing stage not active (`ERR_ROUTE_STAGE_NOT_ACTIVE`), task already decided (`ERR_TASK_ALREADY_DECIDED`), proof state (`ERR_PROOF_STATE_INVALID`), proof dimension already decided (`ERR_PROOF_DIMENSION_ALREADY_DECIDED`), press check not required (`ERR_PRESS_CHECK_NOT_REQUIRED`), plate order not cancellable (`ERR_PLATE_ORDER_NOT_CANCELLABLE`), runout state (`ERR_RUNOUT_STATE_INVALID`), escape state (`ERR_ESCAPE_STATE_INVALID`), and brief-not-approved (`ERR_BRIEF_NOT_APPROVED`). Then regulatory and compliance safety, ahead of every other business rule, in this sequence: failed mandatory element (`ERR_MANDATORY_ELEMENT_FAILED`), compliance score below floor (`ERR_COMPLIANCE_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`), claim not cleared (`ERR_CLAIM_NOT_CLEARED`), claim clearance expired (`ERR_CLAIM_CLEARANCE_EXPIRED`), re-verification required (`ERR_REVERIFICATION_REQUIRED`), and revision cycle cap exceeded (`ERR_REVISION_CYCLE_CAP_EXCEEDED`). Then referential coherence: market code not in brief (`ERR_MARKET_CODE_NOT_IN_BRIEF`), language code not in brief (`ERR_LANGUAGE_CODE_NOT_IN_BRIEF`), claim market mismatch (`ERR_CLAIM_MARKET_MISMATCH`), supersedes version mismatch (`ERR_SUPERSEDES_VERSION_MISMATCH`), proof/version mismatch (`ERR_PROOF_VERSION_MISMATCH`). Then constraint satisfaction: substrate not certified (`ERR_SUBSTRATE_NOT_CERTIFIED`), printer on probation (`ERR_PRINTER_ON_PROBATION`), proof not approved (`ERR_PROOF_NOT_APPROVED`), share inconsistent with party (`ERR_SHARE_INCONSISTENT_WITH_PARTY`), unit conservation violation (`ERR_UNIT_CONSERVATION_VIOLATION`). Then temporal conflicts: press-check overlap (`ERR_PRESS_CHECK_OVERLAP`), claim clearance overlap (`ERR_CLAIM_CLEARANCE_OVERLAP`), depletion record out of order (`ERR_DEPLETION_RECORD_OUT_OF_ORDER`). And finally, deliberately last because this domain places safety before money, the fiscal ceilings: plate budget exceeded (`ERR_PLATE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`) and recovery exceeding the liability cap (`ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_LIABILITY_CAP`).

The practical consequence worth internalising: a submission of a version whose elements failed *and* whose claim was never cleared reports the failed element, not the uncleared claim. A plate order against a probationary printer that would also break the budget reports the probation, not the budget.

### 2.8. Money, Units, Rounding and Precision

Every monetary amount in this service is an integer number of cents and is stored, computed and returned as an integer. Where a percentage or share is applied to a cent amount, the product is rounded half up to zero decimal places — that is, to a whole cent — and, wherever a specification says rounding is applied *per line* before summation, summing the unrounded lines and rounding once at the end is a different and incorrect result.

Two non-monetary quantities carry fractional precision. Compliance scores and vendor performance scores carry one decimal place, rounded half up, and are rendered as decimal numbers that keep that place even when the fraction is zero — a perfect vendor scorecard reads `100.0` — whereas an element row's `deduction_points`, being a whole number of points except when a waiver has halved an odd weight, is rendered as a bare integer whenever it has no fractional part; and the intermediate scheme-scaled deduction subtotal inside the compliance calculation carries two decimal places, rounded half up, before the one-decimal final rounding. Colour measurements supplied by a reviewer carry two decimal places as supplied, while the tolerance they are compared against carries one decimal place. Apportionment shares carry two decimal places. Unit counts, week counts and plate counts are integers throughout, and where a number of whole weeks is derived from a division the result is rounded **up** to the next whole week.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline, Conserved Ledgers and Compensating Actions

Four numeric fields in this system are **conserved ledgers**: they are never set by an operator and are always the exact sum of a defined set of subordinate rows. An asset's `committed_plate_cost_cents` is the sum of `cost_cents` over that asset's plate orders whose `status` is `ORDERED`, so cancelled orders leave the ledger. An asset's `obsolete_units_total` is the sum of `units_scrapped` over that asset's runouts whose `state` is `SCRAPPED`. An asset's `changeover_cost_committed_cents` is the sum of `total_changeover_cost_cents` over the same set of runouts. A printer's `recovery_balance_cents` is the sum of `final_recovery_cents` over that printer's escapes whose `state` is `SETTLED`. Two further computed counters follow the same discipline: a brief's `asset_count` is the number of artwork assets whose `brief_id` names it, a claim's `usage_count` is the number of artwork versions whose claim array contains the claim's `id`, and a printer's `escape_count` is the number of escapes whose `printer_id` names it.

Whenever a commitment is unwound, it is unwound by a **compensating action**: the ledger is debited by exactly the amount that was credited, and the originating row's own stored amount is never rewritten. A cancelled plate order keeps its `cost_cents` forever and the asset ledger simply loses that figure. A reversed scrap disposition debits the asset's scrapped-unit and changeover ledgers by exactly the amounts that disposition credited, and leaves `on_hand_qty` and the stored depletion actuals untouched — so the invariant that on-hand stock equals remaining stock plus the sum of all consumed units holds identically before and after the reversal.

Cascades in this system are chains, not single hops, and every link fires inside the request that triggered the first link. The **approval cascade** has four links: a review decision resolves the final stage of a routing pass, which completes the routing pass, which approves the artwork version, which repoints the owning asset's `current_approved_version_id` and (when that pointer was already populated) moves the asset into `SUPERSEDE_PENDING`, which in turn obsoletes the previously approved version and auto-cancels its outstanding plate orders. The **obsolescence-completion cascade** runs the other way: a runout reaching a terminal disposition moves the obsolete version from `OBSOLETE_PENDING` to `OBSOLETE` and the asset from `SUPERSEDE_PENDING` to `SUPERSEDED`. The **probation cascade** has four links too: settling an escape credits the printer's recovery ledger and subtracts scorecard points, the reduced score may move the printer from `CERTIFIED` to `PROBATION`, and that probation flags every one of the printer's proofs that is not yet approved for re-check. Each entity touched anywhere in a cascade has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1 — including entities the caller never named.

### 2.10. Controlled Vocabularies

Before any business rule is consulted, every write request is checked against the shape its endpoint declares, and a single code covers every way that shape can be violated. `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400, is returned when a required field is absent — a field supplied as an explicit `null` is indistinguishable from an absent one throughout this service, so a required field sent as `null` is missing while an optional field sent as `null` simply falls back to its documented default, and a request that carries no body at all is read as an empty JSON object whose every required field is missing — when the body is present but is not a JSON object, when an enum value is not a declared member of its set, when a numeric value, array length or string length falls outside the bounds stated for it, when a string fails a stated format, when a datetime does not parse as ISO 8601 UTC, when an unknown field is supplied, or when a conditionally required field is missing for the particular request being made — a review comment on a rejection, a condition code on a conditional decision, a cancellation reason on a plate-order cancellation, the three actual invoice amounts on a settlement, the scrapped and reworked unit counts on a scrap disposition, or an interval whose end is not strictly after its start on a press-check booking or a claim clearance.

The following value sets are closed; any other value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A brief's `regulatory_scheme` is one of `FDA_FOOD`, `EU_FIC` or `HEALTH_CANADA`, and its `status` is one of `DRAFT`, `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`. Mandatory element codes are drawn from `NET_CONTENTS`, `INGREDIENT_STATEMENT`, `ALLERGEN_STATEMENT`, `DATE_CODE_ZONE`, `BARCODE`, `RECYCLING_MARK` and `ADDRESS_BLOCK`. Packaging components are `CARTON`, `LABEL`, `FILM` or `SHIPPER`; print processes are `OFFSET`, `FLEXO`, `GRAVURE` or `DIGITAL`; substrate families are `COATED_PAPER`, `UNCOATED_PAPER`, `FILM` or `METALLIZED`. Metric net-contents units are `G`, `KG`, `ML` or `L`, and US units are `OZ`, `LB` or `FLOZ`.

A marketing claim's `claim_type` is one of `NUTRIENT_CONTENT`, `STRUCTURE_FUNCTION`, `ORGANIC`, `FREE_FROM` or `SUSTAINABILITY`, and its `legal_status` is `DRAFT`, `CLEARED` or `REJECTED`. An asset's `obsolescence_state` is `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDE_PENDING` or `SUPERSEDED`. An artwork version's `state` is one of `DRAFT`, `ELEMENTS_VERIFIED`, `IN_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `RELEASED_TO_PRINT`, `OBSOLETE_PENDING` or `OBSOLETE`, and its `reason_for_change_code` is one of `NEW_LAUNCH`, `REGULATORY_UPDATE`, `CLAIM_CHANGE`, `MARKETING_REFRESH`, `PRINTER_CHANGE` or `ERROR_CORRECTION`.

An element check's `severity` is `CRITICAL`, `MAJOR` or `MINOR` and its `result` is `PASS`, `FAIL` or `WAIVED`. A routing pass's `status` is `PENDING`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETE_APPROVED` or `COMPLETE_REJECTED`. A review task's `discipline` is `BRAND`, `LEGAL`, `REGULATORY` or `QUALITY`, its `mode` is `PARALLEL` or `SEQUENTIAL`, and its `decision` is `PENDING`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `SKIPPED`.

A printer's `color_standard` is `G7_MASTER` or `ISO12647` and its `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED` or `PROBATION`. A proof's `color_status` is `COLOR_PENDING`, `COLOR_PASS` or `COLOR_FAIL`; its `substrate_status` is `SUBSTRATE_PENDING`, `SUBSTRATE_APPROVED` or `SUBSTRATE_REJECTED`; its `press_check_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PRESS_CHECK_REQUIRED`, `PRESS_CHECK_SCHEDULED` or `PRESS_CHECK_ATTENDED`; and its derived `proof_state` is `PROOF_IN_REVIEW`, `PROOF_APPROVED` or `PROOF_REJECTED`. A plate order's `status` is `ORDERED` or `CANCELLED`.

A runout's `state` is `PLANNED`, `MONITORING`, `RUNOUT_COMPLETE` or `SCRAPPED`, and its `disposition`, once decided, is `RUNOUT` or `SCRAP`. An escape's `defect_code` is one of `WRONG_ALLERGEN`, `MISSING_NET_CONTENTS`, `WRONG_LANGUAGE_VARIANT`, `COLOR_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`, `WRONG_DATE_CODE` or `OLD_ARTWORK_USED`; its `discovered_stage` is `PRESS`, `PACKING`, `DISTRIBUTION` or `MARKET`; its `state` is `OPEN`, `ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED`, `RECOVERY_CLAIMED`, `DISPUTED`, `SETTLED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`; its `root_cause_party` is `PRINTER`, `BRAND`, `AGENCY` or `SHARED`; and its terminal `resolution` is `SETTLED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Briefing the Pack: Coverage, Mandatory Elements and Net Contents

Everything downstream begins with a brief, registered by a `PackagingCoordinator` for exactly one stock-keeping unit. The coordinator supplies a `sku_code` of between 4 and 32 characters that no other brief already uses — the uniqueness comparison is a case-sensitive exact match, so `ACME-01` and `acme-01` are two different SKUs, and a genuine collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU_CODE`. Alongside it come the consumer-facing `product_name` of between 3 and 120 characters, the `regulatory_scheme` that will govern element verification, and the coverage arrays: `market_codes` holds between 1 and 6 unique uppercase ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 market codes, and `language_codes` holds between 1 and 6 unique lowercase BCP-47 primary language subtags — each two or three lowercase letters and nothing else — that must appear on pack somewhere across the brief.

The brief also fixes what has to be printed and how. `required_element_codes` is a list of between 1 and 7 unique mandatory element codes, and it is this list — not a global default — that decides how many verdict rows an element verification run produces. The `structural_spec` is an object carrying `component_type`, a `dimensions_mm` object with the three integer edges `l`, `w` and `h`, a `substrate_class` and a `print_process` — all four of those keys are required, and each of the three edges is validated as an integer and nothing more, so a zero or negative edge is accepted. And `net_contents_declaration` is the authoritative quantity every artwork version of this brief must reproduce: an object carrying `metric_value`, which must be greater than zero, a `metric_uom`, and an optional pair `us_value` and `us_uom` which may both be null — but when `us_value` is supplied as non-null, `us_uom` must be one of the US units, while `us_value` itself need only be a number and carries no positivity bound of its own. Both quantities may be integral or fractional. Additional keys inside `structural_spec`, `dimensions_mm` or `net_contents_declaration` are ignored rather than rejected, the unknown-field rejection of §2.10 being applied to the top-level body only.

A newly registered brief begins in `DRAFT` with an `asset_count` of 0 and a `version` of 1. While it remains in `DRAFT` the coordinator may revise its `product_name`, `market_codes`, `language_codes`, `required_element_codes`, `structural_spec` and `net_contents_declaration`, each optional and each overwriting the stored value wholesale — a field supplied as an explicit `null` is no no-op here, it simply fails the validation of the field it names and is therefore `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and `sku_code` and `regulatory_scheme` are not editable at all, so supplying either is an unknown field and equally `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An edit that names no field whatsoever is nevertheless accepted; the edit refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` by 1. Once the brief has left `DRAFT` no edit is possible and the attempt is refused with `ERR_BRIEF_STATE_INVALID`.

The regulatory approval decision is the gate. A `RegulatoryReviewer` records a `decision` of either `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` together with a `review_note`, moving the brief `DRAFT → APPROVED` or `DRAFT → REJECTED`; a decision on a brief that is no longer `DRAFT` is `ERR_BRIEF_STATE_INVALID`. Approval additionally requires that `required_element_codes` holds at least one entry and that the declared `metric_value` is greater than zero — each of those two is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when it fails, and each is evaluated only after the brief-state check above, though the create and edit rules already guarantee both so neither is reachable in practice. Rejection requires that `review_note` is present and non-empty, and a rejection without one is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; on approval `review_note` is optional and is stored as null when omitted. Either outcome records `approved_by_user_id` as the deciding `X-User-ID` and `approved_at` as the current logical-clock instant, stores the `review_note`, refreshes `updated_at` and increments the brief's `version` by 1. Both `APPROVED` and `REJECTED` are terminal for the brief.

Approval is deliberately *not* a prerequisite for downstream drafting. Assets and artwork versions may be created and edited under a brief that is still in `DRAFT`; it is only the moment a version is pushed into review that the owning brief's `status` must read `APPROVED`, and a submission under a `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` brief is refused with `ERR_BRIEF_NOT_APPROVED`. Briefs are discoverable as a list, filterable on `status`, and — unusually for this service — returned newest first.

### 3.2. The Marketing Claim Library and Time-Bounded Legal Clearance

On-pack claims are managed as a shared library rather than as free text on each artwork. A `PackagingCoordinator` registers a claim with a `claim_code` of 3 to 40 characters drawn from the uppercase letters, the digits and the dash, the verbatim on-pack `claim_text` of 3 to 300 characters, a regulatory `claim_type`, a single two-character uppercase `market_code`, and a `substantiation_ref` of 3 to 80 characters naming the dossier that supports it. The claim is created with `legal_status` `DRAFT`, a `usage_count` of 0 and a `version` of 1. Note that `claim_code` is deliberately **not** unique on its own — the same code recurs for different markets and for successive clearance periods.

A `LegalReviewer` then records a clearance `decision` of `CLEARED` or `REJECTED`. Clearing a claim requires both `cleared_from_utc` and `cleared_until_utc`, and the first must be strictly earlier than the second; a missing bound or an inverted pair is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The resulting clearance window is half-open — inclusive of `cleared_from_utc` and exclusive of `cleared_until_utc` — and it must not intersect the window of any other claim that shares both the same `claim_code` and the same `market_code` and whose `legal_status` is currently `CLEARED`. The interval comparison carries **no** buffer whatsoever, so a window that begins exactly at another window's `cleared_until_utc` is accepted, while any genuine intersection is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_CLEARANCE_OVERLAP`. A successful clearance moves the claim `DRAFT → CLEARED`, stores both bounds and records `cleared_by_user_id`; a `CLEARED` decision against a claim that is not in `DRAFT` — one that already reads `CLEARED`, for instance — is `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`.

Rejection has two forms and both require a non-empty `rejection_reason`, absent which the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A claim still in `DRAFT` moves `DRAFT → REJECTED`, storing `rejection_reason` and `cleared_by_user_id`. A claim already in `CLEARED` may also be withdrawn, moving `CLEARED → REJECTED` with the same requirement; withdrawal deliberately **retains** the stored `cleared_from_utc` and `cleared_until_utc` for audit rather than nulling them. Because overlap detection consults only rows whose `legal_status` is `CLEARED`, a withdrawal frees that code-and-market interval for a fresh clearance. `REJECTED` is terminal in both forms: any further clearance decision on a rejected claim is `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. Whichever decision is taken, the fields that belong to the other one are neither validated nor stored: a rejection may carry `cleared_from_utc` and `cleared_until_utc` — unparseable ones included — without complaint and they are discarded, and a clearance may likewise carry a `rejection_reason` that is discarded. Each clearance decision refreshes `updated_at` and increments the claim's `version` by 1. Claims are discoverable as a list filterable on `claim_code`, `market_code` and `legal_status`.

### 3.3. Market Variants and the Revision Chain

A brief covers several markets; a printed artwork covers exactly one. An **artwork asset** is that intersection — one market, one packaging component — and a coordinator creates it by naming the owning brief through a payload carrying the `brief_id`, together with the single `market_code` the asset serves, the `language_codes` printed on this particular variant, the `component_type`, the `substrate_class` the artwork is printed on, an optional `brand_critical_color` flag that defaults to false, and a `plate_budget_cents` ceiling that must be zero or greater. No two assets of the same brief may share the same combination of market and component, and a collision on that triple is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSET_VARIANT`. The asset's `market_code` must be one of the brief's `market_codes`, failing which the create is `ERR_MARKET_CODE_NOT_IN_BRIEF`; and every one of the asset's 1 to 6 unique `language_codes` entries must be one of the brief's `language_codes`, failing which it is `ERR_LANGUAGE_CODE_NOT_IN_BRIEF`. Coverage is the only agreement the brief demands of its assets: the asset's own `component_type` and `substrate_class` are free to differ from the ones recorded in the brief's `structural_spec`, and no check compares them.

A new asset begins with `obsolescence_state` `ACTIVE`, a `version_counter` of 0, a null `current_approved_version_id`, and all three of its conserved ledgers — `committed_plate_cost_cents`, `obsolete_units_total` and `changeover_cost_committed_cents` — at 0. The asset's `standard_unit_cost_cents` is derived from its component and is not supplied by the caller: a `CARTON` costs 42 cents per unit, a `LABEL` 9 cents, a `FILM` 15 cents and a `SHIPPER` 120 cents. This one figure reappears twice downstream — it prices scrapped stock in a changeover write-off and it estimates the reprint exposure in a provisional recovery claim. Creating an asset also recomputes the parent brief's `asset_count`. Assets are listed filterable on `brief_id`, on `current_approved_version_id`, on `market_code` and on `obsolescence_state`.

Each asset then accumulates a chain of artwork versions. Creating the next revision is the one write in this service guarded by optimistic concurrency on the asset, so the payload must carry `expected_artwork_asset_version` holding the asset's `version` as the author last read it, supplied as an integer of zero or greater; a mismatch is `ERR_STALE_ASSET_VERSION`. Creating a revision is otherwise admissible whatever the owning asset's `obsolescence_state` reads — `SUPERSEDED` included — and whatever state the versions already in the chain stand in. Beyond that the author supplies the change-control documentation — a `reason_for_change_code` drawn from the closed set of change reasons, and a `change_description` of 10 to 500 characters recording in prose why the revision exists — the `mandatory_elements` artwork payload described in §3.4, an optional `claim_ids` array of 0 to 8 unique marketing claim identifiers that defaults to the empty array, and an optional `supersedes_version_id` naming the predecessor this revision is intended to replace. On the first version of an asset `supersedes_version_id` is null; when it is non-null it must name a version belonging to the *same* asset, and a pointer into another asset's chain is `ERR_SUPERSEDES_VERSION_MISMATCH`.

Creating a version increments the asset's `version_counter` by 1 and stamps the new row's `version_no` with the resulting value, so revision numbers are sequential within the asset and never reused; the asset's own `version` is incremented by 1 as well. The version is created in `DRAFT` with `author_user_id` set to the creating `X-User-ID` — the subject of every segregation-of-duties check that follows — with `check_run_no`, `revision_cycle_count` and `route_count` all at 0, `requires_reverification` false, `open_condition_codes` and `pinned_claim_snapshot` both empty, `compliance_score` null, and `failed_element_count` and `waived_element_count` both 0.

While a version sits in `DRAFT` or in `REVISION_REQUESTED` its author may edit it, optionally replacing `mandatory_elements`, `claim_ids`, `reason_for_change_code` and `change_description`; an edit that names none of the four is accepted and still counts as a mutation, while `mandatory_elements`, `reason_for_change_code` or `change_description` supplied as an explicit `null` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and `claim_ids` supplied as an explicit `null` falls back to its documented default and so resets the array to empty. An edit in any other state — and equally an edit of any version whose owning asset has already reached `SUPERSEDE_PENDING`'s successor state `SUPERSEDED` — is `ERR_VERSION_STATE_INVALID`. Editing is deliberately inert with respect to compliance: it refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` by 1 but does **not** re-run verification, so `compliance_score` keeps its stale value until the next verification run recomputes it.

A version rejected in review lands in `REVISION_REQUESTED` and must be explicitly re-opened before it can be edited into shape and resubmitted; that re-opening carries an optional `revise_note` and moves the version `REVISION_REQUESTED → DRAFT`, closing the cycle `DRAFT → ELEMENTS_VERIFIED → IN_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → DRAFT`. Re-opening leaves existing element verdict rows in place — they survive until the next verification run deletes them. Re-opening is refused with `ERR_VERSION_STATE_INVALID` from any other state, and refused with `ERR_REVISION_CYCLE_CAP_EXCEEDED` once the version has already been bounced back from review three times. Versions are listed in `version_no` order, filterable on `artwork_asset_id`, on `supersedes_version_id` — which is how the self-referential revision chain is walked — and on `state`.

### 3.4. Mandatory Element Verification and the Compliance Score

The `mandatory_elements` payload on an artwork version is the machine-checkable transcription of what the pack actually says. It is an object with one optional sub-object per element the brief may require: `net_contents`, carrying its own `metric_value`, `metric_uom`, `us_value` and `us_uom`; `ingredient_statement`, carrying `text` and the boolean `descending_order`; `allergen_statement`, carrying `text` and the boolean `allergen_emphasis`; `date_code_zone`, carrying the integer `zone_mm2` and a `position`; `barcode`, carrying `gtin14`; `recycling_mark`, carrying the boolean `present`; and `address_block`, carrying `text`. Every sub-object is optional and the payload as a whole may be an empty object; a sub-object naming an element the brief does not require, and any other unrecognised key, is ignored rather than rejected.

A verification run is requested by the version's coordinator with an optional `verification_note`. It is admissible from four states and its effect on `state` differs in each: from `DRAFT` it advances the version `DRAFT → ELEMENTS_VERIFIED`; from `ELEMENTS_VERIFIED` it re-runs and leaves the state unchanged, which is what an author does after editing the payload; from `REVISION_REQUESTED` it re-runs and likewise leaves the state unchanged — re-verification explicitly does **not** clear a requested revision, which still has to be re-opened separately; and from `APPROVED` it is admissible only when `requires_reverification` is true, in which case it re-runs and then sets that flag back to false while leaving `state` at `APPROVED` and `open_condition_codes` exactly as review left it — the codes are never cleared by a re-verification. From `APPROVED` with `requires_reverification` already false, and from any other state, the run is refused with `ERR_VERSION_STATE_INVALID`, as it is whenever the owning asset's `obsolescence_state` has reached `SUPERSEDED`.

Every run is a clean slate. The run first deletes every element verdict row of this version belonging to an earlier run, then increments the version's `check_run_no` by 1, then writes exactly one fresh row per entry of the owning brief's `required_element_codes` — each stamped with the new `check_run_no`. Crucially the rows are emitted in a **fixed** element order and never in the brief's input order, so a brief that lists its required elements in a different sequence produces an identical result: `NET_CONTENTS` first, then `INGREDIENT_STATEMENT`, then `ALLERGEN_STATEMENT`, then `DATE_CODE_ZONE`, then `BARCODE`, then `RECYCLING_MARK`, then `ADDRESS_BLOCK`. Each row records its `element_code`, its `severity`, its `result`, a human-readable `detail`, and its `deduction_points`.

Severity is a fixed property of the element and is not supplied by anyone. The three consumer-safety elements — `NET_CONTENTS`, `INGREDIENT_STATEMENT` and `ALLERGEN_STATEMENT` — are `CRITICAL`. `DATE_CODE_ZONE` and `BARCODE` are `MAJOR`. `RECYCLING_MARK` and `ADDRESS_BLOCK` are `MINOR`.

The verdict itself is decided per element. When the corresponding sub-object is absent from the payload the row is a `FAIL` and its `detail` reads `ELEMENT_ABSENT` — and a sub-object that is present but is not a JSON object, a string or a number or an explicit `null` standing in its place, counts as absent for this purpose rather than as a schema error; when the sub-object is present, the element's own sub-rule decides, `detail` reads `OK` on a pass, and on a failure `detail` carries the element-specific explanation of which sub-rule failed, spelled as the element's own code followed by the literal suffix `_SUBRULE_FAILED` — a failing barcode therefore reads `BARCODE_SUBRULE_FAILED`. The seven sub-rules are these. `NET_CONTENTS` passes when the payload's `metric_value` is greater than zero, its `metric_uom` is a non-empty string, and — additionally, and only when the brief's `regulatory_scheme` is `FDA_FOOD` — its `us_value` is greater than zero and its `us_uom` is a non-empty string; under `EU_FIC` or `HEALTH_CANADA` the US pair is not examined at all. `INGREDIENT_STATEMENT` passes when its `text` is at least 10 characters long and `descending_order` is true. `ALLERGEN_STATEMENT` passes when its `text` is at least 3 characters long and `allergen_emphasis` is true. `DATE_CODE_ZONE` passes when `zone_mm2` is at least 150 and `position` is a non-empty string. `BARCODE` passes when `gtin14` consists of exactly fourteen digit characters and nothing else. `RECYCLING_MARK` passes when `present` is true. `ADDRESS_BLOCK` passes when its `text` is at least 15 characters long.

Each row's `deduction_points` follow from its severity and its result. A `FAIL` deducts the full severity weight: 25 points for a `CRITICAL` element, 10 for a `MAJOR` one, 4 for a `MINOR` one. A `WAIVED` row deducts exactly half of that same weight — 12.5, 5 or 2 points respectively — because a waiver is a documented acceptance of residual risk and not an erasure of it. A `PASS` deducts 0 points.

Four independent inputs then combine into the version's `compliance_score`, and the order of the arithmetic is part of the contract. First, the `deduction_points` of every row of the run just executed are summed; rows from earlier runs have been deleted and can never enter the sum. Second, that sum is multiplied by the scheme factor for the brief's `regulatory_scheme` — 1.00 under `FDA_FOOD`, 1.10 under `EU_FIC` and 1.05 under `HEALTH_CANADA` — and the product is rounded half up to two decimal places **at this point**, before anything else is added. Third, a market-breadth penalty of 2.5 points is charged for every market beyond the first that the brief covers: with one market the penalty is zero, with four markets it is 7.5 points. Fourth, a language-breadth penalty of 1.5 points is charged for every language beyond the second printed on *this asset* — the asset's own `language_codes`, not the brief's wider list — so a bilingual variant is penalty-free and a four-language variant is charged 3.0 points. The scheme-scaled subtotal and the two breadth penalties are added together to give the total penalty; the score is 100 minus that total, rounded half up to one decimal place as the final step, and then clamped so it can be no lower than 0.0 and no higher than 100.0.

The same run recomputes `failed_element_count` as the number of rows of the current run whose `result` is `FAIL` and `waived_element_count` as the number whose `result` is `WAIVED`. Because a fresh run rewrites the row set from scratch, `waived_element_count` returns to 0 on every run and a previously waived element is re-judged on its merits. The run also stores the `verification_note` it was handed, overwriting whatever the previous run left there, so a re-run that omits the note returns the field to null. The run refreshes `updated_at` and increments the version's `version` by 1.

A `RegulatoryReviewer` may waive an individual failing verdict, supplying a mandatory `waiver_justification` that must still be non-empty once surrounding whitespace is discounted. A waiver is admissible whatever `state` the owning version stands in and whatever its asset's `obsolescence_state` reads, and it changes neither. The waiver is admissible only on a row whose `result` is `FAIL` **and** which belongs to its version's current `check_run_no`; a row that passed, a row already waived, or a row left over from a superseded run is refused with `ERR_ELEMENT_CHECK_NOT_FAILED`. A successful waiver moves the row's `result` from `FAIL` to `WAIVED`, records `waived_by_user_id` as the waiving `X-User-ID` and stores the `waiver_justification`, halves the row's `deduction_points` to half the severity weight, and then recomputes the owning version's `compliance_score`, `failed_element_count` and `waived_element_count` through the same four-input arithmetic described above. Both the element row's `version` and the artwork version's `version` are incremented by 1. Element verdict rows are readable individually and as a list filterable on `artwork_version_id`, on `check_run_no` and on `result`.

### 3.5. Routing for Review: Stages, Disciplines and Segregation of Duties

Submitting a version into review is the narrowest gate in the system, and its preconditions are evaluated in a fixed order so that the most safety-relevant obstacle is always the one reported. The version's `state` must be `ELEMENTS_VERIFIED` and its asset must not yet be `SUPERSEDED`, else `ERR_VERSION_STATE_INVALID`. The owning brief's `status` must be `APPROVED`, else `ERR_BRIEF_NOT_APPROVED`. No row of the version's current `check_run_no` may carry a `result` of `FAIL`, else `ERR_MANDATORY_ELEMENT_FAILED` — note that a waived row does not block submission, which is exactly what the half-weight residue is for. The version's `compliance_score` must be at least 70.0, the comparison being inclusive so a score of exactly 70.0 passes, else `ERR_COMPLIANCE_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. Every entry of `claim_ids` must resolve to a claim whose `legal_status` is `CLEARED`, else `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_CLEARED`; each such claim's clearance window must contain the current logical-clock instant, meaning the clock is at or after `cleared_from_utc` and strictly before `cleared_until_utc`, else `ERR_CLAIM_CLEARANCE_EXPIRED`. The version's `revision_cycle_count` must still be below 3, else `ERR_REVISION_CYCLE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. And each claim's own `market_code` must equal the owning asset's `market_code`, else `ERR_CLAIM_MARKET_MISMATCH`. The submission itself carries only an optional `submit_note`, which is recorded on both the artwork version and the routing pass the submission opens and reads null on either when it was omitted — as do the version's other optional notes, `verification_note`, `revise_note` and `release_note`, each persisted on the version by the call that accepted it and each returned on every subsequent version read.

A successful submission increments the version's `route_count` by 1 and opens a **routing pass** whose `route_no` is the resulting value. The pass is created with `status` `PENDING` and `current_stage_no` 1, and it carries a `stage_plan` that is frozen at creation and never renegotiated and is returned as an array of three objects, one per stage in ascending `stage_no`, each carrying exactly `stage_no`, `mode` and a `disciplines` array of discipline names: stage 1 has `mode` `PARALLEL` and the `disciplines` `BRAND` and `REGULATORY`; stage 2 has `mode` `SEQUENTIAL` and the single discipline `LEGAL`; stage 3 has `mode` `SEQUENTIAL` and the single discipline `QUALITY`. Four review tasks are created from that plan — one per discipline — each stamped with its `discipline`, its `mode` and its `stage_no`, each starting with `decision` `PENDING`, and each with `is_active` false except the two stage-1 tasks, which are activated as the pass immediately moves `PENDING → IN_PROGRESS` inside the very same request. The version moves `ELEMENTS_VERIFIED → IN_REVIEW`. Because tasks are never created by a direct call, the task list — filterable on `review_route_id`, on `discipline`, on `stage_no` and on `decision` — is the only way a client discovers them; routing passes themselves are listed filterable on `artwork_version_id` and on `status`.

A reviewer records a verdict on a single task by supplying a `decision` of `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` or `CONDITIONAL`, with an optional `comment` and an optional `condition_code`. A `REJECTED` decision requires a non-empty `comment` and a `CONDITIONAL` decision requires a non-empty `condition_code`; either omission is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Non-empty here means non-blank: a value consisting of whitespace alone counts as empty and is rejected exactly as an omission is, and a value that is not a string at all is rejected too — and that same reading governs every other mandatory free-text field in this document, a plate-order `cancel_reason`, a disposition `reversal_reason`, a `dispute_reason` and an `investigation_note` alike. Both `comment` and `condition_code` are otherwise unconstrained in length and are stored verbatim, whitespace included. Four preconditions guard the write. The task's `decision` must still be `PENDING`, else `ERR_TASK_ALREADY_DECIDED`. The task's `is_active` must be true, its `stage_no` must equal the pass's `current_stage_no`, and the pass's `status` must be `IN_PROGRESS`; any of those failing is `ERR_ROUTE_STAGE_NOT_ACTIVE` — and per the precedence ladder of §2.7 that stage check is reported ahead of the already-decided check. The acting `X-User-Role` must be the role bound to the task's `discipline`: `BRAND` requires a `BrandReviewer`, `LEGAL` a `LegalReviewer`, `REGULATORY` a `RegulatoryReviewer` and `QUALITY` a `QualityReviewer`, and a mismatch is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` — a first-band rejection notwithstanding that it needs the task loaded, so it is evaluated the moment the task itself resolves and therefore ahead of segregation of duties, ahead of the schema validation of the payload and ahead of every state precondition listed here: a wrong-discipline reviewer who also sends a malformed body against an already-decided task is answered `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Finally, segregation of duties: the deciding `X-User-ID` may be neither the routed version's `author_user_id` nor an identifier already recorded as `decided_by_user_id` on any other task of the same pass, and either violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Whatever the verdict, the task stores its `decision`, its `decided_by_user_id` and a `decided_at` stamped from the logical clock, refreshes `updated_at` and increments its `version` by 1. An `APPROVED` verdict stores the supplied `comment`. A `REJECTED` verdict stores the mandatory `comment`. A `CONDITIONAL` verdict stores the supplied optional `comment` as well, alongside its `condition_code`, appends that same code to the routed version's `open_condition_codes` array in decision chronological order, and increments the pass's `conditional_count`. On all three verdicts an omitted `comment` is stored as null. A `condition_code` supplied alongside an `APPROVED` or a `REJECTED` decision is accepted without complaint and then discarded — it is never stored, so those tasks still read `condition_code` null.

Each decision then re-derives the verdict of the deciding task's own stage, considering **only** the tasks of that pass whose `stage_no` equals the stage being resolved — tasks of already-decided earlier stages and of not-yet-active later stages are excluded from the derivation entirely. If any task in the stage carries `REJECTED`, the stage verdict is `REJECTED`. Otherwise, if every task in the stage carries either `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL` — a conditional decision counts as approving for this purpose, which is precisely why a stage can resolve approved while conditions remain open — the stage verdict is `APPROVED`. Otherwise the stage verdict is pending and the pass waits. The pass's `conditional_count` is recomputed on every decision as the number of its tasks whose `decision` is `CONDITIONAL`.

A stage verdict of `APPROVED` while `current_stage_no` is below 3 keeps the pass at `IN_PROGRESS → IN_PROGRESS`, increments `current_stage_no` by 1 and sets `is_active` true on every task of the newly current stage. A stage verdict of `APPROVED` when `current_stage_no` is already 3 moves the pass `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETE_APPROVED` and stamps `completed_at` from the logical clock. A stage verdict of `REJECTED` — at any stage — moves the pass `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETE_REJECTED`, stamps `completed_at`, and moves every still-`PENDING` task of the pass, in all three stages including the inactive later ones, `PENDING → SKIPPED` with `is_active` set false and `decided_by_user_id` left null. Each pass and task touched has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, and the pass counts as touched by every decision — including one that leaves its stage unresolved and alters nothing but the recomputed `conditional_count` — so any accepted decision lifts the pass's `version` by exactly 1. The routed artwork version, by contrast, is lifted only when the decision actually wrote to it, and there is one path on which it does not: a `CONDITIONAL` decision that leaves its stage unresolved does refresh the version's `updated_at` and increment its `version`, but a `CONDITIONAL` decision that resolves its stage `APPROVED` while `current_stage_no` is still below 3 appends its `condition_code` to `open_condition_codes` **without** refreshing the version's `updated_at` or incrementing its `version` — the appended code is visible on the next read while the version counter has not moved. Only when the resolving `CONDITIONAL` lands on stage 3, where the approval cascade writes the version anyway, does that decision lift the version's `version` by 1.

Completion propagates to the artwork version in the same request. A pass reaching `COMPLETE_REJECTED` moves the version `IN_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED` and increments its `revision_cycle_count` by 1 — the counter that, at 3, permanently closes both re-opening and re-submission with `ERR_REVISION_CYCLE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. A pass reaching `COMPLETE_APPROVED` moves the version `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED`, stamps `approved_at` from the logical clock, and freezes `pinned_claim_snapshot` as one entry per `claim_ids` element, in `claim_ids` order, each entry recording that claim's `marketing_claim_id`, its `claim_code` and its `cleared_until_utc` as they stood at the moment of approval. It also sets `requires_reverification` to true when the pass's `conditional_count` is greater than zero, and leaves it false when no conditional decision was recorded — the flag that later blocks release until a fresh verification run clears it.

Approval of a version is the head of the obsolescence chain. The owning asset's `current_approved_version_id` is repointed at the newly approved version. If that pointer was null — the first-ever approval for this asset — the asset stays `ACTIVE` and nothing further happens. If it was already populated, the asset moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDE_PENDING` (or, when a prior obsolescence had already completed, `SUPERSEDED → SUPERSEDE_PENDING`), and that asset mutation is itself the trigger for the next link: the version the pointer previously named — whether it stood in `APPROVED` or in `RELEASED_TO_PRINT` — moves to `OBSOLETE_PENDING`. Both branches behave identically. As that predecessor is obsoleted, every plate order of it whose `status` is `ORDERED` moves `ORDERED → CANCELLED` with `auto_cancelled` set true, `cancel_reason` set to the literal `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_ARTWORK` and `cancelled_at` stamped from the logical clock; orders that were already `CANCELLED` are left entirely untouched. For each such automatic cancellation the asset's `committed_plate_cost_cents` is debited by exactly that order's stored `cost_cents`. Every entity touched along this chain — pass, task, version, predecessor version, asset, each cancelled order — has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1.

### 3.6. Print Vendors and the Three Dimensions of a Proof

Print vendors are onboarded by a `PlatformAdmin`, who supplies the vendor's `name` — 3 to 80 characters, and unique across all vendors by exact string comparison, so two names differing only in letter case or in surrounding whitespace are distinct vendors and only an exact repeat is a collision, which is `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRINTER_NAME` — the `color_standard` the vendor prints to, a `certified_substrates` array of 1 to 4 unique substrate families the vendor is qualified for, and the contractual `recovery_liability_cap_cents` ceiling, which must be zero or greater and which caps what can ever be recovered from that vendor per escape. A new vendor is created `CERTIFIED`, with a `performance_score` of 100.0 that is constrained to remain between 0.0 and 100.0 inclusive, a `recovery_balance_cents` of 0, an `escape_count` of 0 and a `version` of 1. Vendors are listed in `name` order, filterable on `certification_status`.

A `PrinterRep` submits a proof on behalf of its own vendor, naming the `artwork_version_id` being proofed, the `printer_id` — which must equal the caller's own `X-User-ID`, else `ERR_PRINTER_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` — the `substrate_code` the proof was actually pulled on, and an optional free-text `proof_note`. Only a version in `APPROVED` or `RELEASED_TO_PRINT` may be proofed; anything else is `ERR_VERSION_STATE_INVALID`. The `substrate_code` must appear in the vendor's `certified_substrates`, else `ERR_SUBSTRATE_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and the vendor's `certification_status` must read `CERTIFIED`, else `ERR_PRINTER_ON_PROBATION`.

The new proof's `proof_no` is the count of proofs already existing for that version plus one. It opens with `color_status` `COLOR_PENDING`, `substrate_status` `SUBSTRATE_PENDING`, a derived `proof_state` of `PROOF_IN_REVIEW` and `requires_recheck` false. Its `press_check_required` flag is computed once, at creation, and never revised: it is true when the owning asset's `brand_critical_color` is true **or** when the `substrate_code` is `FILM`, and false otherwise. That flag initialises the third dimension — `press_check_status` starts at `PRESS_CHECK_REQUIRED` when a press check is required and at `NOT_REQUIRED` when it is not.

The proof's `effective_tolerance` is also computed at creation. The base tolerance is read from a table indexed by the vendor's `color_standard` and the proof's `substrate_code`: a `G7_MASTER` vendor is allowed 2.0 on `COATED_PAPER`, 3.0 on `UNCOATED_PAPER`, 3.5 on `FILM` and 4.0 on `METALLIZED`, while an `ISO12647` vendor is allowed 2.5, 3.5, 4.0 and 4.5 on the same four substrates respectively. When the owning asset carries `brand_critical_color`, the base tolerance is tightened by subtracting 0.5; when it does not, nothing is subtracted. The result is rounded half up to one decimal place and stored.

Three verdicts then resolve the proof, and they are genuinely independent: colour and substrate may be decided in either order, and the press-check track advances on its own schedule. A `QualityReviewer` resolves the colour dimension by reporting `measured_delta_e_max`, the maximum measured colour difference across the proof, carried to two decimal places as supplied. The measurement must be a number of zero or greater — a negative reading is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and it carries no upper bound whatever; it is stored exactly as supplied and never re-rounded. Both it and `effective_tolerance` are returned as decimal numbers that keep a fractional place even when the fraction is zero, so a tolerance of 2 reads `2.0` and a measurement supplied as the bare integer `3` reads `3.0`. The stored measurement is compared against `effective_tolerance` **inclusively**: a measurement less than or equal to the tolerance yields `color_status` `COLOR_PASS`, and a measurement exactly equal to the tolerance is therefore a pass, while anything greater yields `COLOR_FAIL`. The same reviewer resolves the substrate dimension by supplying a `decision` of `SUBSTRATE_APPROVED` or `SUBSTRATE_REJECTED`, a judgement that reads neither the colour measurement nor the press-check track. Only the single proof named in the request is affected by either verdict; sibling proofs of the same version are untouched.

The press-check dimension is a booking, not a verdict. The reviewer supplies a `press_line_code` naming the line, a half-open window bounded by `press_check_start_utc` inclusive and `press_check_end_utc` exclusive with the end strictly after the start (an inverted or degenerate window is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), and optionally an `attended_by_user_id`. Supplying `attended_by_user_id` records attendance and sets `press_check_status` to `PRESS_CHECK_ATTENDED`; omitting it leaves the track at `PRESS_CHECK_SCHEDULED`, from which a later call may still record attendance. The `attended_by_user_id` is any non-empty string and is stored verbatim, with no existence check and no requirement that it name the calling reviewer; supplied as an empty or blank string it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A later call against a track already reading `PRESS_CHECK_SCHEDULED` is a full re-booking rather than an attendance-only amendment: it overwrites `press_line_code` and both window endpoints as well, and the proof's own standing booking is excluded from the collision scan so that a re-booking never collides with itself. The booked line is a shared physical resource, so the requested window is checked for collision: widened by two hours on **both** sides, it must not overlap the equally widened window of any other proof that shares the same vendor and the same `press_line_code` and whose `press_check_status` is `PRESS_CHECK_SCHEDULED` or `PRESS_CHECK_ATTENDED`. Because each of the two windows carries the buffer, the raw endpoints of the two bookings must be separated by at least **four** hours — two hours for the incoming window and two for the one already on the line — and the comparison is half-open, so a raw gap of exactly four hours is permitted while any narrower gap, a two-hour gap included, is a genuine collision and `ERR_PRESS_CHECK_OVERLAP`. A press-check write is admissible only while `press_check_status` is `PRESS_CHECK_REQUIRED` or `PRESS_CHECK_SCHEDULED`; a track that reads `NOT_REQUIRED` or has already reached `PRESS_CHECK_ATTENDED` refuses the write with `ERR_PRESS_CHECK_NOT_REQUIRED`.

After every one of the three dimension writes, `proof_state` is re-derived from the current values of all three, and the rejecting clause is evaluated **first**. If `color_status` is `COLOR_FAIL` or `substrate_status` is `SUBSTRATE_REJECTED`, the proof is `PROOF_REJECTED` — and this holds even while another dimension is still pending, so a colour failure fixes the outcome immediately without waiting for the substrate judgement. Otherwise, if `color_status` is `COLOR_PASS` **and** `substrate_status` is `SUBSTRATE_APPROVED` **and** `press_check_status` is either `NOT_REQUIRED` or `PRESS_CHECK_ATTENDED`, the proof is `PROOF_APPROVED` and becomes usable as plate-making authorisation. In every other combination the proof remains `PROOF_IN_REVIEW`. Each dimension write refreshes `updated_at` and increments the proof's `version` by 1.

Both terminal proof states are closed to further writes. Once a proof reads `PROOF_APPROVED` or `PROOF_REJECTED`, any dimension write is refused with `ERR_PROOF_STATE_INVALID` — including writes to a dimension that was left pending when the rejection landed. And while a proof is still `PROOF_IN_REVIEW`, a second colour decision on a proof whose `color_status` is no longer `COLOR_PENDING`, or a second substrate decision on a proof whose `substrate_status` is no longer `SUBSTRATE_PENDING`, is refused with `ERR_PROOF_DIMENSION_ALREADY_DECIDED`. Proofs are listed in `proof_no` order, filterable on `artwork_version_id`, on `printer_id`, on `proof_state` and on `requires_recheck`. That last one is the service's boolean filter shape, shared with the `auto_cancelled` filter of §3.7: it accepts `true`, `false`, `1` or `0`, read case-insensitively and ignoring surrounding whitespace, and any other value — unlike the unvalidated vocabulary filters of §2.3 — is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 3.7. Plate Ordering, the Plate Budget, and Release to Print

A plate set is ordered by a `PackagingCoordinator` against an approved proof. The payload names the `artwork_version_id` the plates are cut for, the `printer_id` cutting them, the `printer_proof_id` that authorises the work, a `plate_set_count` of between 1 and 24 plates, and an optional `expedite` flag defaulting to false. The version must be in `APPROVED` or `RELEASED_TO_PRINT`, else `ERR_VERSION_STATE_INVALID`. The named proof must belong to the same version **and** the same vendor as the order itself — a proof whose own `artwork_version_id` or `printer_id` differs from the order's is `ERR_PROOF_VERSION_MISMATCH`. The vendor must still be `CERTIFIED`, else `ERR_PRINTER_ON_PROBATION`. And the named proof's `proof_state` must read `PROOF_APPROVED`, else `ERR_PROOF_NOT_APPROVED`. Those four checks are evaluated in exactly that order.

Cost is a marginal ladder in which each plate is priced at the rate of the band it lands in, not at a single blended rate. The first four plates cost 180.00 each; plates five through eight cost 155.00 each; every plate beyond the eighth costs 130.00 each. Concretely, the first band contributes the lesser of the plate count and 4, multiplied by 18000 cents; the second band contributes the lesser of the plate count and 8 reduced by 4 — floored at zero — multiplied by 15500 cents; the third band contributes the plate count reduced by 8 — floored at zero — multiplied by 13000 cents; and `plate_subtotal_cents` is the sum of the three bands. On top of that sits `setup_fee_cents`, which is 60000 cents for a set of 8 plates or fewer and 90000 cents for a set of 9 or more. When `expedite` is true, `expedite_fee_cents` is a quarter of the sum of `plate_subtotal_cents` and `setup_fee_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent — the surcharge is keyed on plates plus setup and is never re-fed into the band selection or the setup threshold — and when `expedite` is false it is 0. The order's `cost_cents` is `plate_subtotal_cents` plus `setup_fee_cents` plus `expedite_fee_cents`.

The asset's plate budget is checked last of all, after every safety and referential rule: the asset's current `committed_plate_cost_cents` plus the new order's `cost_cents` must not exceed the asset's `plate_budget_cents`, and a breach is `ERR_PLATE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. Because `committed_plate_cost_cents` sums only orders whose `status` is `ORDERED`, cancelled orders free their budget back up. A successful order is created with `status` `ORDERED`, credits the asset's `committed_plate_cost_cents` by its `cost_cents` and increments the asset's `version` by 1.

Cancelling an order is a compensating action. The coordinator supplies a mandatory `cancel_reason`; an order whose `status` is already `CANCELLED` refuses the request with `ERR_PLATE_ORDER_NOT_CANCELLABLE`. The order moves `ORDERED → CANCELLED`, stores the `cancel_reason`, stamps `cancelled_at` from the logical clock and records `auto_cancelled` as false — the flag that distinguishes an operator cancellation from one produced by the obsolescence cascade of §3.5. The asset's `committed_plate_cost_cents` is debited by exactly the order's stored `cost_cents`, and that stored `cost_cents` is itself never rewritten. Both the order's and the asset's `updated_at` are refreshed and both `version` values incremented by 1. Plate orders are listed filterable on `artwork_version_id`, on `printer_id`, on `printer_proof_id`, on `status` and on `auto_cancelled` — the last being how a client finds the orders the cascade cancelled on its behalf.

Releasing a version to print is the coordinator's final act on the artwork itself, and it carries only an optional `release_note`. Four preconditions apply, again in a fixed order. The version's `state` must be `APPROVED`, else `ERR_VERSION_STATE_INVALID`. Every entry of `pinned_claim_snapshot` must still have a `cleared_until_utc` strictly later than the current logical-clock instant, else `ERR_CLAIM_CLEARANCE_EXPIRED` — this is the version-pinning check that catches a claim whose clearance lapsed between approval and release. The version's `requires_reverification` must be false, else `ERR_REVERIFICATION_REQUIRED`, so an approval that carried any conditional decision cannot reach plates until a fresh verification run has cleared the flag. And at least one proof of the version must read `PROOF_APPROVED`, else `ERR_PROOF_NOT_APPROVED`. On success the version moves `APPROVED → RELEASED_TO_PRINT`, `released_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `updated_at` is refreshed and `version` is incremented by 1.

### 3.8. Obsolescence Management and Inventory Runout Coordination

When a new version supersedes an old one, the old artwork rarely disappears overnight — there is printed stock in a warehouse that has to be depleted, relabelled or written off. A `PackagingCoordinator` opens runout coordination by naming the `artwork_asset_id` whose stock is affected and the `obsolete_version_id` whose printed pack is being depleted; that version must stand in `OBSOLETE_PENDING`, and a runout opened against a version in any other state is `ERR_VERSION_STATE_INVALID`. The two references are resolved independently — the asset first, then the version, per §2.5 — and are never cross-checked against one another, so a runout naming an asset that does not own the obsolete version is accepted, and every later step reads the asset the runout itself names: the changeover pricing takes its `standard_unit_cost_cents` from that asset, the two conserved ledgers move on that asset, and the obsolescence completion looks at that asset's `obsolescence_state`. The coordinator also supplies `on_hand_qty`, the units of old-artwork stock in the warehouse, which must be zero or greater; `weekly_usage_qty`, the planned weekly depletion rate, which must be zero or greater and must be at least 1 whenever `on_hand_qty` is greater than zero; and `runout_start_utc`, the instant the runout window opens.

The runout is created in `PLANNED`, with an empty `depletion_records` array, a `remaining_qty` equal to `on_hand_qty`, a null `disposition`, `units_scrapped` and `units_reworked` both 0, every changeover money field at 0, and a `reversal_count` of 0. Its `projected_runout_date` is computed immediately, from the plan and only from the plan. When `on_hand_qty` is exactly 0 the projection is zero whole weeks and the projected date is the calendar date of `runout_start_utc`. Otherwise the number of whole weeks is `on_hand_qty` divided by `weekly_usage_qty`, rounded **up** to the next whole week, and the projected date is the calendar date of `runout_start_utc` advanced by seven days for each of those weeks. Dates are rendered as `YYYY-MM-DD`. This forward-looking projection is blind to actuals: it never reads `depletion_records`, and once written it is never revised for the life of the runout, however far the observed burn diverges from the plan.

An `on_hand_qty` of exactly 0 is a boundary the create request resolves on the spot. There is nothing to deplete, so the runout goes `PLANNED → RUNOUT_COMPLETE` inside that same request, with `actual_runout_date` set equal to `projected_runout_date`, `remaining_qty` 0, `units_scrapped` and `units_reworked` 0, `disposition` `RUNOUT` and all changeover money fields 0 — and the obsolescence cascade completes at once: the obsolete version moves `OBSOLETE_PENDING → OBSOLETE` and the owning asset moves `SUPERSEDE_PENDING → SUPERSEDED`.

Otherwise the coordinator reports actual weekly consumption over time by appending to `depletion_records`. The payload carries `records`, an array of at least one object and with no upper bound on its length, each holding a `week_start_utc` and the `units_consumed` in that week. Each entry must be a JSON object carrying exactly those two keys: inside a records entry — unlike the nested payload objects of §3.1, where the unknown-field rule is not applied — any other key is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, as is a `week_start_utc` that does not parse as an ISO 8601 UTC instant or a `units_consumed` that is not an integer of zero or greater. All of that entry-level validation is decided before the runout's own state is consulted. Ordering is strictly enforced so that the stored array is always already sorted: within the submitted batch the `week_start_utc` values must be strictly ascending, and every submitted `week_start_utc` must be strictly later than the greatest `week_start_utc` already stored. Any violation is `ERR_DEPLETION_RECORD_OUT_OF_ORDER`. Appending is admissible only while the runout is `PLANNED` or `MONITORING`, else `ERR_RUNOUT_STATE_INVALID`; the first append moves the runout `PLANNED → MONITORING`, and subsequent appends leave it at `MONITORING → MONITORING`. An append that drives `remaining_qty` all the way to 0 is no exception and still leaves the runout `MONITORING` with a null `disposition`: nothing but an explicit disposition ever closes a runout that was opened with stock on hand.

Every append re-derives the actual burn-down, and this second projection reads only `on_hand_qty` and the stored records — never `weekly_usage_qty`. The records are replayed in ascending `week_start_utc` order, which is the only permitted processing order. Starting from `on_hand_qty`, each record's `units_consumed` is subtracted in turn; the first time the running remainder reaches zero or goes negative, the remainder is treated as zero, `actual_runout_date` is set to the calendar date of that record's `week_start_utc` advanced by seven days, and the replay stops there. `remaining_qty` is the running remainder floored at zero. `trailing_avg_usage_qty` is null while `depletion_records` is empty; otherwise it is the arithmetic mean of the `units_consumed` of the last three records in ascending order — or of all of them when fewer than three exist — rounded half up to a whole number of units and then floored at 1, so it can never be zero. When the replay finishes with stock still remaining and at least one record exists, `actual_runout_date` is extrapolated instead of observed: the number of extra whole weeks is `remaining_qty` divided by `trailing_avg_usage_qty` rounded **up**, the anchor is the calendar date of the last stored record's `week_start_utc` advanced by seven days, and `actual_runout_date` is that anchor advanced by a further seven days for each extra week. For identical stock this yields a different date from the forward-looking projection whenever actual consumption differs from the plan rate. Each append refreshes `updated_at` and increments the runout's `version` by 1.

Disposition closes the runout, and the coordinator chooses between two forms by supplying `disposition` together with, on one of them, the unit split. Either form is admissible only while the runout is `PLANNED` or `MONITORING`, else `ERR_RUNOUT_STATE_INVALID`. A `RUNOUT` disposition asserts that the stock is fully depleted and therefore requires `remaining_qty` to be exactly 0; a residual balance is `ERR_UNIT_CONSERVATION_VIOLATION`. It sets `disposition` to `RUNOUT`, leaves `units_scrapped` and `units_reworked` at 0, and moves the runout to `RUNOUT_COMPLETE`. The two unit counts are optional on this form and, when supplied anyway, are accepted and discarded without being validated at all — even a negative or non-integer value passes unremarked on a `RUNOUT`, and the stored counters stay 0. A `SCRAP` disposition writes off what is left and therefore requires both `units_scrapped` and `units_reworked` in the payload — omitting either is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and requires their sum to equal `remaining_qty` **exactly**, neither more nor less; any other sum is `ERR_UNIT_CONSERVATION_VIOLATION`. For a runout still in `PLANNED`, where no consumption has been reported, `remaining_qty` is by construction still `on_hand_qty`, so a scrap straight out of `PLANNED` must account for the full opening stock. A `SCRAP` moves the runout to `SCRAPPED`.

A scrap disposition prices the changeover. `material_writeoff_cents` is `units_scrapped` multiplied by the asset's `standard_unit_cost_cents`. `relabel_cost_cents` is `units_reworked` multiplied by 18 cents per unit, relabelling being far cheaper than destruction. `changeover_base_cents` is the sum of those two lines, and because the unit split is required to partition `remaining_qty` exactly, the two lines cover the residual stock once and only once. An expedite surcharge is then keyed on `changeover_base_cents` — the **pre**-surcharge figure, never on the total, which would make the formula circular. A base below 500000 cents attracts 5 percent; a base of at least 500000 but below 2000000 cents attracts 8 percent; a base of 2000000 cents or more attracts 12 percent. The tier ceilings are exclusive upper bounds, so a base of exactly 500000 cents falls in the middle tier. `changeover_expedite_cents` is the base multiplied by the tier percentage, rounded half up to a whole cent, and `total_changeover_cost_cents` is `changeover_base_cents` plus `changeover_expedite_cents`. The asset's `obsolete_units_total` is then credited by `units_scrapped` and its `changeover_cost_committed_cents` by `total_changeover_cost_cents`.

Both dispositions complete the obsolescence chain identically: the obsolete version moves `OBSOLETE_PENDING → OBSOLETE` with `obsoleted_at` stamped from the logical clock, and the owning asset moves `SUPERSEDE_PENDING → SUPERSEDED`. Each of those two steps is conditional on the row still standing in the state it is leaving: a version that already reads `OBSOLETE`, or an asset that no longer reads `SUPERSEDE_PENDING` because an earlier runout of the same asset already completed the chain, is left exactly as it stands. The runout's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, and the asset's `version` is incremented by 1 whenever this request wrote to the asset at all — a `SCRAP` always writes to it because the two ledgers move, a `RUNOUT` only through the completion step above — and never by 2 even when both apply.

A scrap decision is not irrevocable. A `PlatformAdmin` may reverse it, supplying a `reversal_reason`; the reversal is admissible only on a runout whose state is `SCRAPPED`, else `ERR_RUNOUT_STATE_INVALID`. It is a pure compensating action: the asset's `obsolete_units_total` is debited by exactly the `units_scrapped` being reversed and its `changeover_cost_committed_cents` by exactly the `total_changeover_cost_cents` that disposition credited. The runout's `disposition` returns to null, `units_scrapped` and `units_reworked` return to 0, `material_writeoff_cents`, `relabel_cost_cents`, `changeover_base_cents`, `changeover_expedite_cents` and `total_changeover_cost_cents` all return to 0, and `reversal_count` is incremented by 1. The runout moves `SCRAPPED → MONITORING`. Because `on_hand_qty` and `depletion_records` are never rewritten, the identity that on-hand stock equals `remaining_qty` plus the sum of all reported `units_consumed` holds identically before and after. The reversal also unwinds the obsolescence cascade by one step: the obsolete version moves `OBSOLETE → OBSOLETE_PENDING` with `obsoleted_at` cleared back to null, and the asset moves `SUPERSEDED → SUPERSEDE_PENDING` — each step again conditional on the row still standing in the state it is leaving, so a version already back at `OBSOLETE_PENDING` or an asset already at `SUPERSEDE_PENDING` is left as it stands. The runout and the asset each have `updated_at` refreshed and `version` incremented by 1 unconditionally — the asset because both its ledgers are debited whatever its `obsolescence_state` reads, so the increment lands even when the unwind step above found nothing to move — while the obsolete version is lifted only when that conditional actually moved it, a version already standing in `OBSOLETE_PENDING` keeping its `updated_at` and its `version` exactly as they were. The reversal also leaves `remaining_qty`, `actual_runout_date`, `trailing_avg_usage_qty` and `projected_runout_date` exactly as the last append left them; not one of the four is recomputed or cleared. Runouts are listed filterable on `artwork_asset_id`, on `obsolete_version_id`, on `state` and on `disposition`.

### 3.9. Error Escape Investigation, Cost Recovery and Vendor Standing

However good the review, defects occasionally reach the field, and when they do the question is who pays. A `QualityReviewer` opens an escape investigation naming the `artwork_version_id` whose printed pack carried the defect and the `printer_id` of the vendor that printed it — the two references are resolved independently, the version first and then the printer, and are never cross-checked, so the named vendor need hold no proof and no plate order for that version — together with a classified `defect_code`, the `discovered_stage` at which the escape was caught, the `units_affected` — at least 1 — and an optional `discovery_note`. Only a version that actually reached print may carry an escape: the version must stand in `RELEASED_TO_PRINT`, `OBSOLETE_PENDING` or `OBSOLETE`, and anything earlier is `ERR_VERSION_STATE_INVALID`.

The escape opens in `OPEN`, and its `investigation_fee_cents` is fixed at creation by where the defect was caught: 1500.00 when `discovered_stage` is `DISTRIBUTION` or `MARKET`, because a late catch means field investigation and retrieval, and 600.00 when it is `PRESS` or `PACKING`. In cents those figures are 150000 and 60000 respectively. Creating an escape also recomputes the vendor's `escape_count` and increments the vendor's `version` by 1. Escapes are listed filterable on `artwork_version_id`, on `printer_id`, on `state` and on `root_cause_party`.

Apportionment is the next step. The same `QualityReviewer` records a `root_cause_party`, a `printer_share_pct` giving the fraction of each cost line recoverable from the vendor to two decimal places, and a mandatory `investigation_note` capturing the finding. The share must itself be a number between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive; a value outside that range is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and is decided ahead of any consistency check against the party. It is compared numerically, so `1`, `1.0` and `1.00` are the same share, and it is echoed back as the JSON number it was supplied as rather than re-rendered to two places — a share sent as `1.00` reads `1.0` and one sent as `0.5` reads `0.5`. Share and party must be consistent, and an inconsistency is `ERR_SHARE_INCONSISTENT_WITH_PARTY`: a `PRINTER` root cause requires a share of exactly 1.00, a `BRAND` or `AGENCY` root cause requires exactly 0.00, and a `SHARED` root cause requires a share strictly greater than 0.00 and strictly less than 1.00 — the endpoints are excluded on `SHARED` precisely because they belong to the unambiguous parties. Apportionment is admissible from `OPEN`, moving the escape `OPEN → ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED`, and also from `DISPUTED`, moving it `DISPUTED → ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED` and overwriting the earlier `root_cause_party`, `printer_share_pct` and `investigation_note`; on that revising path `provisional_recovery_cents` is cleared back to null and `liability_cap_applied` back to false, because the prior claim basis has been withdrawn. The dispute history is not withdrawn with it: `dispute_reason` and `dispute_count` survive a revising apportionment entirely unchanged. From any other state the request is `ERR_ESCAPE_STATE_INVALID`.

The recovery claim quantifies the provisional exposure and is the second write in the service guarded by optimistic concurrency: the payload carries `expected_error_escape_version`, and a value that does not equal the escape's current `version` is `ERR_STALE_ESCAPE_VERSION`. The field must itself be an integer of zero or greater, and a negative or non-integer value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` decided ahead of the staleness comparison. The escape must stand in `ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED`, else `ERR_ESCAPE_STATE_INVALID`. Four cost lines are evaluated in a fixed order — reprint, then scrap, then expedited freight, then investigation — and, because no vendor invoice exists yet, the first two are estimated: the reprint line is `units_affected` multiplied by the asset's `standard_unit_cost_cents`, the scrap line is `units_affected` multiplied by 7 cents of handling per unit, the freight line is 0 because no freight estimate exists before settlement, and the investigation line is the escape's `investigation_fee_cents`. Each of the four lines is multiplied by `printer_share_pct` and rounded half up to a whole cent **individually, in that order**, and only then are the four rounded figures summed; summing the raw lines and rounding once at the end is a different and incorrect result. The gross recoverable sum is then clamped to the vendor's `recovery_liability_cap_cents`: `provisional_recovery_cents` is the lesser of the gross sum and the cap, and `liability_cap_applied` is set true exactly when the gross sum exceeded the cap. The escape moves `ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED → RECOVERY_CLAIMED`.

A vendor that disagrees pushes back. The `PrinterRep` whose `X-User-ID` equals the escape's `printer_id` — anyone else being refused with `ERR_PRINTER_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` — supplies a non-empty `dispute_reason`, which is stored, and `dispute_count` is incremented by 1. The escape moves `RECOVERY_CLAIMED → DISPUTED`, from where a revised apportionment re-opens the cycle `ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED → RECOVERY_CLAIMED → DISPUTED → ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED`. A dispute from any other state is `ERR_ESCAPE_STATE_INVALID`.

Settlement is the `PlatformAdmin`'s decision and takes a `resolution` of `SETTLED` or `WRITTEN_OFF` plus an optional `settlement_note`. A `SETTLED` resolution is admissible from `RECOVERY_CLAIMED` and requires all three actual invoice amounts — `actual_reprint_cost_cents`, `actual_scrap_cost_cents` and `actual_expedite_freight_cents` — to be present and zero or greater, any of them missing or negative being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The final recovery re-runs the same four-line arithmetic over the same fixed line order, but now with real figures: the reprint line is `actual_reprint_cost_cents`, the scrap line is `actual_scrap_cost_cents`, the freight line is `actual_expedite_freight_cents` — a real line here, where the provisional variant always carried 0 — and the investigation line is again `investigation_fee_cents`. Each line is multiplied by `printer_share_pct` and rounded half up to a whole cent individually, in that order, before the four are summed. At settlement the liability cap is **not** silently applied: if the pre-clamp sum exceeds the vendor's `recovery_liability_cap_cents` the settlement is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_LIABILITY_CAP`, so that the apportionment can instead be revised through the dispute cycle. When the sum is within the cap, `final_recovery_cents` is that unclamped sum and `recovery_variance_cents` is `final_recovery_cents` minus `provisional_recovery_cents`. The escape stores its three actuals, sets `resolution` to `SETTLED`, stamps `settled_at` from the logical clock and moves `RECOVERY_CLAIMED → SETTLED`.

Settlement is also where vendor standing is adjusted, and this is a four-link cascade. First, the vendor's `recovery_balance_cents` is credited by `final_recovery_cents`. Second, `performance_penalty_points` — a transient intermediate of the settlement arithmetic, persisted on neither the escape nor the vendor and absent from every response body — is computed and subtracted from the vendor's `performance_score`: the raw penalty is 2.0 points for every thousand units affected — `units_affected` divided by 1000, multiplied by 2.0, without any intermediate rounding — plus a stage penalty of 2.0 points when `discovered_stage` is `PRESS`, 4.0 when it is `PACKING`, 8.0 when it is `DISTRIBUTION` and 10.0 when it is `MARKET`; that raw figure is rounded half up to one decimal place and then capped at 40.0 points. The vendor's new `performance_score` is its current score minus that penalty, rounded half up to one decimal place and floored at 0.0. Third, if the new score is **strictly** below 70.0 the vendor moves `CERTIFIED → PROBATION`; a score of exactly 70.0 stays `CERTIFIED`. Fourth, that probation transition sets `requires_recheck` to true on every proof of that vendor whose `proof_state` is not `PROOF_APPROVED` — already-approved proofs are left alone. A vendor on `PROBATION` can accept neither new proof submissions nor new plate orders, both being refused with `ERR_PRINTER_ON_PROBATION`.

A `WRITTEN_OFF` resolution abandons the claim and is admissible from `RECOVERY_CLAIMED` and also from `DISPUTED`, which is how a contested claim is dropped. It sets `final_recovery_cents` to 0 and `recovery_variance_cents` to zero minus `provisional_recovery_cents` — which is 0 when no claim was ever raised and the provisional figure is null — sets `resolution` to `WRITTEN_OFF`, stamps `settled_at` from the logical clock, and moves the escape to `WRITTEN_OFF`. The vendor's `recovery_balance_cents` and `performance_score` are both left entirely unchanged, and no scorecard penalty is computed. The three actual invoice amounts are not required on this form and, when supplied anyway, are accepted and discarded without being validated at all, so `actual_reprint_cost_cents`, `actual_scrap_cost_cents` and `actual_expedite_freight_cents` all still read null on a written-off escape. Any settlement attempt from a state other than `RECOVERY_CLAIMED` or `DISPUTED` is `ERR_ESCAPE_STATE_INVALID`. Every settlement refreshes the escape's `updated_at` and increments its `version` by 1, and a `SETTLED` outcome additionally increments the vendor's `version` and the `version` of each proof the probation cascade touched.
