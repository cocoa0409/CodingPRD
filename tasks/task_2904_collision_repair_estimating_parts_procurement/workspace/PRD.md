# Collision Repair Estimating API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A collision repair facility earns or loses its margin in the gap between what it wrote on the estimate and what it actually spent on the car. That gap opens because the work is discovered rather than known: the vehicle arrives with visible damage, the body is torn down, and hidden damage surfaces days later — after the estimate has been committed, after parts have been ordered, and sometimes after the panel has already been painted. Every one of those discoveries has to be re-priced, re-authorized by the insurer, re-consented to by the vehicle owner, and re-sequenced through a shop whose stalls and calibration bays are already booked. The Collision Repair Estimating API is the system of record for that whole negotiation.

This document specifies an API that governs the complete arc of a collision repair: damage analysis with teardown and blueprinting, estimate construction from labor time guides with refinish overlap arithmetic and not-included operations, parts type selection across original-equipment, aftermarket, recycled and reconditioned channels under insurer policy and written customer consent, supplier price negotiation, ordering, receipt and the return of unused or incorrect parts, repair procedure research and adherence documentation including driver-assistance calibration, stall and bay scheduling with cycle-time accounting, insurer supplement approval against the customer-pay portion, and finally post-repair quality control, scan verification, warranty issue and vehicle release.

Three design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **money is never guessed**: every figure the customer or the insurer sees is derived by a pinned calculation from persisted inputs, in a fixed evaluation order, at a stated precision. Second, **nobody ships an uncalibrated car**: safety gates outrank fiscal gates, so an order that is simultaneously over budget and short of a passed calibration is told about the calibration. Third, **the estimator who wrote the blueprint may not be the inspector who signs the repair off** — segregation of duties is enforced on the acting user identity, not on a checkbox.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies across the whole API. All endpoints are mounted at the API root; there is no version prefix and no per-tenant path segment.

### 2.1. Shop Operators, Outside Parties and Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent, empty, or blank, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Seven roles exist, and their spelling is exact: `ShopAdmin`, `Estimator`, `PartsManager`, `Technician`, `QcInspector`, `InsurerAdjuster`, and `Customer`. A request whose `X-User-Role` is not one of those seven, or is one of those seven but is not on the acting endpoint's authorized list, is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

Five of the roles are shop staff and their identity is opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `ShopAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `PartsManager`, for `Technician`, and for `QcInspector`. When `X-User-Role` is `Estimator`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier, but the server does record it: the estimator who publishes a blueprint has their `X-User-ID` written verbatim into that order's `blueprint_by_user_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `QcInspector`, the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded on the inspection as `inspector_user_id`, and it is compared — not to an entity `id`, but to the stored `blueprint_by_user_id` of the order being inspected. If the two are equal, the inspection is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Holding the `QcInspector` role does not rescue the person who blueprinted the order; the check is on the identifier, not on the role.

Two roles are outside parties and are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `InsurerAdjuster`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Insurer`; ownership checks on any repair order compare that order's `insurer_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer`; ownership checks compare the order's `customer_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is likewise refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This applies to reads as well as writes: an adjuster may read only the orders their own program is funding, and a vehicle owner may read only their own order.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0; it may not be negative. Both must be integers. A `limit` below 1, a `limit` above 100, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource in the system carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recording when the row was first written.

Most resources are mutable and therefore also carry `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every change to the row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of that row. Recomputing a derived field counts as a mutation: when the eight-step totals rewrite a repair order's money fields, or when a stall's booked minutes are rolled up, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments. Three record types are immutable after creation and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version` at all: the vehicle-owner registry row, the teardown damage finding, and the parts delay event. The delay event is closed by the server rather than by a client update, and closing it does not introduce a `version` field.

Timestamps that the server stamps are read from a **logical clock**, never from wall-clock time. Before the clock has been set for the first time it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. An admin-only test-driving capability moves the clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and answers with the resulting `current_utc`; instants already stamped onto existing rows are left untouched. The logical clock is what stamps `checkin_at`, `submitted_at`, `decided_at`, `received_at`, `opened_at`, `closed_at`, `delivered_at` and `warranty_expires_at`, and it is what decides whether a promised parts arrival has been breached and how old the vehicle is.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and the Validation Ladder

When a path carries an identifier, that identifier is resolved before anything in the request body is examined, and nested paths are resolved strictly left to right. A request naming a parts line under a non-existent order fails on the order first. The relevant not-found error is the one belonging to the leftmost identifier that does not resolve.

More generally, every request is evaluated against an ordered ladder of checks, and **the first violated rule decides the response**. No side effect of any kind is applied when a check fails. A request that breaks several rules at once returns the error belonging to the earliest rung, which is why the ladder below is normative rather than descriptive:

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the seven declared roles and appears on the endpoint's authorized list — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. For the two entity-backed roles, the actor owns the addressed order: an `InsurerAdjuster`'s `X-User-ID` equals the order's `insurer_id`, and a `Customer`'s `X-User-ID` equals the order's `customer_id` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This rung applies to every endpoint either of those two roles can reach.
4. On filing a post-repair inspection only: the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the order's `blueprint_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
5. Request shape: every required input field is present, non-null and of the declared JSON type, and unknown fields are rejected — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Applies to every creating, acting and patching request.
6. Every enum-typed input holds one of that enum's declared members — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`. This also covers enum-valued filter parameters on list endpoints.
7. Pagination bounds as stated in §2.3 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
8. On booking a stall and on scheduling a calibration: `window_end` is strictly after `window_start` and both parse as ISO-8601 instants — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`.
9. Numeric bounds on the field itself — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Specifically: `guide_hours` is greater than zero and carries at most one decimal place; `qty` is at least 1; `list_price_cents` is greater than zero; `requested_amount_cents` is greater than zero; `post_scan_dtc_count` is zero or greater; `discount_pct` is at least zero and strictly below one; `parts_price_ceiling_pct` is at least 1.0; and `rate_ceiling_cents` carries an entry for all five labor types.
10. Existence of everything the request names, in this order: the order identifier in the path (`ERR_REPAIR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`), then `customer_id` (`ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`), then `insurer_id` (`ERR_INSURER_NOT_FOUND`), then the labor-line identifier in the path or an `estimate_line_id` filter (`ERR_ESTIMATE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`), then `damage_line_id` (`ERR_DAMAGE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`), then a supplement identifier in the path or a `supplement_id` reference (`ERR_SUPPLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`), then `vendor_id` (`ERR_PARTS_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`), then the parts-line identifier in the path (`ERR_PARTS_LINE_NOT_FOUND`), then the procedure-record identifier in the path (`ERR_PROCEDURE_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`), then `bay_id` (`ERR_CALIBRATION_BAY_NOT_FOUND`), then `stall_id` (`ERR_STALL_NOT_FOUND`), then the stall-booking identifier in the path (`ERR_STAGE_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`). A `damage_line_id` that names a finding belonging to a different order, and a `supplement_id` that names a supplement belonging to a different order, are treated as not found.
11. Optimistic concurrency on the two guarded writes — see §2.7 — otherwise `ERR_STALE_REPAIR_ORDER_VERSION`. This rung sits above every state precondition on purpose: a caller working from a superseded read is told to re-read rather than handed a domain error computed against data it never saw.
12. The order is neither `DELIVERED` nor `TOTAL_LOSS` — otherwise `ERR_ORDER_TERMINAL`. This applies to every mutation that touches a repair order or any of its children.
13. State preconditions in this order: the order's `stage` lies in the operation's declared from-state set (`ERR_STAGE_PRECONDITION`); then, for a stage advance, the requested `to_stage` is the declared successor (`ERR_STAGE_SEQUENCE_INVALID`); then the parts line's `procurement_status` lies in the operation's declared from-state set (`ERR_PARTS_LINE_STATE`); then the order's `authorization_posture` is `OPEN` (`ERR_AUTHORIZATION_POSTURE_BLOCKED`); then the supplement is open for the requested action (`ERR_SUPPLEMENT_NOT_OPEN`); then the procedure record's `calibration_status` permits the requested action (`ERR_CALIBRATION_STATE`); then the stall booking is still `SCHEDULED` (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`).
14. Safety and compliance, ranked above every constraint and every fiscal check: calibration verified (`ERR_CALIBRATION_NOT_VERIFIED`), then procedure adherence documented (`ERR_PROCEDURE_ADHERENCE_MISSING`), then ADAS procedure research present at blueprint (`ERR_ADAS_PROCEDURE_RESEARCH_MISSING`), then not-included justification present at estimate lock (`ERR_NOT_INCLUDED_JUSTIFICATION_MISSING`). An ADAS system that has not been proven to see the road is a safety defect, and the answer must name that defect even when the same request is also short of parts or over budget.
15. Referential policy — is this counterparty allowed to play at all: supplier not suspended (`ERR_VENDOR_SUSPENDED`), then the supplier offers the chosen part type (`ERR_PART_TYPE_NOT_OFFERED`), then the insurer program permits the chosen part type (`ERR_PART_TYPE_NOT_ALLOWED_BY_INSURER`), then the stall type fits the stage (`ERR_STALL_TYPE_MISMATCH`), then the bay supports the calibration modality (`ERR_BAY_TYPE_UNSUPPORTED`), then the stall is available (`ERR_STALL_UNAVAILABLE`). Supplier suspension is ranked above the supplier's own quantity cap because a suspended supplier is not a counterparty at all — its cap is irrelevant.
16. Constraint satisfaction, in this order: supplier quantity cap (`ERR_VENDOR_QTY_CAP_EXCEEDED`), stall window conflict (`ERR_STALL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`), calibration bay capacity (`ERR_CALIBRATION_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), supplement round limit (`ERR_SUPPLEMENT_ROUND_LIMIT`), at least one teardown finding (`ERR_NO_TEARDOWN_FINDINGS`), at least one draft labor line (`ERR_NO_ESTIMATE_LINES`), parts procured (`ERR_PARTS_NOT_PROCURED`), parts installed or returned (`ERR_PARTS_NOT_INSTALLED`), a scheduled stall booking for the stage being entered (`ERR_NO_STAGE_ASSIGNMENT`), return quantity in range (`ERR_INVALID_RETURN_QUANTITY`), receive and install quantities in range (`ERR_INVALID_RECEIVE_QUANTITY`), counter strictly lower (`ERR_COUNTER_NOT_LOWER`), revised quote from the same supplier (`ERR_VENDOR_MISMATCH`), parts line authorized for money (`ERR_CUSTOMER_CONSENT_REQUIRED`), original-equipment upgrade actually applicable (`ERR_OEM_UPGRADE_NOT_APPLICABLE`), latest inspection passed (`ERR_QC_NOT_PASSED`), no open parts delay (`ERR_OPEN_PARTS_DELAY`), partial amount strictly inside the request (`ERR_INVALID_PARTIAL_AMOUNT`), and a supplement behind any post-lock labor line (`ERR_SUPPLEMENT_REQUIRED`).
17. Fiscal checks run last: the insurer's per-unit parts price ceiling (`ERR_PARTS_PRICE_CEILING_EXCEEDED`), then the supplier's rolling fulfillment score floor (`ERR_VENDOR_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`), then the customer-exposure ceiling (`ERR_AUTHORIZED_AMOUNT_EXCEEDED`). The exposure ceiling is deliberately the very last rung: a request that is both frozen by an unsettled money picture and over budget reports the freeze, and a request that is both from a suspended supplier and over budget reports the suspension.

### 2.6. Money, Hours and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer number of cents, and every field name that carries money ends in `_cents`. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a stored value or in a response body. Wherever a monetary result is produced from a multiplication or a percentage, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent at the point the calculation states — never earlier, never later, and never twice.

The single most consequential instance of that discipline is parts pricing. A supplier discount lands on **one unit**: the agreed unit price is multiplied by one minus the supplier's fractional discount, that product is rounded half up to a whole cent, and only then is the rounded per-unit figure multiplied by the quantity. Rounding after the multiplication produces a different answer, and that difference propagates into the parts gross, the volume-rebate tier and ultimately the customer's share. The same rule reads in reverse for restocking: the restocking fee is computed once, at return time, from the already-rounded per-unit figure, and is thereafter treated as a fixed literal rather than recomputed.

Labor time is carried to **one decimal place**, rounded half up. Refinish allocation rounds each line to one decimal place first and only then sums the already-rounded values; summing raw hours and rounding the total afterwards is wrong and will disagree at the tenth. Durations — stall occupancy, parts delay, cycle time — are whole minutes obtained by flooring, and where several intervals are involved the flooring happens after the intervals have been combined, never on the pieces before addition.

Supplier fulfillment scores are numbers rounded to zero decimal places, half up, and therefore read as whole values between 0 and 100. The component ratios inside a score are exact rationals and are never rounded individually.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Repair Order

Two writes are guarded by optimistic concurrency, and both are guarded against the same parent: the repair order.

Raising a supplement requires the request to carry `expected_repair_order_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the repair order. It is not optional and there is no default. If it does not equal the order's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REPAIR_ORDER_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A shop may legitimately raise several supplements against one order, so a second supplement is not blocked by the existence of the first; what is blocked is a supplement whose `requested_amount_cents` was computed from a superseded money picture. If approved lines, parts prices or insurer authorization have moved since the estimator's read, the requested amount rests on a basis the order no longer holds, and the estimator must re-read before asking again.

Recording a consent decision requires the same field, `expected_repair_order_version`, again non-optional, and again rejected on mismatch with `ERR_STALE_REPAIR_ORDER_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A vehicle owner may record consent repeatedly and a later decision legitimately supersedes an earlier one, so an existing consent does not block a new one. What must never happen is a customer consenting to a `customer_pay_cents` figure the order no longer holds.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, the parts-line negotiation writes, the stage advance, the stall bookings, the calibration writes, the inspection and the delivery carry no expected-version input and are never refused for staleness.

### 2.8. The Two Parallel Dimensions of a Repair Order, and of a Parts Line

A repair order carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and neither constrains the other's value.

The first is `stage`, the repair workflow. It begins at `INTAKE` and moves through `TEARDOWN`, `BLUEPRINTED`, `ESTIMATE_LOCKED`, `PARTS_STAGED`, `REPAIR`, `REFINISH_CALIBRATION`, `READY_FOR_QC` and, when an inspection fails, `QC_FAILED`. Two states are terminal: `DELIVERED` and `TOTAL_LOSS`. The dimension is deliberately non-linear on three counts — a rework cycle back from `QC_FAILED` to `REPAIR`, a divergent branch out of `BLUEPRINTED` to either `ESTIMATE_LOCKED` or `TOTAL_LOSS` whose side-effect sets are disjoint, and an involuntary rollback from `REFINISH_CALIBRATION` or `READY_FOR_QC` back to `REPAIR` when a staged part goes back to the supplier.

The second is `authorization_posture`, the money-and-consent gate. It begins at `OPEN` and takes the values `OPEN`, `SUPPLEMENT_PENDING` and `CONSENT_REQUIRED`. It has no terminal state; it oscillates for the life of the order. No client ever writes it directly: it is recomputed by the server as the final step of every mutation that can move the customer's share, a supplement's status, or a parts line's money authorization. Its precedence is pinned — money first, paperwork second — so `CONSENT_REQUIRED` outranks `SUPPLEMENT_PENDING`, which outranks `OPEN`. While the posture is anything other than `OPEN` the order is frozen: no stage may advance, no part may be ordered, and the vehicle may not be released. Each of those three refusals carries `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_POSTURE_BLOCKED`.

A parts line mirrors that structure with two dimensions of its own. `procurement_status` runs `PLANNED`, `QUOTED`, `COUNTERED`, `PRICE_LOCKED`, `ORDERED`, `RECEIVED`, `INSTALLED`, and terminates at `RETURNED` or `VOID`; it is cyclic between `QUOTED` and `COUNTERED` for as long as the price negotiation runs. `authorization_status` runs `INSURER_ALLOWED`, `PENDING_CONSENT`, `CUSTOMER_CONSENTED` and the terminal `CUSTOMER_DECLINED`; it is decided at creation from insurer policy, vehicle age and the owner's standing preference, and is thereafter moved only by the owner's consent decision. A line can be `PRICE_LOCKED` and `PENDING_CONSENT` at the same time — the price is agreed, the money is not.

### 2.9. Cascades, Derived Fields and Conserved Quantities

Several operations in this API mutate more than one row, and in some cases the second mutation is caused by the first rather than requested by the client. Those chains are specified explicitly in §3, hop by hop, because the intermediate hop is what a naive implementation drops. The general rule is that a server-initiated hop is a real mutation of the row it touches: it refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments that row's `version`, exactly as a client-initiated write would.

Three derived quantities must reconcile after **every** operation that can disturb them, not merely after the operation that created them.

A stall's `booked_minutes` always equals the sum of `occupied_minutes` over the bookings on that stall whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`; bookings that are `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing. Because a booking's `occupied_minutes` is fixed at creation, and because every exit path releases exactly that figure, booking a stall and then cancelling the booking returns the stall to precisely the value it held before, with no drift however many cascades ran in between. Promoting a booking from `SCHEDULED` to `ACTIVE` leaves `booked_minutes` untouched, since both statuses occupy the stall. A stall's `cleanup_buffer_minutes` is deliberately **not** part of `booked_minutes`: the buffer widens the conflict window checked when a new booking is requested, and nothing else.

An order's parts commitment must satisfy a conservation identity at all times: `parts_ordered_gross_cents` equals `parts_committed_cents` plus `parts_return_credit_cents` plus `parts_restocking_loss_cents`. The gross figure is append-only — it rises at every successful order and is never decremented — so a restocking fee shows up as a loss bucket rather than as a leak in the identity.

Three counters on the order are pure roll-ups and are recomputed rather than incremented: `teardown_finding_count` is the number of teardown findings on the order, `open_parts_delay_count` is the number of parts delay events on the order whose `closed_at` is still null, and `qc_inspection_count` is the number of inspections filed on the order. A bay's `scheduled_window_count` is the same kind of roll-up: the number of procedure records booked into that bay whose `calibration_status` is `SCHEDULED`.

### 2.10. Insurer Policy, Shop Posted Rates and the Consent Threshold

An insurer program is a configuration record, and it is the gate through which every commercial decision on the order passes. Its `insurer_code` is a short program code of 3 to 16 characters, unique across programs, and it carries a display `name`. Three boolean policy flags decide which sourcing channels are open at all: `aftermarket_allowed` and `recycled_allowed` both default to true, while `reconditioned_allowed` defaults to false. `alternate_parts_min_vehicle_age_years` — an integer of zero or more, defaulting to 3 — is the vehicle age in whole years at which a policy-permitted alternate part becomes insurer-allowed without asking the owner; the vehicle's age is the logical clock's calendar year minus the order's `vehicle_model_year`. `parts_price_ceiling_pct` is the largest multiple of a part's list price the program will pay per unit; it defaults to 1.1 and may not be below 1.0. `supplement_round_limit` caps how many negotiation rounds a supplement may reach and defaults to 3, with a floor of 1. Finally `rate_ceiling_cents` is an object of per-hour ceilings in cents, keyed by labor type, and **every one of the five keys is required**: `BODY`, `FRAME`, `REFINISH`, `MECHANICAL` and `ALUMINUM_BODY`.

Against those ceilings stands the shop's own posted hourly rate card, which is a system constant rather than configuration: body work is posted at 6,500 cents per hour, frame work at 8,500, refinish at 6,000, mechanical at 12,500, and aluminium body work at 9,500. Whenever labor is priced, the rate actually applied to a line is the **lesser** of the shop's posted rate for that line's labor type and the insurer program's ceiling for the same labor type.

One further constant governs when the vehicle owner has to be consulted at all: written consent becomes mandatory as soon as the customer's share reaches **500.00 dollars — that is, 50,000 cents**. The comparison is inclusive: a share of exactly 50,000 cents already requires consent, and 49,999 does not.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Registries: Owners, Programs, Suppliers and Shop Capacity

Before a single vehicle is written up, a `ShopAdmin` populates the registries the rest of the system references. A vehicle owner is registered with their legal `full_name`, a contact `phone` and a contact `email`. One further field on the owner reaches deep into the parts workflow: `preferred_alternate_parts_ok`, a boolean defaulting to false, is the owner's *standing written preference* authorising non-original parts. When it is true, a parts line that would otherwise have to wait for a signature is created already consented; when it is false, that same line waits. The owner record is immutable once written — there is no update capability for it, and consequently no `version` on it.

Insurer programs are registered by a `ShopAdmin` with the policy fields and rate ceilings enumerated in §2.10. Both the owner registry and the program registry are readable by `ShopAdmin` and `Estimator`, since those are the two roles that have to reason about who pays for what.

Parts suppliers are registered by either a `ShopAdmin` or a `PartsManager`. A supplier carries a `name` and a `vendor_type` describing its channel — `OEM_DEALER`, `AFTERMARKET_DISTRIBUTOR`, `RECYCLER` or `RECONDITIONER` — together with `part_types_offered`, a non-empty array of unique members drawn from `OEM`, `AFTERMARKET`, `RECYCLED` and `RECONDITIONED`. The supplier's `status` is an input rather than a derived value, so a supplier can be created directly as `SUSPENDED` as well as `ACTIVE`; the default is `ACTIVE`. Quotes may be recorded against a suspended supplier — negotiating with a supplier you are not currently buying from is normal commercial practice — but an order may never be placed with one. Commercial terms follow: `discount_pct` is the fractional discount off the quoted unit price, at least zero and strictly below one, defaulting to zero; `core_charge_cents` is a per-unit core charge of zero or more, defaulting to zero, that exists for reconditioned cores; `restocking_fee_pct` is the fractional fee the supplier levies on returned units, between zero and one inclusive, defaulting to 0.2; and `max_qty_per_line` is the largest quantity the supplier will accept on a single line order, at least 1, defaulting to 4.

Two system-maintained values sit alongside those terms. `order_seq_counter` starts at zero and is a monotonic per-supplier counter that increments on every successful order; it exists so that the supplier's performance window can be ordered deterministically without appealing to clocks. `fulfillment_score_rolling` and `fulfillment_score_lifetime` both start at 50 and are the two variants of the supplier's performance score described in §3.6. A supplier read returns both variants side by side precisely so that their divergence is visible.

Production capacity is registered in two forms. A stall carries a `name`, a `stall_type` of `BODY`, `FRAME` or `PAINT`, a `cleanup_buffer_minutes` of zero or more defaulting to 30, and a `status` of `AVAILABLE` or `MAINTENANCE` — again an input, so a stall under maintenance can be created directly, with `AVAILABLE` the default. Its `booked_minutes` is derived and never supplied. A calibration bay carries a `name`, a non-empty array of unique `supported_types` drawn from `STATIC`, `DYNAMIC` and `BOTH`, and a `concurrent_capacity` of at least 1 defaulting to 2. That capacity is an **aggregate** limit — several static targets can genuinely be set up in one bay at once — so it is not a per-window flag but a count over simultaneous bookings. The bay's `scheduled_window_count` is derived. Stall and bay reads are open to `ShopAdmin` and `Technician`; the derived fields are the point of those reads.

### 3.2. Intake, Teardown and the Blueprint

A `ShopAdmin` opens the repair order, supplying the `customer_id` of the vehicle owner, the `insurer_id` of the paying program, the insurer's `claim_number`, the 17-character `vehicle_vin`, the `vehicle_model_year` (which must lie between 1980 and 2100 inclusive), the `vehicle_make_model` text, and `initial_authorized_cents` — the authorisation the insurer granted at first notice of loss, which must be zero or greater. One optional field completes the picture: `deductible_cents`, defaulting to 50,000 cents, is the owner's deductible and is always part of the owner's share, whatever else happens. On creation the order's `checkin_at` is stamped from the logical clock, opening the cycle-time window; `stage` is `INTAKE`, `authorization_posture` is `OPEN`, `consent_state` is `NONE`, and `version` is 1. Orders can afterwards be listed and filtered by `customer_id`, by `insurer_id` and by `stage`, which is how the shop reverses the lookup from an owner or a program back to the work in the building.

Teardown is where the real damage is found. An `Estimator` records one finding at a time, each carrying a `panel_code` such as `L_FRONT_DOOR`, a `severity` of `LIGHT`, `MODERATE` or `SEVERE`, and three booleans that all default to false: `requires_blend`, meaning an adjacent panel will need a refinish blend; `adas_component_touched`, meaning the finding disturbs a driver-assistance sensor or its bracket; and `structural`, meaning the finding is on a structural member. Every finding is stamped with `discovered_at_stage`, the order's `stage` at the instant the finding was recorded, which may be any of `TEARDOWN`, `BLUEPRINTED`, `ESTIMATE_LOCKED`, `PARTS_STAGED`, `REPAIR`, `REFINISH_CALIBRATION`, `READY_FOR_QC` or `QC_FAILED`. That stamp is what later distinguishes hidden damage from damage that was on the sheet from the beginning. Each finding also belongs to its order through the `repair_order_id` taken from the path, and the findings of an order can be listed in creation order.

The **first** finding on an order moves `stage` from `INTAKE` to `TEARDOWN`, recomputes `teardown_finding_count`, and increments the order's `version`. Every subsequent finding leaves `stage` where it is and only raises the count — the transition fires once, on the first finding, not on each one.

Publishing the blueprint closes teardown and is the `Estimator`'s act of committing to a diagnosis. It advances `stage` from `TEARDOWN` to `BLUEPRINTED`. The order's `stage` must be `INTAKE` or `TEARDOWN` for the operation to be considered at all, and anything else is refused with `ERR_STAGE_PRECONDITION`; an `INTAKE` order is admitted that far only so that the finding-count refusal is the error the caller actually sees, because in practice an order leaves `INTAKE` the moment its first finding is recorded and therefore can never blueprint from `INTAKE`. The order must have at least one teardown finding, and an order with a `teardown_finding_count` of zero is refused with `ERR_NO_TEARDOWN_FINDINGS` — the only refusal an `INTAKE` order can receive here. Third, if **any** finding on the order has `adas_component_touched` set to true, then at least one procedure record on the order must have `operation_scope` equal to `ADAS` *and* `requires_calibration` equal to true; otherwise the blueprint is refused with `ERR_ADAS_PROCEDURE_RESEARCH_MISSING`. You may not publish a blueprint for a car whose sensors you have disturbed without first having researched how the manufacturer wants them recalibrated.

Blueprinting has four effects. It stamps `blueprint_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, which is the segregation-of-duties key consumed much later at quality control. It runs the `PROVISIONAL` variant of the refinish allocation over the order's draft refinish lines, writing `refinish_hours_provisional`. It re-derives `authorization_posture` as its final step. And it increments the order's `version` by one.

### 3.3. Procedure Research, Adherence Documentation and ADAS Calibration

Repair procedure research is shared work: either an `Estimator` or a `Technician` may record a researched procedure against an order. Each record names the manufacturer's `procedure_code`, the `source` where it was researched — `OEM_PORTAL`, `ALLDATA` or `IREPAIR` — and the `operation_scope` the procedure governs, one of `STRUCTURAL`, `REFINISH`, `ADAS` or `MECHANICAL`. Two optional inputs decide whether a calibration is in play: `requires_calibration`, defaulting to false, and `calibration_type`, defaulting to `NONE` and otherwise one of `STATIC`, `DYNAMIC` or `BOTH`. Each record also belongs to its order via the `repair_order_id` from the path, and records can be listed filtered by `repair_order_id`, by `bay_id` and by `calibration_status`.

At creation, a record whose `requires_calibration` is false is born at `calibration_status` `NOT_REQUIRED` and stays there permanently; a record whose `requires_calibration` is true is born at `REQUIRED`. `adherence_documented` starts false on every record, and `adherence_doc_ref` starts null.

Documenting adherence is a `Technician` capability that sets `adherence_documented` and, optionally, `adherence_doc_ref` — the reference of the paperwork proving the procedure was actually followed. It increments the record's `version`. Two later gates depend on it, and they differ in scope: leaving `REPAIR` requires only the `STRUCTURAL`-scoped records on the order to be documented, whereas filing a post-repair inspection requires **every** record on the order to be documented. Both refusals carry `ERR_PROCEDURE_ADHERENCE_MISSING`.

Scheduling a calibration books the record into a bay for a window, moving `calibration_status` from `REQUIRED` to `SCHEDULED`. The request names a `bay_id`, a `window_start` and a `window_end`. Four things are checked, in this order. The record's `calibration_status` must be `REQUIRED` or `FAILED`, and anything else is refused with `ERR_CALIBRATION_STATE`. The `window_end` must be strictly after the `window_start`, and both must parse as ISO-8601 instants, or the booking is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. The record's `calibration_type` must appear in the bay's `supported_types`, with one deliberate asymmetry: a record whose type is `BOTH` is satisfied **only** by a bay that itself lists `BOTH`, not by a bay listing `STATIC` and `DYNAMIC` separately; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_BAY_TYPE_UNSUPPORTED`. Finally, the number of procedure records already booked into that bay with `calibration_status` `SCHEDULED` whose windows *strictly overlap* the requested window must be **fewer than** the bay's `concurrent_capacity`; when that count has already reached the capacity the booking is refused with `ERR_CALIBRATION_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Overlap here is half-open — a window runs from its start inclusive to its end exclusive — there is no buffer at all, and two windows that merely touch at a single instant do not overlap. This is what makes the bay an aggregate resource: each individual booking can be perfectly legal while the aggregate count is what refuses the next one. A successful booking sets `bay_id`, `window_start` and `window_end` on the record, recomputes the bay's `scheduled_window_count`, and increments both the record's `version` and the bay's `version`.

Recording the calibration outcome is also a `Technician` act, and it takes a `result`, a `post_scan_dtc_count` and an optional `scan_report_ref`. The record must be `SCHEDULED`, or the write is refused with `ERR_CALIBRATION_STATE`. The verdict is not simply what was submitted: `calibration_status` becomes `PASSED` only when the submitted `result` is `PASS` **and** `post_scan_dtc_count` is zero. Any diagnostic trouble code still active forces `FAILED` regardless of the submitted result. A pass sets `post_scan_dtc_count` and `scan_report_ref`, recomputes the bay's `scheduled_window_count` because the window is released, and increments the record's `version` and the bay's `version`. A failure sets the same two fields, and additionally **clears** `bay_id`, `window_start` and `window_end` so the bay window is freed for a re-book, recomputes the bay's `scheduled_window_count`, and increments the record's `version` and the bay's `version`. A failed record blocks the hop into `READY_FOR_QC` with `ERR_CALIBRATION_NOT_VERIFIED` until a re-calibration passes.

That re-book is the cycle in this machine: a record at `FAILED` can be scheduled again, returning it to `SCHEDULED` under exactly the same window, bay-type and capacity rules as the first booking, and the re-scheduling clears `post_scan_dtc_count` and `scan_report_ref` along with setting the new `bay_id` and window. `NOT_REQUIRED` and `PASSED` are the two resting states; nothing moves out of either.

### 3.4. Building the Estimate, Allocating Refinish Time, and the Two Ways Out of a Blueprint

With the blueprint published, the `Estimator` writes the estimate one labor operation at a time. Each labor line names the `operation_code` from the labor time guide, the `labor_type` that decides its rate family — `BODY`, `FRAME`, `REFINISH`, `MECHANICAL` or `ALUMINUM_BODY` — and the `guide_hours` the guide allows for that operation, which must be greater than zero and carry at most one decimal place. A line normally answers a teardown finding, and the `damage_line_id` names it; a line that answers no particular finding leaves that reference null. The line belongs to its order through the `repair_order_id` taken from the path. Labor lines are readable individually and listable with filters on `repair_order_id`, `damage_line_id`, `supplement_id` and `line_status`.

Several optional inputs on a labor line describe how the operation deviates from the guide. `not_included_operation`, defaulting to false, marks work the guide does not include in its published time; such a line **must** carry a non-empty `justification_note` in writing, and if it does not, the estimate cannot be committed. `is_blend`, defaulting to false, marks a refinish line that blends colour into an adjacent panel rather than refinishing it fully. `is_adjacent_panel`, also defaulting to false, marks a refinish line on a panel adjacent to the major panel, which takes the overlap deduction. `two_stage`, defaulting to false, marks colour that needs a two-stage or clearcoat application. Finally `pay_type` records which party is billed for the line and defaults to `INSURER`, its only other value being `CUSTOMER`.

Where the line lands depends entirely on how far the order has travelled. While the estimate is still open — the order at `BLUEPRINTED` — a new line is created with `line_status` `DRAFT` and no supplement reference. Once the order's `stage` is **past** `BLUEPRINTED`, an unsupplemented line is no longer acceptable: the line must carry a `supplement_id` naming a supplement of the *same* order whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED`, and a line offered without one is refused with `ERR_SUPPLEMENT_REQUIRED`. A line created under a supplement is born `PENDING_SUPPLEMENT` and is invisible to every total until the adjuster decides its fate. Either way, adding a line re-derives the order's `authorization_posture` as the last step and increments the order's `version`. The five values `line_status` can take are `DRAFT`, `PENDING_SUPPLEMENT`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` and `VOID`.

**Refinish allocation** is the most intricate arithmetic in the estimate, because a paint gun that is already loaded and a booth that is already masked do not cost full time on the second panel. The allocation considers only the order's refinish lines — those whose `labor_type` is `REFINISH` — and it runs in two distinct variants over two distinct input sets. The `PROVISIONAL` variant reads the lines whose `line_status` is `DRAFT`; the `FINAL` variant reads the lines whose `line_status` is `APPROVED`. Lines sitting at `PENDING_SUPPLEMENT`, `REJECTED` or `VOID` are excluded from both variants.

The `PROVISIONAL` variant is raw guide time and nothing else. It applies no ranking, no overlap deduction, no blend factor and no clearcoat adder: it simply sums the `guide_hours` of the lines in its input set, each rounded half up to one decimal place, and writes the total to the order's `refinish_hours_provisional`. It is deliberately the higher of the two figures, so that the shop quotes conservatively before the estimate is committed, and it never writes `allocated_hours` onto any line. It runs at blueprint time.

The `FINAL` variant is the real allocation, and it runs at estimate lock and again on every supplement decision that moves refinish lines into or out of `APPROVED`. It first ranks its input set: descending by `guide_hours`, then — for ties — ascending by `created_at`, and finally ascending by `operation_code` as the last tie-break. The first line in that ranking is the **major** panel. Then, for each line in the ranked set, the allocated time is determined by exactly one of four mutually exclusive branches:

- The major panel keeps its full `guide_hours`, plus a clearcoat adder of 0.3 hours if and only if its `two_stage` flag is true.
- Otherwise, a line whose `is_blend` is true gets **half** of its `guide_hours` — the blend factor is 0.5.
- Otherwise, a line whose `is_adjacent_panel` is true gets its `guide_hours` **less** the overlap deduction of 0.4 hours.
- Otherwise, the line gets its `guide_hours` unchanged.

Each line's result is rounded half up to one decimal place and then floored at zero, so a heavily deducted small panel can reach 0.0 but never goes negative, and the outcome is persisted as that line's `allocated_hours`. The order's `refinish_hours_final` is the sum of those **already-rounded** per-line values, not a rounding of the raw sum. A worked illustration: three refinish lines of 2.8, 2.2 and 1.6 guide hours, where the 2.8 line is two-stage, the 2.2 line is an adjacent panel, and the 1.6 line is a blend. Ranking makes the 2.8 line the major panel, so it becomes 2.8 plus 0.3, or 3.1. The adjacent panel becomes 2.2 less 0.4, or 1.8. The blend becomes half of 1.6, or 0.8. `refinish_hours_final` is therefore 3.1 plus 1.8 plus 0.8, which is 5.7, while `refinish_hours_provisional` over the identical rows is 2.8 plus 2.2 plus 1.6, which is 6.6. The 0.9-hour gap between the two figures is the reconciliation delta the shop absorbs at lock. If the input set is empty, the refinish hours figure is 0.0 and no line is written.

Two consequences deserve emphasis. Because the major panel is chosen by descending `guide_hours`, adding one larger refinish line later re-ranks the whole set and changes the `allocated_hours` of lines that were not themselves touched. And non-refinish approved lines are not part of this allocation at all: their `allocated_hours` is simply their `guide_hours`, verbatim, with no deduction of any kind.

**Committing the estimate** advances `stage` from `BLUEPRINTED` to `ESTIMATE_LOCKED`. The order must be at `BLUEPRINTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_STAGE_PRECONDITION`. At least one labor line on the order must be `DRAFT`, or the lock is refused with `ERR_NO_ESTIMATE_LINES`. And every `DRAFT` line whose `not_included_operation` is true must carry a non-null `justification_note`, or the lock is refused with `ERR_NOT_INCLUDED_JUSTIFICATION_MISSING`. On success, every `DRAFT` line becomes `APPROVED`; the `FINAL` refinish allocation runs, writing `allocated_hours` on every approved refinish line and `refinish_hours_final` on the order; the approved-hours roll-up runs; the eight-step totals run in their pinned order; and the posture derivation runs last. The order's `version` increments by one and so does the `version` of each labor line the lock touched.

The **approved-hours roll-up** is simple but load-bearing: `approved_labor_hours` is the sum of `allocated_hours` over the order's labor lines whose `line_status` is `APPROVED` — every labor type, refinish and non-refinish alike — rounded half up to one decimal place. Continuing the illustration above, three refinish lines at 3.1, 1.8 and 0.8 plus a 4.5-hour body line and a 3.0-hour frame line give 13.2. This figure is an invariant rather than an accumulator: it always equals the sum over the currently approved lines, which is why a denied supplement that flips its lines from `APPROVED` to `REJECTED` restores exactly the figure the order held before those lines were written.

**The eight-step totals** are the money picture, and they run in exactly this order, each step reading the results of the ones above it. They run at estimate lock, on every supplement decision, on price acceptance, on a parts order, on a parts return, on a consent decision, and on a total-loss declaration.

1. *Labor.* For each approved labor line, `effective_rate_cents` is the lesser of the shop's posted rate for that line's `labor_type` (§2.10) and the insurer program's `rate_ceiling_cents` entry for the same `labor_type`. That line's `line_amount_cents` is its `allocated_hours` multiplied by its `effective_rate_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent. `labor_subtotal_cents` is the sum of those per-line amounts.
2. *Parts gross.* `parts_gross_cents` is the sum of `extended_price_cents` over the order's qualifying parts lines. A parts line qualifies only when its `authorization_status` is `INSURER_ALLOWED` or `CUSTOMER_CONSENTED` **and** its `procurement_status` is `PRICE_LOCKED`, `ORDERED`, `RECEIVED` or `INSTALLED`. Lines at `PLANNED`, `QUOTED`, `COUNTERED`, `RETURNED` or `VOID` contribute nothing, and neither do lines whose `authorization_status` is `PENDING_CONSENT` or `CUSTOMER_DECLINED`.
3. *Volume rebate.* `parts_discount_cents` applies an **average** rate to the whole parts gross rather than a marginal rate per tier: the rate is zero when `parts_gross_cents` is below 100,000 cents, three percent when it is at least 100,000 cents but below 500,000 cents, and five percent when it is 500,000 cents or more. `parts_discount_cents` is the parts gross multiplied by that single rate, rounded half up. `parts_subtotal_cents` is then `parts_gross_cents` less `parts_discount_cents`.
4. *Betterment.* When the owner voluntarily upgrades to an original-equipment part, the owner funds one fifth of that line. `betterment_cents` is the sum, over the qualifying parts lines whose `oem_upgrade` flag is true, of each line's `unit_extended_cents` multiplied by its `qty` and then by 0.2, each such product rounded half up before it is added.
5. *Paint and materials.* `supplies_fee_cents` is four percent of `labor_subtotal_cents`, rounded half up, but capped: it is the **lesser** of that computed figure and 25,000 cents. A big enough labor bill therefore contributes a flat 250.00 dollars of supplies and no more.
6. *Pre-tax total.* `pre_tax_total_cents` is `labor_subtotal_cents` plus `supplies_fee_cents` plus `parts_subtotal_cents`. The betterment share is not added here; it is a split instruction, not a charge.
7. *Insurer authorization.* The authorization ceiling is the order's `initial_authorized_cents` plus the sum of `approved_amount_cents` over the order's supplements whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `PARTIAL`. `insurer_authorized_cents` is then the pre-tax total less the `deductible_cents` less the `betterment_cents`, capped at that ceiling, and floored at zero — the insurer never funds the deductible and never funds the betterment, and never pays beyond what it authorised.
8. *The customer's share.* `customer_pay_cents` is `pre_tax_total_cents` less `insurer_authorized_cents`.

A worked pass through those steps: with insurer ceilings of 6,000 for `BODY`, 8,000 for `FRAME`, 5,800 for `REFINISH`, 11,000 for `MECHANICAL` and 8,800 for `ALUMINUM_BODY`, a `deductible_cents` of 25,000, an `initial_authorized_cents` of 400,000, final refinish allocations of 3.1, 1.8 and 0.8 hours plus a 4.5-hour `BODY` line and a 3.0-hour `FRAME` line, and two priced parts lines extending to 78,163 and 29,241 cents: step 1 gives 17,980, 10,440, 4,640, 27,000 and 24,000, so `labor_subtotal_cents` is 84,060. Step 2 gives a parts gross of 107,404. Step 3 lands in the three-percent band, so the discount is 3,222 and the parts subtotal is 104,182. Step 4 gives zero betterment. Step 5 gives 3,362, comfortably under the 25,000 cap. Step 6 gives a pre-tax total of 191,604. Step 7 caps 191,604 less 25,000 less 0 at the ceiling of 400,000, which leaves 166,604. Step 8 leaves the owner paying 25,000 — the deductible, and nothing more.

**Declaring a total loss** is the divergent twin of committing the estimate: the same `BLUEPRINTED` starting point, and the exact opposite treatment of the open lines. An `Estimator` supplies a `total_loss_note`, and the order goes from `BLUEPRINTED` to the terminal `TOTAL_LOSS`. The order must be at `BLUEPRINTED`, or the declaration is refused with `ERR_STAGE_PRECONDITION`. The cascade then runs in three caused hops plus a scheduling release, and each hop must actually land:

- Hop one, requested directly: every labor line whose `line_status` is `DRAFT` or `PENDING_SUPPLEMENT` becomes `VOID`, and every parts line whose `procurement_status` is `PLANNED`, `QUOTED`, `COUNTERED` or `PRICE_LOCKED` becomes `VOID`. A voided parts line has its `extended_price_cents` set to zero and leaves every totals input set. Parts lines already at `ORDERED` or beyond are untouched by a total loss — the shop has already bought them.
- Hop two, caused by hop one: the approved-hours roll-up, the parts commitment ledger and the eight-step totals all rerun over the shrunken line sets, so `approved_labor_hours`, `parts_gross_cents`, `pre_tax_total_cents` and `customer_pay_cents` all change.
- Hop three, caused by hop two: the posture derivation reruns on the new `customer_pay_cents` and returns `authorization_posture` to `OPEN`.
- Alongside the cascade, every stall booking on the order whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` becomes `CANCELLED`, and each cancellation lowers that stall's `booked_minutes` by that booking's `occupied_minutes`.

The order's `version` increments by one. Because a total loss can only void lines that were never ordered, it can never break the parts conservation identity of §2.9.

### 3.5. Choosing a Part, Authorising the Money, and Negotiating the Price

Parts hang off labor lines, not off the order directly: a `PartsManager` attaches a part to the labor operation that will fit it, and the labor line's identifier reaches the new row as its `estimate_line_id`. The request names the supplier's `part_number`, a `part_description`, the sourcing `part_type` — one of `OEM`, `AFTERMARKET`, `RECYCLED` or `RECONDITIONED` — the `list_price_cents`, which is the original-equipment list price per unit and must be greater than zero, and the `qty` of units required, which must be at least 1. A `vendor_id` may be supplied at creation, though it is normally set from the accepted quote instead. Parts lines are readable individually and listable with filters on `estimate_line_id`, on `vendor_id` and on `procurement_status`.

The `list_price_cents` field earns its keep later, as the basis of the insurer's per-unit ceiling; it is not what the shop pays. A new line is born with `procurement_status` `PLANNED` and `quote_round` zero, with `quoted_unit_price_cents`, `counter_unit_price_cents` and `unit_price_cents` all null.

**Deriving the money authorisation at creation** is where insurer policy, vehicle age and the owner's standing preference meet. First the insurer program must permit the chosen channel at all: `AFTERMARKET` requires the program's `aftermarket_allowed`, `RECYCLED` requires `recycled_allowed`, `RECONDITIONED` requires `reconditioned_allowed`, and `OEM` is always permitted. A part type the program forbids outright is refused with `ERR_PART_TYPE_NOT_ALLOWED_BY_INSURER`. Given a permitted type, the resulting `authorization_status` is decided in this order:

- An `OEM` line is always `INSURER_ALLOWED`.
- A policy-permitted alternate on a vehicle at least `alternate_parts_min_vehicle_age_years` old — measured as the logical clock's calendar year minus the order's `vehicle_model_year` — is also `INSURER_ALLOWED`, because the program has already agreed to alternates on cars of that age.
- Otherwise, if the owner's `preferred_alternate_parts_ok` is true, the line is created already `CUSTOMER_CONSENTED` on the strength of that standing written preference.
- Otherwise the line lands at `PENDING_CONSENT`, and the owner must be asked.

Creating a `PENDING_CONSENT` line has a consequence beyond the line itself: the posture derivation, running last as always, sees an alternate part awaiting a signature and drives the order's `authorization_posture` to `CONSENT_REQUIRED`. The order's `version` increments. That is the mechanism by which a single unsigned bumper cover freezes the whole car.

One optional flag on creation, `oem_upgrade`, defaults to false and means something narrow: the owner declined a policy-permitted alternate and chose an original-equipment part anyway, so the owner funds the upgrade share of the line. It may only be true on a line whose `part_type` is `OEM`, whose insurer program permits at least one alternate type, and whose vehicle is at least `alternate_parts_min_vehicle_age_years` old. If any of those three fails the request is refused with `ERR_OEM_UPGRADE_NOT_APPLICABLE`: there is no upgrade to fund when original equipment was the only lawful choice in the first place.

**Price negotiation** is a cycle, and the `PartsManager` runs it. Recording a supplier quote from a `PLANNED` line names the `vendor_id` and the `quoted_unit_price_cents`; it moves `procurement_status` from `PLANNED` to `QUOTED`, sets the `vendor_id` on the line, and sets `quote_round` to 1. Before that transition is allowed, the line's `part_type` must appear in the supplier's `part_types_offered`, or the quote is refused with `ERR_PART_TYPE_NOT_OFFERED`. The line's `version` increments.

Countering the supplier takes a `counter_unit_price_cents`, moves the line from `QUOTED` to `COUNTERED`, and increments the line's `version`. The counter must be **strictly lower** than the standing `quoted_unit_price_cents`; a counter equal to or above the standing quote is refused with `ERR_COUNTER_NOT_LOWER`.

The supplier's answer to a counter is another quote, and this is the cycle: recording a quote against a `COUNTERED` line returns it to `QUOTED`, overwrites `quoted_unit_price_cents` with the revised figure, clears `counter_unit_price_cents`, and increments `quote_round` by one. The revised quote must name the same `vendor_id` already on the line — you cannot swap suppliers mid-negotiation — and a different supplier is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_MISMATCH`. The `quote_round` this cycle accumulates is not bookkeeping: it feeds the first-round component of the supplier's performance score, so a supplier that has to be haggled with repeatedly scores worse than one whose opening price was accepted.

Accepting the price moves the line from `QUOTED` to `PRICE_LOCKED` and takes no input at all — the standing quote is what gets frozen. One fiscal gate stands in front of it: the `quoted_unit_price_cents` must not exceed the `list_price_cents` multiplied by the insurer program's `parts_price_ceiling_pct`, that product rounded half up to a whole cent. A quote above that ceiling is refused with `ERR_PARTS_PRICE_CEILING_EXCEEDED`. On success `unit_price_cents` is frozen at the accepted quote and the per-unit extension runs: `unit_extended_cents` is the `unit_price_cents` multiplied by one minus the supplier's `discount_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent, plus the supplier's `core_charge_cents` when — and only when — the line's `part_type` is `RECONDITIONED`. `extended_price_cents` is then `unit_extended_cents` multiplied by `qty` less `returned_qty`. Two illustrations of the unit-first rule: an `OEM` line at a unit price of 84,500 cents with a 7.5 percent discount and a quantity of 1 gives a rounded unit figure of 78,163 (from 78,162.5, rounded half up) and therefore an extended price of 78,163; an `AFTERMARKET` line at 10,101 cents with a 3.5 percent discount and a quantity of 3 gives a rounded unit figure of 9,747 (from 9,747.465) and an extended price of 29,241. Multiplying first and rounding afterwards would give 29,242 for that second line — one cent high — and that cent propagates into the parts gross, possibly into the rebate tier, and out into the owner's share.

Accepting the price also pulls the line into the money picture: the eight-step totals rerun on the order because the line has now entered the parts-gross input set, and the posture derivation runs after them. Both the parts line's `version` and the order's `version` increment.

### 3.6. Ordering, Receipt, Returns, and the Commitment Ledger

Placing the order with the supplier is the most heavily guarded write in the API, and the order in which its gates fire is normative. The request carries a single input, the supplier's `promised_eta`. The line must be at `PRICE_LOCKED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PARTS_LINE_STATE`. The order's `stage` must be one of `ESTIMATE_LOCKED`, `PARTS_STAGED`, `REPAIR`, `REFINISH_CALIBRATION` or `READY_FOR_QC`, or it is refused with `ERR_STAGE_PRECONDITION`. The order's `authorization_posture` must be `OPEN`, or it is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. The supplier's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or it is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_SUSPENDED`. The line's `qty` must not exceed the supplier's `max_qty_per_line`, or it is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_QTY_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The line's `authorization_status` must be `INSURER_ALLOWED` or `CUSTOMER_CONSENTED`; a `PENDING_CONSENT` or `CUSTOMER_DECLINED` line may never be ordered, and the refusal is `ERR_CUSTOMER_CONSENT_REQUIRED`. The supplier's `fulfillment_score_rolling` must be at least 40, or the request is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`. And last of all, the order's `customer_pay_cents` must not exceed the sum of its `deductible_cents` and its `betterment_cents`, or the request is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZED_AMOUNT_EXCEEDED` — the owner's exposure has to stay inside what the insurer authorised and the owner agreed.

A successful order moves the line from `PRICE_LOCKED` to `ORDERED`, stamps the supplied `promised_eta`, and stamps `vendor_order_seq` from the supplier's `order_seq_counter` after incrementing it — so the sequence numbers a supplier hands out are strictly increasing and unique to that supplier. It adds `unit_extended_cents` multiplied by `qty` to the order's `parts_ordered_gross_cents`, reruns the commitment ledger so that `parts_committed_cents` rises by the line's `extended_price_cents`, reruns the eight-step totals, and reruns the posture derivation. The parts line's `version`, the supplier's `version` and the order's `version` all increment by one.

**The commitment ledger** maintains three derived figures on the order, and reruns on every parts order, every receipt, every return and every total-loss declaration. `parts_ordered_gross_cents` is append-only: it rises by `unit_extended_cents` multiplied by `qty` at order time and is *never* decremented. `parts_committed_cents` is the sum, over the order's parts lines whose `procurement_status` is `ORDERED`, `RECEIVED` or `INSTALLED`, of each line's `unit_extended_cents` multiplied by its `qty` less its `returned_qty`. `parts_return_credit_cents` is the sum, over the lines that have had units returned, of `unit_extended_cents` multiplied by `returned_qty` less that line's `restocking_fee_cents` — the credit the shop actually expects back. `parts_restocking_loss_cents` is the sum of `restocking_fee_cents` over those same lines. The identity of §2.9 must hold after every one of those four triggers. Concretely: one original-equipment line ordered at a per-unit extension of 78,163 cents and a quantity of 1 leaves the gross at 78,163, the committed figure at 78,163, and the credit and loss buckets at zero. A full unused return against a supplier whose `restocking_fee_pct` is 0.2 charges a `restocking_fee_cents` of 15,633, dropping the committed figure to zero, raising the credit to 62,530 and the loss to 15,633 — and zero plus 62,530 plus 15,633 is 78,163 again. The fee is a loss, not a leak.

Booking the part in moves the line from `ORDERED` to `RECEIVED`. The request carries the `received_qty`, which must be greater than zero and no greater than the line's `qty`, or the receipt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RECEIVE_QUANTITY`; and a `condition`, an enum with exactly three members, `CORRECT`, `INCORRECT` and `DAMAGED`. Receipt stamps `received_at` from the logical clock, records the `received_qty`, and computes `on_time` as whether `received_at` is at or before the `promised_eta` — a part arriving exactly at its promised instant is on time. When the `condition` is `CORRECT`, receipt **closes** every open delay event on that line, stamping each event's `closed_at` from the logical clock and computing its `delay_minutes` as the whole minutes, floored, between its `opened_at` and its `closed_at`; the order's `open_parts_delay_count` is then recomputed. When the `condition` is anything other than `CORRECT`, nothing is closed; instead a new delay event is opened against the line with `reason` `INCORRECT_PART`, so the order's `open_parts_delay_count` stays raised. Either way the supplier is re-scored on both variants, and the parts line's, the supplier's and the order's `version` each increment.

Fitting the part to the car moves the line from `RECEIVED` to `INSTALLED`, takes no input, and is a `Technician` act rather than a `PartsManager` one. The order's `stage` must be `REPAIR` or `REFINISH_CALIBRATION`, or the request is refused with `ERR_STAGE_PRECONDITION`, and at least one un-returned unit must remain — `received_qty` less `returned_qty` must be 1 or more — or it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RECEIVE_QUANTITY`. Installation increments only the parts line's `version`; the line stays inside the committed input set, because a fitted part is still money the shop has spent.

**Returning units** is available from `RECEIVED` and also from `INSTALLED` — a part already bolted to the car can be pulled back off when it turns out to be the wrong one, and that is the only path by which a return can reach an order that has already left `REPAIR`. The request carries a `return_qty` and a `return_reason` of `UNUSED`, `INCORRECT` or `DAMAGED`. The `return_qty` must be greater than zero and no greater than `received_qty` less `returned_qty`, or the return is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RETURN_QUANTITY`; a line at any other `procurement_status` is refused with `ERR_PARTS_LINE_STATE`. A **full** return — a `return_qty` equal to `qty` less the already-`returned_qty` — moves the line to the terminal `RETURNED`; a **partial** return leaves the `procurement_status` exactly where it was and fires the identical ledger cascade over the returned units only.

The return sets `returned_qty` and `return_reason`, then recomputes `restocking_fee_cents` and `extended_price_cents`. The restocking fee is charged **only** when the shop over-ordered: it is `unit_extended_cents` multiplied by the `return_qty` and then by the supplier's `restocking_fee_pct`, rounded half up, when the `return_reason` is `UNUSED`, and zero when the reason is `INCORRECT` or `DAMAGED` — a supplier's own error is free to send back. From there the cascade runs in two caused hops:

- Hop one, caused by the line leaving the committed input set: the commitment ledger reruns, so `parts_committed_cents` falls by the returned value, `parts_return_credit_cents` rises by `unit_extended_cents` multiplied by `return_qty` less `restocking_fee_cents`, and `parts_restocking_loss_cents` rises by `restocking_fee_cents`. The eight-step totals then rerun, moving `parts_gross_cents`, `parts_subtotal_cents`, `pre_tax_total_cents` and `customer_pay_cents`.
- Hop two, caused by hop one's new `customer_pay_cents`: the posture derivation reruns and may drive `authorization_posture` to `CONSENT_REQUIRED`.

Two further effects attach to a return. When the `return_reason` is `INCORRECT`, a delay event is opened against the line with `reason` `INCORRECT_PART`, raising the order's `open_parts_delay_count`. And when the order had already moved past `REPAIR` — that is, when its `stage` is `REFINISH_CALIBRATION` or `READY_FOR_QC` and the returned line was `RECEIVED` or `INSTALLED` before the return — the return **drags the order back**: `stage` rolls back to `REPAIR`; every stall booking on the order whose `stage` is `REFINISH_CALIBRATION` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `CANCELLED`; and each cancelled booking releases exactly its `occupied_minutes` from its stall's `booked_minutes`. Those three are caused hops in sequence, and each must land. The supplier is re-scored on both variants, and the parts line's, the supplier's and the order's `version` increment, along with the `version` of any stall booking and stall the rollback touched.

**Supplier performance** is a weighted score over closed outcomes, and it is recomputed on every receipt and every return for that line's supplier. The input set is the supplier's parts lines whose `received_at` is not null — equivalently, those whose `procurement_status` is `RECEIVED`, `INSTALLED` or `RETURNED`. Lines still at `PLANNED`, `QUOTED`, `COUNTERED`, `PRICE_LOCKED`, `ORDERED` or `VOID` have no outcome yet and are excluded from both variants. Those closed lines are ordered by `vendor_order_seq` descending — deterministically, never by identifier. The `LIFETIME` variant takes the whole set as its window; the `ROLLING` variant takes only the three most recent lines in that ordering. If the window is empty the score is 50, the value a newly registered supplier starts with. Otherwise three ratios are computed over the window: the share of lines whose `on_time` is true, the share of lines whose `returned_qty` is zero, and the share of lines whose `quote_round` is 1 or less. The score is 100 multiplied by the sum of those three ratios each weighted in turn by 0.6 for on-time delivery, 0.25 for no returns and 0.15 for first-round agreement, the final product rounded half up to a whole number. The ratios themselves are exact and are never rounded individually. The `ROLLING` result is written to `fulfillment_score_rolling`; the `LIFETIME` result is written to `fulfillment_score_lifetime`.

The two variants diverge, and the divergence is the point. Consider a supplier with four closed lines carrying `vendor_order_seq` 1 through 4, where only sequence 1 arrived on time, none had units returned, and all four were agreed at the first quote round. The `LIFETIME` score is 100 times the quantity 0.6 times one quarter, plus 0.25 times one, plus 0.15 times one — that is 55. The `ROLLING` score, over sequences 4, 3 and 2 only, is 100 times the quantity 0.6 times zero, plus 0.25 times one, plus 0.15 times one — that is 40. Identical rows, fifteen points apart, because an old good record decays out of the rolling window. The ordering gate at §3.6 tests the `ROLLING` figure and only the `ROLLING` figure: a comfortable lifetime score does not rescue a supplier whose recent window has collapsed.

**Parts delay events** are an append-only interruption log. Each event belongs to an order through its `repair_order_id` and to the specific line whose supply slipped through its `parts_line_id`. Its `reason` is either `ETA_BREACH` or `INCORRECT_PART`; its `opened_at` is the logical clock at detection and marks the start of a non-touch interval; its `closed_at` is the logical clock when the part finally landed, and stays null while the delay is open; and its `delay_minutes` is the whole minutes, floored, between the two, staying null while the delay is open. Events carry no `updated_at` and no `version`. They are listable per order and per parts line, both in creation order.

`ETA_BREACH` events come from the late-parts sweep, a `PartsManager` capability that takes no input. Running the sweep opens one event, with `reason` `ETA_BREACH` and `opened_at` at the logical clock, for every `ORDERED` line on the order whose `promised_eta` has passed and that has no open event already. It answers with an `opened_count` and the resulting `open_parts_delay_count`. The sweep is idempotent within a clock instant: repeating it before the clock moves opens nothing, because every eligible line now already has an open event. Running the sweep recomputes the order's `open_parts_delay_count` and increments the order's `version` when at least one event was opened. An order with any open delay cannot be delivered.

### 3.7. Supplements, Customer Consent, and the Authorisation Posture

Hidden damage, a procedure the manufacturer turns out to demand, a parts price that came in over the estimate, a calibration nobody planned for — each of these forces the shop back to the insurer. An `Estimator` raises a supplement against the locked estimate, naming a `reason_code` of `HIDDEN_DAMAGE`, `PROCEDURE_REQUIREMENT`, `PARTS_PRICE_VARIANCE` or `CALIBRATION_REQUIRED`, a `requested_amount_cents` greater than zero, and the `expected_repair_order_version` demanded by §2.7. The supplement belongs to its order via the `repair_order_id` from the path. It is created with `status` `SUBMITTED`, `round_count` 1, and `submitted_at` stamped from the logical clock — which simultaneously opens a cycle-time exclusion interval. The posture derivation then runs and drives the order's `authorization_posture` to `SUPPLEMENT_PENDING`, and the order's `version` increments by one. Supplements are readable individually and listable per order.

The adjuster answers, and only the `InsurerAdjuster` whose `X-User-ID` equals the order's `insurer_id` may do so; anyone else is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The supplement's `status` must be `SUBMITTED`, or the answer is refused with `ERR_SUPPLEMENT_NOT_OPEN`. The answer carries a `decision` and, depending on the decision, an `approved_amount_cents` or a `note`. There are four decisions, and their treatments are deliberately different:

- **`APPROVE`** moves the supplement from `SUBMITTED` to `APPROVED`, sets `approved_amount_cents` equal to `requested_amount_cents`, stamps `decided_at` from the logical clock (closing the cycle-time exclusion interval), and records the `note` as the `adjuster_note`. Then four caused hops: hop one, every labor line whose `supplement_id` is this supplement and whose `line_status` is `PENDING_SUPPLEMENT` becomes `APPROVED`; hop two, caused by hop one, the `FINAL` refinish allocation, the approved-hours roll-up and the eight-step totals all rerun, so `approved_labor_hours`, `labor_subtotal_cents`, `insurer_authorized_cents`, `pre_tax_total_cents` and `customer_pay_cents` all move; hop three, caused by hop two, the posture derivation reruns on the new `customer_pay_cents` and may set `authorization_posture` to `CONSENT_REQUIRED`; hop four, caused by hop three, a consent recorded earlier whose `consent_basis_cents` now sits below the new `customer_pay_cents` is invalidated, so `consent_state` returns to `NONE`, `consent_basis_cents` returns to null, and `consent_alternate_parts` returns to false. The supplement's `version`, each touched labor line's `version` and the order's `version` all increment.
- **`PARTIAL`** moves the supplement from `SUBMITTED` to `PARTIAL` and requires an `approved_amount_cents` that is strictly greater than zero and strictly less than the `requested_amount_cents`; an amount of zero, a negative amount, or an amount at or above the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PARTIAL_AMOUNT`, as is an `APPROVE` or `DENY` decision that carries an amount at all. On success `approved_amount_cents` takes the adjuster's figure and `decided_at` is stamped. The linked `PENDING_SUPPLEMENT` labor lines still become `APPROVED` — the *work* is authorised even though the *money* is only partly authorised — and then the approved-hours roll-up, the eight-step totals and the posture derivation rerun in that order. The shortfall lands squarely in `customer_pay_cents`. The supplement's `version`, each touched labor line's `version` and the order's `version` increment.
- **`DENY`** moves the supplement from `SUBMITTED` to `DENIED`, sets `approved_amount_cents` to 0, and stamps `decided_at`. Its cascade is a compensation rather than an approval: every labor line whose `supplement_id` is this supplement and whose `line_status` is `PENDING_SUPPLEMENT` becomes `REJECTED`, so its `allocated_hours` leaves the approved input set entirely. The `FINAL` refinish allocation, the approved-hours roll-up and the eight-step totals then rerun, restoring `approved_labor_hours` to exactly the value it held before those lines were written, and the posture derivation reruns after them. The supplement's `version`, each touched labor line's `version` and the order's `version` increment.
- **`REQUEST_INFO`** moves the supplement from `SUBMITTED` to `INFO_REQUESTED` and requires a present, non-empty `note`, which is recorded as the `info_request_note`; a missing or empty note is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Crucially, `decided_at` stays null, so the supplement's wait interval keeps running for cycle-time purposes — an adjuster cannot stop the clock by asking a question. The linked labor lines stay `PENDING_SUPPLEMENT` and the order's `authorization_posture` stays `SUPPLEMENT_PENDING`. The supplement's `version` and the order's `version` increment.

Answering an information request is the `Estimator`'s move and completes the negotiation cycle: it returns the supplement from `INFO_REQUESTED` to `SUBMITTED`. The supplement's `status` must be `INFO_REQUESTED`, or the resubmission is refused with `ERR_SUPPLEMENT_NOT_OPEN`. The supplement's `round_count` must be strictly less than the order's insurer program's `supplement_round_limit`, or the resubmission is refused with `ERR_SUPPLEMENT_ROUND_LIMIT` — with the default limit of 3, a supplement can be submitted, re-submitted and re-submitted once more, and the fourth attempt is refused. The resubmission takes a fresh `requested_amount_cents`, which overwrites the previous figure, increments `round_count` by one, resets `submitted_at` to the logical clock so that the supplement's wait interval restarts from that instant, and clears `info_request_note`. The supplement's `version` and the order's `version` increment.

**Consent** is the owner's side of the same conversation, and only the `Customer` whose `X-User-ID` equals the order's `customer_id` may record it. The request carries a `decision`, an `alternate_parts_ok` boolean, and the `expected_repair_order_version` demanded by §2.7. A `CONSENT` decision sets `consent_state` to `CONSENTED`, freezes `consent_basis_cents` at the order's *current* `customer_pay_cents` — the figure the owner was actually shown — and sets `consent_alternate_parts` from the supplied `alternate_parts_ok`. A `DECLINE` decision sets `consent_state` to `DECLINED`. The three values `consent_state` can hold are `NONE`, `CONSENTED` and `DECLINED`, and it starts at `NONE` with `consent_basis_cents` null.

The consent decision also resolves the order's pending parts lines. Every parts line whose `authorization_status` is `PENDING_CONSENT` becomes `CUSTOMER_CONSENTED` when `consent_alternate_parts` is true, and `CUSTOMER_DECLINED` otherwise — which means a `CONSENT` decision carrying `alternate_parts_ok` false has the same effect on those lines as an outright `DECLINE`. A `CUSTOMER_CONSENTED` line becomes eligible for ordering and enters the parts-gross input set once it is priced. A `CUSTOMER_DECLINED` line is permanently excluded from the parts gross and can never be ordered; any attempt refuses with `ERR_CUSTOMER_CONSENT_REQUIRED`. Each touched parts line's `version` increments, the eight-step totals and then the posture derivation rerun, and the order's `version` increments by one.

**Deriving the posture** is the final step of every mutation that can move the owner's share, a supplement's status, or a parts line's money authorisation — that is, of publishing a blueprint, committing the estimate, declaring a total loss, adding a labor line, raising a supplement, deciding a supplement, resubmitting a supplement, recording a consent decision, creating a parts line, accepting a price, placing a parts order, and returning parts. Its input is the `customer_pay_cents` and the consent fields as the totals wrote them **in the same request** — never a stale read — together with all of the order's supplements and all of the order's parts lines that are not `VOID`. Three predicates are evaluated:

- A **consent gap** exists when `customer_pay_cents` is at or above 50,000 cents *and* either `consent_state` is not `CONSENTED`, or `consent_basis_cents` is null, or `consent_basis_cents` is strictly below the current `customer_pay_cents`.
- A **pending alternate** exists when at least one of the order's parts lines has `authorization_status` `PENDING_CONSENT`.
- An **open supplement** exists when at least one of the order's supplements has `status` `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED`.

Before the posture is chosen, one compensation is applied: if `consent_state` is `CONSENTED` and `consent_basis_cents` is strictly below the current `customer_pay_cents`, that consent is stale and is thrown away — `consent_state` becomes `NONE`, `consent_basis_cents` becomes null, and `consent_alternate_parts` becomes false. The shop has to go back to the owner. Then the posture is chosen by pinned precedence: if there is a consent gap **or** a pending alternate, `authorization_posture` becomes `CONSENT_REQUIRED`; otherwise, if there is an open supplement, it becomes `SUPPLEMENT_PENDING`; otherwise it becomes `OPEN`.

That precedence produces the posture's whole transition map. From `OPEN`, an order acquires `SUPPLEMENT_PENDING` when a supplement is raised and no consent condition holds. From `SUPPLEMENT_PENDING` it returns to `OPEN` once no supplement is open any longer and no consent condition holds, which re-enables stage advancement. From `SUPPLEMENT_PENDING` a decision can instead land the order at `CONSENT_REQUIRED`, when no supplement is open but the newly recomputed share has opened a consent gap or a pending alternate remains — and that is the transition at which a stale consent is invalidated. From `OPEN` the order goes straight to `CONSENT_REQUIRED` when a `PENDING_CONSENT` parts line is created, or when a price acceptance or a parts return recomputes the totals into a consent gap. From `CONSENT_REQUIRED` the order returns to `OPEN` once `consent_state` is `CONSENTED` with a `consent_basis_cents` at or above the current `customer_pay_cents`, no parts line is `PENDING_CONSENT`, and no supplement is open. And from `CONSENT_REQUIRED` a consent recorded while a supplement is still open moves the order to `SUPPLEMENT_PENDING` instead of `OPEN` — the freeze persists, now for the paperwork reason rather than the money reason.

One asymmetry is worth stating outright, because it is easy to get backwards: a `DECLINE` decision does **not** release the order from `CONSENT_REQUIRED`. Declining flips the pending parts lines to `CUSTOMER_DECLINED`, which removes the pending-alternate predicate, but it leaves `customer_pay_cents` at or above the threshold with no valid consent behind it, so the consent gap still holds and the order stays frozen until the estimate itself is reduced.

A boundary illustration, on an order with a single 4.5-hour `BODY` line at an effective 6,000 cents and a 3.0-hour `FRAME` line at an effective 8,000 cents and no parts at all: labor comes to 51,000, supplies to 2,040, and the pre-tax total to 53,040. With a `deductible_cents` of 49,999 the insurer is authorised 3,041 and the owner pays 49,999, which leaves the posture `OPEN`. With a `deductible_cents` of 50,000 the insurer is authorised 3,040 and the owner pays 50,000, which makes the posture `CONSENT_REQUIRED` — the threshold is inclusive, so exactly 50,000 cents already requires a signature. A consent recorded at a `consent_basis_cents` of 50,000 survives a later `customer_pay_cents` of 50,000 but is invalidated by 50,001.

### 3.8. Booking the Shop and Driving the Stages

Production capacity is consumed by bookings. A `Technician` books a stall for a stage, supplying the `stall_id`, the `stage` the occupancy serves — either `REPAIR` or `REFINISH_CALIBRATION`, and nothing else — an inclusive `window_start` and an exclusive `window_end`. The booking belongs to its order via the `repair_order_id` from the path, and bookings are readable individually and listable with filters on `repair_order_id`, on `stall_id` and on `status`.

Four gates guard a booking, in this order. The `window_end` must be strictly after the `window_start` and both must parse as ISO-8601 instants, or the booking is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. The stall's `stall_type` must fit the stage: a `REPAIR` occupancy needs a `BODY` or `FRAME` stall, and a `REFINISH_CALIBRATION` occupancy needs a `PAINT` stall; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALL_TYPE_MISMATCH`. The stall's `status` must be `AVAILABLE`, and a stall under `MAINTENANCE` is refused with `ERR_STALL_UNAVAILABLE`. Finally, no other booking on the same stall whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` may come within the stall's `cleanup_buffer_minutes` of the requested window on either side; a violation is refused with `ERR_STALL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. That buffer is symmetric and its exclusion zone is **closed**, which means a window starting exactly `cleanup_buffer_minutes` after an existing window ends is still refused, while one starting a single minute later is accepted. A stall is a single-capacity resource with mandatory dead time for masking and cleanup, so back-to-back bookings are simply not allowed.

A successful booking is created with `status` `SCHEDULED` and an `occupied_minutes` fixed at creation as the whole minutes, floored, between its `window_start` and its `window_end`. It raises the stall's `booked_minutes` by that figure and increments the stall's `version`. Note again that `cleanup_buffer_minutes` never enters `occupied_minutes`: the buffer widens the conflict check and nothing else, so it never inflates `booked_minutes`.

A booking that has not started can be released. Cancellation requires `status` `SCHEDULED`, and a booking that is `ACTIVE`, `COMPLETED` or already `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`. It moves the booking to `CANCELLED` and lowers the stall's `booked_minutes` by exactly the booking's `occupied_minutes`, restoring the stall to the value it held before the booking existed; both the booking's `version` and the stall's `version` increment. The four values `status` can hold are `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE`, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED`, with the last two terminal.

Bookings are also promoted and retired by the server rather than by a client. When an order enters the stage a `SCHEDULED` booking serves, that booking becomes `ACTIVE` and its `version` increments — but the stall's `booked_minutes` is *unchanged*, because `SCHEDULED` and `ACTIVE` both occupy the stall. When an order leaves that stage, or when the vehicle is released, an `ACTIVE` booking becomes `COMPLETED`, its stall's `booked_minutes` drops by its `occupied_minutes`, and both the booking's and the stall's `version` increment. And when a parts return drags an order back to `REPAIR`, an `ACTIVE` booking for the `REFINISH_CALIBRATION` stage becomes `CANCELLED` rather than `COMPLETED`, again releasing its minutes and again incrementing both versions.

**Advancing the stage** is a `Technician` capability that takes a single `to_stage` input and moves the order one hop along a fixed successor map. `PARTS_STAGED` follows `ESTIMATE_LOCKED`. `REPAIR` follows either `PARTS_STAGED` or `QC_FAILED` — that second edge is the rework cycle, and an order that re-enters `REPAIR` must traverse the remaining stages again from there. `REFINISH_CALIBRATION` follows `REPAIR`. `READY_FOR_QC` follows `REFINISH_CALIBRATION`. Any other pair, including an attempt to re-enter the current stage and any attempt to skip a stage, is refused with `ERR_STAGE_SEQUENCE_INVALID`. Every hop, without exception, requires the order's `authorization_posture` to be `OPEN`, and a `SUPPLEMENT_PENDING` or `CONSENT_REQUIRED` order is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_POSTURE_BLOCKED`.

Beyond the posture, each hop has its own admission conditions:

- Into `PARTS_STAGED` from `ESTIMATE_LOCKED`: no parts line on the order may still be at `PLANNED`, `QUOTED`, `COUNTERED` or `PRICE_LOCKED` — every part is either ordered or off the estimate — or the hop is refused with `ERR_PARTS_NOT_PROCURED`. The only side effect is the order's `version` incrementing.
- Into `REPAIR` from `PARTS_STAGED`: at least one booking on the order with `stage` `REPAIR` and `status` `SCHEDULED` must exist, or the hop is refused with `ERR_NO_STAGE_ASSIGNMENT`. On success every such booking becomes `ACTIVE` and the order's `version` increments.
- Into `REPAIR` from `QC_FAILED`: identically, at least one `SCHEDULED` `REPAIR` booking must exist or the hop is refused with `ERR_NO_STAGE_ASSIGNMENT`; on success every such booking becomes `ACTIVE` and the order's `version` increments.
- Into `REFINISH_CALIBRATION` from `REPAIR`: no parts line may still be at `ORDERED` or `RECEIVED` — everything bought is on the car or has been sent back — or the hop is refused with `ERR_PARTS_NOT_INSTALLED`; and every procedure record on the order whose `operation_scope` is `STRUCTURAL` must have `adherence_documented` true, or the hop is refused with `ERR_PROCEDURE_ADHERENCE_MISSING`; and at least one booking with `stage` `REFINISH_CALIBRATION` and `status` `SCHEDULED` must exist, or the hop is refused with `ERR_NO_STAGE_ASSIGNMENT`. On success, every `ACTIVE` `REPAIR` booking on the order becomes `COMPLETED` and releases its `occupied_minutes` from its stall, every `SCHEDULED` `REFINISH_CALIBRATION` booking becomes `ACTIVE`, and the order's `version` increments.
- Into `READY_FOR_QC` from `REFINISH_CALIBRATION`: every procedure record on the order that needs calibration must have reached `PASSED` — no record may still be at `REQUIRED`, `SCHEDULED` or `FAILED` — or the hop is refused with `ERR_CALIBRATION_NOT_VERIFIED`; and **every** procedure record on the order, in every scope, must have `adherence_documented` true, or the hop is refused with `ERR_PROCEDURE_ADHERENCE_MISSING`. On success every `ACTIVE` booking on the order becomes `COMPLETED`, releasing its `occupied_minutes` from its stall, and the order's `version` increments.

### 3.9. Quality Control, the Warranty, Delivery, and Cycle Time

The last technical gate is the post-repair inspection, and it is the one place where the shop's own paperwork is checked against a second pair of eyes. A `QcInspector` records an inspection against an order that is at `READY_FOR_QC`; any other stage is refused with `ERR_STAGE_PRECONDITION`. The inspection belongs to its order via the `repair_order_id` from the path and carries a `checklist_passed` verdict, a `post_scan_dtc_count` of zero or more diagnostic trouble codes still active on the post-repair scan, and a `scan_report_ref` naming the scan report. Inspections are readable individually and listable per order, newest last in the `created_at` ordering of §2.3.

Five conditions guard the inspection, and they are the same five whatever the verdict turns out to be. The order must be at `READY_FOR_QC`, or `ERR_STAGE_PRECONDITION`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `blueprint_by_user_id`, or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the person who wrote the plan may not be the person who signs off on its execution, which is the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.1 in its only enforcement site. Every procedure record on the order whose `requires_calibration` is true must have `calibration_status` `PASSED`, or `ERR_CALIBRATION_NOT_VERIFIED`. Every procedure record on the order, calibration or not, must have `adherence_documented` true, or `ERR_PROCEDURE_ADHERENCE_MISSING`. And no parts line on the order may still be at `ORDERED` or `RECEIVED`, or `ERR_PARTS_NOT_INSTALLED`.

An inspection is created directly in its final state — the verdict is not a later transition between two persisted values but a property of the row at birth. Its `result` is `PASS` only when `checklist_passed` is true **and** `post_scan_dtc_count` is exactly 0; every other combination is a `FAIL`. On a `FAIL` the `failure_reason` is set, and its two values are ranked rather than combined: `CHECKLIST` when `checklist_passed` is false, and `DTC_PRESENT` only when the checklist passed but codes remain — so an inspection that fails on both counts reports `CHECKLIST`. A `FAIL` leaves `warranty_certificate_number` and `warranty_expires_at` null, and it drives the order's `stage` from `READY_FOR_QC` to `QC_FAILED`, which is the head of the rework cycle described in §3.8.

Every inspection, pass or fail, takes a `sequence_number` equal to the order's `qc_inspection_count` plus one, so the first inspection on an order is number 1 and a re-inspection after rework is number 2, and it records the acting `X-User-ID` as its `inspector_user_id`. The order's `qc_inspection_count` is then recomputed and its `version` increments by one.

A `PASS` additionally issues the warranty. The `warranty_certificate_number` is composed as the literal `"WC-"`, then the order's `claim_number`, then a literal `"-"`, then the `sequence_number` — so the second inspection on claim `CLM-2026-0001`, if the first one failed, issues `WC-CLM-2026-0001-2`. The `warranty_expires_at` is the logical clock at the moment of the pass plus exactly 365 days, treating a day as 86,400 seconds with no calendar or leap adjustment. A `PASS` deliberately does **not** advance the stage: the order stays at `READY_FOR_QC`, now awaiting release rather than awaiting inspection.

Releasing the vehicle is a `ShopAdmin` capability and moves the order from `READY_FOR_QC` to the terminal `DELIVERED`. It requires the stage to be `READY_FOR_QC`, the `authorization_posture` to be `OPEN` (else `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_POSTURE_BLOCKED`), the inspection on the order with the highest `sequence_number` to have `result` `PASS` (else `ERR_QC_NOT_PASSED` — an earlier pass followed by a later fail does not qualify), and the order's `open_parts_delay_count` to be exactly 0 (else `ERR_OPEN_PARTS_DELAY`). On success `delivered_at` is stamped from the logical clock, freezing the cycle-time window; every stage assignment on the order whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` becomes `COMPLETED` and drops its `occupied_minutes` from its stall's `booked_minutes`; and the order's `version` increments by one.

**Cycle time** is the shop's report card and is computed on demand rather than stored, so no field on the order holds it and no `version` moves when it is read. Any shop operator or the order's own insurer or owner may ask for it. The window runs from the order's `checkin_at` to its `delivered_at` if it has been delivered, or to the current logical clock if it has not; `gross_minutes` is the floor of the whole minutes between those two instants and is the keys-to-keys number quoted to the insurer, with no interval excluded, so it is always at least the net figure.

The interruptions are then assembled. Each supplement on the order contributes one interval from its `submitted_at` to its `decided_at`, or to the window end if it is still open — and a supplement that went through an information request and came back contributes a single interval starting at its **latest** `submitted_at`, not at its original one. Each parts delay event contributes one interval from its `opened_at` to its `closed_at`, or to the window end if it is still open. Zero-length intervals are dropped. Every surviving interval is clamped into the window, then sorted by start and by end, then merged — and the merge is closed at the boundary, so intervals that merely touch, with a gap of exactly zero, merge into one. Only after the merge is each merged interval floored to whole minutes and the results added, giving `excluded_minutes`; flooring the pieces first and adding afterwards is wrong and will disagree. Then `net_touch_minutes` is `gross_minutes` minus `excluded_minutes`, never below zero, and is the shop's own touch-time number.

Two further figures are reported alongside and are deliberately **not** merged, either with each other or with anything else. The `supplement_wait_minutes` is the sum of the individual clamped supplement durations, and the `parts_delay_minutes` is the sum of the individual clamped delay durations. When a supplement wait and a parts delay overlap in time, those two sums together exceed `excluded_minutes`, and only `excluded_minutes` may ever be subtracted from the gross — which is precisely why the net figure is strictly greater than `gross_minutes` minus the two sums whenever two hold-ups overlap: two concurrent hold-ups are charged once, not twice. Finally, `open_interval_count` is the number of assembled intervals whose end came from the window end rather than from a real closing instant, and it tells the reader how much of the report is still provisional.
