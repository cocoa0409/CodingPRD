# Ambient Air Monitoring Network Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state air quality monitoring programme lives or dies by the defensibility of its numbers. Every three-year attainment determination that a regional office signs off on rests on a chain of custody that begins with where a sampling probe was bolted to a rooftop, continues through every span check and zero drift reading a technician logged, and ends with a certified annual data set lodged in the national database before a statutory deadline. When any link in that chain is undocumented — a monitor whose calibration lapsed, a quarter whose hours were quietly written off, a wildfire whose smoke was never formally excluded — the determination becomes contestable, and the programme loses years of work.

This document specifies the API for the system that holds that chain together. The service is responsible for network design and siting review, reference-method designation and instrument approval, field quality-control scheduling and posture tracking, quarterly data ingest with hour-level invalidation accounting, exceptional-event demonstrations that remove wildfire and dust hours from regulatory use, design-value computation and attainment banding, annual certification against a hard deadline, and the periodic network assessments that authorise sites to be relocated, suspended for a season, or shut down permanently.

Two design pressures shape almost every rule that follows. The first is **segregation of duties**: the federal programme deliberately splits authority so that no single operator can both create a data defect and bless the result, and no state agency can grant itself the approvals a regional reviewer owes it. The second is **auditable arithmetic**: completeness percentages, adequacy scores and design values are all computed by fixed, ordered procedures whose rounding steps are normative, because two defensible-looking orders of operation can flip a site from attainment to nonattainment. Implementers should treat the evaluation order stated in each calculation as part of the contract, not as a suggestion.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that operator is acting under. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check precedes everything else, including request-body parsing and path resolution. When both headers are present and well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the requested method and path, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `NetworkAdmin`, `FieldTechnician`, `QaOfficer`, `ProgramManager` and `EpaRegionalReviewer`, spelled exactly so.

A `NetworkAdmin` is the state air programme's configuration officer. The role maintains the pollutant catalogue, registers federal method designations, creates networks, proposes sites, installs instruments into the inventory, and owns the server's logical clock that every temporal rule reads. A `FieldTechnician` performs field operations: commissioning approved instruments, logging calibration, span, zero, precision and preventive-maintenance activity, and taking instruments out of and back into service. A `QaOfficer` is the quality-assurance officer who ingests monitor-quarters, invalidates and restores hour ranges, and decides each quarter's validation outcome. A `ProgramManager` runs the monitoring programme: bringing approved sites into operation, reinstating suspended sites, preparing exceptional-event demonstrations, computing design values, opening assessment cycles, raising network-change requests and certifying the annual data set. An `EpaRegionalReviewer` is the federal regional reviewer who holds every approval gate the state cannot self-grant — method designation changes, site siting approval, instrument approval, exceptional-event concurrence, design-value finalization, certification rejection, assessment decisions and network-change decisions.

All five roles are **opaque identities**. When `X-User-Role` is `NetworkAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `FieldTechnician`, when it is `QaOfficer`, when it is `ProgramManager`, and when it is `EpaRegionalReviewer`. No resource in this system is "owned" by a user, and no ownership check ever resolves `X-User-ID` against a foreign key.

The server does, however, persist the acting `X-User-ID` on the records where accountability matters, and two of those recorded identifiers are later compared against a *subsequent* caller's `X-User-ID` to enforce segregation of duties (see §2.10). A logged quality-control activity records the technician as `performed_by_user_id`. An hour-range invalidation records its author as `applied_by_user_id`, and the affected quarter accumulates the distinct authors in `invalidation_applied_by_user_ids`. A quarter that passes review records `validated_by_user_id`. An exceptional-event concurrence records `determined_by_user_id`; a finalized design value records `finalized_by_user_id`; an approved assessment records `approved_by_user_id`; a decided network-change request records `decided_by_user_id`; and a certified annual package records `certified_by_user_id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the 1-to-100 band, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint's description states another order, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Where an endpoint does state its own order — quality-control history newest-first by `performed_at`, invalidations and exclusions by `start_hour_index`, bulk-validated quarters by `year` then `quarter`, the exclusions of one demonstration by `data_batch_id` then `start_hour_index` — that stated order governs.

For list responses, the `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` as applied, `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is taken, and `has_more` as a boolean that is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and the Revision Counter

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers are bare UUIDs with no type prefix and no embedded semantics; clients must never parse them. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, taken from the logical clock at the moment the row was created.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format that is refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. The `version` counter is what makes optimistic concurrency possible (§2.7) and what makes cascades observable: when an operation on one resource mutates a second resource, the second resource's `version` increments and its `updated_at` is refreshed too, even though the client never named it.

Two resources are exceptions. A pollutant catalogue entry is immutable once registered, and a quality-control activity record is immutable once logged. Both therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`: neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on a pollutant, and neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on a quality-control event. Every other resource in the system carries all four common properties.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The service does not read wall-clock time. It reads a **logical clock** whose startup default is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and which never falls back to the host's real time. Every deadline, every interval, every elapsed-day computation and every "has this period ended" test in this document resolves against the logical clock.

A `NetworkAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and that instant becomes the clock for every subsequent request. Moving the clock has consequences that no other request body triggers: because instrument quality-control posture is measured as days elapsed since the last passing span check, advancing the clock alone can carry a monitor from `QC_CURRENT` through `QC_DUE` to `QC_DELINQUENT` on the next read, and each network's count of delinquent monitors is recomputed on the next read accordingly. The clock-setting capability returns no resource payload; its `data` is an empty object.

### 2.6. Resolving Path Segments and Body References

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that does not resolve to a live row determines the error. A request that names a monitor and then a nested collection resolves the monitor first; if the monitor does not exist, the answer is `ERR_MONITOR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) and no further work is attempted. The full set of path-resolution failures is `ERR_POLLUTANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERENCE_METHOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NETWORK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MONITOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DATA_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVALIDATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEMONSTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DESIGN_VALUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, each returned with HTTP 404.

References supplied inside a request body are resolved the same way, in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, and they raise the same not-found codes: `ERR_POLLUTANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERENCE_METHOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NETWORK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MONITOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DATA_BATCH_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Body-reference resolution applies to every creation that carries a foreign key: registering a reference method, proposing a site, installing a monitor, reporting a single quarter, bulk-reporting quarters for one monitor, bulk-validating a monitor's quarters, opening an exceptional-event demonstration, nominating an exclusion under a demonstration, computing a design value, opening a network assessment, raising a site change request, and opening a certification package. A missing replacement site nominated on a relocation request surfaces as `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND` like any other site reference.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Quarters and Sites

Two write operations in this system can legitimately be repeated against an unchanged parent, which means no state precondition can protect them from a client acting on a stale read. Both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency field that the client must supply.

Creating an hour-range invalidation against a quarter — the `POST /data-batches/{id}/invalidations` endpoint of §4 — requires `expected_data_batch_version` in the request body. The field is not optional. Its value must equal the target quarter's current `version` at the moment the request is processed; if it does not, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BATCH_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason this guard is necessary is that adding a second, non-overlapping invalidation to the same quarter is a perfectly legitimate operation that no state check blocks — so a client whose `expected_data_batch_version` predates an earlier invalidation's hour-accounting recompute would be sizing its new hour range against a completeness figure that no longer exists. On success the quarter's `version` increments, which in turn invalidates any other client's stale expectation.

Computing a design value — the `POST /design-values` endpoint of §4 — requires `expected_monitoring_site_version` in the request body, likewise non-optional. Its value must equal the referenced site's current `version`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SITE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Again the state machine cannot help: a new computation for the same site, pollutant, period and variant legitimately supersedes the prior provisional row, so nothing blocks a second attempt. The version check is what guarantees the requester's view of the site — its attainment standing and its monitor roster — was current when the determination was framed. A successful computation increments the site's `version`.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Requests that supply one where it is not declared are simply not taking part in optimistic concurrency.

### 2.8. Precision, Rounding and Truncation

Wherever this document says a value is *rounded*, the method is round half up: a digit sequence exactly on the boundary rounds away from zero. Wherever it says a value is *truncated*, the method is truncation toward zero, which never increases the magnitude of the value.

Four precision conventions recur. Completeness percentages — both the per-quarter `completeness_pct` and the aggregate `rollup_completeness_pct` of an annual package — are rounded to one decimal place, once, at the point the ratio is formed; every downstream comparison uses that rounded figure and never the unrounded ratio. Absolute drift readings on a span or zero check are rounded to two decimal places *before* being compared against the method's tolerance. A network assessment's `coverage_ratio` is rounded to four decimal places and its `adequacy_score` to one decimal place. Annual statistics and design values are rounded and then truncated to the number of decimals the pollutant declares in `rounding_decimals`, in the two-step order set out in §3.7.

Siting geometry is the one place where no rounding occurs at all: probe heights, setbacks and clearances are compared at full input precision.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and the Network Census

Several operations in this system mutate rows the caller never named. A regional decision on a network-change request can, in a single request, change a site's lifecycle state, decommission every instrument at that site, invalidate every unfinished quarter those instruments produced, push submitted certification packages back to draft, and demote every quarter those packages had submitted. Cascades of this kind are **immediate and complete within the request**: by the time the response is written, every affected row has been mutated, its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed. There is no deferred or eventual settlement.

A monitoring network maintains a live census of its sites, and that census is a hard invariant rather than a convenience field. `total_site_count` is the number of sites belonging to the network in any status whatsoever. Alongside it, `proposed_site_count`, `approved_site_count`, `operating_site_count`, `suspended_site_count`, `shutdown_site_count` and `relocated_site_count` count the sites in the `PROPOSED`, `SITING_APPROVED`, `OPERATING`, `SUSPENDED`, `SHUTDOWN` and `RELOCATED` states respectively. Because every lifecycle transition moves a site from one bucket to another rather than adding or removing one, `total_site_count` is unchanged by every transition after creation — the per-status counters shift, their sum does not. Two further network counters are derived from deeper in the graph: `delinquent_monitor_count` is the number of instruments at the network's sites whose calendar quality-control posture is `QC_DELINQUENT` and whose lifecycle status is `APPROVED`, `OPERATING` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, and `waiver_required_site_count` is the number of the network's sites that need a siting waiver and are currently `SITING_APPROVED`, `OPERATING` or `SUSPENDED`.

A comparable conservation law governs each quarter's hour budget. Across every recompute — an invalidation added, an invalidation withdrawn, an event exclusion made effective — the quarter's `scheduled_hour_count` always equals the sum of `valid_hour_count`, `invalidated_hour_count`, `excused_hour_count` and `event_excluded_hour_count`. If an implementation can produce a state where that identity fails, its hour accounting is wrong.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Two hard segregation rules run through the quality-assurance chain, and both compare a *later* caller's `X-User-ID` against an identifier the system recorded earlier.

First, the officer who wrote off hours of a quarter may not be the officer who accepts that quarter for regulatory use. Every hour-range invalidation appends its author's `X-User-ID` to the quarter's `invalidation_applied_by_user_ids` list, and when a validation is attempted the calling `X-User-ID` must not appear in that list. Second, the manager who validated data may not be the manager who certifies it: certification of an annual package is refused when the calling `X-User-ID` equals the `validated_by_user_id` of any quarter the package selected. Both refusals carry the same code, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, with HTTP 403.

Note that the list of invalidation authors is append-only and ordered by first append. Withdrawing an invalidation returns its hours to the completeness computation but does **not** remove its author from `invalidation_applied_by_user_ids` — once an officer has touched the hours of a quarter, that officer is permanently barred from validating it.

### 2.11. Rejection Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fixed rather than incidental. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first failure terminates the request.

Authentication comes first (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`), then role authorisation (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`), then structural validity of the request body and pagination parameters (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), then resolution of path identifiers left to right, then resolution of body references in declared field order. Only once every identifier is known to resolve does optimistic concurrency run: `expected_data_batch_version` on an invalidation create, `expected_monitoring_site_version` on a design-value computation. After concurrency come the state checks — the target's own from-state and the cross-row preconditions its transition declares (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`), whether the target is already terminal (`ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`), and the specific state guards for certified quarters (`ERR_BATCH_CERTIFIED`), non-operating sites (`ERR_SITE_NOT_OPERATING`) and unvalidated member quarters (`ERR_MEMBER_BATCH_NOT_VALIDATED`).

Referential business rules follow the state layer: current method designation, primary-monitor availability, replacement-site presence, assessment approval and replacement-site eligibility. Segregation-of-duties and safety rules come next, in the order: the invalidating-officer bar on validation, the validating-manager bar on certification, delinquent quality-control posture, and siting-criteria satisfaction. Uniqueness and range constraints follow — duplicate site code, duplicate quarter period, duplicate parameter occurrence code, hour-range bounds, an already-final design value, an incomplete design value. Temporal rules come after those: invalidation range overlap, exclusion-versus-invalidation overlap, event window conflict, event window containment, whether the reported quarter has ended, and the certification deadline. Fiscal-style readiness gates are last: package readiness and the presence of at least one eligible quarter for a bulk validation.

The practical consequence to keep in mind is that a delinquent-posture failure on a quarter validation surfaces as `ERR_MONITOR_QC_DELINQUENT` only when the segregation-of-duties bar has already passed, and a withdrawn method designation surfaces as `ERR_METHOD_NOT_DESIGNATED` before either of them, because referential rules precede safety rules.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Pollutant Catalogue and Federal Method Designation

Everything downstream is calibrated by the pollutant catalogue, so a `NetworkAdmin` populates it first. Registering a criteria pollutant fixes its `code` — one of `PM25`, `PM10`, `O3`, `NO2`, `SO2`, `CO` or `LEAD`, and no two catalogue entries may share a code — together with `averaging_period_hours`, the averaging period of the primary standard expressed in hours, which must be at least 1 and at most 8760. The `standard_form` field records the statistical form of the standard and takes one of `ANNUAL_MEAN`, `FOURTH_MAX_8HR` or `PERCENTILE_98`; it is the switch that later selects which annual statistic a year of data contributes. `primary_standard_value` carries the numeric level of the standard in the pollutant's own reporting units and must be greater than zero.

Three catalogue fields carry defaults and exist purely to parameterise the arithmetic that follows. `design_value_years` is the number of consecutive calendar years averaged into a design value; it defaults to 3, and every pollutant in this system uses exactly 3. `completeness_threshold_pct` is the minimum rounded quarterly completeness a quarter must reach before it may be used for regulatory purposes; it defaults to 75.0 and must be greater than 0 and no more than 100. `rounding_decimals` is the number of decimal places used both for annual-statistic rounding and for design-value truncation; it defaults to 1 and must be at least 0 and at most 3. A catalogue entry is immutable after registration — there is no update path — and, as noted in §2.4, it therefore carries no revision counter and no modification timestamp.

Against each pollutant the administrator registers the federal methods approved to measure it. A method carries `pollutant_id` naming the catalogue entry it serves, a `method_code` that is unique across all methods and between 1 and 40 characters long, and a `designation_type` drawn from `FRM`, `FEM`, `ARM` and `NON_REFERENCE`. Four numbers on the method define the quality-control regime for every instrument that implements it: `max_span_drift_pct` is the largest absolute span drift percentage that still counts as a passing span check, `zero_drift_limit` is the largest absolute zero drift reading that still passes a zero check, `required_span_check_interval_days` is the maximum number of whole days permitted between successive passing span checks, and `required_calibration_interval_days` is the maximum number of whole days permitted between successive calibrations. Both tolerances must be greater than zero and both intervals must be at least 1 day. `effective_from` records the instant from which the designation becomes usable for regulatory data. A newly registered method enters `designation_status` `DESIGNATED` with `designation_change_count` at 0 and `withdrawn_on` null.

Federal designations do change, and only an `EpaRegionalReviewer` may change them, by supplying a `new_designation_status` and, optionally, a `determination_note`. A method in `DESIGNATED` may move to `CONDITIONAL`, and a method in `CONDITIONAL` may move back to `DESIGNATED` — the pair forms a cycle that a method can traverse repeatedly. Either state may move to `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal: no designation change is accepted on a method that has already been withdrawn. Every accepted change increments `designation_change_count` by 1 and increments the method's `version` by 1. A change to `WITHDRAWN` additionally sets `withdrawn_on` to the logical-clock instant; a change to any other status leaves `withdrawn_on` null. Attempting a change whose declared source state does not match the method's present `designation_status` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409); attempting any change on a `WITHDRAWN` method is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409).

The reason `CONDITIONAL` exists as a distinct state rather than as a soft flag is that it remains *usable*. Three operations consult the governing method's standing and demand that all three of the following hold: `designation_status` is either `DESIGNATED` or `CONDITIONAL`; `effective_from` is at or before the logical clock; and `withdrawn_on` is either null or strictly after the logical clock. Instrument approval, instrument commissioning, single-quarter validation and bulk quarter validation all apply this test, and all four reject a failure with `ERR_METHOD_NOT_DESIGNATED` (HTTP 422). A method sitting in `CONDITIONAL` passes; a method withdrawn yesterday does not, which is precisely why withdrawing a designation retroactively blocks every subsequent validation of every quarter produced by every instrument implementing that method. Consumers of the method's tolerances see the new values from the next request onward and are never retroactively rescored.

Any operator in any of the five roles may read a single pollutant, read a single method, or list the methods designated for a pollutant. That listing returns methods in any standing and accepts an optional `designation_status` filter along with the usual `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.2. Network Registration, Site Siting and Regional Approval

A `NetworkAdmin` registers a monitoring network with a programme `name`, an `agency_code` that must be unique across all networks and is the code carried on national-database submissions, and `metropolitan_population`, the population of the metropolitan area the network covers, which may not be negative. On creation every site-census counter and the delinquency counter initialise to 0, `network_status` initialises to `ACTIVE`, and `current_assessment_year` initialises to null. `network_status` has exactly two values: `ACTIVE` in normal operation, and `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` while an assessment cycle for the network sits with the regional office awaiting decision.

Proposing a site is where siting policy bites. The administrator supplies `network_id`, a `site_code` between 1 and 20 characters that must be unique within that network across sites in *any* status — a collision, including with a site long since shut down, is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SITE_CODE` (HTTP 409) — and a `site_type` naming the siting objective class: `POPULATION_ORIENTED`, `SOURCE_ORIENTED`, `BACKGROUND` or `TRANSPORT`. Location is given as `latitude` in decimal degrees north, between -90 and 90 inclusive, and `longitude` in decimal degrees east, between -180 and 180 inclusive. `population_served` records the population inside the site's representative area and may not be negative. Then comes the probe geometry: `probe_height_m`, the height of the sample probe inlet above ground in metres, which must be greater than zero; `distance_to_roadway_m`, the horizontal distance from the probe to the nearest traffic lane in metres; `distance_to_obstruction_m`, the horizontal distance to the nearest vertical obstruction; `obstruction_height_m`, the height of that obstruction above the probe's ground elevation; and `distance_to_source_m`, the horizontal distance to the targeted emission source, which defaults to 0.0 and is 0 when no source is targeted. None of the four distances or heights may be negative.

Siting criteria are evaluated the instant the site is proposed, so the response already carries the verdict. Five criteria are tested and each failure contributes one code to `siting_violation_codes`. The probe-height band applies to every site type: a violation code of `PROBE_HEIGHT` is recorded unless `probe_height_m` is at least 2.0 metres and at most 15.0 metres. Roadway setback applies only to a `POPULATION_ORIENTED` site: `ROADWAY_SETBACK` is recorded when `distance_to_roadway_m` is less than 10.0 metres. Source proximity applies only to a `SOURCE_ORIENTED` site, which is meant to sit close to what it measures: `SOURCE_DISTANCE` is recorded when `distance_to_source_m` is greater than 500.0 metres. Background isolation applies only to a `BACKGROUND` site, which must be far from traffic: `BACKGROUND_ISOLATION` is recorded when `distance_to_roadway_m` is less than 100.0 metres. A `TRANSPORT` site is subject to neither the roadway, source nor background criterion. Finally, the two-to-one obstruction clearance applies to every site type: the required clearance in metres is twice the amount by which `obstruction_height_m` exceeds `probe_height_m`, and when that required clearance is greater than zero and `distance_to_obstruction_m` is less than it, `OBSTRUCTION_CLEARANCE` is recorded. An obstruction no taller than the probe yields a required clearance of zero or less and therefore never produces a violation. The resulting codes are stored sorted ascending alphabetically so that the list is deterministic, and `siting_waiver_required` is set true exactly when the list is non-empty. All of these comparisons are made at the full precision of the submitted numbers; nothing is rounded.

A proposed site enters `status` `PROPOSED` with `siting_waiver_granted` false, and the network's `total_site_count`, `proposed_site_count` and `waiver_required_site_count` are recomputed.

Siting approval belongs to the `EpaRegionalReviewer` and moves a site from `PROPOSED` to `SITING_APPROVED`. The reviewer may supply an `approval_note` and, where the site fails a criterion, must supply `waiver_granted` as true. The criteria are re-evaluated from the site's current geometry before the gate is applied, and the gate is simple: the approval proceeds when `siting_waiver_required` is false, or when the request body carries `waiver_granted` as true. Approving a site whose violation list is non-empty without a granted waiver is rejected with `ERR_SITING_CRITERIA_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422). On success `siting_waiver_granted` takes whatever value `waiver_granted` carried, the network's `proposed_site_count` decrements by 1 and its `approved_site_count` increments by 1, `waiver_required_site_count` is recomputed, and both the site's `version` and the network's `version` increment by 1.

Bringing a site into service is a `ProgramManager` action that advances `SITING_APPROVED → OPERATING`, optionally annotated with an `operations_note`. It stamps `operations_start_at` from the logical clock — the field is null until then — and it is safe with respect to siting because either the violation list was empty or a waiver was already granted at approval time. The network's `approved_site_count` decrements by 1 and `operating_site_count` increments by 1, and both rows' `version` counters increment.

An operating site may later be suspended for a season, shut down permanently or relocated, but never directly: all three are consequences of an approved network-change request and are described in §3.9. The reverse direction is direct. A `ProgramManager` reinstates a suspended site, advancing `SUSPENDED → OPERATING` with an optional `reinstatement_note`; `suspended_at`, which held the instant the site entered suspension, is cleared to null. `SHUTDOWN` and `RELOCATED` are terminal states for a site — no transition leads out of either, and an attempt is met with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. A site that arrived at its position through a relocation carries `replaces_site_id` pointing at the site it took over from; the field is null on every site that has not replaced another.

Two further site fields are always computed fresh on read. `operating_monitor_count` is the number of instruments at the site whose lifecycle status is `OPERATING`. `current_attainment_status` is the attainment standing of the site's most relevant design value: among the site's design-value rows whose `status` is `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`, order by `created_at` descending and then by `period_end_year` descending and take the first row's attainment verdict; when no such row exists the value is `UNDETERMINED`. The field takes one of `UNDETERMINED`, `ATTAINMENT`, `NONATTAINMENT` or `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`.

Sites can be read individually — the response recomputes the siting evaluation, the operating monitor count and the attainment standing — or listed with filters on `network_id`, on `replaces_site_id` to find the successor of a relocated site, on `site_type`, on `status`, and on `siting_waiver_required`. Networks can be read individually with a freshly recomputed census, and each network exposes listings of its assessment cycles, filterable by `status` and `cycle_year`, and of its certification packages, filterable by `year` and `status`.

### 3.3. Instrument Lifecycle, Quality-Control Activity and QC Posture

An instrument enters the inventory when a `NetworkAdmin` installs it. The installation names `site_id` for the site it is bolted to and `reference_method_id` for the designated method it implements, and carries `poc`, the parameter occurrence code that distinguishes co-located instruments measuring the same pollutant at one site. A `poc` must be at least 1 and at most 9, and the triple of the site, the pollutant reached through the instrument's method, and the `poc` must be unique among instruments that are not decommissioned — a decommissioned instrument frees its code for reuse. A collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MONITOR_POC` (HTTP 409). The operator also supplies `installed_at`, the instant the instrument was physically installed, and may set `is_primary_for_design_value`, which defaults to false and is the operator's nomination of this instrument as the site's primary sampler for design-value work.

A newly installed instrument enters `monitor_status` `PENDING_APPROVAL` with all four quality-control timestamps — `last_calibration_at`, `last_span_check_at`, `last_zero_check_at` and `last_maintenance_at` — null, with `consecutive_qc_failure_count` at 0, with `out_of_service_day_count` at 0, and with `out_of_service_since` null. Both posture fields are computed immediately from `installed_at`, because no quality-control activity yet exists to serve as a baseline.

Regional instrument approval advances `PENDING_APPROVAL → APPROVED` and confirms that the instrument implements a currently designated method. An `EpaRegionalReviewer` performs it, optionally recording an `approval_note`. Two cross-row preconditions apply beyond the instrument's own state: the instrument's site must be in `SITING_APPROVED` or `OPERATING` — an approval attempted against a site still merely `PROPOSED`, or one already `SUSPENDED`, `SHUTDOWN` or `RELOCATED`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and the governing method must satisfy the designation test of §3.1, failing which the answer is `ERR_METHOD_NOT_DESIGNATED`. On success both postures are computed with `last_span_check_at` still null, so the baseline is `installed_at`, and the network's `delinquent_monitor_count` is recomputed.

Commissioning is the `FieldTechnician`'s act and advances `APPROVED → OPERATING`, optionally with a `commission_note`. Here the site must be fully `OPERATING`, not merely approved, and the method-designation test applies again. On success the site's `operating_monitor_count` increments by 1, both the instrument's and the site's `version` increment, and the instrument becomes visible to the primary-monitor selector described in §3.7.

An operating instrument can be taken out of service and returned to it, and the same technician-facing operation covers both directions through a `new_status` field plus an optional `reason`. Supplying `new_status` as `OUT_OF_SERVICE` advances `OPERATING → OUT_OF_SERVICE`, stamps `out_of_service_since` from the logical clock, and decrements the site's `operating_monitor_count` by 1; no additional condition applies beyond the from-state. Supplying `new_status` as `OPERATING` advances `OUT_OF_SERVICE → OPERATING` and imposes two extra conditions: `consecutive_qc_failure_count` must be 0, meaning a passing or warning activity has been logged since the last failure, and the instrument's site must itself be `OPERATING`. A return-to-service attempted while the failure counter is above zero, or while the site is not operating, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Returning to service closes out the accrual: `out_of_service_day_count` grows by the number of whole days elapsed from `out_of_service_since` to the logical clock, computed by dividing the elapsed seconds by 86400 and discarding any fraction, and `out_of_service_since` is cleared to null. Both postures are recomputed, the site's `operating_monitor_count` increments by 1, and both rows' `version` counters increment. `DECOMMISSIONED` is the terminal state for an instrument and is only ever reached by cascade.

Field activity is logged as immutable quality-control events. A `FieldTechnician` records an `event_type` from `CALIBRATION`, `SPAN_CHECK`, `ZERO_CHECK`, `PRECISION_CHECK` and `PREVENTIVE_MAINTENANCE`, together with `performed_at`, the instant the activity took place, which may not be later than the logical clock. A span check must carry `span_drift_pct`, the signed span drift percentage read during the check; a zero check must carry `zero_drift_value`, the signed zero drift reading in the pollutant's reporting units. Each field is ignored for event types other than its own. For the remaining three activity types the technician instead sets `declared_failure`, which defaults to false, to declare that the activity failed. Every event records `monitor_id` for the instrument it was performed on and `performed_by_user_id` for the technician. Because the event row is immutable, it carries only an identifier and a creation timestamp, with no revision counter.

The event's `outcome` is graded by the server, never supplied, and takes one of `PASS`, `WARN` or `FAIL`. For a span check, take the absolute value of `span_drift_pct`, round it to two decimal places, and compare it against the governing method's `max_span_drift_pct`: an absolute drift at or below the tolerance is `PASS`, a drift above the tolerance but at or below one and a half times the tolerance is `WARN`, and anything beyond that is `FAIL`. For a zero check the identical banding applies to the absolute value of `zero_drift_value`, rounded to two decimal places, against the method's `zero_drift_limit`. Both comparisons at the limit are inclusive: an absolute reading exactly equal to the tolerance is `PASS`, not `WARN`, and a reading exactly equal to one and a half times the tolerance is `WARN`, not `FAIL`. For a calibration, a precision check or a preventive-maintenance activity there is no reading to band — the outcome is `FAIL` when `declared_failure` is true and `PASS` otherwise, and `WARN` never arises. Grading consults only the submitted body and the method's two tolerances; no earlier event is examined.

Logging an event has four consequences beyond persisting the row. First, when the outcome is `PASS` or `WARN`, the timestamp on the instrument that corresponds to the activity type advances to the request's `performed_at` — a calibration refreshes `last_calibration_at`, a span check refreshes `last_span_check_at`, a zero check refreshes `last_zero_check_at`, a preventive-maintenance activity refreshes `last_maintenance_at`. A precision check refreshes none of them. On a `FAIL` outcome no timestamp moves at all. Second, `consecutive_qc_failure_count` increments by 1 on a `FAIL` and resets to 0 on a `PASS` or a `WARN`; it counts the run of failures since the last non-failing event. Third, an instrument that is `OPERATING` when it records a `FAIL` is automatically driven `OPERATING → OUT_OF_SERVICE` in the same request, with `out_of_service_since` stamped from the logical clock and the site's `operating_monitor_count` decremented — the instrument is pulled from service without anyone asking. Fourth, both postures are recomputed and the network's `delinquent_monitor_count` is recomputed.

**Quality-control posture** is the system's judgement of whether an instrument is current on its span checks, and it is the gate that decides whether that instrument's data may be validated. The baseline is `last_span_check_at` when a passing or warning span check has ever been logged, and `installed_at` otherwise. The permitted interval is the governing method's `required_span_check_interval_days`. An elapsed-days figure no greater than the interval yields `QC_CURRENT`; an elapsed figure greater than the interval but no greater than the interval plus a grace period of one week yields `QC_DUE`; anything beyond that yields `QC_DELINQUENT`. Elapsed days are always whole days, obtained by discarding any partial day, and both posture fields default to `QC_DUE` before any computation has run.

The elapsed figure is computed twice, in two variants that differ in exactly one respect, and both are stored on every instrument. The **calendar-elapsed** variant is written to `qc_posture`: elapsed days are the whole days from the baseline to the logical clock, so time the instrument spent out of service counts against it. This is the field that gates quarter validation. The **in-service-elapsed** variant is written to `qc_posture_in_service`: elapsed days are the same whole-day figure less `out_of_service_day_count`, floored at zero, so days the instrument was formally out of service are forgiven. This is the field reported on certification packages and network assessments. The two therefore diverge by exactly the accrued out-of-service days, and an instrument can be `QC_DELINQUENT` on `qc_posture` while only `QC_DUE` on `qc_posture_in_service` at the very same clock instant. Postures are recomputed whenever a quality-control event is logged, on instrument approval, on commissioning, on either direction of the service-status change, on site reinstatement, when a seasonal suspension cascade fires, and whenever the logical clock is moved. A decommissioned instrument keeps its last computed posture and is excluded from the network's `delinquent_monitor_count` regardless of what that posture says.

Instruments may be read individually — the response recomputes both postures — or listed with filters on `site_id`, `reference_method_id`, `monitor_status`, `qc_posture` and `is_primary_for_design_value`. An instrument's quality-control history is listed most recent `performed_at` first, with optional filters on `event_type` and on `outcome`.

### 3.4. Reporting Monitor-Quarters and the Hour Budget

Ambient data reaches the system one monitor-quarter at a time. A `QaOfficer` reports a quarter by naming `monitor_id`, the `year` — at least 2000 and at most 2100 — and the `quarter`, an integer from 1 to 4. The triple of instrument, year and quarter must be unique across all reported quarters in any validation standing; a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BATCH_PERIOD` (HTTP 409). `scheduled_hour_count` records how many sampling hours were scheduled for the quarter and must be at least 1 and at most 2232; it is the denominator before any hours are removed. The measured results are reported as `quarterly_mean_concentration`, the quarterly mean as measured in the pollutant's reporting units, which may not be negative. Two further measurements are conditional. `quarterly_mean_concentration_event_adjusted` is the quarterly mean recomputed with exceptional-event hours removed; it is null when no exceptional event affects the quarter and it is consumed by exactly one design-value variant. `fourth_high_daily_max` is the fourth-highest daily maximum eight-hour value for the quarter; it is null for pollutants whose standard form is an annual mean and is consumed only when the pollutant's `standard_form` is `FOURTH_MAX_8HR`.

Data may not be reported for a quarter that has not finished. The test is that the first instant of the *following* calendar quarter must be at or before the logical clock; reporting a quarter still in progress is rejected with `ERR_BATCH_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED` (HTTP 422).

A quarter is born with `validation_status` `RAW` and `submission_status` `UNSUBMITTED`, with `reopen_count` at 0 and `invalidation_applied_by_user_ids` an empty list. The two status fields are **independent dimensions on the same row**: a quarter's quality-assurance standing and its national-database standing evolve separately, and an operation that moves one leaves the other exactly where it was. `validation_status` takes one of `RAW`, `IN_VALIDATION`, `VALIDATED`, `INVALIDATED` or `REOPENED`; `submission_status` takes one of `UNSUBMITTED`, `SUBMITTED` or `CERTIFIED`.

Reporting many quarters for one instrument in a single call is also supported, and it is strictly transactional. The request names `monitor_id` once and supplies `batches` as the list of quarter payloads, each carrying the same per-quarter fields as a single report. Items are validated in list order against exactly the same rules that govern a single report, including the requirement that each reported quarter has already ended on the logical clock and the uniqueness of the instrument-year-quarter triple — checked not only against quarters already in the system but also against the request's own earlier items, so two items colliding on the same period fail with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BATCH_PERIOD`. If any item fails for any reason, nothing at all is persisted and the first offending item's error is returned; there is no partial quarter set. Hour accounting runs once per created quarter.

**Hour accounting** is the arithmetic that turns a scheduled hour count into a completeness percentage, and its evaluation order is normative. It runs on every quarter report, on every quarter in a bulk report, whenever an invalidation is created against the quarter, whenever an invalidation is withdrawn, on single-quarter validation, on bulk validation, and whenever an exceptional-event exclusion touching the quarter becomes effective. The steps are performed in this sequence.

First, `excused_hour_count` is the total of `hours_affected` over every applied invalidation on this quarter whose reason code is an *excused* code. Second, `invalidated_hour_count` is the total of `hours_affected` over every applied invalidation on this quarter whose reason code is not an excused code. Third, `event_excluded_hour_count` is the total of `hours_affected` over every event exclusion on this quarter that has become effective; proposed and rejected exclusions contribute nothing. Fourth, `adjusted_scheduled_hour_count` is `scheduled_hour_count` less `excused_hour_count` less `event_excluded_hour_count` — excused hours and event-excluded hours leave the denominator altogether. Fifth, `valid_hour_count` is `adjusted_scheduled_hour_count` less `invalidated_hour_count` — non-excused invalidated hours stay in the denominator and count against the quarter. Sixth, `completeness_pct` is `valid_hour_count` divided by `adjusted_scheduled_hour_count`, multiplied by 100 and rounded to one decimal place, except that when `adjusted_scheduled_hour_count` is zero the value is 0.0 rather than an error. Seventh, `regulatory_use_eligible` is true exactly when that *rounded* `completeness_pct` is greater than or equal to the pollutant's `completeness_threshold_pct`; the comparison is inclusive at the threshold, and it uses the rounded figure and never the raw ratio.

The order matters in two specific ways that implementers routinely get wrong. Computing the valid-hour figure before the adjusted denominator, or forming the ratio against `scheduled_hour_count` instead of against `adjusted_scheduled_hour_count`, produces a different completeness percentage and therefore a different regulatory-use verdict. Only applied invalidations participate; withdrawn ones are excluded entirely. The pollutant whose threshold is compared against is reached from the quarter's instrument through that instrument's reference method.

Which reason codes are excused is a policy decision embedded in the invalidation vocabulary. An `invalidation_code` is one of `QC_FAILURE`, `OPERATOR_ERROR`, `INSTRUMENT_MALFUNCTION`, `SCHEDULED_MAINTENANCE`, `POWER_OUTAGE_UTILITY` or `SITE_SHUTDOWN`. Exactly two of those — `SCHEDULED_MAINTENANCE` and `POWER_OUTAGE_UTILITY` — are treated as excused, because the agency could not have collected the data and is not penalised for its absence: those hours leave the denominator. The other four — `QC_FAILURE`, `OPERATOR_ERROR`, `INSTRUMENT_MALFUNCTION` and `SITE_SHUTDOWN` — are not excused: those hours remain in the denominator and are subtracted from the valid hours, so they drag completeness down.

Invalidating hours is a `QaOfficer` action against a specific quarter. The officer supplies `start_hour_index`, the first invalidated hour counted from 0 at the quarter's first scheduled hour and treated as inclusive; `end_hour_index`, the index one past the last invalidated hour and treated as exclusive; the `invalidation_code`; and `expected_data_batch_version` as described in §2.7. The range must satisfy all of: `start_hour_index` at least 0, `end_hour_index` strictly greater than `start_hour_index`, and `end_hour_index` no greater than the quarter's `scheduled_hour_count`. An empty, negative or overrunning range is rejected with `ERR_INVALIDATION_RANGE_OUT_OF_BOUNDS` (HTTP 422). The half-open range must also not intersect the range of any other applied invalidation on the same quarter; an intersection is rejected with `ERR_INVALIDATION_RANGE_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Because the interval is half-open and carries no buffer, two ranges that merely abut — where one range's `end_hour_index` equals the next range's `start_hour_index` — do not intersect and are both accepted.

An accepted invalidation is created with `status` `APPLIED`, with `hours_affected` set to `end_hour_index` less `start_hour_index`, with `data_batch_id` naming the quarter, and with `applied_by_user_id` set to the calling `X-User-ID`. The parent quarter's `version` increments by 1, the caller's `X-User-ID` is appended to `invalidation_applied_by_user_ids` if it is not already there, and hour accounting reruns, moving the new hours into `excused_hour_count` or `invalidated_hour_count` according to the code and refreshing `completeness_pct` and `regulatory_use_eligible`. Every certification package that had selected this quarter re-runs its rollup in the same request, so a package still in draft sees `included_batch_count`, `eligible_batch_count`, `rollup_completeness_pct` and `certification_ready` all move at once.

Withdrawing an applied invalidation returns its hours to the completeness computation. The `QaOfficer` supplies `withdrawn_reason`, a free-text justification that is required on withdrawal and is null while the invalidation is applied. The invalidation advances `APPLIED → WITHDRAWN`, `withdrawn_reason` is stored, the parent quarter re-runs hour accounting with the withdrawn hours restored to `valid_hour_count`, every certification package selecting the parent quarter re-runs its rollup, and both the invalidation's and the parent quarter's `version` counters increment. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal for an invalidation: a second withdrawal is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

One guard applies uniformly to every child write against a quarter. A quarter whose `validation_status` is already `INVALIDATED` accepts no invalidation creation, no invalidation withdrawal, and no event-exclusion attachment; all three are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Once a quarter has been written off wholesale, its hour ledger is closed.

Quarters can be read individually with the hour accounting recomputed and both dimensions shown, or listed with filters on `monitor_id`, `year`, `quarter`, `validation_status`, `submission_status` and `regulatory_use_eligible`. Each quarter also exposes two child listings: its invalidations, applied and withdrawn alike, ordered by `start_hour_index` and filterable by `status` and `invalidation_code`; and its exceptional-event exclusions, ordered by `start_hour_index` and filterable by `status`.

### 3.5. The Validation Dimension: Review, Rejection and Reopening

Quality-assurance review of a quarter is a small cyclic workflow owned entirely by the `QaOfficer`, and it is worth reading the cycle before the individual steps: a quarter travels `RAW → IN_VALIDATION → VALIDATED`, may be pulled back to `VALIDATED → REOPENED`, and from `REOPENED` re-enters `IN_VALIDATION` and can be validated again. `INVALIDATED` is the dimension's only terminal state.

Opening a quarter for review advances either `RAW → IN_VALIDATION` or `REOPENED → IN_VALIDATION`. The operation takes no request body. It clears `validated_by_user_id` to null, so a quarter under review never shows a stale approver, and it increments the quarter's `version`.

Accepting a quarter for regulatory use advances `IN_VALIDATION → VALIDATED` and may carry an optional `validation_note`. Four checks guard it, and they are evaluated in a fixed order so that the reported error is deterministic. The from-state check comes first: a quarter not in `IN_VALIDATION` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and a quarter already `INVALIDATED` is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Next comes the method-designation check of §3.1 — the reference method governing the quarter's instrument must be `DESIGNATED` or `CONDITIONAL`, effective at or before the logical clock, and not withdrawn as of the logical clock — failing which the answer is `ERR_METHOD_NOT_DESIGNATED` (HTTP 422). Third comes segregation of duties: the calling `X-User-ID` must not appear in the quarter's `invalidation_applied_by_user_ids`, failing which the answer is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Fourth and last comes quality-control posture: the quarter's instrument must not have `qc_posture` equal to `QC_DELINQUENT`, failing which the answer is `ERR_MONITOR_QC_DELINQUENT` (HTTP 422). Because the designation check precedes the segregation check and the segregation check precedes the posture check, an officer who both invalidated hours *and* whose instrument is delinquent sees the segregation refusal, not the posture refusal — and if the method has also been withdrawn, they see the designation refusal ahead of both.

On success `validated_by_user_id` is set to the calling `X-User-ID`, hour accounting is re-run so `completeness_pct` and `regulatory_use_eligible` are refreshed, every certification package that selects this quarter re-runs its rollup, `submission_status` is left exactly where it was because the two dimensions are independent, and the quarter's `version` increments.

Validating every raw quarter of one instrument in a single call is also available. The request names `monitor_id` and may narrow the selection with an optional `year`. The selector matches quarters of that instrument, optionally restricted to that year, whose `validation_status` is `RAW`; quarters sitting in `IN_VALIDATION`, `VALIDATED`, `REOPENED` or `INVALIDATED` are silently skipped rather than rejected. If the selector matches no raw quarter at all, the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_BATCHES` (HTTP 422). Each matched quarter is then put through the same four checks the single-quarter acceptance applies, evaluated per quarter, and the operation is transactional: the first quarter that fails any check rejects the entire request with that quarter's error and no quarter is advanced. When every matched quarter passes, each advances from `RAW` straight to `VALIDATED` as one atomic step — `IN_VALIDATION` is never observable for a bulk-validated quarter — each records `validated_by_user_id` as the calling `X-User-ID`, each re-runs hour accounting, and each has its `version` incremented. The response lists only the quarters actually transitioned, ordered by `year` and then `quarter`.

Rejecting a quarter wholesale advances either `IN_VALIDATION → INVALIDATED` or `REOPENED → INVALIDATED`, optionally with an `invalidation_note`. It sets `batch_invalidation_reason` to `QA_REJECTED`, clears `validated_by_user_id` to null, causes every certification package covering the quarter to re-run its rollup, and increments the quarter's `version`. `batch_invalidation_reason` is null in every validation standing other than `INVALIDATED`, and the only other value it ever takes is `MONITOR_DECOMMISSIONED`, which is set when the cascade of §3.9 writes off a quarter because its instrument was decommissioned.

Reopening a validated quarter advances `VALIDATED → REOPENED` and requires a `reopen_reason`. Certified data is immutable: if the quarter's `submission_status` is `CERTIFIED` the reopen is refused with `ERR_BATCH_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 409), and this check is what makes the certification step of §3.8 a genuine point of no return. A permitted reopen increments `reopen_count` by 1, clears `validated_by_user_id` to null, leaves `submission_status` untouched, and increments the quarter's `version`. It also reaches forward into the determination layer: every provisional design value for the quarter's site and pollutant whose three-year period includes the quarter's year — that is, whose `period_end_year` is the quarter's year, the year after, or two years after — moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `stale_reason` set to `BATCH_REVALIDATED`, and the site's `current_attainment_status` is recomputed from whatever rows remain.

### 3.6. Exceptional-Event Demonstrations and Hour Exclusion

Wildfires and dust storms produce real ambient concentrations that no control programme could have prevented, and federal policy allows those hours to be removed from regulatory determinations if the state can demonstrate the event. A `ProgramManager` opens a demonstration for one `site_id` and one `pollutant_id`, declaring an `event_type` of `WILDFIRE`, `HIGH_WIND_DUST`, `PRESCRIBED_FIRE` or `STRATOSPHERIC_INTRUSION`. The claimed window is given as `event_start_date`, the first instant of the event, and `event_end_date`, the last instant, which must be at or after the start; the window is treated as **closed on both ends**. A `narrative` between 1 and 4000 characters carries the conceptual-model argument, and `clear_causal_relationship` records whether the submitter asserts a clear causal relationship between the event and the exceedance.

Overlapping claims for the same phenomenon at the same site are not allowed, and the conflict test carries a one-day buffer on each side so that two claims cannot be filed nose-to-tail. A new demonstration is compared against every other demonstration for the same `site_id` and the same `event_type` whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `INFO_REQUESTED` or `CONCURRED` — a demonstration already non-concurred no longer blocks anything. The new claim is rejected when both of the following hold: the new `event_start_date` less 24 hours is at or before the other demonstration's `event_end_date`, and the other demonstration's `event_start_date` less 24 hours is at or before the new `event_end_date`. The rejection is `ERR_EVENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). A new demonstration enters `status` `DRAFT` with `info_request_count` at 0 and `excluded_hour_total` at 0.

Under a demonstration the manager nominates the specific hours to be removed. Each nomination names `data_batch_id` for the quarter it touches and supplies `start_hour_index` and `end_hour_index` on the same half-open, zero-based convention used for invalidations, and it records `demonstration_id` for the demonstration that justifies it. Three range rules apply. The bounds rule is identical to the invalidation bounds rule — `start_hour_index` at least 0, `end_hour_index` strictly greater than it, `end_hour_index` no greater than the quarter's `scheduled_hour_count` — and a breach yields `ERR_INVALIDATION_RANGE_OUT_OF_BOUNDS` (HTTP 422). The collision rule forbids the exclusion range from intersecting the range of any applied invalidation on the same quarter, because an hour cannot be both invalid and excluded; a collision yields `ERR_EXCLUSION_OVERLAPS_INVALIDATION` (HTTP 409). The containment rule requires the exclusion to lie inside the demonstration's event window projected onto the quarter's own hour axis: taking the first instant of the quarter as hour zero, `start_hour_index` must be at least the whole number of hours from the start of the quarter to `event_start_date`, and `end_hour_index` must be no greater than the whole number of hours from the start of the quarter to `event_end_date` plus 24 hours — the extra day being the same closing-day allowance the conflict test uses. The quarter must also wholly contain that projected window. A nomination outside the projected window is rejected with `ERR_EVENT_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_BATCH_PERIOD` (HTTP 422). As stated in §3.4, no nomination may attach to a quarter whose `validation_status` is already `INVALIDATED`.

An accepted nomination is created with `status` `PROPOSED`, `hours_affected` set to `end_hour_index` less `start_hour_index`, and `effective_at` null. Crucially, nothing else moves: the demonstration's `excluded_hour_total` is unchanged because only effective exclusions count toward it, and the target quarter's hour accounting is unchanged for exactly the same reason. Proposed hours are a claim, not yet a deduction.

Submitting the demonstration for regional review advances `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, optionally with a `submission_note`. Two conditions guard the first submission: at least one exclusion with `status` `PROPOSED` must exist under the demonstration, and `clear_causal_relationship` must be true. A submission failing either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Should the reviewer come back for more information, the demonstration is returned `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED` and `info_request_count` increments by 1; the reviewer must supply an `info_request_note`, and the proposed exclusions stay `PROPOSED`, so hour accounting remains untouched throughout the exchange. The manager then resubmits, advancing `INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` and closing the review cycle. The resubmission re-checks only the proposed-exclusion clause — at least one exclusion must still be `PROPOSED` — and does **not** re-test `clear_causal_relationship`, which was settled at first submission.

The determination is the `EpaRegionalReviewer`'s and carries a `determination` of either `CONCUR` or `NON_CONCUR` plus an optional `determination_note`. Both branches advance out of `SUBMITTED`, both stamp `determined_by_user_id` from the calling `X-User-ID` and `determined_at` from the logical clock, both increment the demonstration's `version`, and both are terminal: `CONCURRED` and `NON_CONCURRED` admit no further transition, and a second determination is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

A concurrence advances `SUBMITTED → CONCURRED` and sets off a five-hop cascade. The first hop is the demonstration's own state change. In the second hop, every exclusion under this demonstration whose `status` is `PROPOSED` moves to `EFFECTIVE`, has `effective_at` stamped from the logical clock, and has its `version` incremented; the demonstration's `excluded_hour_total` is then recomputed as the sum of `hours_affected` across its effective exclusions. In the third hop, every quarter referenced by those newly effective exclusions re-runs hour accounting, which raises `event_excluded_hour_count`, lowers `adjusted_scheduled_hour_count` by the same amount, and refreshes `completeness_pct` and `regulatory_use_eligible` accordingly. In the fourth hop, because those quarters' completeness figures changed, every provisional design value for the affected quarter's site and pollutant whose `period_end_year` is the quarter's year, the year after, or two years after moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `stale_reason` set to `EVENT_EXCLUSION_APPLIED`. In the fifth hop, each affected site's `current_attainment_status` is recomputed, falling back to `UNDETERMINED` when no provisional or final row survives.

A non-concurrence advances `SUBMITTED → NON_CONCURRED` and stops far earlier. Every exclusion under the demonstration whose `status` is `PROPOSED` moves to `REJECTED` with its `version` incremented, and `effective_at` stays null. No quarter's hour accounting changes, no design value is superseded, and no site's attainment standing moves — the divergence between the two branches is precisely the third, fourth and fifth hops. `EFFECTIVE` and `REJECTED` are both terminal for an exclusion; once decided, an exclusion never changes again.

Demonstrations can be read individually, with `excluded_hour_total` refreshed, or listed with filters on `site_id`, `pollutant_id`, `event_type` and `status`. A demonstration's own exclusions are listed ordered by `data_batch_id` and then `start_hour_index`, filterable by `status` and by `data_batch_id`.

### 3.7. Design Values and Attainment Determination

A design value is the statistic compared against the national standard, and computing one is the `ProgramManager`'s act. The request names `site_id`, `pollutant_id`, `period_end_year` — the last calendar year of the three-year period, at least 2000 and at most 2100 — a `variant`, and the `expected_monitoring_site_version` described in §2.7. Once the version check has passed, two further gates apply and their order is fixed: the primary-sampler selection described immediately below must find a candidate, and only after it succeeds does the system check that no design value for the same site, pollutant, `period_end_year` and `variant` has already been finalized. A computation against a finalized key is refused with `ERR_DESIGN_VALUE_FINALIZED` (HTTP 409), but a site with no eligible primary sampler surfaces its own refusal first.

**Choosing the primary sampler.** A site may carry several co-located instruments, and only one of them supplies the regulatory record. The candidate pool is the instruments at the requested site whose reference method measures the requested pollutant, whose `is_primary_for_design_value` flag is true, and whose lifecycle status is `OPERATING`, `OUT_OF_SERVICE` or `DECOMMISSIONED`. Instruments still `PENDING_APPROVAL` or merely `APPROVED` are excluded, because they have never produced regulatory data — and a decommissioned instrument is deliberately kept in the pool, because the data it produced before shutdown remains part of the record. If the pool is empty the request is refused with `ERR_PRIMARY_MONITOR_MISSING` (HTTP 422). Otherwise the candidates are ordered by `poc` ascending and, where two share a `poc`, by `created_at` ascending; the first is recorded as `primary_monitor_id`. The tie-break is on creation time and never on identifier, and creation times are compared at millisecond resolution.

**Building the annual statistics.** The three years of the period are the `period_end_year` less 2, the `period_end_year` less 1, and the `period_end_year` itself. Each year is examined independently, and only the selected primary instrument's quarters count. A quarter qualifies for a year when it belongs to that instrument, falls in that year, has `validation_status` `VALIDATED`, and has `regulatory_use_eligible` true. Quarters that are `RAW`, `IN_VALIDATION`, `REOPENED` or `INVALIDATED` are excluded, and so is any quarter below the completeness threshold. A year that does not have all four of its qualifying quarters contributes no annual statistic at all and is simply skipped; `complete_year_count` is the number of the three years that did have four qualifying quarters.

For each complete year one raw annual figure is formed, and which figure depends on the pollutant's `standard_form`. When the standard form is `ANNUAL_MEAN`, the raw annual figure is the sum of the four quarterly concentrations divided by 4. When it is `FOURTH_MAX_8HR`, the raw annual figure is the largest `fourth_high_daily_max` among the year's four quarters. When it is `PERCENTILE_98`, the raw annual figure is the highest of the four quarterly concentrations. That raw figure is then rounded to the pollutant's `rounding_decimals` and appended to `annual_statistics`. This is the **first rounding step, and it happens once per year** — three roundings for a three-year period. The array is ordered earliest year first: its first element belongs to the `period_end_year` less 2 and its last belongs to the `period_end_year`. When no year is complete the array is empty.

**Forming the design value.** If `complete_year_count` does not equal the pollutant's `design_value_years`, `design_value` is null and `attainment_status` is `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`. Otherwise the already-rounded annual statistics are summed and divided by `design_value_years`, and that mean is then **truncated** toward zero to the pollutant's `rounding_decimals` to produce `design_value`. Truncation, not rounding, is the second step, so the design value never rises above the mean of the annual statistics. The verdict follows from a strict comparison: `attainment_status` is `NONATTAINMENT` when `design_value` is strictly greater than the pollutant's `primary_standard_value`, and `ATTAINMENT` otherwise — a design value exactly equal to the standard is attainment.

The two-step rounding order is normative and its alternative is forbidden. Averaging all twelve unrounded quarterly values across the three years and rounding once at the end is a *different* procedure and must not be implemented. To see that the difference is not academic, consider a pollutant with one decimal of precision, a standard level of 9.0, and quarterly means of 9.2, 9.4, 9.3 and 9.1 in 2023; 9.0, 9.1, 9.2 and 8.9 in 2024; and 8.9, 9.0, 9.1 and 8.8 in 2025. The normative order yields annual statistics of 9.3, 9.1 and 9.0, a mean of 9.13333, a truncated design value of 9.1 and a verdict of `NONATTAINMENT`. The forbidden order yields per-year means of 9.25, 9.05 and 8.95, an overall mean of 9.08333, a design value of 9.0 and a verdict of `ATTAINMENT`. The order of operations decides the regulatory outcome.

**The two variants.** `variant` selects which quarterly concentration each qualifying quarter contributes and takes one of two values. Under `WITH_EVENT_DATA`, every quarter contributes its `quarterly_mean_concentration` regardless of whether the quarter carries an effective event exclusion — exceptional-event hours still influence the reported mean. Under `EXCLUDING_CONCURRED_EVENTS`, a quarter that is referenced by at least one exclusion whose `status` is `EFFECTIVE` contributes its `quarterly_mean_concentration_event_adjusted` instead, while every other quarter still contributes its `quarterly_mean_concentration`. Nothing else differs between the variants: the qualifying-quarter filter, the four-quarters-per-year requirement, the standard-form branch, the two rounding steps and the strict attainment comparison are identical. Continuing the example above, replacing the 9.2 of the third quarter of 2024 with an event-adjusted 7.9 moves the annual statistics to 9.3, 8.7 and 9.0, the design value to 9.0, and the verdict to `ATTAINMENT` — which is exactly the point of demonstrating an exceptional event.

A completed computation is stored with `status` `PROVISIONAL`. Any earlier design value for the same site, pollutant, `period_end_year` and `variant` that was still `PROVISIONAL` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `stale_reason` set to `RECOMPUTED` and its `version` incremented; the referenced site's `version` increments by 1 and its `current_attainment_status` is refreshed. `stale_reason` is null unless the row is superseded, and takes one of `EVENT_EXCLUSION_APPLIED`, `BATCH_REVALIDATED` or `RECOMPUTED` depending on which of the three paths of §3.5, §3.6 or this subsection retired the row.

Finalization is the `EpaRegionalReviewer`'s act and advances `PROVISIONAL → FINAL`, optionally recording a `finalization_note`. It is permitted only when `complete_year_count` equals the pollutant's `design_value_years`; a row resting on fewer complete years is refused with `ERR_DESIGN_VALUE_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). On success `finalized_by_user_id` is stamped from the calling `X-User-ID`, the site's `current_attainment_status` is refreshed from this row, and both the design value's and the site's `version` counters increment. `FINAL` and `SUPERSEDED` are both terminal — a finalized or superseded row admits no further transition, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

Design values can be read individually or listed with filters on `site_id`, `pollutant_id`, `primary_monitor_id`, `period_end_year`, `variant` and `status`.

### 3.8. Annual Certification and the Submission Dimension

Each calendar year of data is certified to the national database one network and one pollutant at a time. A `ProgramManager` opens the package by naming `network_id`, `pollutant_id` and `year`; the combination must be unique, so a network cannot hold two packages for the same pollutant and year, and `year` must be at least 2000 and at most 2100. Two things are fixed at that moment. First, `certification_deadline` is pinned to the first of May of the year following `year`, at midnight UTC. Second, the package's rollup is computed for the first time. The package enters `status` `DRAFT` with `rejection_count` at 0.

**The rollup** selects the package's member quarters and summarises them, and it is re-run at four moments: when the package is opened, when it is submitted, when it is certified, and whenever any selected quarter changes its validation standing or re-runs hour accounting. The selection walks the graph rather than storing a membership list: a quarter is selected when its instrument's site belongs to the package's network, when the pollutant of that instrument's reference method is the package's pollutant, when the quarter's `year` equals the package's `year`, and when the quarter's `validation_status` is `VALIDATED`. Quarters in `RAW`, `IN_VALIDATION`, `REOPENED` or `INVALIDATED` are excluded from every counter — which is why writing off a member quarter lowers `included_batch_count` rather than raising a mismatch. Selected quarters are ordered by `year`, then `quarter`, then `created_at`; identifiers are never used to order them.

From that selection, `included_batch_count` is the number of selected quarters and `eligible_batch_count` is the number of them whose `regulatory_use_eligible` is true. `rollup_completeness_pct` is computed from summed hours, not by averaging per-quarter percentages: sum `valid_hour_count` across the selected quarters, sum `adjusted_scheduled_hour_count` across the same quarters, divide the first total by the second, multiply by 100 and round to one decimal place — with the value taken as 0.0 when the second total is zero. Finally, `certification_ready` is true exactly when `included_batch_count` is at least 1 **and** `eligible_batch_count` equals `included_batch_count`; a single member quarter below its pollutant's completeness threshold makes the whole package unready, as does a package with no members at all.

Submitting the package advances `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, optionally with a `submission_note`. The rollup is refreshed immediately before the readiness gate is applied, and the gate requires `certification_ready` to be true; a package that is not ready is refused with `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_READY` (HTTP 422). On success every selected quarter whose `submission_status` is `UNSUBMITTED` advances to `SUBMITTED` while its `validation_status` stays at `VALIDATED` — the dimensions remain parallel and independent — and both the package's `version` and each moved quarter's `version` increment by 1.

Certification advances `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED` and may carry a `certification_statement`. Three gates guard it, and their order follows §2.11. The state gate comes first: every quarter in the package's scope that is already in `submission_status` `SUBMITTED` must still have `validation_status` `VALIDATED`, so a quarter reopened after the package was submitted blocks certification with `ERR_MEMBER_BATCH_NOT_VALIDATED` (HTTP 409). The segregation gate comes next: the calling `X-User-ID` must not be the `validated_by_user_id` of any quarter the package selected, failing which the answer is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The temporal gate comes last: the logical clock must be **strictly before** `certification_deadline`, so an attempt at exactly midnight on the first of May of the following year is already too late and is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 422). On success `certified_by_user_id` and `certified_at` are stamped, every selected quarter whose `submission_status` is `SUBMITTED` advances to `CERTIFIED`, and each of those quarters becomes permanently unreopenable as described in §3.5. Both the package's `version` and each moved quarter's `version` increment. `CERTIFIED` is terminal for a package.

Rejection belongs to the `EpaRegionalReviewer`, requires a `rejection_reason`, and returns the package `SUBMITTED → DRAFT` rather than moving it to any distinct rejected state. `rejection_count` increments by 1, and — this is the cascade that catches implementers out — every quarter matching the package's network, pollutant and year whose `submission_status` is `SUBMITTED` is demoted back to `UNSUBMITTED`, while its `validation_status` stays at `VALIDATED`. A rejected submission does not un-validate data; it only withdraws it from the national database. Both the package's `version` and each demoted quarter's `version` increment. Because a returned package can be reworked and resubmitted, the `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → DRAFT` cycle can be traversed repeatedly, with `rejection_count` recording how often.

The submission dimension therefore has exactly three moves, all of them driven by the package rather than by the quarter: `UNSUBMITTED → SUBMITTED` when a package that selected the quarter is submitted and the quarter is `VALIDATED`; `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED` when the certifying package selected the quarter; and `SUBMITTED → UNSUBMITTED` whenever a package covering the quarter returns to `DRAFT`, whether by explicit rejection or by the network-change cascade of §3.9. In all three moves the quarter's `validation_status` is left untouched and its `version` increments by 1.

Packages can be read individually with a freshly recomputed rollup, or listed with filters on `network_id`, `pollutant_id`, `year` and `status`.

### 3.9. Network Assessment, Relocation, Suspension and Shutdown

No site may be moved or retired on the state's own authority. A network must first assess itself, the regional office must approve that assessment, and only then may a change request citing it be granted. This subsection covers the assessment cycle, the change-request workflow and the multi-hop cascade an approval fires.

**Opening an assessment cycle** is a `ProgramManager` action naming `network_id` and `cycle_year`, which must be at least 2000 and at most 2100 and unique for the network — one assessment per network per cycle year. Adequacy scoring runs immediately, and the cycle enters `status` `DRAFT` with `return_count` at 0.

**Adequacy scoring** proceeds in four steps. The first step converts the network's `metropolitan_population` into `required_population_site_count`, the number of population-oriented sites the network owes: a population below five hundred thousand requires 1, a population from five hundred thousand up to but not including one million requires 2, a population from one million up to but not including two and a half million requires 3, and a population of two and a half million or more requires 4. The second step credits the network's actual site mix, counting only sites whose `status` is `OPERATING` — proposed, siting-approved, suspended, shut-down and relocated sites earn nothing. Population credit is the number of operating `POPULATION_ORIENTED` sites, capped at `required_population_site_count`. Source credit is the number of operating `SOURCE_ORIENTED` sites, capped at the single source-oriented site every network is required to run. Background credit is the number of operating `BACKGROUND` sites, capped at the single background site every network is required to run. `TRANSPORT` sites earn no credit in this scoring. The three credits are summed and divided by the requirement total — `required_population_site_count` plus one required source site plus one required background site — and `coverage_ratio` is that quotient rounded to four decimal places. Because each credit is individually capped, surplus sites of one class can never compensate for a missing site of another.

The third step degrades that coverage by two multiplicative penalty factors, each of which is itself capped. The quality-control factor is 1 less the smaller of thirty hundredths and one tenth per delinquent monitor — that is, ten percentage points are deducted for each monitor counted in the network's `delinquent_monitor_count`, but the deduction stops at thirty percentage points, so a fourth delinquent monitor costs nothing more than the third. The siting factor is 1 less the smaller of twenty hundredths and one twentieth per waivered site — five percentage points per site counted in `waiver_required_site_count`, capped at twenty percentage points, so the penalty saturates at four waivered sites. `adequacy_score` is `coverage_ratio` multiplied by the quality-control factor, multiplied by the siting factor, multiplied by 100, and rounded to one decimal place. The fourth step bands the result into `adequacy_tier`: a score of 90.0 or above is `ADEQUATE`, a score below 90.0 but at or above 75.0 is `CONDITIONAL`, and anything below 75.0 is `INADEQUATE`. Both band boundaries are inclusive at their lower edge, so a score of exactly 90.0 is `ADEQUATE` and a score of exactly 75.0 is `CONDITIONAL`.

**Submitting the assessment** advances `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, optionally with a `submission_note`. Scoring is re-run from the network's present census immediately before submission, refreshing `required_population_site_count`, `coverage_ratio`, `adequacy_score` and `adequacy_tier`, so a site that came into operation between opening and submitting is credited. `submitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The submission also reaches the network itself: `network_status` becomes `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` and `current_assessment_year` becomes the assessment's `cycle_year`, and both rows' `version` counters increment.

**The regional decision** on an assessment carries a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `RETURN`, plus an optional `decision_note`. `APPROVE` advances `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, stamps `approved_at` from the logical clock and `approved_by_user_id` from the calling `X-User-ID`, and — critically — makes every change request citing this assessment eligible for approval. `RETURN` advances `SUBMITTED → DRAFT` and increments `return_count` by 1; a returned assessment goes back to draft rather than to any distinct returned state, so the `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → DRAFT` cycle can be traversed repeatedly. Under **either** decision the network's `network_status` returns to `ACTIVE`, and under either decision both the assessment's and the network's `version` counters increment. `APPROVED` is terminal for an assessment.

**Raising a change request** is again the `ProgramManager`'s act. The request names `site_id` for the target site, a `request_type` of `RELOCATION`, `SHUTDOWN` or `SEASONAL_SUSPENSION`, a `justification` between 1 and 2000 characters, and `assessment_id` for the assessment cited as justification, whose approval standing is checked at decision time rather than at creation. `replacement_site_id` names the site that will take over monitoring after a relocation; it is required when `request_type` is `RELOCATION` and must be absent when `request_type` is `SHUTDOWN` or `SEASONAL_SUSPENSION`. Either mistake — a relocation without a replacement, or a shutdown or seasonal suspension carrying one — is refused with `ERR_REPLACEMENT_SITE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). A new request enters `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_request_count` at 0.

Submitting the request advances `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, optionally with a `submission_note`, and requires that the target site is still `OPERATING`; a request submitted against a site in any other status is refused with `ERR_SITE_NOT_OPERATING` (HTTP 409).

**The regional decision** on a change request carries a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `DENY` or `REQUEST_REVISION`, plus an optional `decision_note`. `REQUEST_REVISION` advances `SUBMITTED → DRAFT` and increments `revision_request_count` by 1, closing the revision cycle in the same fashion as a returned assessment. `DENY` advances `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, stamps `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at`, leaves `effective_at` null, and changes nothing whatsoever on the target site — the deny branch has no cascade at all. `APPROVED` and `DENIED` are both terminal for a change request.

`APPROVE` is the heavy path. Three gates guard it and they are evaluated in the order §2.11 lays down. First the state gate: the target site must still be `OPERATING`, or the answer is `ERR_SITE_NOT_OPERATING` (HTTP 409). Then the referential gates, in priority order: the cited assessment must exist, have `status` `APPROVED` and belong to the same network as the target site, or the answer is `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 422); and for a relocation the nominated replacement site must have `status` `SITING_APPROVED` or `OPERATING` and belong to the same network, or the answer is `ERR_REPLACEMENT_SITE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). When all three pass, the request advances `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at` and `effective_at` are all stamped from the calling identity and the logical clock, and the request-type-specific cascade fires within the same request.

For a `SEASONAL_SUSPENSION`, the first hop drives the site `OPERATING → SUSPENDED` and stamps `suspended_at` from the logical clock; the network's `operating_site_count` decrements by 1 and its `suspended_site_count` increments by 1, with `total_site_count` unchanged. The second hop, fired because the site became suspended, moves every instrument at that site whose `monitor_status` is `OPERATING` to `OUT_OF_SERVICE` with `out_of_service_since` stamped from the logical clock. The third hop, fired because those instruments left service, recomputes the site's `operating_monitor_count` and the network's `delinquent_monitor_count`. Both the site's and the network's `version` counters increment, along with each moved instrument's.

Reinstatement, introduced in §3.2, is the exact inverse and carries its own two follow-on hops. Clearing `suspended_at` to null moves the network's `suspended_site_count` down by 1 and its `operating_site_count` up by 1, again leaving `total_site_count` untouched. The second hop then returns to `OPERATING` every instrument at the site whose `monitor_status` is `OUT_OF_SERVICE` **and** whose `out_of_service_since` equals the value `suspended_at` held immediately before it was cleared — which is precisely the set the suspension pulled out of service, and deliberately not an instrument a technician had taken out of service for its own reasons beforehand. Each such instrument adds to `out_of_service_day_count` the whole days elapsed from its `out_of_service_since` to the logical clock and then clears `out_of_service_since`. The third hop recomputes both `qc_posture` and `qc_posture_in_service` on each of those instruments and recomputes the network's `delinquent_monitor_count`.

For a `SHUTDOWN`, the first hop drives the site `OPERATING → SHUTDOWN`; the network's `operating_site_count` decrements by 1 and its `shutdown_site_count` increments by 1, with `total_site_count` unchanged. The second hop decommissions every instrument at the site whose `monitor_status` is `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED`, `OPERATING` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, moving each to `DECOMMISSIONED`; an instrument that was `OPERATING` also decrements the site's `operating_monitor_count`, and an instrument that was `OUT_OF_SERVICE` closes out its accrual, adding the whole days elapsed since `out_of_service_since` to `out_of_service_day_count` and clearing `out_of_service_since`. The third hop, fired because those instruments were decommissioned, moves every quarter of theirs whose `validation_status` is `RAW`, `IN_VALIDATION` or `REOPENED` to `INVALIDATED` with `batch_invalidation_reason` set to `MONITOR_DECOMMISSIONED`; quarters already `VALIDATED` or already `INVALIDATED` are left alone. The fourth hop, fired because those quarters were written off, makes every certification package that had selected any of them re-run its rollup — which drops `certification_ready` to false — and any such package whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` returns to `DRAFT` with `rejection_count` incremented by 1. The fifth hop, fired because those packages returned to draft, demotes every quarter matching such a package's network, pollutant and year whose `submission_status` is `SUBMITTED` back to `UNSUBMITTED`. Every row touched at every hop has its `version` incremented, and the site's and network's counters are all recomputed.

For a `RELOCATION`, the first hop drives the site `OPERATING → RELOCATED`; the network's `operating_site_count` decrements by 1 and its `relocated_site_count` increments by 1, with `total_site_count` unchanged. The second hop sets `replaces_site_id` on the nominated replacement site to the retiring site's identifier, incrementing the replacement site's `version` as well as the retiring site's. The third hop decommissions every instrument at the retiring site from `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED`, `OPERATING` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE` to `DECOMMISSIONED`, exactly as a shutdown does, and the fourth and fifth hops then continue through the quarters, the certification packages and the submission dimension in precisely the same way as the shutdown cascade — a relocation is as destructive to unfinished data as a shutdown is, and the only structural difference between the two is the site's terminal state and the `replaces_site_id` link the relocation leaves behind. The network's `version` increments once for the decision.

Change requests can be read individually or listed with filters on `site_id`, `replacement_site_id`, `assessment_id`, `request_type` and `status`. Assessments can be read individually with their adequacy scoring recomputed.
