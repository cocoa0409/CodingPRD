# Gluten-Free Certification & Facility Audit API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A gluten-free certification body sells exactly one thing: the credibility of a mark printed on a package. That credibility is manufactured by a long chain of evidence — a supplier's allergen statement, a certificate of analysis, a process flow diagram, an auditor's walk of a shared production line, a swab taken off a cleaned mixer, a laboratory result on a randomly pulled retail lot — and it is destroyed the moment any single link in that chain is allowed to go stale unnoticed. The certification organization that commissioned this system operates today on spreadsheets and shared mailboxes. Reviewers approve ingredients whose supporting documents expired the week before; auditors close facility audits without knowing that a critical finding has already been raised against the site; certificates keep sitting in the public register while the supplier behind a key ingredient has been suspended for undisclosed wheat handling. Every one of those failures is a recall waiting to happen, and every recall costs the program more trust than a year of clean audits earns it.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces those spreadsheets. The service owns the whole certification lifecycle: applicant and product intake, ingredient and supplier documentation review, selection of the numeric gluten threshold each product must meet, the lot sampling plan that proves it keeps meeting it, on-site facility audits of shared equipment, air handling and sanitation, certificate issuance with mark tier and label rules, annual surveillance and unannounced retail testing, nonconformance handling with a bounded corrective-action loop, mandatory notification of ingredient and supplier changes, and consumer complaint investigation up to and including recall coordination. All of it is mounted under the `/gfco` prefix.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, **evidence propagates**: when a supplier is suspended, the suspension must travel — without any operator intervention — down through the ingredients that supplier feeds, up through any compound ingredient those ingredients are components of, across into every product formula that consumes them, and out to the certificates and label marks those products carry. Second, **decisions are separated**: the officer who issues a certificate may not be the same person who cleared the product formula or closed the facility's most recent audit, and the reviewer who accepts a corrective-action plan may not be the person who raised the finding or the person who later verifies that the fix worked. A certification body that lets one person walk a file from complaint to clean certificate has no certification program at all, and the API enforces that structurally rather than by policy memo.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Program Roles and Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below spelled exactly, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When both headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Authorization is decided from the headers alone; there is no session, token or login step.

The six roles are:

- **`ApplicantContact`** — the certified company's own representative. Declares manufacturing sites, ingredients, supplier documents, products and formula lines, submits ingredients for review, answers nonconformances with corrective-action plans, and files change notifications.
- **`CertificationReviewer`** — the program's desk reviewer. Verifies supplier documents, reviews ingredients, product formulas and labels, activates testing plans, decides corrective-action plans, assesses change notifications, and triages and investigates consumer complaints.
- **`FieldAuditor`** — the on-site auditor. Schedules, starts and closes facility audits, records sanitation validations, raises nonconformances, and verifies corrective-action effectiveness on site.
- **`CertificationDecisionMaker`** — the certification authority. The only role that may issue, suspend, reinstate or revoke a certificate.
- **`LabTechnician`** — the accredited laboratory user. Records lot test results and reads products and lot tests; touches nothing else.
- **`ProgramAdministrator`** — program operations. Registers applicants and suppliers, changes supplier standing, files consumer complaints, runs the surveillance sweep and drives the logical clock.

When `X-User-Role` is `ApplicantContact`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Applicant`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Applicant` compare that resource's applicant foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, an `ApplicantContact` may only act where the owning applicant reached from the target resource — the `applicant_id` of a facility, of an ingredient, of a product or of a nonconformance, or the `applicant_id` of the certificate named by a change notification — equals `X-User-ID`. When it does not, the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `CertificationReviewer`, `FieldAuditor`, `CertificationDecisionMaker`, `LabTechnician` or `ProgramAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever a workflow attributes a decision to its author — as `verified_by_user_id` on a supplier document, `reviewed_by_user_id` on an ingredient, `formula_cleared_by_user_id` and `label_reviewed_by_user_id` on a product, `lead_auditor_user_id` and `closed_by_user_id` on an audit, `issued_by_user_id` on a certificate, `raised_by_user_id` and `ca_decided_by_user_id` on a nonconformance, `assessed_by_user_id` on a change notification, and `investigated_by_user_id` on a consumer complaint — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`. Those recorded identifiers are what the segregation-of-duties rules of §2.8 later read.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Ordering and Collection Filters

Every collection endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be below 1 and may not exceed 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Collections are always returned sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, so the oldest row appears first and a stable page boundary is guaranteed across successive requests. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as they were resolved for the request, `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filters before pagination, and `has_more` as a boolean that is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

Beyond `limit` and `offset`, each collection accepts a small set of optional equality filters, and supplying several of them narrows the result by all of them at once. Facilities may be filtered by `applicant_id` and by `sanitation_validation_status`. Supplier documents may be filtered by `supplier_id`, by the evidenced `ingredient_id`, and by `status`. Ingredients may be filtered by the declaring `applicant_id`, by `supplier_id`, by the compound `parent_ingredient_id`, and by `status`. Products may be filtered by `applicant_id`, by `facility_id`, and by `formula_status`. Product-to-ingredient lines may be filtered by `product_id` and by `ingredient_id`. Lot tests may be filtered by `product_id`, by `test_type`, and by `verdict`. Audits may be filtered by `facility_id`, by `status`, and by `audit_type`. Certificates may be filtered by `product_id`, by `applicant_id`, by `status`, and by `surveillance_status`. Nonconformances may be filtered by `applicant_id`, by `certificate_id`, and by any of the three polymorphic source keys `audit_id`, `lot_test_id` and `complaint_id`, as well as by `status` and by `severity`. Change notifications may be filtered by `certificate_id`, by `replacement_ingredient_id`, and by `status`. Consumer complaints may be filtered by `certificate_id`, by `status`, and by `triage_priority`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every resource the service persists carries a server-minted `id` in canonical UUID form with no type prefix and no client influence over its value, together with a `created_at` timestamp recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the current logical instant on every mutation that changes the row and rendered with three fractional digits exactly as `created_at` is, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `version` is the value the stale-read protections of §2.7 compare against.

`created_at` and `updated_at` are the only resource fields rendered with a fractional part, and the `api_request_timestamp` of §2.2 is the only other place three fractional digits appear. Every other datetime the API surfaces is rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant at second precision with a trailing `Z` and no fractional digits at all, in the shape §2.5 uses (`2027-08-11T00:00:00Z`) — this covers the dated fields of §3 such as `issued_on`, `expires_on`, `surveillance_due_on`, `suspended_on`, `revoked_on`, `verified_on`, `approved_on`, `sanitation_validated_on`, `sanitation_expires_on`, `onsite_start_at`, `onsite_end_at`, `closed_on`, `last_closed_audit_closed_on`, `ca_due_on`, `sample_collected_on`, `last_scheduled_sample_on`, `exposure_date`, `reported_on`, `effective_on`, `assessed_on` and `current_cycle_started_on`, and equally the `run_at` of the surveillance-sweep summary and the `current_utc` the clock endpoint answers with. A value a caller supplied with fractional digits is echoed back at second precision, the sub-second part simply omitted.

One resource is deliberately leaner. A product-to-ingredient formula line is immutable once written: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`. There is no endpoint that edits or removes such a line, so a formula is corrected by adding lines rather than by rewriting them, and the recomputed counters on the owning product are the only observable consequence of a new line.

Reads never mutate. A read of a certificate does not advance its surveillance dimension, a read of a facility does not expire its sanitation validation, and a read of a nonconformance does not escalate it past its due date; all clock-driven change is materialized by the surveillance sweep described in §3.10 and never by observation.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this program is dated from wall-clock time. The server keeps a **logical clock** that starts at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` when the process boots and never falls back to real time. Every deadline, expiry, look-back window, grace period and freshness check in this specification reads that clock: an audit may not be started before its on-site start instant has been reached, a supplier document expires relative to it, a corrective action becomes overdue against it, and a certificate's term and surveillance date are measured from it.

A `ProgramAdministrator` may move the clock forward or backward through the test-driving clock endpoint by supplying `to_iso8601`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant; the endpoint answers with the resulting `current_utc` and mutates nothing else. Advancing the clock is deliberately inert — no certificate expires, no document lapses and no nonconformance escalates merely because the clock moved. Clock-driven consequences are materialized only when the surveillance sweep runs, which is what makes clock-dependent behaviour reproducible: set the clock, then sweep, then observe.

Day arithmetic is exact rather than calendar-approximate. Adding a number of days to an instant adds that many 24-hour periods, so an instant 365 days after `2026-08-11T00:00:00Z` is exactly `2027-08-11T00:00:00Z`. Window boundaries are stated explicitly wherever they appear, because in almost every rule in this document the boundary case is the interesting one.

### 2.6. The Order in Which a Request Is Judged

A single write can be rejectable for a dozen reasons at once, and the program's auditors need the answer to be deterministic: the same request against the same state must always produce the same `error_id`. The service therefore evaluates checks in one fixed ladder and returns the **first** failure it reaches, never a later one. The ladder below is that order, from highest precedence to lowest. Where a rule's condition is restated later in §3 alongside the workflow it guards, the restatement is a reminder, not a second, independent check.

1. **Authentication.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared role names — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Request shape.** On every write endpoint, each non-optional input field is present, each enum-typed field carries one of its declared values, `inclusion_pct` is greater than 0 and at most 100, `ppm_result` and `swab_ppm` are at least 0, `onsite_end_at` is strictly after `onsite_start_at`, and every datetime is a well-formed ISO 8601 value with a `Z` offset. Any breach is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
4. **Path existence.** Every templated identifier in the URL resolves to a live row, checked strictly left to right so that the leftmost unresolvable segment decides the answer. The error is the not-found code of the entity that failed to resolve: `ERR_APPLICANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPLIER_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INGREDIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_INGREDIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NONCONFORMANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CHANGE_NOTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, each carrying HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint with a templated path segment.
5. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign key carried in the request body resolves to a live row, checked in the order the endpoint declares its inputs, again returning the matching not-found code with HTTP 404. This governs facility declaration, ingredient declaration, supplier-document upload, product declaration, formula-line linking, lot-test recording, audit scheduling, certificate issuance, nonconformance creation, change-notification filing and complaint filing. For nonconformance creation specifically the declared order is `applicant_id`, then `certificate_id`, then `audit_id`, then `lot_test_id`, then `complaint_id`, so a request naming two unresolvable keys reports the earlier one in that sequence.
6. **Resource ownership.** When the acting role is `ApplicantContact`, the owning applicant reached from the target resource equals `X-User-ID` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This guards facility declaration, ingredient declaration, ingredient submission for review, supplier-document upload, product declaration, formula-line linking, corrective-action plan submission and change-notification filing.
7. **Stale-read protection.** The supplied `expected_product_version` or `expected_nonconformance_version` equals the current `version` of the row it names — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_NONCONFORMANCE_VERSION`, both HTTP 412. This guards testing-plan activation, certificate issuance and corrective-action plan submission; see §2.7.
8. **Document already decided.** A supplier document offered for verification is still `PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_DOCUMENT_ALREADY_DECIDED` (409).
9. **Ingredient state.** The ingredient is in a state that permits the requested operation: submission for review requires `DRAFT`, `REJECTED` or `BLOCKED`; a review decision requires `UNDER_REVIEW`; linking the ingredient into a product formula requires any state other than `REJECTED`. A breach is `ERR_INVALID_INGREDIENT_STATE` (409).
10. **Product dimension state.** The product dimension the operation targets is in a permitted state: formula review requires `formula_status` of `DRAFT` or `REJECTED`; testing-plan activation requires `formula_status` of `CLEARED`; label review requires `label_status` of `NOT_SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED` or `APPROVED`; linking a formula line requires `formula_status` of `DRAFT` or `REJECTED`. A breach is `ERR_INVALID_PRODUCT_STATE` (409).
11. **Audit state.** Starting an audit requires `status` of `SCHEDULED` and a logical clock that has reached `onsite_start_at`; closing one requires `status` of `IN_PROGRESS` and a logical clock that has reached `onsite_end_at`. A breach is `ERR_INVALID_AUDIT_STATE` (409).
12. **Certificate not terminal.** For certificate suspension, certificate revocation, change-notification filing and complaint filing, the certificate's `status` is neither `REVOKED` nor `EXPIRED`, and suspension additionally requires `ISSUED`. A breach is `ERR_CERTIFICATE_TERMINAL` (409). This rule deliberately does not cover reinstatement: a terminal certificate offered for reinstatement is judged by the next rule instead, so the two never both fire on one request.
13. **Certificate suspended.** Reinstatement requires `status` of exactly `SUSPENDED`; every other value, including the terminal `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED`, fails with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_SUSPENDED` (409).
14. **Nonconformance state.** Submitting a corrective-action plan requires `OPEN` or `CA_REJECTED`; deciding a plan requires `CA_SUBMITTED`; verifying effectiveness requires `CA_ACCEPTED`. A breach is `ERR_INVALID_NONCONFORMANCE_STATE` (409).
15. **Complaint state.** Triage requires `RECEIVED` and recording an investigation outcome requires `INVESTIGATING` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE` (409).
16. **Change-notification state.** Assessment requires `SUBMITTED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CHANGE_NOTIFICATION_STATE` (409).
17. **Supplier standing actually changes.** A supplier standing change must request a `status` different from the supplier's current one — otherwise `ERR_SUPPLIER_STATUS_UNCHANGED` (409).
18. **Swab within limit.** On a sanitation validation, `swab_ppm` is at most 5 ppm; a swab of exactly 5 ppm is accepted and 5.1 ppm is not. A breach is `ERR_SANITATION_SWAB_ABOVE_LIMIT` (422).
19. **Sanitation currently validated.** The relevant facility's `sanitation_validation_status` is `VALIDATED` at the instant of the call; both `UNVALIDATED` and `EXPIRED` fail with `ERR_FACILITY_SANITATION_NOT_VALIDATED` (422). Three operations are guarded: closing an audit, issuing a certificate and reinstating a certificate.
20. **Facility has passed an audit.** At issuance, the product's facility has `last_closed_audit_outcome` of `CLOSED_PASS` or `CLOSED_CONDITIONAL`; both `NONE` (no audit has ever closed there) and `CLOSED_FAIL` fail with `ERR_FACILITY_AUDIT_NOT_PASSED` (422).
21. **No unresolved critical finding.** No nonconformance of the target certificate — or, at issuance, of the product's applicant referencing the product's facility audits — has `severity` of `CRITICAL` together with a `status` of `OPEN`, `CA_SUBMITTED`, `CA_REJECTED` or `ESCALATED`. A breach is `ERR_OPEN_CRITICAL_NONCONFORMANCE` (422), and it guards both certificate issuance and certificate reinstatement.
22. **Supplier not suspended.** The ingredient's supplier does not have `status` of `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED` (422). This guards ingredient submission for review, ingredient review decisions and formula-line linking.
23. **Required documents verified.** On an approving ingredient review, the ingredient's `verified_doc_types` covers every document class its risk class demands — otherwise `ERR_REQUIRED_SUPPLIER_DOCS_MISSING` (422). The required sets are given in §3.3.
24. **Certificate-of-analysis claim within cap.** On an approving document verification of a certificate of analysis, `lab_ppm_claim` is at most the cap for the referenced ingredient's risk class — otherwise `ERR_COA_PPM_CLAIM_TOO_HIGH` (422). A claim exactly at the cap is accepted.
25. **Linked ingredients approved.** Every ingredient referenced by a formula line of the product has `status` of `APPROVED` — otherwise `ERR_INGREDIENT_NOT_APPROVED` (422). This guards a clearing formula review and certificate issuance.
26. **Formula not empty.** The product's `linked_ingredient_count` is at least 1 — otherwise `ERR_NO_INGREDIENTS_LINKED` (422). This guards a clearing formula review and testing-plan activation.
27. **Testing plan active.** The product's `testing_plan_status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_TESTING_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE` (422). This guards lot-test recording and certificate issuance.
28. **Label approved.** At issuance the product's `label_status` is `APPROVED` — otherwise `ERR_LABEL_NOT_APPROVED` (422).
29. **Mark tier granted.** On an approving label review, the payload `mark_tier_granted` is present and equal to the product's `requested_mark_tier`; at issuance the product's `mark_tier_granted` is not `NONE` and equals `requested_mark_tier`. A breach is `ERR_MARK_TIER_NOT_GRANTED` (422).
30. **Corrective action against a live certificate.** When a nonconformance carries a `certificate_id`, that certificate's `status` is neither `REVOKED` nor `EXPIRED` at the moment a corrective-action plan is submitted — otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATE_TERMINAL_NO_CA` (422). A corrective action cannot be filed against a certificate that is no longer in force.
31. **Segregation of duties.** The `X-User-ID` performing the step has not already performed the incompatible upstream step on the same object, as specified in §2.8 — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). This guards certificate issuance, corrective-action decisions and effectiveness verification.
32. **Unique SKU.** A product's `sku` is unique within its owning `applicant_id` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU` (409).
33. **Unique formula line.** No formula line already links this `product_id` to this `ingredient_id` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRODUCT_INGREDIENT` (409).
34. **One live certificate per product.** No certificate of this `product_id` already has `status` of `ISSUED` or `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_CERTIFICATE` (409).
35. **On-site window free.** A newly scheduled audit's half-open interval from `onsite_start_at` up to but excluding `onsite_end_at` does not overlap the interval of any other audit of the same facility whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Because the interval is half-open, an audit starting exactly at an existing audit's `onsite_end_at` is accepted. A breach is `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
36. **Sample not in the future.** `sample_collected_on` is at or before the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_SAMPLE_DATE_IN_FUTURE` (422).
37. **Sampling exclusion window respected.** For a `SCHEDULED` lot only, `sample_collected_on` falls outside every exclusion interval described in §3.6 — otherwise `ERR_SAMPLING_EXCLUSION_WINDOW` (409). `UNANNOUNCED` and `FOR_CAUSE` lots are exempt.
38. **Documents still in force.** At verification the supplier document's `expires_on` is strictly after the logical clock, and at an approving ingredient review every document counted toward the required set has `expires_on` strictly after the logical clock. A breach is `ERR_SUPPLIER_DOCUMENT_EXPIRED` (422).
39. **Corrective action on time.** The logical clock is at or before the nonconformance's `ca_due_on` when a plan is submitted — otherwise `ERR_CORRECTIVE_ACTION_OVERDUE` (422).
40. **Change notice long enough.** For change types `INGREDIENT_SUBSTITUTION` and `SUPPLIER_CHANGE`, `effective_on` is at least 30 days after the logical clock; a notice exactly 30 days ahead is accepted and 29 days ahead is not. A breach is `ERR_LATE_CHANGE_NOTIFICATION` (422). `FORMULA_PCT_CHANGE` and `FACILITY_LINE_CHANGE` are exempt from this rejection and only raise the lateness flag.
41. **Applicant posture permits the write.** The owning applicant's `compliance_posture` is neither `PROBATION` nor `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_APPLICANT_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (422). This guards certificate issuance, certificate reinstatement and change-notification filing.
42. **Facility belongs to the applicant.** On product declaration, the referenced facility's `applicant_id` equals the `applicant_id` supplied on the product — otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_APPLICANT_MISMATCH` (422).
43. **Document matches the ingredient's supplier.** On document upload, the referenced ingredient's `supplier_id` equals the `supplier_id` supplied on the document — otherwise `ERR_DOCUMENT_SUPPLIER_MISMATCH` (422).
44. **Ingredient graph stays acyclic.** When `parent_ingredient_id` is supplied on ingredient declaration, it belongs to the same `applicant_id` and does not close a cycle in the parent chain — otherwise `ERR_INGREDIENT_GRAPH_CYCLE` (422).

Two consequences of this ordering are worth spelling out, because they are the cases operators will report as bugs. A request that is both unauthorized for its role and malformed in its body reports the role failure, not the shape failure. And an issuance attempt against an applicant on probation whose facility has never passed an audit reports the audit failure first, because the facility-audit rule sits above the posture rule in the ladder.

### 2.7. Stale-Read Protection on Plans, Issuance and Corrective Actions

Three writes in this program are decisions rather than data entry: the reviewer who activates a testing plan is asserting that the plan is right for the ingredient set they just read, the decision maker who issues a certificate is asserting that the whole product file they just read is complete, and the applicant contact who submits a corrective-action plan is answering the nonconformance as they last read it. Each of those assertions can be invalidated by another writer in between, and silently accepting the write would persist a decision derived from data the decider never saw.

Testing-plan activation therefore requires `expected_product_version` as a non-optional input field, carrying the `version` of the product as the caller last observed it. If that value does not equal the product's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing whatsoever is persisted — no threshold, no sampling figure, no revision increment. On success the product's `version` increases by 1, which means a second attempt replaying the same `expected_product_version` will itself be rejected. This is the intended serialization point between formula assembly and plan activation: a new formula line, an ingredient block or an ingredient re-review all advance the product's `version`, so a plan computed against a formula that has since changed can never be activated.

Certificate issuance requires `expected_product_version` on exactly the same terms, carrying the product `version` the decision maker last observed and rejecting a mismatch with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) before anything is written. Issuance itself advances the product's `version` by 1.

Corrective-action plan submission requires `expected_nonconformance_version`, carrying the `version` of the nonconformance as the applicant last observed it. A mismatch with the nonconformance's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_NONCONFORMANCE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and leaves the record untouched; a successful submission increases `version` by 1. The rule exists because a reviewer's rejection of the previous plan and the sweep's escalation of an overdue record both advance the nonconformance's `version` and both change what a plan would have to answer — a different revision count, a different due date — so an answer written against the older revision must not overwrite the newer state.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Suspension, reinstatement, revocation, label review, formula review, audit close, document verification, ingredient review, complaint triage, investigation outcomes, change-notification assessment and lot-test recording are all guarded by state rules rather than by expected versions.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties

Three pairings of steps may never be performed by the same `X-User-ID`, and each violation reports `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

At certificate issuance the acting `CertificationDecisionMaker` must differ from **both** the product's `formula_cleared_by_user_id` and the `closed_by_user_id` of the facility's most recently closed audit. Either collision alone is enough to reject the issuance; the check is not satisfied by differing from just one of them.

When a corrective-action plan is decided, the acting `CertificationReviewer` must differ from the nonconformance's `raised_by_user_id` — the person who found the problem may not be the person who accepts the promise to fix it. When corrective-action effectiveness is verified, the acting `FieldAuditor` must differ from the nonconformance's `ca_decided_by_user_id` — the person who accepted the plan may not be the person who signs off that it worked.

### 2.9. Numeric Precision and Rounding

The program uses two numeric disciplines and never mixes them. Gluten thresholds, penalty points, posture scores, audit scores and sampling counts are **integers**; every constant that feeds them is an integer, so no fractional result survives to storage. Money is held in **integer cents** throughout — there is no decimal currency field anywhere in the API.

Where a rounding step is required it is always **round half up**: a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero to the next integer. Where a formula rounds, the specification below states exactly *which* intermediate value is rounded and how many times, because the placement changes the answer: the sampling plan rounds each formula line separately and then sums the rounded lines, whereas the audit score sums exact decimal products first and rounds the single total. Day-count divisions that the text describes as a floor discard the fractional part rather than rounding it.

Laboratory results are the one place decimals reach the API surface: `ppm_result`, `lab_ppm_claim` and `swab_ppm` are decimal values compared against integer bounds, and the comparison is exact. A result of 10.0 against a bound of 10 does not exceed it; 10.1 does.

### 2.10. How Consequences Propagate

This is a compliance system, so a change to one row is almost never confined to that row. Whenever a mutation changes a resource, that resource's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by 1 — and the same is true of every *other* resource the mutation touches as a consequence. A supplier suspension does not merely stamp the supplier; it stamps every ingredient it blocks, every product those ingredients sit in, every certificate those products carry and every applicant whose posture it moves.

Propagation is **immediate and complete within the request**. By the time a write returns 2xx, every derived count, every cascaded state and every recomputed score is already readable through the corresponding read endpoints; there is no deferred queue and no eventual convergence. Derived aggregates are always recomputed from their input set rather than adjusted by increments, because several of them are non-monotonic — a nonconformance that returns from `CA_ACCEPTED` to `CA_REJECTED` contributes its penalty points all over again, and an incremental adjustment would lose that.

Cascades never run backwards on their own. Blocking travels downward automatically, but recovery is always an explicit act: reinstating a suspended supplier does not re-approve its ingredients, returning a product's formula to `CLEARED` does not un-suspend its certificate, and closing a surveillance audit does not lift a suspension that the missed surveillance caused. Each of those requires the corresponding review or reinstatement to be performed by a human in the authorized role.

### 2.11. The Fee Ledger Invariant

Certification fees are held on the applicant as `fee_balance_cents`, and that figure is a conserved quantity rather than a free-standing number. At every observable instant it equals the sum of `total_fee_cents` over that applicant's certificates whose `fee_charged` is true and whose `fee_refunded` is false. Issuance sets `fee_charged` to true and adds the certificate's `total_fee_cents` to the balance. A refund-eligible revocation sets `fee_refunded` to true and subtracts exactly that same `total_fee_cents`, restoring the balance the applicant held before the certificate existed. Suspension, reinstatement, expiry and every non-refund-eligible revocation leave the balance completely untouched — a suspended certificate is still a billed certificate.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Applicant Onboarding and the Compliance Posture Register

A certification file begins when a `ProgramAdministrator` registers the applying company, supplying its `legal_name`, a `contact_email` for certification correspondence, and a `country_code` of incorporation given as exactly two uppercase letters. The identifier minted for that applicant is the value an `ApplicantContact` from that company will subsequently present as `X-User-ID`, which is why registration is an administrator's act and not a self-service one. A freshly registered applicant starts in the cleanest possible position: `compliance_posture` is `GOOD_STANDING`, `open_nc_penalty_points` is 0, `active_certified_product_count`, `suspended_certificate_count` and `revoked_certificate_count` are all 0, `fee_balance_cents` is 0, both `posture_score_rolling_12m` and `posture_score_cycle` are 100, and `current_cycle_started_on` is null because no certificate has ever been issued to them.

`current_cycle_started_on` is the earliest `issued_on` among that applicant's certificates whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `SUSPENDED`, and it stays null while no such certificate exists. It marks the opening of what the program calls the **certification cycle**, and it is what makes the applicant's two posture figures diverge.

The applicant's live penalty balance, `open_nc_penalty_points`, is the sum of the severity points of every nonconformance charged to that applicant whose `status` is `OPEN`, `CA_SUBMITTED`, `CA_REJECTED` or `ESCALATED`, with no time window at all. Severity points are 2 for a `MINOR` finding, 8 for a `MAJOR` one and 25 for a `CRITICAL` one. Findings whose `status` is `CA_ACCEPTED` or `CLOSED` contribute nothing. Because a record can travel back from `CA_ACCEPTED` into `CA_REJECTED`, this balance is not monotonic and must be recomputed from the whole population every time any contributing record changes, rather than nudged up and down.

Two posture scores are then derived, and they differ **only** in their observation window and in which complaint counter they read. The rolling figure, `posture_score_rolling_12m`, is the one published to the program's public register. Start from 100 and subtract three terms: first, the sum of `penalty_points` over this applicant's nonconformances whose `status` is `OPEN`, `CA_SUBMITTED`, `CA_REJECTED` or `ESCALATED` **and** whose `created_at` falls inside the window running from 365 days before the logical clock up to the logical clock; second, 5 points for each certificate counted in `suspended_certificate_count`; third, 15 points for each complaint counted in `substantiated_complaint_count_rolling`. If the arithmetic would go below zero the score is 0. The cycle figure, `posture_score_cycle`, applies identical arithmetic over the window running from `current_cycle_started_on` up to the logical clock — or from the applicant's own `created_at` up to the logical clock when `current_cycle_started_on` is null — and takes its complaint term from `substantiated_complaint_count_cycle` instead. Every constant is an integer, so both figures are integers.

`substantiated_complaint_count_rolling` counts the consumer complaints filed against this applicant's certificates whose `status` is `SUBSTANTIATED`, `RECALL_COORDINATION` or `CLOSED`, whose `outcome_evidence` is anything other than `NO_EVIDENCE`, and whose `reported_on` falls between 365 days before the logical clock and the logical clock inclusive. `substantiated_complaint_count_cycle` counts exactly the same population over the window from `current_cycle_started_on` to the logical clock, and is 0 whenever `current_cycle_started_on` is null.

The divergence between the two figures is intentional and consequential. A cycle that opened before the rolling window did keeps counting findings the rolling figure has already forgotten, so an applicant carrying one old open nonconformance can simultaneously show a perfect published score and a degraded cycle score. **The gate reads only the cycle score.** `compliance_posture` is banded from `posture_score_cycle`: a score of at least 85 is `GOOD_STANDING`, a score from 60 up to but not including 85 is `WATCH`, and a score below 60 is `PROBATION`. After that band lookup, and independent of the score, one override applies: if at least one certificate of this applicant has `status` of `REVOKED` with a `revocation_reason` of `FRAUD` or `REPEAT_FAILURE`, `compliance_posture` becomes `SUSPENDED` regardless of how high the score is. A posture of `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED` blocks certificate issuance, certificate reinstatement and change-notification filing with `ERR_APPLICANT_POSTURE_BLOCKED`; the published rolling figure never unblocks anything.

`active_certified_product_count` is a plain count of this applicant's certificates whose `status` is `ISSUED`. Suspended, revoked and expired certificates are excluded, which is what makes a suspend-then-reinstate round trip return the count to its exact pre-suspension value while `suspension_count` on the certificate keeps a permanent record of how many laps were run. All of these applicant figures — the penalty balance, both posture scores, the banded posture, the three certificate counters, the two complaint counters and `fee_balance_cents` — are readable on the applicant read endpoint and are recomputed whenever any contributing nonconformance, certificate or complaint changes, and on every surveillance sweep.

### 3.2. Manufacturing Sites: Shared Equipment, Air Handling and Sanitation

An `ApplicantContact` declares each manufacturing site the company wants covered, naming the operating `applicant_id`, a human-readable `site_name`, and the `air_handling_class` of the gluten-free production area, which is one of `OPEN_PLENUM`, `FILTERED_ZONED` or `DEDICATED_NEGATIVE_PRESSURE`. Two further declarations describe how close the site runs to gluten: `shared_equipment_present`, a boolean defaulting to false that is true when at least one production line also runs gluten-containing product, and `gluten_containing_operations`, defaulting to `NONE` and otherwise `ADJACENT_LINES` or `SAME_LINE`, describing the degree of physical co-location. Both feed directly into the threshold arithmetic of §3.6 and the fee arithmetic of §3.9, so an applicant who understates them will find the resulting certificate cheaper and looser than the site deserves — which is exactly what the on-site audit exists to catch.

A new site begins with `sanitation_validation_status` of `UNVALIDATED`, with `sanitation_method`, `sanitation_swab_ppm`, `sanitation_validated_on` and `sanitation_expires_on` all null, and with `last_closed_audit_outcome` of `NONE` and `last_closed_audit_closed_on` null.

Sanitation validation is the act of proving that the changeover cleaning actually works. Either a `CertificationReviewer` or a `FieldAuditor` records an accepted post-sanitation swab by supplying `swab_ppm`, the assay `method` used — one of `R5_ELISA`, `G12_ELISA` or `COMPETITIVE_ELISA` — and `validated_on`, the date the swab was taken, which may not be later than the logical clock. The swab must read at most 5 ppm; exactly 5 ppm is accepted, and 5.1 ppm is rejected with `ERR_SANITATION_SWAB_ABOVE_LIMIT`. On acceptance the facility copies the submitted values into `sanitation_swab_ppm`, `sanitation_method` and `sanitation_validated_on`, sets `sanitation_expires_on` to 365 days after `validated_on`, and moves `sanitation_validation_status` to `VALIDATED`. The same operation is accepted from all three of `UNVALIDATED`, `VALIDATED` and `EXPIRED`: a facility already `VALIDATED` may be re-validated, which replaces the stored swab record and re-bases `sanitation_expires_on` from the new `validated_on`, and a facility that has lapsed to `EXPIRED` returns to `VALIDATED` on the same terms. In all three cases the accepted record is the newest one; there is no history of superseded swabs on the facility itself.

Validation lapses on the clock, not on a read. Once `sanitation_expires_on` has been reached, the next surveillance sweep moves the facility from `VALIDATED` to `EXPIRED`. That lapse deliberately cascades nowhere — certificates already in force are untouched — but while the facility sits at `EXPIRED` (or has never left `UNVALIDATED`), three operations against it fail with `ERR_FACILITY_SANITATION_NOT_VALIDATED`: closing an audit at that site, issuing a certificate for a product made there, and reinstating a suspended certificate of such a product. Re-validation is the only way out.

The two audit-history fields on a facility are derived, never written by hand. `last_closed_audit_outcome` is the `status` of the audit of this facility with the greatest `closed_on` among those whose `status` is `CLOSED_PASS`, `CLOSED_CONDITIONAL` or `CLOSED_FAIL`, with ties broken by taking the audit with the earlier `created_at`; it reads `NONE` while no audit has ever closed there. `last_closed_audit_closed_on` is that same audit's `closed_on`. Together they are the gate the issuance ladder consults: a facility whose latest closed audit reads `CLOSED_FAIL`, and equally a facility reading `NONE`, cannot have certificates issued for its products, and the rejection is `ERR_FACILITY_AUDIT_NOT_PASSED`.

### 3.3. Supplier Standing and the Ingredient Documentation Package

Suppliers are registered by a `ProgramAdministrator` or a `CertificationReviewer` with a `name`, a two-uppercase-letter `country_code` for the supplying site, and `gluten_handling_disclosed`, a boolean defaulting to false that records whether the supplier has disclosed handling gluten-containing grains. A new supplier's `status` is `ACTIVE` and its `verified_document_count` is 0; that counter is always the number of this supplier's documents whose `status` is `VERIFIED`.

Only a `ProgramAdministrator` may move a supplier's standing, and the requested `status` must differ from the current one or the request fails with `ERR_SUPPLIER_STATUS_UNCHANGED`. `ACTIVE → UNDER_REVIEW` is advisory: it refreshes the supplier's `updated_at`, increases its `version` by 1, and deliberately leaves every ingredient that supplier feeds exactly where it is. `UNDER_REVIEW → ACTIVE` simply reverses that, again increasing `version` by 1.

`SUSPENDED` is a different matter, and it is reachable from both `ACTIVE` and `UNDER_REVIEW`. Suspension runs a five-hop cascade in one request, and the hops must be applied in order because each is caused by the one before it. **Hop one:** every ingredient supplied by this supplier whose `status` is `APPROVED` moves `APPROVED → BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` set to `SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED`. **Hop two:** for each ingredient blocked by hop one, the compound ingredient it is a sub-component of — the row its `parent_ingredient_id` names — moves `APPROVED → BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` set to `SUB_COMPONENT_BLOCKED`, if that parent was `APPROVED`. **Hop three:** every product with a formula line referencing any now-blocked ingredient recomputes `blocked_ingredient_count`, and when that count becomes at least 1 while the product's `formula_status` is `CLEARED`, the product moves `CLEARED → BLOCKED`. **Hop four:** every certificate of a product blocked in hop three whose `status` is `ISSUED` moves `ISSUED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `INGREDIENT_BLOCKED`, `suspended_on` set to the logical clock, `suspension_count` increased by 1 and `mark_usage_locked` set to true. **Hop five:** each of those products moves `label_status` from `APPROVED` to `FROZEN`, and each owning applicant recomputes `active_certified_product_count`, `suspended_certificate_count`, `posture_score_rolling_12m`, `posture_score_cycle` and `compliance_posture`.

Reinstating a suspended supplier back to `ACTIVE` is emphatically not the inverse of that cascade. It touches exactly one class of row: every ingredient of this supplier whose `status` is `BLOCKED` **and** whose `blocked_reason` is `SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED` moves `BLOCKED → UNDER_REVIEW`, with `review_cycle_count` increased by 1 and `blocked_reason` cleared to null. Ingredients blocked for any other reason are left blocked. No ingredient is auto-approved, so the products above them stay `BLOCKED` until a reviewer approves the ingredient again, and their certificates stay `SUSPENDED` until someone reinstates them explicitly.

Documentation is what makes an ingredient reviewable. An `ApplicantContact` uploads each supplier document naming the issuing `supplier_id`, the `ingredient_id` whose gluten status the document evidences, a `doc_type` of `ALLERGEN_STATEMENT`, `PROCESS_FLOW`, `COA` or `GF_CERTIFICATE_OF_ORIGIN`, the `issued_on` date, the `expires_on` date after which the document no longer evidences anything, and — for a certificate of analysis — `lab_ppm_claim`, the gluten concentration in ppm the laboratory certifies. `lab_ppm_claim` is null for every non-`COA` document class. Because a document sits on the edge between one supplier and one ingredient, the referenced ingredient's own `supplier_id` must equal the `supplier_id` supplied on the document, or the upload is rejected with `ERR_DOCUMENT_SUPPLIER_MISMATCH`. Uploaded documents begin with `status` of `PENDING` and with `verified_by_user_id`, `verified_on` and `rejection_reason` all null.

A `CertificationReviewer` then decides each pending document, supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and, on rejection, a `rejection_reason` of `ILLEGIBLE`, `PPM_ABOVE_LIMIT`, `EXPIRED_ON_SUBMISSION` or `SCOPE_MISMATCH`. Only a `PENDING` document may be decided; a second decision on an already-decided document is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENT_ALREADY_DECIDED`. Approval requires two things beyond the state check. The document's `expires_on` must be strictly after the logical clock — a document that has already lapsed cannot be verified, and the refusal is `ERR_SUPPLIER_DOCUMENT_EXPIRED`. And for a `COA`, `lab_ppm_claim` must be at most the cap set by the referenced ingredient's `risk_class`: 20 ppm for `INHERENTLY_GF`, 10 ppm for `GF_PROCESSED` and 5 ppm for `HIGH_RISK_GRAIN`. A claim exactly at the cap is accepted; anything above it is `ERR_COA_PPM_CLAIM_TOO_HIGH`. On approval the document moves `PENDING → VERIFIED`, records `verified_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID` and `verified_on` as the logical clock, and triggers a recompute of the supplier's `verified_document_count` and of the evidenced ingredient's `verified_doc_types`.

Rejection is where a document decision reaches beyond the document. The document moves `PENDING → REJECTED` with the supplied `rejection_reason`, and then: if the evidenced ingredient was `APPROVED` and the rejection removes a document class its `risk_class` requires, that ingredient moves `APPROVED → BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` of `DOCUMENT_REJECTED`; every product carrying that ingredient in its formula and sitting at `formula_status` of `CLEARED` moves `CLEARED → BLOCKED`; and every `ISSUED` certificate of those products moves `ISSUED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` of `INGREDIENT_BLOCKED`, freezing the product label and forcing a recompute of the owning applicant's posture fields.

Verified documents also lapse on the clock. When a surveillance sweep finds a document whose `status` is `VERIFIED` and whose `expires_on` has been reached, the document moves `VERIFIED → EXPIRED`, the evidenced ingredient's `verified_doc_types` is recomputed, and if a required document class is thereby missing while the ingredient is `APPROVED`, the ingredient moves `APPROVED → BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` of `DOCUMENT_EXPIRED`, propagating onward to products (`CLEARED → BLOCKED`) and certificates (`ISSUED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` of `INGREDIENT_BLOCKED`) exactly as a rejection does. `REJECTED` and `EXPIRED` are both terminal for a document; neither can be revived, and a replacement must be uploaded instead.

### 3.4. Ingredient Review, the Compound-Ingredient Graph and the Block Cycle

An `ApplicantContact` declares each ingredient with the owning `applicant_id`, the supplying `supplier_id`, a trade `name`, and a `risk_class` of `INHERENTLY_GF`, `GF_PROCESSED` or `HIGH_RISK_GRAIN`. Optionally the declaration carries `parent_ingredient_id`, naming the compound ingredient this row is a sub-component of; it is null for a top-level ingredient. That parent must belong to the same `applicant_id` and must not close a loop in the parent chain — an ingredient cannot be its own ancestor, and it must never equal the row's own identifier — or the declaration is refused with `ERR_INGREDIENT_GRAPH_CYCLE`. A new ingredient starts with `status` of `DRAFT`, `review_cycle_count` of 0, `verified_doc_types` as an empty list, and `blocked_reason`, `rejection_reason`, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `approved_on` all null.

`verified_doc_types` is derived, not supplied: it is the distinct set of `doc_type` values of this ingredient's documents whose `status` is `VERIFIED`, held in ascending sorted order so that two ingredients with the same evidence always present the same list. What that list must contain depends entirely on the risk class. An `INHERENTLY_GF` ingredient requires only `ALLERGEN_STATEMENT`. A `GF_PROCESSED` ingredient requires `ALLERGEN_STATEMENT` and `PROCESS_FLOW`. A `HIGH_RISK_GRAIN` ingredient requires `ALLERGEN_STATEMENT`, `PROCESS_FLOW` and `COA`. Extra verified classes beyond the required set are harmless.

Submitting an ingredient for review is the applicant's move, and it is accepted from `DRAFT`, from `REJECTED` and from `BLOCKED` — those three states are the entry points, and any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INGREDIENT_STATE`. Submission additionally requires that the ingredient's supplier is not `SUSPENDED`, or it fails with `ERR_SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED`. In every case the ingredient moves to `UNDER_REVIEW` and `review_cycle_count` increases by 1; `blocked_reason` is cleared to null, and on a resubmission after a rejection `rejection_reason` is cleared to null as well. Those three entry points are what make the ingredient lifecycle genuinely cyclic: `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED → UNDER_REVIEW` closes the review loop, and `APPROVED → BLOCKED → UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` closes the block loop, with `review_cycle_count` counting every lap.

A `CertificationReviewer` decides an ingredient that is `UNDER_REVIEW` by supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, plus a `rejection_reason` of `DOCS_INSUFFICIENT`, `PPM_CLAIM_TOO_HIGH` or `SUPPLIER_UNDISCLOSED_GLUTEN` when rejecting. Approval has three substantive preconditions on top of the state check: `verified_doc_types` must cover every class the `risk_class` requires (`ERR_REQUIRED_SUPPLIER_DOCS_MISSING`), no document counted toward that required set may have an `expires_on` at or before the logical clock (`ERR_SUPPLIER_DOCUMENT_EXPIRED`), and the ingredient's supplier must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED`). On approval the ingredient moves `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`, records `reviewed_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID` and `approved_on` as the logical clock, and clears `blocked_reason` to null. It also reaches upward into the formulas: every product with a formula line referencing this ingredient recomputes `blocked_ingredient_count`, and a product whose `formula_status` is `BLOCKED` returns `BLOCKED → CLEARED` once that count has reached 0 **and** every ingredient in its formula is `APPROVED`. Recovery stops there — no certificate is un-suspended by an ingredient approval.

Rejection moves the ingredient `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED` with the supplied `rejection_reason` recorded and `reviewed_by_user_id` set, and every product carrying it whose `formula_status` is `CLEARED` moves `CLEARED → BLOCKED` with `blocked_ingredient_count` recomputed, cascading onward to `ISSUED` certificates (`SUSPENDED`, with `suspension_reason` of `INGREDIENT_BLOCKED`), to `label_status` (`APPROVED → FROZEN`) and to the applicant's posture fields.

The `APPROVED → BLOCKED` transition itself is never requested directly; it is only ever a system consequence of a supplier suspension, a document rejection, a document expiry, or a sub-component ingredient becoming blocked. Whichever cause fired sets `blocked_reason` accordingly — `SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED`, `DOCUMENT_REJECTED`, `DOCUMENT_EXPIRED` or `SUB_COMPONENT_BLOCKED`. Blocking then walks the compound chain upward: any `APPROVED` ancestor reached through `parent_ingredient_id` also moves `APPROVED → BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` of `SUB_COMPONENT_BLOCKED`. And every product linked to any newly blocked ingredient recomputes `blocked_ingredient_count` and, if it was `CLEARED`, moves to `BLOCKED`. This upward-then-outward walk is the reason a single supplier suspension can suspend certificates for products that never named that supplier directly.

### 3.5. Building a Product: Formula Clearance and Label & Mark Review

An `ApplicantContact` declares a product for certification with the owning `applicant_id`, the `facility_id` of the site where it is manufactured, a `name` as it appears on the label, and a `sku`, the applicant-scoped stock keeping unit. That `sku` must be unique among the products of the same `applicant_id`, or the declaration is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU`, and the named facility must itself belong to the supplied `applicant_id`, or the refusal is `ERR_FACILITY_APPLICANT_MISMATCH`. The applicant also states `requested_mark_tier`, which defaults to `STANDARD` and may instead be `PREMIUM` or `ELITE`; that request caps the certified threshold, so a company asking for the strictest mark is asking for the strictest number. A new product opens with `formula_status` of `DRAFT`, `label_status` of `NOT_SUBMITTED`, `testing_plan_status` of `NONE`, `testing_plan_revision` of 0, `mark_tier_granted` of `NONE`, `linked_ingredient_count` and `blocked_ingredient_count` of 0, `threshold_ppm` and `sampling_lots_per_year` and `sampling_exclusion_days` all null, and `formula_cleared_by_user_id`, `label_reviewed_by_user_id`, `label_rejection_reason` and `last_scheduled_sample_on` all null.

A product carries **two independent review dimensions**, and this is the single most misread part of the domain. `formula_status` tracks the ingredient side and moves through `DRAFT`, `CLEARED`, `REJECTED` and `BLOCKED`. `label_status` tracks the artwork and mark-usage side and moves through `NOT_SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` and `FROZEN`. The two dimensions advance through separate operations and separate cascades; neither one reads or writes the other. Rejecting a formula leaves an approved label approved, and rejecting a label leaves a cleared formula cleared. Only certificate movement ever writes `FROZEN`.

The formula itself is assembled one line at a time. Each line names the `product_id` and the `ingredient_id` it consumes, together with `inclusion_pct`, the percentage of finished-product mass that ingredient contributes, which must be greater than 0 and at most 100. A line may not duplicate an existing pairing of the same `product_id` and `ingredient_id` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PRODUCT_INGREDIENT`); the ingredient may not be in `REJECTED` state (`ERR_INVALID_INGREDIENT_STATE`); the ingredient's supplier may not be `SUSPENDED` (`ERR_SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED`); and the product's `formula_status` must be `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` (`ERR_INVALID_PRODUCT_STATE`), because a formula may not be edited under or after clearance. On creation the line computes `is_risk_carrier`, which is true exactly when the referenced ingredient's `risk_class` is anything other than `INHERENTLY_GF`, evaluated at link-creation time and never revisited. Creating a line then recomputes the product's `linked_ingredient_count` and `blocked_ingredient_count`.

Formula clearance is a `CertificationReviewer` act. The reviewer supplies a `decision` of `CLEAR` or `REJECT`, with a `rejection_reason` accompanying a rejection. Clearance is accepted from `DRAFT` and from `REJECTED` — so `DRAFT → CLEARED` and `REJECTED → CLEARED` are both legal — and requires that `linked_ingredient_count` is at least 1 (`ERR_NO_INGREDIENTS_LINKED`) and that every ingredient in the formula has `status` of `APPROVED` (`ERR_INGREDIENT_NOT_APPROVED`). On clearance `formula_status` becomes `CLEARED` and `formula_cleared_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`; that recorded identifier is precisely the value the segregation-of-duties check at issuance later compares against. A rejection from either `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` sets `formula_status` to `REJECTED` and leaves `formula_cleared_by_user_id` null, and it does not touch `label_status`. Either decision increases the product's `version` by 1.

Label review is the other dimension, also a `CertificationReviewer` act, and it accepts `NOT_SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED` **and** `APPROVED` as starting states — an already-approved label can be re-reviewed and withdrawn. The reviewer supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`. An approval must carry `mark_tier_granted` in the payload, and that value must equal the product's `requested_mark_tier`; a missing or mismatched tier is refused with `ERR_MARK_TIER_NOT_GRANTED`. On approval — whether from `NOT_SUBMITTED` or from `REJECTED`, and the two behave identically — `label_status` becomes `APPROVED`, the product's `mark_tier_granted` is set to the supplied tier, `label_reviewed_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and `label_rejection_reason` is cleared to null. A rejection must carry a `rejection_reason`, which is stored as `label_rejection_reason` and is one of `MARK_PLACEMENT_INVALID`, `UNSUPPORTED_CLAIM`, `MISSING_CERT_NUMBER` or `TIER_MISMATCH`; on rejection, whether the label was previously `NOT_SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`, `label_status` becomes `REJECTED`, `mark_tier_granted` is reset to `NONE`, `label_reviewed_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID` on this branch exactly as it does on approval — the rejection is a label decision with an author too — and `formula_status` is untouched. Every label decision increases the product's `version` by 1.

`FROZEN` is unreachable from any review. `APPROVED → FROZEN` happens only as a consequence of a certificate of this product leaving `ISSUED` — by suspension, revocation or expiry — and it means mark usage is frozen while the certificate's `mark_usage_locked` reads true. `FROZEN → APPROVED` happens only when a certificate of this product returns to `ISSUED` through an explicit reinstatement, which clears `mark_usage_locked` and leaves `mark_tier_granted` exactly as it was.

### 3.6. The Threshold Standard, the Sampling Plan and Lot Testing

Once a product's formula is `CLEARED`, a `CertificationReviewer` activates its threshold standard and lot sampling plan in one operation, which requires `expected_product_version` and refuses a stale value with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_VERSION` as described in §2.7. The product must be `CLEARED` on the formula dimension (`ERR_INVALID_PRODUCT_STATE`) and must have at least one formula line (`ERR_NO_INGREDIENTS_LINKED`). Activation computes `threshold_ppm`, then `sampling_lots_per_year` and `sampling_exclusion_days` from it, sets `testing_plan_status` to `ACTIVE`, increases `testing_plan_revision` by 1 — so the counter records how many plans this product has had, each superseding the last — and increases the product's `version` by 1.

**Selecting the threshold.** The certified threshold is the strictest of five independent ceilings, then adjusted for one aggravating combination, then floored. First compute all five in ppm. The program ceiling is a flat 20 ppm for every product. The tier ceiling comes from `requested_mark_tier`: 20 ppm for `STANDARD`, 10 ppm for `PREMIUM`, 5 ppm for `ELITE`. The ingredient ceiling is the smallest per-risk-class ceiling across every formula line of the product, where a line's ceiling is 20 ppm if its ingredient is `INHERENTLY_GF`, 15 ppm if `GF_PROCESSED` and 10 ppm if `HIGH_RISK_GRAIN`. The equipment ceiling is 10 ppm when the manufacturing facility has `shared_equipment_present` true and 20 ppm when it is false. The operations ceiling comes from the facility's `gluten_containing_operations`: 20 ppm for `NONE`, 10 ppm for `ADJACENT_LINES` and 5 ppm for `SAME_LINE`. Second, take the smallest of those five ceilings as a working value. Third, apply the co-location penalty **to that smallest value and to no individual ceiling**: when the facility's `gluten_containing_operations` is `SAME_LINE` and at least one formula line of the product references an ingredient whose `risk_class` is `HIGH_RISK_GRAIN`, subtract 2 ppm from the working value. Fourth, `threshold_ppm` is the larger of 5 ppm and the working value. The order is load-bearing and must not be rearranged: because the penalty is subtracted after the minimum is taken and the floor is applied after the penalty, a `SAME_LINE` facility running a `HIGH_RISK_GRAIN` ingredient lands on 5 ppm — the floor catching a working value of 3 — and never on 3 ppm. Every input and the penalty are integers, so `threshold_ppm` is an integer and no rounding arises.

**Selecting the sampling frequency.** How often the program pulls a lot is driven by the same formula lines, weighted by how tight the threshold turned out to be. One threshold multiplier is shared by every line and is read off the freshly computed `threshold_ppm`: 2.0 when the threshold is at most 5 ppm, 1.5 when it is above 5 ppm and at most 10 ppm, and 1.0 when it is above 10 ppm. Then, for **each** formula line separately, compute an inclusion factor of 0.5 when that line's `inclusion_pct` is below 2 and 1.0 otherwise, and multiply four things together: a base of 4 lots per year, the line's risk weight (0.25 for `INHERENTLY_GF`, 0.5 for `GF_PROCESSED`, 1.0 for `HIGH_RISK_GRAIN`), the shared threshold multiplier, and that inclusion factor. Round that per-line product half up to a whole number of lots **before** going on to the next line. `sampling_lots_per_year` is then the larger of 2 and the sum of those already-rounded per-line values, and the sum is never rounded again. The placement of the rounding is the whole point: two lines each computing 1.5 give 2 plus 2, which is 4 lots per year, whereas rounding a sum of 3.0 would have given 3.

`sampling_exclusion_days` follows immediately: divide the 365 days of a year by `sampling_lots_per_year`, discard any fractional part, and take the larger of that quotient and 7 days. It is a floor division and never a half-up rounding. This figure is the length of the closed interval that each scheduled sample reserves on the product, which spreads a year's sampling out instead of letting a laboratory clear its quota in a fortnight.

**Recording a lot test.** A `LabTechnician` records each laboratory result naming the `product_id`, the manufacturer `lot_code` sampled, a `test_type` of `SCHEDULED`, `UNANNOUNCED` or `FOR_CAUSE`, the `sample_collected_on` date, the `ppm_result` in ppm, and the `lab_method` used (`R5_ELISA`, `G12_ELISA` or `COMPETITIVE_ELISA`). The product's `testing_plan_status` must be `ACTIVE` or the recording is refused with `ERR_TESTING_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`; `sample_collected_on` may not be after the logical clock (`ERR_SAMPLE_DATE_IN_FUTURE`); and `ppm_result` must be at least 0. For a `SCHEDULED` lot only, `sample_collected_on` must fall outside the exclusion interval of every existing `SCHEDULED` lot test of the same product whose `status` is `RECORDED`. That interval is closed and runs from the earlier lot's `sample_collected_on` through the day one day short of `sampling_exclusion_days` later — so with an exclusion span of 7 days, a sample on the earlier lot's date and each of the six days after it are all blocked, and the seventh day after is free. A collision is `ERR_SAMPLING_EXCLUSION_WINDOW`. `UNANNOUNCED` and `FOR_CAUSE` lots are entirely exempt from this interval and may be pulled at any time.

Every recorded lot snapshots `threshold_applied_ppm` from the product's `threshold_ppm` at record time, and that snapshot — not the product's later value — is the bound the verdict is judged against. `verdict` is `FAIL` when `ppm_result` is strictly greater than `threshold_applied_ppm` and `PASS` otherwise, so a result exactly equal to the threshold passes. A new lot's `status` is `RECORDED`, `nonconformance_created` is false and `in_recall_scope` is false. Recording a `SCHEDULED` lot also refreshes the product's `last_scheduled_sample_on`, which is the greatest `sample_collected_on` among that product's `SCHEDULED` lots whose `status` is `RECORDED`.

A failing lot raises a finding automatically: a nonconformance is created with `source_type` of `LOT_TEST`, `category` of `THRESHOLD_EXCEEDANCE`, and a `severity` of `CRITICAL` when `ppm_result` is strictly more than twice `threshold_applied_ppm` and `MAJOR` otherwise — exactly twice the threshold is `MAJOR`, not `CRITICAL`. The lot's `nonconformance_created` becomes true and the applicant's penalty and posture fields are recomputed. What happens to the certificate depends on how the lot was pulled. When the failing lot's `test_type` is `UNANNOUNCED` and the product has a certificate with `status` of `ISSUED`, that certificate moves `ISSUED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` of `FAILED_UNANNOUNCED_TEST` and the product's `label_status` freezes. A failing `SCHEDULED` or `FOR_CAUSE` lot does **not** suspend on its own account; if it suspends anything it does so indirectly, through the critical-nonconformance cascade of §3.8.

One lot result can retire another. When a `FOR_CAUSE` lot is recorded for the same `product_id` and the same `lot_code` as an existing lot whose `status` is `RECORDED`, and the earlier lot's `sample_collected_on` is at or before the new one's, the earlier lot moves `RECORDED → SUPERSEDED_BY_RETEST`. A superseded lot keeps its `verdict` and its `threshold_applied_ppm` as historical record but stops being eligible for recall scoping, and its `in_recall_scope` is recomputed to false. `SUPERSEDED_BY_RETEST` is terminal.

### 3.7. On-Site Audits: Scheduling, Conduct, Scoring and the Three-Way Close

A `FieldAuditor` schedules each visit against a `facility_id`, declaring an `audit_type` of `INITIAL` (which precedes a site's first issuance), `SURVEILLANCE` (the annual visit) or `FOR_CAUSE` (following an incident), together with the on-site occupation interval given as `onsite_start_at` and `onsite_end_at`. `onsite_end_at` must be strictly after `onsite_start_at`. The interval is **half-open** — inclusive of its start, exclusive of its end — and it reserves the site: a new audit may not overlap the interval of any other audit of the same facility whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, though because the end is exclusive an audit that begins exactly at an existing audit's `onsite_end_at` is accepted. An overlap is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A scheduled audit opens with `status` of `SCHEDULED`, `finding_count` and `critical_finding_count` at 0, `provisional_score` and `final_score` null, and `lead_auditor_user_id`, `closed_by_user_id` and `closed_on` null.

An audit is started only once the logical clock has reached `onsite_start_at`; a premature attempt, or an attempt on an audit that is not `SCHEDULED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AUDIT_STATE`. Starting moves `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS` and records `lead_auditor_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`. An audit that is never started simply lapses: when a surveillance sweep finds a `SCHEDULED` audit whose `onsite_end_at` has been reached, it moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, which releases the facility's on-site interval so a replacement visit can be scheduled over the same dates.

Closing an audit requires the clock to have reached `onsite_end_at` and the audit to be `IN_PROGRESS` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_AUDIT_STATE`), and it requires the facility's `sanitation_validation_status` to read `VALIDATED` at that moment (otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_SANITATION_NOT_VALIDATED`). The closing auditor submits three attestations as booleans: `shared_equipment_verified`, that changeover controls on shared equipment were checked; `air_handling_verified`, that air-handling containment was checked; and `sanitation_validation_reviewed`, that the site's sanitation validation record was reviewed.

**Scoring.** The close computes the audit's provisional compliance score from the findings raised against it. Each finding contributes its severity points — 2 for `MINOR`, 8 for `MAJOR`, 25 for `CRITICAL` — multiplied by a category weight read off its `category`: 1.5 for `SHARED_EQUIPMENT`, 1.25 for `AIR_HANDLING`, 1.25 for `SANITATION`, 1.0 for `DOCUMENTATION`, 1.0 for `LABELING` and 1.5 for `THRESHOLD_EXCEEDANCE`. The **provisional on-site** figure counts only those nonconformances whose `audit_id` names this audit **and** whose `created_at` is at or before the audit's `onsite_end_at`; it sums those severity-times-weight products exactly as decimals, rounds the single total half up to a whole number, subtracts it from 100, and takes the larger of that result and 0. The provisional figure deliberately ignores `ca_verified` altogether and applies no escalation penalty. It is written into `provisional_score` at close and **frozen there forever**.

The **final adjudicated** figure is computed into `final_score` at close as well — initially identical to the provisional one — and is then recomputed on every creation of, and every status change of, any nonconformance whose `audit_id` names this audit, including changes recorded long after the audit closed. That recompute does not wait for the close either: a nonconformance raised against an audit that is still `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` writes the adjudicated figure into that audit's `final_score` there and then, so a not-yet-closed audit with findings against it reads a non-null `final_score` while its `provisional_score` stays null until close — and the close then overwrites `final_score` with the provisional value as described above. It counts every such nonconformance regardless of `created_at`, and it differs in two further ways: each finding whose `ca_verified` is true contributes only half of its severity-times-weight product, and after the summed products have been rounded half up and subtracted from 100, a further flat 10 points is deducted for each of those nonconformances whose `status` is `ESCALATED`. The result is again floored at 0. Rounding happens once, on the total of the per-finding products, and never per finding — a `CRITICAL` `SANITATION` finding contributes 31.25 points of deduction, or 15.625 once its corrective action is verified, and those decimals must survive into the total before the single rounding step.

The two figures therefore disagree whenever a linked finding was raised after `onsite_end_at`, or has had its corrective action verified, or has escalated. That divergence is reporting only: **the outcome branch is bound to the provisional figure forever**, and a later improvement in `final_score` never reopens an audit that closed as `CLOSED_FAIL`.

**The three-way close.** From `IN_PROGRESS` the audit forks into exactly one of three terminal outcomes, chosen from the provisional score and `critical_finding_count`.

- **`CLOSED_PASS`** is selected when the provisional score is at least 90 **and** `critical_finding_count` is 0. It additionally requires, when the facility's `shared_equipment_present` is true, that the submitted `shared_equipment_verified` is true; if that attestation is absent on a shared-equipment site the close is graded down to `CLOSED_CONDITIONAL` instead. On this branch `provisional_score` and `final_score` are both set to the provisional value, `closed_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID` and `closed_on` records the logical clock, and the facility's `last_closed_audit_outcome` becomes `CLOSED_PASS` with `last_closed_audit_closed_on` set to the logical clock. If the `audit_type` was `SURVEILLANCE`, every certificate of a product of this facility whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `SUSPENDED` has `surveillance_due_on` rebased to 365 days after the logical clock and `surveillance_status` reset to `CURRENT`.
- **`CLOSED_CONDITIONAL`** is selected when the provisional score is at least 75 and below 90, with `critical_finding_count` still 0. `provisional_score`, `final_score`, `closed_by_user_id` and `closed_on` are set exactly as on the pass branch, and the facility's `last_closed_audit_outcome` becomes `CLOSED_CONDITIONAL`. Certificate issuance for the site's products remains permitted on this outcome — but every nonconformance of this audit that is still open keeps charging penalty points against the applicant, so the posture gate may block issuance anyway. If the `audit_type` was `SURVEILLANCE`, `surveillance_due_on` and `surveillance_status` are rebased and reset for the facility's certificates exactly as on the pass branch.
- **`CLOSED_FAIL`** is selected when the provisional score is below 75 **or** `critical_finding_count` is at least 1. `provisional_score`, `final_score`, `closed_by_user_id` and `closed_on` are set as on the other two branches, and the facility's `last_closed_audit_outcome` becomes `CLOSED_FAIL`, which blocks issuance for every product of that facility. In addition, every certificate of a product of this facility whose `status` is `ISSUED` moves `ISSUED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` of `CRITICAL_NONCONFORMANCE`, each of those products moves `label_status` from `APPROVED` to `FROZEN`, and each owning applicant recomputes its posture fields. On this branch `surveillance_due_on` is **not** rebased even when the `audit_type` was `SURVEILLANCE` — a failed surveillance visit does not buy the certificate another year.

All four of `CLOSED_PASS`, `CLOSED_CONDITIONAL`, `CLOSED_FAIL` and `CANCELLED` are terminal; a closed audit is never reopened, amended or re-closed.

### 3.8. Nonconformances and the Bounded Corrective-Action Cycle

A nonconformance is the program's unit of accountability, and it can arise from five different places. A `FieldAuditor` or a `CertificationReviewer` raises one explicitly, naming the accountable `applicant_id`, a `source_type` of `AUDIT`, `LOT_TEST`, `COMPLAINT`, `CHANGE_NOTIFICATION` or `SURVEILLANCE_RUN`, a `category` from `SHARED_EQUIPMENT`, `AIR_HANDLING`, `SANITATION`, `DOCUMENTATION`, `LABELING` or `THRESHOLD_EXCEEDANCE`, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`, and a `description` narrating what was observed. The polymorphic source is carried by whichever of the optional keys matches: `audit_id` for an audit finding, `lot_test_id` for a laboratory failure, `complaint_id` for a consumer complaint, and `certificate_id` for the affected certificate, which is null when the finding is raised before any certificate exists. Exactly one of those source keys must be supplied and it must be the one consistent with `source_type`. The system also raises nonconformances on its own account — a failing lot test raises one, a substantiated complaint raises one, and a change notification rejected as requiring recertification raises one.

Creation records `status` of `OPEN`, `raised_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, `ca_revision_count` of 0, `ca_verified` of false, `ca_plan_text` null and `ca_decided_by_user_id`, `ca_rejection_reason` and `closed_on` null. `penalty_points` is fixed at creation from `severity` — 2 for `MINOR`, 8 for `MAJOR`, 25 for `CRITICAL`. `ca_due_on`, the deadline for the applicant's corrective-action plan, is set to the record's `created_at` plus a severity-dependent window: 15 days for a `CRITICAL` finding, 30 days for a `MAJOR` one and 90 days for a `MINOR` one, because a critical exceedance cannot be left to drift for a quarter.

Creation then propagates. The accountable applicant recomputes `open_nc_penalty_points` and, from it, `posture_score_rolling_12m`, `posture_score_cycle` and `compliance_posture`. When `audit_id` is present, the named audit's `finding_count` increases by 1, its `critical_finding_count` increases by 1 as well if the `severity` is `CRITICAL`, and its `final_score` is recomputed on the adjudicated basis. And when the `severity` is `CRITICAL` the linked certificates are resolved — the single certificate named by `certificate_id` whenever the record names one, and otherwise every certificate reached from the source, which for an `audit_id` source is every certificate of every product of the audited facility, for a `lot_test_id` source is the tested product's own `ISSUED` or `SUSPENDED` certificate and for a `complaint_id` source is the certificate the complaint names — and every one of those whose `status` is `ISSUED` moves `ISSUED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` of `CRITICAL_NONCONFORMANCE` unless the raising workflow names another reason (the substantiated-complaint finding of §3.10 stamps `SUBSTANTIATED_COMPLAINT`), freezing the product's `label_status` to `FROZEN`. This is why a `CRITICAL` audit finding raised during a visit both fails the audit at close and suspends the certificate the moment it is raised.

The applicant answers with a corrective-action plan, supplying `expected_nonconformance_version` and `ca_plan_text`. Submission is accepted from `OPEN` and from `CA_REJECTED` — the second of those is what makes the loop a loop — and requires that the logical clock is at or before `ca_due_on` (`ERR_CORRECTIVE_ACTION_OVERDUE`) and that the linked certificate, if there is one, is neither `REVOKED` nor `EXPIRED` (`ERR_CERTIFICATE_TERMINAL_NO_CA`). A successful submission records `ca_plan_text`, replacing any previous text on a resubmission, moves the record to `CA_SUBMITTED`, refreshes `updated_at` to the logical clock, increases `version` by 1, and recomputes the adjudicated score of the audit named by `audit_id` if there is one.

A `CertificationReviewer` who is not the person named in `raised_by_user_id` then decides the plan, supplying a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` and, on rejection, a `ca_rejection_reason` of `INSUFFICIENT_ROOT_CAUSE`, `NO_VERIFICATION_EVIDENCE` or `TIMELINE_TOO_LONG`. Acceptance moves `CA_SUBMITTED → CA_ACCEPTED`, records `ca_decided_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID` and clears `ca_rejection_reason` to null. Rejection is where the loop is bounded, and the order of operations decides the outcome: `ca_revision_count` is increased by 1 **first**, and only then is the branch chosen. While the incremented count is still below 2 the record moves `CA_SUBMITTED → CA_REJECTED` with `ca_decided_by_user_id` recorded, and the applicant may submit a revised plan, re-entering `CA_SUBMITTED`. Once the incremented count reaches 2 the record moves `CA_SUBMITTED → ESCALATED` instead of cycling again. Either decision increases the record's `version` by 1.

Escalation is expensive. On reaching `ESCALATED` the record's `ca_revision_count` is increased and `updated_at` refreshed, the applicant's `open_nc_penalty_points` and posture fields are recomputed, the audit named by `audit_id` recomputes `final_score` with the additional flat 10-point escalation deduction, and — if the linked certificate's `status` is `ISSUED` — that certificate moves `ISSUED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` of `CRITICAL_NONCONFORMANCE`. Escalation resolves that linked certificate more narrowly than creation does, because it speaks of *the* linked certificate: it is the certificate named by `certificate_id`, or, when the record names none, the certificate reached from a `lot_test_id` or a `complaint_id` source. A record whose only source key is `audit_id` therefore has no linked certificate on this path and its escalation suspends nothing. A record can also escalate without any reviewer involvement at all: when a surveillance sweep finds a nonconformance still `OPEN` whose `ca_due_on` has passed, it moves `OPEN → ESCALATED` with precisely the same set of consequences.

An accepted plan is not the end of the story. A `FieldAuditor` who is not the person named in `ca_decided_by_user_id` verifies effectiveness on site, supplying `effectiveness_confirmed`. When it is true the record moves `CA_ACCEPTED → CLOSED`, sets `ca_verified` to true and `closed_on` to the logical clock, drops out of the applicant's penalty balance and triggers a posture recompute, and causes the audit named by `audit_id` to recompute `final_score` so that this finding's deduction is halved rather than removed. If the record carries a `complaint_id` and that consumer complaint's `status` is `SUBSTANTIATED` or `RECALL_COORDINATION`, the complaint is closed at the same time. When `effectiveness_confirmed` is false the record moves `CA_ACCEPTED → CA_REJECTED` with `ca_verified` left false, and — importantly — `ca_revision_count` is **not** increased by a failed verification, so a site whose fix did not hold gets another attempt at the plan without consuming a revision. Verification increases the record's `version` by 1 either way. `CLOSED` is the only terminal state; because `CA_ACCEPTED` can return to `CA_REJECTED`, the applicant's penalty balance can rise again after having fallen, which is exactly why it is always recomputed from the full population.

### 3.9. The Certificate: Issuance, Fees, Suspension, Reinstatement and Revocation

Issuance is the program's most heavily guarded write, and only a `CertificationDecisionMaker` may perform it. The request names the `product_id` and carries `expected_product_version`, the product `version` the decision maker last read; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_VERSION` before anything is evaluated further. Beyond that, the whole product file must line up: the manufacturing facility's `sanitation_validation_status` must read `VALIDATED` (`ERR_FACILITY_SANITATION_NOT_VALIDATED`); that facility's `last_closed_audit_outcome` must be `CLOSED_PASS` or `CLOSED_CONDITIONAL`, so neither `NONE` nor `CLOSED_FAIL` (`ERR_FACILITY_AUDIT_NOT_PASSED`); there must be no unresolved `CRITICAL` nonconformance in the way (`ERR_OPEN_CRITICAL_NONCONFORMANCE`); every ingredient in the formula must be `APPROVED` (`ERR_INGREDIENT_NOT_APPROVED`); `testing_plan_status` must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_TESTING_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`); `label_status` must be `APPROVED` (`ERR_LABEL_NOT_APPROVED`); `mark_tier_granted` must not be `NONE` and must equal `requested_mark_tier` (`ERR_MARK_TIER_NOT_GRANTED`); no certificate of this product may already hold `status` of `ISSUED` or `SUSPENDED` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_CERTIFICATE`); and the owning applicant's `compliance_posture` must be neither `PROBATION` nor `SUSPENDED` (`ERR_APPLICANT_POSTURE_BLOCKED`). Finally the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the product's `formula_cleared_by_user_id` and from the `closed_by_user_id` of the facility's most recently closed audit, or the issuance is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

A certificate that clears all of that is created with `status` of `ISSUED`, `surveillance_status` of `CURRENT`, `suspension_count` of 0, `mark_usage_locked` of false, `fee_charged` of true, `fee_refunded` of false, `issued_on` set to the logical clock and `issued_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, with `suspended_on`, `suspension_reason`, `revoked_on` and `revocation_reason` all null. Three values are snapshotted at issuance and never move afterwards: `applicant_id` is copied from the product's `applicant_id` so posture aggregation can read it without a join, `mark_tier_granted` is copied from the product's granted tier and governs the mark-usage rules the applicant must follow, and `certified_threshold_ppm` is copied from the product's `threshold_ppm` and is the ppm bound printed alongside the mark. The certificate's term and first surveillance date follow from `issued_on`: `expires_on` is 1095 days later — a three-year term — and `surveillance_due_on` is 365 days later. Issuance then increases the applicant's `active_certified_product_count`, sets the applicant's `current_cycle_started_on` if it was still null, recomputes the applicant's posture fields, and increases the product's `version` by 1.

**The fee.** Certification is billed once, at issuance, in integer cents, in four steps that run exactly once and in this order. Step one computes `base_fee_cents` as 98000 cents of product base fee, plus 6000 cents for each ingredient counted in the product's `linked_ingredient_count`, plus a further 45000 cents when the manufacturing facility has `shared_equipment_present` true and nothing extra when it is false. Step two selects the surveillance rate by comparing `base_fee_cents` — and specifically the base, never the eventual total — against two ceilings: a base below 150000 cents takes a rate of 0.20, a base from 150000 cents up to but not including 250000 cents takes 0.15, and a base of 250000 cents or more takes 0.10. Step three sets `surveillance_surcharge_cents` to `base_fee_cents` multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents. Step four sets `total_fee_cents` to `base_fee_cents` plus `surveillance_surcharge_cents`. Keying the tier on the base fee is the deliberate resolution of what would otherwise be circular — the surcharge depends on the rate, the rate looks as though it should depend on the billed total, and the total depends on the surcharge — and the rate is never re-selected once step four has run. `total_fee_cents` is then added to the applicant's `fee_balance_cents`.

**Suspension.** A `CertificationDecisionMaker` may suspend an `ISSUED` certificate directly, supplying a `suspension_reason` from `FAILED_UNANNOUNCED_TEST`, `CRITICAL_NONCONFORMANCE`, `SURVEILLANCE_OVERDUE`, `INGREDIENT_BLOCKED`, `SUBSTANTIATED_COMPLAINT` and `CHANGE_REQUIRES_RECERT`; a certificate that is not `ISSUED`, or that is `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED`, is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_TERMINAL`. The same `ISSUED → SUSPENDED` transition is also reached automatically by six different cascades, each stamping the matching reason — a failed unannounced lot, a critical nonconformance or an escalation, missed surveillance, a blocked ingredient anywhere in the formula, a substantiated consumer complaint, and a change assessed as requiring recertification. However it is reached, suspension sets `suspended_on` to the logical clock, records the `suspension_reason`, increases `suspension_count` by 1 and sets `mark_usage_locked` to true, which is the flag that tells the applicant the mark may no longer be displayed. It moves the product's `label_status` from `APPROVED` to `FROZEN`. It decreases the applicant's `active_certified_product_count`, increases `suspended_certificate_count`, and recomputes `posture_score_rolling_12m`, `posture_score_cycle` and `compliance_posture`. It leaves `fee_balance_cents` completely alone — suspension never touches the fee ledger.

**Reinstatement.** Bringing a certificate back is possible only from `SUSPENDED`; any other value, including the terminal `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED`, is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_SUSPENDED`. Reinstatement additionally requires that no nonconformance of this certificate has `severity` of `CRITICAL` with a `status` of `OPEN`, `CA_SUBMITTED`, `CA_REJECTED` or `ESCALATED` (`ERR_OPEN_CRITICAL_NONCONFORMANCE`), that the product's `formula_status` is `CLEARED`, that the certificate's `surveillance_status` is not `OVERDUE`, that the facility's sanitation validation reads `VALIDATED` (`ERR_FACILITY_SANITATION_NOT_VALIDATED`), and that the applicant's `compliance_posture` is neither `PROBATION` nor `SUSPENDED` (`ERR_APPLICANT_POSTURE_BLOCKED`). On success the certificate moves `SUSPENDED → ISSUED`, clears `suspended_on` and `suspension_reason` to null and sets `mark_usage_locked` back to false, while `suspension_count` is **not** decreased — it is the permanent record of how many suspensions this certificate has survived. The product's `label_status` returns `FROZEN → APPROVED` with `mark_tier_granted` unchanged. The applicant's `active_certified_product_count` increases again and `suspended_certificate_count` decreases, and the posture fields are recomputed, restoring exactly the values that held before the suspension.

**Revocation.** Revocation is permanent and is accepted from both `ISSUED` and `SUSPENDED`, refused only for a certificate that is already `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED` (`ERR_CERTIFICATE_TERMINAL`). The decision maker supplies a `revocation_reason` of `VOLUNTARY_WITHDRAWAL`, `UNRESOLVED_NONCONFORMANCE`, `REPEAT_FAILURE` or `FRAUD`. The certificate records `revoked_on` as the logical clock and the supplied reason, sets `mark_usage_locked` to true, and freezes the product's `label_status` from `APPROVED` to `FROZEN`. The applicant's counters are then adjusted according to where the certificate came from: revoking an `ISSUED` certificate decreases `active_certified_product_count`, whereas revoking a `SUSPENDED` one — whose active count was already decreased at suspension — decreases `suspended_certificate_count` instead; in both cases `revoked_certificate_count` increases by 1 and the posture fields are recomputed, applying the override that forces `compliance_posture` to `SUSPENDED` when the reason was `FRAUD` or `REPEAT_FAILURE`.

The fee is refunded on revocation only when **all three** of the following hold: the `revocation_reason` is `VOLUNTARY_WITHDRAWAL`; the logical clock is at or before 30 days after `issued_on`, with the boundary inclusive so that revoking exactly 30 days after issuance still refunds and 30 days plus one second does not; and no lot test of the certificate's product exists whose `test_type` is `UNANNOUNCED`. When all three hold, `fee_refunded` becomes true and the applicant's `fee_balance_cents` decreases by exactly this certificate's `total_fee_cents`, restoring the balance held before the certificate was issued. Otherwise `fee_refunded` stays false and the balance is untouched. The middle condition is the cooling-off period; the third is the anti-gaming rule, because a company that has already been visited by an unannounced retail test may not withdraw its way out of the finding.

`REVOKED` and `EXPIRED` are both terminal. `EXPIRED` is never requested: it is reached only when a surveillance sweep finds a certificate whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `SUSPENDED` and whose `expires_on` has been reached. Expiry sets `mark_usage_locked` to true and freezes the product's `label_status` from `APPROVED` to `FROZEN`. An expiring `ISSUED` certificate decreases the applicant's `active_certified_product_count`; an expiring `SUSPENDED` one decreases `suspended_certificate_count` instead. Either way the posture fields are recomputed.

### 3.10. Staying Certified: Surveillance, Change Notification, and Consumer Complaints

**The surveillance dimension.** Every certificate carries `surveillance_status` as a dimension entirely independent of its `status`: it advances on the logical clock whether the certificate is `ISSUED` or `SUSPENDED`, and it can feed back into `status`. A certificate whose `surveillance_due_on` has not yet been reached reads `CURRENT`. Once the clock has reached `surveillance_due_on` but is still short of 60 days past it, the certificate reads `DUE`, which is the program's signal that a `SURVEILLANCE` audit is now expected; reaching `DUE` changes nothing else. Once the clock reaches 60 days past `surveillance_due_on` — exactly 60 days past is already `OVERDUE`, not `DUE` — the certificate reads `OVERDUE`, and that is not merely a label: if its `status` is `ISSUED` it is thereupon moved `ISSUED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` of `SURVEILLANCE_OVERDUE`, freezing the product's `label_status` to `FROZEN` and forcing an applicant posture recompute. A certificate that was never observed during its `DUE` window jumps `CURRENT → OVERDUE` directly, with identical consequences; the window edges are inclusive on the left and exclusive on the right, so a certificate whose `surveillance_due_on` equals the clock exactly is already `DUE` rather than `CURRENT`.

Recovery of the surveillance dimension comes from the audit floor. When a `SURVEILLANCE` audit of the certificate's product's facility closes as `CLOSED_PASS` or `CLOSED_CONDITIONAL`, every affected certificate has `surveillance_due_on` rebased to 365 days after the logical clock and `surveillance_status` re-derived from the rules above, which lands it back on `CURRENT` — from `DUE` and from `OVERDUE` alike. Crucially, a certificate that was suspended for `SURVEILLANCE_OVERDUE` is **not** automatically un-suspended by that audit; the surveillance clock is reset but the suspension must still be lifted by an explicit reinstatement.

**The sweep.** All clock-driven change in this program is materialized by a single `ProgramAdministrator` operation, the program surveillance sweep, which takes no input and evaluates the whole register against the current logical clock. In one run it re-derives `surveillance_status` for every certificate whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `SUSPENDED` and suspends the newly `OVERDUE` `ISSUED` ones with `suspension_reason` of `SURVEILLANCE_OVERDUE`; moves every certificate whose `expires_on` has been reached to `EXPIRED`; moves every `VERIFIED` supplier document whose `expires_on` has been reached to `EXPIRED` and blocks every `APPROVED` ingredient whose required document set is thereby incomplete with `blocked_reason` of `DOCUMENT_EXPIRED`, cascading onward to products and certificates; moves every `VALIDATED` facility whose `sanitation_expires_on` has been reached to `EXPIRED`; cancels every `SCHEDULED` audit whose `onsite_end_at` has been reached; escalates every `OPEN` nonconformance whose `ca_due_on` has passed; and recomputes `open_nc_penalty_points`, `active_certified_product_count`, `suspended_certificate_count`, both posture scores and `compliance_posture` for every applicant. The sweep answers with a summary of what it did: `run_at` carrying the logical instant of the run, and the counts `certificates_marked_due`, `certificates_marked_overdue`, `certificates_suspended`, `certificates_expired`, `documents_expired`, `ingredients_blocked`, `facility_validations_expired`, `audits_cancelled` and `nonconformances_escalated`.

**Change notification.** A certified scope is not allowed to drift. An `ApplicantContact` must notify the program of changes by filing against the `certificate_id` whose certified scope the change touches, declaring a `change_type` of `INGREDIENT_SUBSTITUTION`, `SUPPLIER_CHANGE`, `FORMULA_PCT_CHANGE` or `FACILITY_LINE_CHANGE`, the `effective_on` date the change is intended to take effect in production, and a `description` of it. An `INGREDIENT_SUBSTITUTION` additionally names the `replacement_ingredient_id` being introduced; the other change types introduce no new ingredient and leave it null. The named certificate must be neither `REVOKED` nor `EXPIRED` (`ERR_CERTIFICATE_TERMINAL`), and the applicant's `compliance_posture` must be neither `PROBATION` nor `SUSPENDED` (`ERR_APPLICANT_POSTURE_BLOCKED`). Filing computes `notified_days_before_effective` as the number of whole days from the logical clock to `effective_on`, discarding any fractional day, and the value may be negative when the notice is retroactive. `late_notification` is true exactly when `notified_days_before_effective` is below 30. For `INGREDIENT_SUBSTITUTION` and `SUPPLIER_CHANGE` the notice period is mandatory rather than advisory: at least 30 days of notice is required, exactly 30 days is accepted and 29 is not, and a short notice is refused with `ERR_LATE_CHANGE_NOTIFICATION`. `FORMULA_PCT_CHANGE` and `FACILITY_LINE_CHANGE` are exempt from that rejection and merely carry `late_notification` as a flag. A filed notification opens with `status` of `SUBMITTED`, `required_retest` of false, `impact_level` null, and `assessed_by_user_id` and `assessed_on` null.

A `CertificationReviewer` assesses a `SUBMITTED` notification — any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CHANGE_NOTIFICATION_STATE` — by supplying a `decision` and an `impact_level`, and the branch is chosen by the assessed impact rather than by the decision alone. Every branch records `assessed_on` as the logical clock, `assessed_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, and the supplied `impact_level`. An `ACCEPT` with an `impact_level` of `NONE` moves the notification to `ACCEPTED_NO_IMPACT` and leaves `required_retest` false; nothing else changes. An `ACCEPT` with an `impact_level` of `LOW` moves it to `ACCEPTED_WITH_CONDITIONS`, sets `required_retest` to true — obliging a `FOR_CAUSE` lot test before the effective date — and makes the certificate's product recompute `threshold_ppm`, `sampling_lots_per_year` and `sampling_exclusion_days` from its current ingredient set, using exactly the arithmetic of §3.6. A `REJECT`, and equally an `impact_level` of `HIGH` whatever the decision says, moves it to `REJECTED_REQUIRES_RECERT`, sets `required_retest` to true, suspends an `ISSUED` linked certificate with `suspension_reason` of `CHANGE_REQUIRES_RECERT` — freezing the product's `label_status` and recomputing the applicant's posture — and raises a nonconformance with `source_type` of `CHANGE_NOTIFICATION`, `category` of `DOCUMENTATION`, `severity` of `MAJOR`, `certificate_id` set to the linked certificate, `applicant_id` taken from that certificate, `raised_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and a `ca_due_on` 30 days after the finding's `created_at`. All three assessment outcomes are terminal.

**Consumer complaints.** Complaints reach the program through operations rather than through the applicant, so a `ProgramAdministrator` files each one against the `certificate_id` the complainant named, recording the `exposure_date` on which the product was consumed, a `symptom_severity` of `MILD`, `MODERATE`, `SEVERE` or `ANAPHYLAXIS`, and the `lot_code` printed on the pack when the complainant supplied one. The named certificate must be neither `REVOKED` nor `EXPIRED` (`ERR_CERTIFICATE_TERMINAL`). A filed complaint opens with `status` of `RECEIVED`, `reported_on` set to the logical clock, `triage_priority` null, `recall_action` of `NONE`, `recall_scope_lot_codes` as an empty list, and `investigated_by_user_id`, `outcome_evidence` and `closed_on` null.

A `CertificationReviewer` triages a `RECEIVED` complaint — any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE` — which moves it `RECEIVED → INVESTIGATING` and sets `triage_priority` from `symptom_severity`: `P1` for `SEVERE` and `ANAPHYLAXIS`, `P2` for `MODERATE`, `P3` for `MILD`. Triage is also where the recall footprint is fixed, and its selector policy is pinned in all four of its elements. **Eligibility:** a lot test of the complaint's certified product is a candidate only if its `status` is `RECORDED` — a lot superseded by a retest is never a candidate — and its `sample_collected_on` falls inside the closed interval running from 90 days before `exposure_date` through `exposure_date` itself, so a lot collected exactly 90 days before exposure is eligible, one collected 91 days before is not, and one collected exactly on `exposure_date` is eligible. **Ranking:** candidates are ordered by `sample_collected_on` with the most recent first. **Tie-break:** candidates sharing a `sample_collected_on` are ordered by `created_at` ascending, so the earlier-recorded row takes the higher rank. **Take:** the first 3 candidates in that order are selected, and if fewer than 3 candidates exist all of them are selected. `recall_scope_lot_codes` is then the `lot_code` of each selected lot test in selection order, and each selected lot test has its `in_recall_scope` set to true.

`recall_action` follows from that selection. When there are no candidates at all, `recall_scope_lot_codes` stays empty and `recall_action` is `ADVISORY_ONLY`. When at least one **selected** lot test has a `verdict` of `FAIL`, `recall_action` is `MANDATORY_RECALL_SUPPORT`. Otherwise `recall_action` is `VOLUNTARY_RECALL`. A failing lot that was eligible but ranked fourth or lower does not upgrade the action — only the selected three can.

The investigation outcome is recorded on an `INVESTIGATING` complaint by a `CertificationReviewer` supplying an `outcome` and an `outcome_evidence` of `LOT_TEST_FAILURE`, `LABEL_ERROR`, `CROSS_CONTACT` or `NO_EVIDENCE`. An outcome of `UNSUBSTANTIATED` moves the complaint `INVESTIGATING → UNSUBSTANTIATED`, records `investigated_by_user_id`, and changes nothing else at all: no finding is raised, no certificate moves and no posture is recomputed. An outcome of `SUBSTANTIATED` forks on the `recall_action` computed at triage: while `recall_action` is `ADVISORY_ONLY` the complaint moves `INVESTIGATING → SUBSTANTIATED`, and while `recall_action` is `VOLUNTARY_RECALL` or `MANDATORY_RECALL_SUPPORT` it moves `INVESTIGATING → RECALL_COORDINATION` instead, with `recall_scope_lot_codes` necessarily non-empty on that branch. Both substantiated branches carry the identical consequence set: `investigated_by_user_id` is recorded; a nonconformance is raised with `source_type` of `COMPLAINT`, `complaint_id` naming this complaint, `applicant_id` taken from the certificate, `category` of `THRESHOLD_EXCEEDANCE`, and `severity` of `CRITICAL` when `symptom_severity` is `SEVERE` or `ANAPHYLAXIS` and `MAJOR` otherwise; when that finding is `CRITICAL` and the complaint's certificate is `ISSUED`, the certificate moves `ISSUED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` of `SUBSTANTIATED_COMPLAINT` and the product's `label_status` freezes; and the applicant recomputes `substantiated_complaint_count_rolling` and `substantiated_complaint_count_cycle`, then `posture_score_rolling_12m`, `posture_score_cycle` and `compliance_posture`.

A complaint is never closed by hand. Both `SUBSTANTIATED` and `RECALL_COORDINATION` reach `CLOSED` only as a consequence of the nonconformance raised from that complaint having its corrective action verified as effective, at which point the complaint's `closed_on` is set to the logical clock. The certificate suspension is not lifted by that closure, and on the recall branch `recall_scope_lot_codes` and `recall_action` are left exactly as computed, as the permanent historical record of what was recalled. `UNSUBSTANTIATED` and `CLOSED` are the two terminal states of a complaint.
