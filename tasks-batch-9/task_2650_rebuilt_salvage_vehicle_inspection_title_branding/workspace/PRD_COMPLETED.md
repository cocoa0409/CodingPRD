# Rebuilt Salvage Title Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When an insurance carrier writes a vehicle off as a total loss, the vehicle does not disappear — it re-enters commerce, often after a rebuilder has replaced its structure, restraints and lighting with parts of wildly varying provenance. The state's obligation is to make that history permanently legible: a buyer three owners downstream must be able to see that the car in front of them was once a wreck, and a wreck that was declared beyond repair must never be retitled at all. This document specifies the service that carries that obligation. It ingests carrier total-loss evaluations, derives the statutory brand from the damage-to-value ratio, licenses and polices the rebuilders who perform the repairs, adjudicates the physical inspection that a rebuilt vehicle must pass, mints the branded title instruments, carries those brands forward across every subsequent conveyance, negotiates recognition of those brands with other jurisdictions, and refers odometer and identity irregularities to the fraud unit.

The program's pain points are the reason the specification is unusually strict. Rebuilders assemble evidence packages from receipts, invoices, donor certificates and photographs whose traceability is uneven, and the program has historically had no quantitative handle on which rebuilders keep good records. Inspection capacity is scarce: inspectors are commissioned individuals with expiring credentials and stations have a fixed number of bays, so double-booking one of them silently destroys a citizen's appointment. Money moves through the program as escrow rather than as revenue, so a withdrawal, a denial and a title issuance must each dispose of the same escrowed charge in a different way without ever leaking a cent. And because branding is the whole point of the program, brand severity must be monotonic: a brand may be carried, downgraded by a foreign jurisdiction or refused, but never softened by a transfer and never escalated by a recognition decision.

Every endpoint is mounted under the `/v1` prefix. The service is deterministic and single-threaded from the caller's point of view: there are no background jobs, every derived quantity is recomputed inside the request that could have changed it, and every time-dependent rule reads a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time so that expiries, windows and rolling scoring windows can be driven exactly during acceptance testing. All state lives in process memory and every collection starts empty on each boot: no row is ever seeded, preloaded or hardcoded.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Program Participants and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role the principal is acting in. If either header is absent, empty or made up only of whitespace, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined. When both headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called (see the endpoint table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `StateTitleClerk`, `InsurerAdjuster`, `Rebuilder`, `Inspector`, `HearingOfficer` and `FraudInvestigator`.

A `ProgramAdmin` is the state program administrator. Administrators license rebuilders and commission inspectors, open inspection stations, credit fee deposits, take licence actions, decide interstate recognition requests, and drive the logical clock. A `StateTitleClerk` staffs the title office: clerks register vehicles, issue and surrender titles, review documentation packages, schedule and cancel inspection attempts, record conveyances, submit titles for interstate recognition, and may file fraud referrals. An `InsurerAdjuster` files the total-loss damage evaluation whose damage ratio triggers the salvage brand, and does nothing else. A `Rebuilder` is a licensed repair business: rebuilders open applications, file parts documentation and photographs, submit for inspection, withdraw, and appeal failures, and may act only on their own applications. An `Inspector` is a commissioned individual who verifies or rejects parts documents, starts and completes inspection attempts, records findings, and may file fraud referrals. A `HearingOfficer` reviews and decides appeals of failed inspections and has no inspection, titling or investigation authority. A `FraudInvestigator` takes up and resolves odometer, identity-tampering and forgery referrals; substantiation voids titles and applications. Every role may read every collection and every single resource except where the endpoint table in §4 narrows the audience.

Two of the roles are entity-backed, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `Rebuilder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Rebuilder` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Rebuilder` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — concretely, the target `RebuiltApplication`'s `rebuilder_id` (or, when the application is being created, the `rebuilder_id` carried in the request body) must equal `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Inspector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Inspector` row, and the target `Inspection`'s `inspector_id` must equal `X-User-ID`. A mismatch in either case is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership rule guards nine operations: opening an application, submitting it, filing a parts document against it, withdrawing it, reading its fee quotation, filing an appeal on one of its inspections, and starting, recording findings on, and completing an inspection. On the three operations that additionally read the acting inspector's own credential — verifying a parts document, rejecting one, and starting an attempt — an `X-User-ID` that resolves to no `Inspector` row at all is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), evaluated after the request body has been checked for well-formedness rather than at the step-3 position §2.5 gives path parameters.

The other five roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it (as `suspended_by_user_id` on a `Rebuilder` whose licence the administrator suspended, and as `last_license_action_by_user_id` on every licence action) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `HearingOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier; it is recorded as `hearing_officer_user_id` on the `Appeal` the officer takes up, and is not compared to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `FraudInvestigator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier recorded as `investigator_user_id` on the referral the investigator takes up, and compared only against that referral's `opened_by_user_id` for the segregation-of-duties rule described in §2.10 — never against an entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `StateTitleClerk`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier; the server records it as `opened_by_user_id` when a clerk files a fraud referral, and otherwise does not persist or compare it. When `X-User-Role` is `InsurerAdjuster`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that the server neither persists nor compares to any entity's `id`.

Two further identity fields are written from the acting principal rather than from the request body. `recorded_by_inspector_id` on an inspection finding is the `X-User-ID` of the `Inspector` who recorded it, and it always equals the owning inspection's `inspector_id` because only the assigned inspector may record findings. `verified_by_inspector_id` on a parts document is the `X-User-ID` of the `Inspector` who verified or rejected it, and remains null while the document is still `SUBMITTED`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from uppercase A through Z and the digits 0 through 9.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one instant in the whole program taken from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, so it does not move when a caller moves that clock.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range from 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and draws `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and so is either parameter supplied as anything that is not an integer; either parameter supplied as an empty string is instead treated as absent and falls back to its default.

Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending in every list response, with no exceptions and no alternative sort parameter. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the current page, being true exactly when `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned on this page is strictly less than `total_count`.

Beyond pagination, each list endpoint accepts a small set of optional equality filters that exist to make every foreign-key relationship reverse-reachable. Damage reports may be filtered by `vehicle_id` and `damage_type`. Title records may be filtered by `vehicle_id`, by `predecessor_title_id` (which is how the self-referential chain is walked downward), by `source_damage_report_id`, by `brand_code`, by `is_current` and by `status`. Applications may be filtered by `vehicle_id`, `rebuilder_id`, `salvage_title_id` and `status`. Parts documents may be filtered by `application_id`, `donor_title_id`, `verified_by_inspector_id`, `status` and `part_class`. Inspections may be filtered by `application_id`, `inspector_id`, `station_id`, `status` and `result`. Findings may be filtered by `inspection_id`, `parts_document_id`, `category` and `severity`. Appeals may be filtered by `inspection_id` and `status`. Fraud referrals may be filtered by `vehicle_id`, `application_id`, `inspection_id`, `referral_type` and `status`. Recognition requests may be filtered by `title_record_id`, `requesting_state_code` and `status`. Recorded transfers may be filtered by `from_title_id`, `to_title_id` and `odometer_flag`. Any filter supplied as an empty string is ignored, as is any query parameter the endpoint does not declare. The one non-string filter, `is_current`, accepts `true`, `false`, `1` and `0` in any letter case and rejects anything else with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every resource in the program carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `Vehicle`, `Rebuilder`, `Inspector`, `InspectionStation`, `TitleRecord`, `RebuiltApplication`, `PartsDocument`, `Inspection`, `Appeal`, `FraudReferral`, `InterstateRecognitionRequest` and `TitleTransfer` all carry the full set — `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Two entities are deliberately append-only and carry only `id` and `created_at`: `InsurerDamageReport` and `InspectionFinding` have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all. A carrier's total-loss evaluation is a filed document, and an inspector's finding is a contemporaneous observation; neither is ever edited in place, so neither exposes those two properties.

All time-dependent behaviour reads a single server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time. Before any caller moves it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `ProgramAdmin` may move the clock forward or backward by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the resulting `current_utc`, and its `data` object carries that single field and nothing else, formatted like every other server-stamped instant. The clock stamps every `created_at` and `updated_at`, and it also stamps the domain instants `issued_at`, `started_at`, `completed_at`, `filed_at`, `decided_at`, `resolved_at` and `terminal_at`. Instants the caller supplies instead — `license_expires_at`, `credential_expires_at`, `report_effective_at`, `scheduled_start_at`, `scheduled_end_at`, `transfer_effective_at` and the clock's own `to_iso8601` — are parsed as ISO 8601 carrying any UTC offset, or none at all in which case they are read as UTC, and each is stored and echoed back in the exact string the caller sent rather than renormalised to the three-fractional-digit form. Moving the clock re-derives every clock-dependent quantity, including each inspector's `credential_status`, the submission and appeal windows, and the trailing window of the rolling record-keeping score; the move itself writes no row, however — it refreshes no `updated_at` and increments no `version` — so each of those quantities is recomputed the next time its own row is read or written.

### 2.5. Validation Precedence

Several checks can fail on the same request, and the program is explicit about which one the caller sees, because clients build their retry logic on the returned `error_id`. Checks are evaluated in the following fixed order, and the first failure short-circuits the request:

1. Header authentication — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorisation against the endpoint's role list — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. Existence of every resource named by a path segment, validated strictly left to right, so that a nested path reports the leftmost unresolvable segment. Depending on the segment, this yields `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REBUILDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTS_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TITLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECOGNITION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DAMAGE_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404. This step applies to every endpoint that has path parameters.
4. Existence of every foreign key supplied in the request body, validated in the order the endpoint declares its body fields. This step applies to every creating operation that carries foreign keys and yields the same 404 family: `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REBUILDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TITLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTS_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DAMAGE_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`. Any title-shaped foreign key — `salvage_title_id`, `predecessor_title_id`, `donor_title_id`, `from_title_id` or `title_record_id` — that fails to resolve reports `ERR_TITLE_NOT_FOUND`. A foreign-key field that is absent, explicitly null, or not a string never reaches the existence check at all: it is malformed and reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` from this position in the order.
5. Request well-formedness — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). This covers a missing required field — a required string that is empty or made up only of whitespace counts as missing, while a string that clears that bar is stored exactly as supplied with no trimming — an enum value that is not a declared member, a negative money or mileage amount where the field is constrained to be non-negative, a timestamp that is not ISO 8601, a `scheduled_end_at` that is at or before `scheduled_start_at`, a `limit` outside 1 through 100 or a negative `offset`, a `vin` that is not exactly seventeen legal characters, a `checkpoint_results` array that is missing one of the weighted checkpoints, and an `attestation_signed` value that is not true. It also covers an integer-typed field supplied as a boolean or as a number with a fractional part, though a whole-valued number such as `7500.0` is accepted for one; and supplying `null` for an optional field is exactly equivalent to omitting it, so that field's documented default applies.
6. Uniqueness of natural keys — `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY` (HTTP 409). This applies when registering a vehicle, licensing a rebuilder, commissioning an inspector, opening a station, or filing a damage report, and covers `vin`, `license_number`, `badge_number`, `station_code`, and the pair of `insurer_name` with `claim_number`.
7. Entity-backed ownership — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, as described in §2.1.
8. Optimistic concurrency — `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` first, then `ERR_STALE_TITLE_VERSION`, as described in §2.8.
9. State-machine admissibility, in this internal order: `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_DRAFT`, then `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`, then `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_SCHEDULED`, then `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, then `ERR_PARTS_DOCUMENT_STATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_TITLE_NOT_ACTIVE`, then `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_REFERRAL_STATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_LICENSE_ACTION_INVALID`, then `ERR_RECOGNITION_ALREADY_DECIDED`.
10. Business safety, in this internal order: `ERR_VEHICLE_FRAUD_HOLD`, then `ERR_NON_REPAIRABLE_BRAND`, then `ERR_STOLEN_COMPONENT_UNRESOLVED`, then `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
11. Referential business rules, in this internal order: `ERR_REBUILDER_LICENSE_INACTIVE`, then `ERR_INSPECTOR_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`, then `ERR_SALVAGE_TITLE_REQUIRED`, then `ERR_DONOR_TITLE_NOT_TRACEABLE`, then `ERR_STATION_NOT_FLOOD_CAPABLE`.
12. Quantitative business constraints, in this internal order: `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_APPLICATION`, then `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE`, then `ERR_PHOTO_SET_INCOMPLETE`, then `ERR_ATTEMPT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_APPEAL_PENDING_BLOCKS_SCHEDULING`, then `ERR_INSPECTOR_INTERVAL_CONFLICT`, then `ERR_STATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
13. Temporal windows, in this internal order: `ERR_SUBMISSION_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, then `ERR_INSPECTION_SLOT_IN_PAST`, then `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`, then `ERR_TRANSFER_PREDATES_TITLE`.
14. Identity and brand integrity: `ERR_VIN_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_BRAND_ESCALATION_FORBIDDEN`.
15. Fiscal capacity, last of all: `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FEE_BALANCE`, then `ERR_RECORDKEEPING_SCORE_BLOCKS_ACTION`.

The practical consequence worth internalising is that a fraud hold on the vehicle surfaces before any licence, credential, capacity, window or fiscal problem, and that the fiscal checks are always the last to speak — a caller who is both broke and scheduling into an occupied bay hears about the bay.

### 2.6. The Brand Severity Ladder

Five brand codes exist, and they are totally ordered by a fixed severity value that the whole program reasons with: `NONE` has severity 0, `SALVAGE` has severity 30, `REBUILT` has severity 40, `FLOOD` has severity 50, and `NON_REPAIRABLE` has severity 90. Because the five values are distinct integers, the most severe member of any set of brands is unique and no tie-break rule is ever needed. A title record publishes its own severity as `brand_severity`, the integer severity of its `brand_code`, and a freshly created title with no brand publishes 0.

Severity is monotonic in two directions that must not be confused. Within a vehicle's own title chain, severity never softens: a conveyance carries forward the most severe brand anywhere in the chain, and a decision that would carry something less severe than the chain maximum is refused with `ERR_BRAND_ESCALATION_FORBIDDEN`. Across jurisdictions, severity never hardens: a foreign state asked to recognise a brand may carry it as-is, downgrade it to a strictly less severe brand, or refuse to carry any brand at all, but it may never map it to a strictly more severe brand — that attempt is also refused with `ERR_BRAND_ESCALATION_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 422).

Wherever a brand is quoted the exact enum spelling is used: `NONE`, `SALVAGE`, `REBUILT`, `FLOOD` and `NON_REPAIRABLE`.

### 2.7. The Two Independent Dimensions of a Title Row

A title record carries two lifecycle dimensions on the same row, and they evolve independently of one another. The first is `status`, whose members are `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED`, `VOID` and `SURRENDERED`, defaulting to `ACTIVE`. A title becomes `SUPERSEDED` the moment a successor title is minted over it, becomes `VOID` when fraud against the vehicle is substantiated, and becomes `SURRENDERED` when a `NON_REPAIRABLE` vehicle is handed in. The second is `recognition_state`, whose members are `NOT_SUBMITTED`, `RECOGNITION_PENDING`, `RECOGNIZED`, `DOWNGRADED` and `STALE`, defaulting to `NOT_SUBMITTED`. It moves from `NOT_SUBMITTED` to `RECOGNITION_PENDING` when the title is submitted to another state, from `RECOGNITION_PENDING` to `RECOGNIZED` or `DOWNGRADED` when that state decides, back from `RECOGNIZED` or from `DOWNGRADED` to `RECOGNITION_PENDING` when the title is submitted to a further state, and from `RECOGNITION_PENDING` to `STALE` whenever `status` leaves `ACTIVE` while a request is still pending.

A companion boolean, `is_current`, is true exactly when the row's `status` is `ACTIVE` and no other title record of the same vehicle is `ACTIVE`; it defaults to true on creation and is driven to false whenever the row leaves `ACTIVE`. The chain position is published as `transfer_sequence`, the number of title records in this row's predecessor chain, which is 0 for the first title ever issued on a vehicle.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Applications and Titles

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because both are deliberately allowed to supersede work that another writer performed, and only a version check can stop a client from destroying a state it never observed.

Scheduling an inspection attempt against an application requires `expected_application_version` as a mandatory, non-optional body field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `RebuiltApplication`. If that value does not equal the application's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rule exists because a later scheduling call legitimately supersedes a still-`SCHEDULED` attempt by cancelling it with `cancellation_reason` set to `RESCHEDULED`; without the version check, a writer holding a stale read would silently annul a booking made after its own read. Every successful scheduling call increments the application's `version` by 1, so a client must re-read before scheduling again.

Submitting a title for interstate recognition requires `expected_title_version` as a mandatory, non-optional body field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `TitleRecord`. If that value does not equal the title's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TITLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here too the operation is a supersede: a second recognition request for the same still-`ACTIVE` title moves the earlier pending request to `SUPERSEDED`, so the stale-version check is the only thing preventing a writer holding an outdated brand snapshot from replacing a newer submission. A successful recognition submission increments the title's `version` by 1.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Operations not named in this subsection take no `expected_application_version`, no `expected_title_version` and no version field of any other name.

### 2.9. Fraud Holds as a Program-Wide Gate

Every vehicle publishes a `fraud_hold` posture whose members are `NONE`, `UNDER_REFERRAL` and `CONFIRMED`, defaulting to `NONE`. It reads `NONE` until a fraud referral is opened against the vehicle. It reads `UNDER_REFERRAL` while any referral against the vehicle has status `OPEN` or `INVESTIGATING`. It reads `CONFIRMED` once any referral against the vehicle reaches `SUBSTANTIATED`, and `CONFIRMED` is sticky — it is never walked back.

A `fraud_hold` other than `NONE` blocks six operations, and both `UNDER_REFERRAL` and `CONFIRMED` block equally: opening an application, submitting an application, scheduling an inspection attempt, issuing a rebuilt title, starting an inspection attempt, and recording a title conveyance. In all six the rejection is `ERR_VEHICLE_FRAUD_HOLD` (HTTP 409). Separately, a title may not be created at all for a vehicle whose `fraud_hold` is `CONFIRMED`.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Three pairs of actions in this program must not be performed by the same principal, and all three failures report `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). First, when a re-inspection is scheduled, the `inspector_id` in the body must differ from the `inspector_id` of the application's most recent `COMPLETED_FAIL` inspection — the inspector who failed a vehicle may not be the one who re-inspects it. Second, a licence reinstatement may not be performed by the administrator recorded in the rebuilder's `suspended_by_user_id`; whoever suspended the licence may not lift the suspension. Third, a fraud referral may not be resolved by the principal recorded in its `opened_by_user_id`; the person who raised the allegation may not adjudicate it. The same code is reserved for any other case in which the acting `X-User-ID` has already performed the incompatible earlier step in a review chain.

### 2.11. Precision, Rounding and the Money Discipline

All monetary quantities in this program are integers denominated in cents, and every field that holds one ends in `_cents`. Wherever a monetary computation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Where a formula sums several rounded line items, the rounding order is pinned by the formula that defines it and is never rearranged: rounding each line item and then summing is not interchangeable with summing and then rounding once, and the specification always says which one applies.

Percentage and score quantities — `damage_ratio` excepted — are decimal numbers carried to exactly 2 decimal places and rounded half up. This governs `documentation_completeness_pct`, `adequacy_score`, `recordkeeping_score_rolling` and `recordkeeping_score_lifetime`, each of which is additionally clamped to the closed range from 0.00 through 100.00. The single exception is `damage_ratio`, which is carried to exactly 4 decimal places and rounded half up, because the salvage and non-repairable thresholds are quoted to four places. Both kinds of quantity are published as JSON numbers rather than as strings, so the stated number of decimal places governs the rounding and not the rendering: a `damage_ratio` that rounds to 0.7500 is published as `0.75`, and a score that rounds to 100.00 is published as `100.0`, neither of them zero-padded back out to its nominal width.

Every threshold comparison in this document states its own inclusivity explicitly, and inclusivity is never approximated. A score of exactly the pass threshold passes; a ratio of exactly the salvage threshold brands.

### 2.12. Cascades, Counters and Derived Fields

The program keeps no stale derived data. Whenever an operation changes something a counter or computed field depends on, that counter or field is recomputed inside the same request, and every row whose published values that recomputation actually changed has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1 — once for each write the request makes to that row, so a row that a multi-stage cascade writes at two separate stages advances by 2 within that single request, while a recomputation landing on the value the row already held moves neither field. A read sits at the other boundary: it re-derives the computed quantities of the rows it publishes, so no response is ever stale, but it writes no row metadata at all, refreshing no `updated_at` and incrementing no `version`. Two derived values are refreshed in place even inside a mutating request: a rebuilder's `recordkeeping_score_rolling` and `recordkeeping_score_lifetime`, and an inspector's `credential_status`, always publish freshly derived values, but a change confined to them never by itself refreshes that row's `updated_at` or increments its `version`. Counters that behave this way include a vehicle's `open_application_count`, `active_brand_code`, `brand_history` and `last_odometer_miles`; a rebuilder's `fees_deposited_cents`, `fee_balance_cents`, `escrowed_fee_total_cents`, `fees_consumed_cents`, `open_referral_count`, `substantiated_referral_count`, `applications_titled_count`, `recordkeeping_score_rolling` and `recordkeeping_score_lifetime`; an inspector's `credential_status` and `scheduled_inspection_count`; a station's `scheduled_occupancy_count`; an application's `documented_parts_cost_cents`, `documentation_completeness_pct`, `photo_view_count`, `assessed_fee_cents`, `total_program_charge_cents` and `attempts_completed`; an inspection's `effective_end_at`, `blocking_findings_count`, `forced_fail`, `vin_verified` and `adequacy_score`; a parts document's `traceable`; a title's `brand_history`, `brand_severity`, `is_current` and `transfer_sequence`; a finding's `blocking`; and an appeal's `remand_count`. Of that list only an inspection's `effective_end_at` is exempt from later recomputation, being fixed once at booking as §3.7 describes.

A vehicle's `open_application_count` is the number of that vehicle's applications whose `status` is not one of `TITLE_ISSUED`, `DENIED_FINAL`, `WITHDRAWN` or `VOID_FRAUD`. Its `active_brand_code` is the `brand_code` of the title record of this vehicle whose `is_current` is true, and reads `NONE` when the vehicle has no `ACTIVE` title record. Its `brand_history` is the set of distinct `brand_code` values, excluding `NONE`, ever carried by any title record of this vehicle whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED`, `VOID` or `SURRENDERED`, sorted by brand severity descending. Its `last_odometer_miles` is the highest `odometer_reading_miles` among the vehicle's damage reports — every one of them, whatever its `report_effective_at` — and among its `RECORDED` conveyances whose `transfer_effective_at` is at or before the logical clock, the clock filter binding on the conveyances alone; it is null when no reading exists at all. A title record's own `brand_history` is the union of the brand codes found along its `predecessor_title_id` chain together with its own `brand_code`, excluding `NONE`, sorted by brand severity descending.

Cascades in this program are always immediate and always complete before the response is written. Where a single operation mutates several entities, §3 states every affected row, and an implementation that applies only the primary effect is incorrect.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Program Registry

Nothing in the program can happen until the four registry entities exist. A `StateTitleClerk` registers a vehicle by supplying its `vin`, its `model_year`, its `make`, its `model`, its `body_class` and its `registered_state_code`. The `vin` is the seventeen-character identification number as stamped on the public VIN plate; it must be exactly seventeen characters drawn from uppercase A through Z and the digits 0 through 9 with the letters I, O and Q excluded, and it must be globally unique across all vehicles. The `model_year` is the year printed on the certification label and must be at least 1950 and at most 2030. The `body_class` is one of `SEDAN`, `SUV`, `TRUCK`, `MOTORCYCLE` or `TRAILER`, and it is more consequential than it looks: it selects the list of part classes the rebuilder will later be required to document. Both `registered_state_code` and, elsewhere, every other state code in the program are exactly two uppercase letters. A newly registered vehicle starts with `fraud_hold` of `NONE`, `active_brand_code` of `NONE` and an empty `brand_history`.

Licensing a rebuilder is a `ProgramAdmin` act and takes the rebuilder's `legal_name`, its state `license_number` (unique across all rebuilders), the `license_expires_at` instant recorded as the licence's nominal expiry — the program stores and republishes it but never reads it, so `license_status` is moved only by an explicit licence action or by one of the automatic cascades of §3.12 and never by that instant passing — and the `bond_amount_cents` surety bond the rebuilder has posted, which must be at least zero. A new rebuilder starts with `license_status` of `ACTIVE` and with every fee counter at zero: `fees_deposited_cents`, `fee_balance_cents`, `escrowed_fee_total_cents`, `fees_consumed_cents`, `open_referral_count`, `substantiated_referral_count` and `applications_titled_count` are all 0. Both `suspended_by_user_id` and `last_license_action_by_user_id` begin null. Both record-keeping score variants of §3.5 read 100.00 on a brand-new rebuilder, that being the vacuous score of a rebuilder which has filed no parts documents at all.

Commissioning an inspector is likewise administrative and takes `full_name`, a `badge_number` unique across all inspectors, a `credential_expires_at` instant, a `certifications` array whose members are drawn from `STRUCTURAL`, `FLOOD` and `THEFT_RECOVERY` — the array itself is required but may legitimately be empty, and it is stored and republished in the order supplied — and a `home_state_code` naming the jurisdiction the inspector is commissioned in. The inspector's `credential_status` is never supplied: it reads `EXPIRED` when `credential_expires_at` is at or before the logical clock and `CURRENT` otherwise, which means that advancing the clock past an expiry silently disqualifies that inspector from every operation that requires a current credential. An inspector's `scheduled_inspection_count` is the number of that inspector's attempts whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, and starts at 0.

Opening an inspection station takes a program-assigned `station_code` unique across all stations, a `bay_count` of at least 1 which caps how many attempts may occupy the station at overlapping times, an `address_state_code`, and an optional `supports_flood` boolean that defaults to false and records whether the station is equipped to inspect `FLOOD`-branded vehicles. A new station's `scheduled_occupancy_count` — the number of its attempts whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` — starts at 0.

Finally, an administrator credits program funds to a rebuilder by depositing an `amount_cents` sum, which must be at least 0. A deposit is not itself a persisted resource and carries no `id` of its own: the call answers HTTP 200 with the updated `Rebuilder` object. Each accepted deposit adds its `amount_cents` to the rebuilder's `fees_deposited_cents` and the same amount to its `fee_balance_cents`, and increments the rebuilder's `version` by 1. `fees_deposited_cents` is therefore the cumulative sum of every accepted deposit amount, and it never decreases. Attempting to register a vehicle whose `vin` already exists, license a rebuilder whose `license_number` already exists, commission an inspector whose `badge_number` already exists, open a station whose `station_code` already exists, or file a damage report whose `insurer_name` and `claim_number` pair already exists, is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY`.

### 3.2. The Carrier's Total-Loss Evaluation and the Brand It Triggers

Salvage branding starts with the insurer, not with the state. An `InsurerAdjuster` files a total-loss evaluation naming the `vehicle_id` it describes, the `insurer_name` of the reporting carrier, the carrier's `claim_number`, a `basis_code`, the pre-loss actual cash value as `acv_cents`, the carrier's parts-plus-labour `repair_estimate_cents`, the dominant loss cause as `damage_type`, the appraiser's `odometer_reading_miles`, the `report_effective_at` instant at which the determination took effect, and a `total_loss_declared` boolean. Two further fields are optional: `salvage_retention_cents`, the value the carrier retains by selling the wreck, which defaults to 0, must be at least 0 and must be strictly less than `acv_cents`; and `flood_water_depth_cm`, the measured standing-water depth inside the passenger compartment in centimetres, which defaults to 0 and must be at least 0. `acv_cents` must be strictly greater than 0 and `repair_estimate_cents` must be at least 0. `damage_type` is one of `COLLISION`, `FLOOD`, `HAIL`, `THEFT_RECOVERY` or `FIRE`. `total_loss_declared` is recorded for audit and never changes any computed value.

Filing the report immediately derives two published quantities from exactly the fields of the row being created — no other report of the same vehicle participates in the arithmetic. The first is `damage_ratio`, the damage-to-value ratio. Its denominator is chosen by `basis_code`, which has exactly two members. Under the gross-estimate basis, declared as `GROSS_ESTIMATE`, the denominator is `acv_cents` alone and `salvage_retention_cents` is ignored entirely. Under the net-of-salvage basis, declared as `NET_OF_SALVAGE`, the denominator is `acv_cents` reduced by `salvage_retention_cents`; because that denominator is smaller, the same repair estimate and the same actual cash value produce a strictly higher ratio, and a vehicle that would have earned no brand at all under the gross basis can be branded `SALVAGE` under the net basis with no change whatsoever in the underlying damage. In either case `damage_ratio` is `repair_estimate_cents` divided by the chosen denominator, rounded half up to four decimal places. A report whose net-of-salvage denominator would be zero or negative never reaches this step, because `salvage_retention_cents` is required to be strictly below `acv_cents`.

The second derived quantity is `recommended_brand_code`, and it is produced by a four-rung ladder evaluated strictly from the top down, where the first rung that matches wins and the rest are not consulted. Rung one: if `damage_type` is `FLOOD` and `flood_water_depth_cm` is at least 46 centimetres, the recommendation is `FLOOD`. Rung two: otherwise, if `damage_ratio` is at least 1.20, the recommendation is `NON_REPAIRABLE`. Rung three: otherwise, if `damage_ratio` is at least 0.75, the recommendation is `SALVAGE`. Rung four: otherwise the recommendation is `NONE`. All three thresholds are inclusive — a depth of exactly 46 centimetres brands `FLOOD`, a ratio of exactly 1.20 brands `NON_REPAIRABLE`, and a ratio of exactly 0.75 brands `SALVAGE`. Note the ordering consequence carefully: a flood loss deep enough to trip rung one is branded `FLOOD` even when its ratio would otherwise have earned `NON_REPAIRABLE`. Filing the report also recomputes the subject vehicle's `last_odometer_miles`.

### 3.3. Issuing, Superseding and Surrendering Title Instruments

A `StateTitleClerk` mints a title record by naming the `vehicle_id` it covers, the `issuing_state_code` of the issuing jurisdiction, and the `owner_name` to be printed as titled owner. Two optional references shape what the instrument says. When `source_damage_report_id` is supplied, the title's `brand_code` is taken from that report's `recommended_brand_code`, and this is the sole route by which `SALVAGE`, `FLOOD` and `NON_REPAIRABLE` brands enter the system; the referenced report's own `vehicle_id` must equal the `vehicle_id` in the body, and a report naming some other vehicle is a malformed request drawing `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. When no report is supplied, the new title's `brand_code` is `NONE`. When `predecessor_title_id` is supplied, it names the title this instrument replaces in the vehicle's chain, and that predecessor — which must itself still be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_TITLE_NOT_ACTIVE` — moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` with its `is_current` driven to false; the first title ever issued on a vehicle leaves `predecessor_title_id` null and receives `transfer_sequence` 0. Creation is refused when the vehicle's `fraud_hold` is `CONFIRMED`, and it is refused when some other title record of the same vehicle is `ACTIVE` unless `predecessor_title_id` names exactly that title.

On issuance the clerk supplies neither the brand nor the timestamps. The server sets `issued_at` to the logical clock, sets `is_current` to true, computes `transfer_sequence` as the length of the predecessor chain, computes `brand_severity` from the assigned `brand_code`, and writes `odometer_at_issue_miles` — the reading carried onto the face of the title. That reading has three sources depending on how the title came to exist: at salvage issuance from a carrier report it is the report's `odometer_reading_miles`; at rebuilt issuance it is the vehicle's `last_odometer_miles`; and at a conveyance it is the odometer disclosure recorded on the conveyance. Issuing a title recomputes the vehicle's `active_brand_code` and its `brand_history`, and recomputes the new title's own `brand_history`.

A `NON_REPAIRABLE` vehicle is meant never to be retitled, and surrender is how the program makes that permanent. A clerk surrenders a title with an optional `surrender_note`. Surrender is admissible only from `ACTIVE`, only when the title's `brand_code` is exactly `NON_REPAIRABLE`, and only when no application of the vehicle is still live — that is, when every application of the vehicle has status `TITLE_ISSUED`, `DENIED_FINAL`, `WITHDRAWN` or `VOID_FRAUD`. A title that is not `ACTIVE` with `is_current` true is refused with `ERR_TITLE_NOT_ACTIVE`, and so is an `ACTIVE` title whose `brand_code` is anything other than `NON_REPAIRABLE`; a still-live application of the vehicle is instead refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_APPLICATION`. A successful surrender moves the title `ACTIVE → SURRENDERED`, drives `is_current` to false, increments the title's `version` by 1, and drives the vehicle's `active_brand_code` to `NONE` while its `brand_history` keeps `NON_REPAIRABLE` forever. That retained history is what later blocks any attempt to rebuild the vehicle.

### 3.4. Opening an Application and Assembling the Parts Record

A rebuilder who has repaired a branded wreck opens an application to convert its title. The rebuilder names the `vehicle_id` presented for inspection, its own `rebuilder_id`, the `salvage_title_id` of the branded title it seeks to convert, and its `declared_repair_cost_cents` — the rebuilder's own declared total repair cost, which must be at least 0. An optional `expedite_requested` boolean, defaulting to false, records whether the rebuilder is willing to pay the expedite tier on the program charge; the tier itself is described in §3.5. The acting rebuilder's `X-User-ID` must equal the body `rebuilder_id`.

Five gates stand in front of creation. The rebuilder's `license_status` must be `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`, and a `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED` licence is refused with `ERR_REBUILDER_LICENSE_INACTIVE` (HTTP 403) — the same code answers any action that demands a fully `ACTIVE` licence while the rebuilder sits on `PROBATION`. The vehicle's `fraud_hold` must be `NONE`. The referenced title must have a `brand_code` of either `SALVAGE` or `FLOOD`, must have `status` of `ACTIVE` with `is_current` true, and its own `vehicle_id` must equal the application's `vehicle_id`; anything else is `ERR_SALVAGE_TITLE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409). The vehicle's `brand_history` must not contain `NON_REPAIRABLE`, and neither may the referenced title's `brand_code` be `NON_REPAIRABLE`; a vehicle the state has already written off as beyond repair can never be rebuilt, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_NON_REPAIRABLE_BRAND` (HTTP 409). Finally the vehicle's `open_application_count` must be 0 — a vehicle may have at most one live application at a time, and a second one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_APPLICATION` (HTTP 409).

A newly created application begins in `DRAFT`. Its `fee_escrowed_cents` is 0, its `attempt_limit` is 3, and its `assessed_fee_cents` is initialised to the base application fee of 7,500 cents. Its `documented_parts_cost_cents` is 0, its `documentation_completeness_pct` is 0.00 — except on a vehicle whose `body_class` requires no part classes at all, where the vacuous completeness of §3.5 makes it read 100.00 from the moment the row is created — its `photo_view_count` is 0, its `total_program_charge_cents` is 0, its `attempts_completed` is 0, and `last_inspection_result`, `withdrawn_reason` and `terminal_at` are all null. Creation recomputes the vehicle's `open_application_count`, which rises by one.

While the application sits in `DRAFT`, and only while it sits in `DRAFT`, the rebuilder files the evidence package. Each filing names a `doc_type` drawn from `RECEIPT`, `PHOTOGRAPH`, `PARTS_INVOICE` and `DONOR_CERTIFICATE`; a `part_class` drawn from `STRUCTURAL`, `RESTRAINT`, `POWERTRAIN`, `BODY_PANEL`, `GLAZING`, `LIGHTING`, `WHEEL` and `PHOTO_SET`, where `PHOTO_SET` is the class used for photographs; and a `source_type` drawn from `NEW`, `USED_DONOR`, `RECYCLER` and `OWNER_SUPPLIED`, which is the parts-sourcing channel that drives both traceability and the donor surcharge. Optional fields fill in the paperwork: `amount_cents` is the amount on the receipt or invoice, defaulting to 0 and required to be at least 0; `vendor_name` is the supplier printed on it; `part_serial_number` is the serial or casting number that supports replacement-part traceability; `donor_vin` is the VIN stamped on the donor vehicle the part was harvested from, stored exactly as supplied and never checked against the seventeen-character VIN alphabet that governs a vehicle's own `vin`; `donor_title_id` names the title of that donor vehicle; and `photo_view_code` names the camera angle, drawn from `FRONT`, `REAR`, `LEFT`, `RIGHT`, `VIN_PLATE` and `ENGINE_BAY`. Two conditional requirements bind at filing time: `photo_view_code` must be non-null when `doc_type` is `PHOTOGRAPH` and null otherwise, and `donor_title_id` must be non-null when `source_type` is `USED_DONOR` and null otherwise. A filing against an application whose `status` is anything other than `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_DRAFT` (HTTP 409). A filing whose `donor_vin` equals the subject vehicle's own `vin` is refused with `ERR_VIN_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) — a part cannot be harvested from the very car it was installed in.

Each filed document starts with `status` of `SUBMITTED` and a null `rejection_reason`, and the server computes its `traceable` flag, which is true exactly when `source_type` is `NEW` or when `donor_title_id` is not null, and false otherwise. Filing recomputes the owning application's `photo_view_count` and its `documentation_completeness_pct`. `photo_view_count` is the number of *distinct* `photo_view_code` values among the application's documents whose `doc_type` is `PHOTOGRAPH` and whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `VERIFIED` — filing the same angle twice therefore buys nothing, and a rejected photograph stops counting.

### 3.5. Verifying Parts Evidence, Completeness and Record-Keeping Quality

An `Inspector` adjudicates each filed document. Verification carries an optional `verification_note` and moves the document `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED`. It is admissible only from `SUBMITTED`; a document already `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED` draws `ERR_PARTS_DOCUMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The owning application must be in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `DOCS_UNDER_REVIEW`, and any other application status draws `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE` (HTTP 409), which by the internal order of §2.5 is reported ahead of the document's own state error. The acting inspector's `credential_status` must be `CURRENT`; an inspector whose `credential_expires_at` is at or before the logical clock is refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). Replacement-part traceability is enforced here: a document whose `source_type` is `USED_DONOR` or `RECYCLER` must carry a `donor_title_id`, and the referenced donor title's `status` must not be `VOID` and its `brand_code` must not be `NON_REPAIRABLE`; a violation is `ERR_DONOR_TITLE_NOT_TRACEABLE` (HTTP 422). A successful verification records `verified_by_inspector_id` as the acting inspector's `X-User-ID`, adds the document's `amount_cents` to the application's `documented_parts_cost_cents`, and recomputes the application's `documentation_completeness_pct` and — once the application has been submitted — its `assessed_fee_cents`.

Rejection carries a mandatory `rejection_reason` drawn from `STOLEN_COMPONENT`, `UNTRACEABLE_SOURCE`, `ILLEGIBLE` and `DUPLICATE`, plus an optional `rejection_note`. Rejection is admissible from either `SUBMITTED` or `VERIFIED`; a document that is already `REJECTED` draws `ERR_PARTS_DOCUMENT_STATE_INVALID`, and the acting inspector must again hold a `CURRENT` credential. No application-status gate applies to a rejection, which therefore stays available whatever status the owning application has reached. Rejecting a `SUBMITTED` document simply records the reason and sets `verified_by_inspector_id` to the acting inspector. Rejecting a `VERIFIED` document is a compensating action and must also subtract that document's `amount_cents` from the application's `documented_parts_cost_cents`, restoring the invariant that `documented_parts_cost_cents` always equals the sum of `amount_cents` over exactly the application's `VERIFIED` documents. Either way the application's `documentation_completeness_pct` and `assessed_fee_cents` are recomputed, which means a rejection that lands after submission lowers the assessment on its next recomputation.

**Documentation completeness.** The required part classes are selected by the vehicle's `body_class`. A `SEDAN`, an `SUV` and a `TRUCK` each require three classes: `STRUCTURAL`, `RESTRAINT` and `LIGHTING`. A `MOTORCYCLE` requires two: `STRUCTURAL` and `LIGHTING`. A `TRAILER` requires none at all, and an empty required list is vacuously complete, so a trailer's `documentation_completeness_pct` is 100.00 from the moment the application exists. When the required list is non-empty, the program counts how many of the required classes are covered — a class counts as covered when at least one document of this application has that `part_class` and `status` of `VERIFIED` — then divides the covered count by the size of the required list, multiplies by 100 and rounds half up to two decimal places. Only `VERIFIED` documents count; `SUBMITTED` and `REJECTED` documents never satisfy a required class, and a `PHOTO_SET` document never satisfies a required class either, because photographic coverage is policed separately through `photo_view_count`. The submit gate compares `documentation_completeness_pct` against exactly 100.00.

**The record-keeping score.** The program grades each rebuilder on the traceability of its parts paperwork, and publishes the grade in two variants that differ only in which documents they consider. Both variants look at every parts document belonging to any application whose `rebuilder_id` is this rebuilder — applications in every status, terminal ones included — and both exclude documents whose `part_class` is `PHOTO_SET`, because a photograph says nothing about sourcing. The rolling variant, published as `recordkeeping_score_rolling`, keeps only those documents whose `created_at` falls within the trailing 365-day window ending at the logical clock, inclusive at both ends, so that old rejections age out. The lifetime variant, published as `recordkeeping_score_lifetime`, keeps every document the rebuilder has ever filed; because the lifetime denominator can only be larger, identical data yields a score that is lower than or equal to the rolling score whenever any document predates the window.

Within the chosen set, if there are no documents at all the score is 100.00 by convention. Otherwise the program counts two populations: documents whose `status` is `VERIFIED` and whose `traceable` flag is true, and documents whose `status` is `VERIFIED` and whose `traceable` flag is false. Each traceable verified document earns 100 points and each untraceable verified document earns 40 points. Documents that are still `SUBMITTED` and documents that are `REJECTED` earn zero points but nevertheless enter the denominator — this is the whole mechanism by which sloppy paperwork depresses the grade. The total points are divided by the count of every document in the set, rounded half up to two decimal places, and clamped into the closed range 0.00 through 100.00. Both variants are recomputed whenever a parts document is verified or rejected, whenever a licence action is taken, and on every single-rebuilder read, so a read of a rebuilder always reports freshly derived scores; as §2.12 records, a score is refreshed in place rather than written as a row mutation, so a verification or rejection that moves nothing but these two scores leaves the rebuilder's `updated_at` and `version` exactly where they were. The rolling score gates reinstatement at 60.00 and the lifetime score gates revocation at 40.00, as §3.12 describes.

### 3.6. Submission, Fee Assessment and the Escrow Ledger

Submission is the rebuilder's attestation that the package is complete, and it is the moment money moves. The rebuilder submits with a single body field, `attestation_signed`, which must be exactly true; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The application must be in `DRAFT` — `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_DRAFT` otherwise — and the acting rebuilder must own it. The vehicle's `fraud_hold` must be `NONE` and the rebuilder's `license_status` must be `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`. Beyond those, four substantive gates apply. `documentation_completeness_pct` must be exactly 100.00, or the submission is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). `photo_view_count` must be at least 4 — the program requires four distinct camera angles on file — or the submission is refused with `ERR_PHOTO_SET_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). The logical clock must be no later than the salvage title's `issued_at` plus 180 days; a rebuilder who has not submitted by the end of that 180-day window loses it and is refused with `ERR_SUBMISSION_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). And the rebuilder's `fee_balance_cents` must be at least the `total_program_charge_cents` that this very call computes, or the submission is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FEE_BALANCE` (HTTP 402).

A successful submission runs the fee assessment first, then the program charge, in that order. It then sets `fee_escrowed_cents` equal to the freshly computed `total_program_charge_cents` — that application field is the only escrow figure the operation writes — and moves the application `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`; the rebuilder's `escrowed_fee_total_cents` consequently rises by that charge and its `fee_balance_cents` falls by the same charge, both of them re-derived from the ledger invariant stated at the end of this subsection rather than written directly.

**The fee assessment.** `assessed_fee_cents` is built from a base plus two surcharges, and it looks at exactly the application's documents whose `status` is `VERIFIED` — `SUBMITTED` and `REJECTED` documents are excluded from the arithmetic entirely. The donor surcharge considers those verified documents whose `source_type` is `USED_DONOR` or `RECYCLER`. For each such document the program multiplies that document's `amount_cents` by the donor-part surcharge rate of one and a half percent and rounds the product half up to a whole number of cents; only after every line has been rounded individually are the rounded line values added together. The rounding order here is pinned and is not interchangeable: rounding each line first and then summing is the specified behaviour, and summing the raw amounts and rounding the product once would produce a different figure — three lines of 12,499 cents each round to 187 cents apiece for a donor surcharge of 561 cents, whereas rounding a single summed product would give 562. The structural surcharge is a flat 12,500 cents, applied once when at least one of the application's documents has `status` of `VERIFIED` and `part_class` of `STRUCTURAL`, and 0 when none does — it is never applied twice no matter how many structural documents exist. The two surcharges are added together and the sum is capped at 45,000 cents; the cap is applied after the summation, never to either surcharge in isolation. `assessed_fee_cents` is then the base application fee of 7,500 cents plus that capped surcharge total.

**The program charge.** `total_program_charge_cents` is built in a pinned order from three intermediates. The subtotal is `assessed_fee_cents` plus the re-inspection fee of 4,000 cents multiplied by the application's `attempts_completed`, where a negative attempt count is impossible and the multiplier is therefore never below zero. The expedite tier is then selected *by the subtotal*, never by any running total: a subtotal below 10,000 cents draws a ten percent expedite rate, a subtotal below 25,000 cents draws seven percent, and a subtotal of 25,000 cents or more draws five percent. When `expedite_requested` is true, the expedite fee is the subtotal multiplied by the selected rate and rounded half up to whole cents; when `expedite_requested` is false, the expedite fee is 0. The volume discount rate is also keyed on nothing but the rebuilder's standing: it is five percent when the owning rebuilder's `applications_titled_count` is at least 5, and zero otherwise. The discount amount is the subtotal plus the expedite fee, multiplied by the discount rate, rounded half up once. `total_program_charge_cents` is the subtotal plus the expedite fee minus the discount. Keying either tier on the running total rather than on the subtotal is the circular reading and produces the wrong answer; the inputs are the application's own `assessed_fee_cents` and `attempts_completed` — counting only attempts that reached `COMPLETED_PASS` or `COMPLETED_FAIL`, never `CANCELLED` ones — together with the rebuilder's `applications_titled_count` as it stands at the instant of the call.

Because the tiering order is easy to get wrong, the program publishes a non-mutating quotation that a `ProgramAdmin`, a `StateTitleClerk` or the owning `Rebuilder` may read at any time. It exposes every intermediate of the charge computation side by side — `assessed_fee_cents`, `subtotal_cents`, `expedite_fee_cents`, `discount_cents` and `total_program_charge_cents` — and it never changes the application. Its `data` object carries exactly those five fields and nothing else: no `id`, no timestamps and no copy of the application. It reads `assessed_fee_cents` as the application row currently stands rather than re-assessing it from the documents, so while the application still sits in the `DRAFT` it was created in the quotation carries the base 7,500 cents however many documents have been verified — the assessment is re-derived from the documents only once the application has left `DRAFT`, which is also why an application returned to `DRAFT` for correction under §3.7 keeps the assessment it last computed until it is submitted again — and it reads the owning rebuilder's `applications_titled_count` as it stands at the instant of the read.

**The ledger invariant.** A rebuilder's four money counters are tied together at all times. `fees_deposited_cents` is the cumulative sum of every accepted deposit. `escrowed_fee_total_cents` is the sum of `fee_escrowed_cents` over exactly this rebuilder's applications whose `status` is not one of `TITLE_ISSUED`, `DENIED_FINAL`, `WITHDRAWN` or `VOID_FRAUD`. `fees_consumed_cents` is the sum of the `fee_escrowed_cents` the program captured at the instant each of this rebuilder's applications reached `TITLE_ISSUED` or `DENIED_FINAL`. And `fee_balance_cents` — the unescrowed, unconsumed money the rebuilder can still spend — equals `fees_deposited_cents` reduced by `escrowed_fee_total_cents` and reduced again by `fees_consumed_cents`. Three dispositions of an escrow exist and they differ exactly in which counter absorbs it. A capture (title issued, or final denial) moves the escrowed amount into `fees_consumed_cents` — the one rebuilder counter any disposition writes directly — and then zeroes `fee_escrowed_cents`, so `escrowed_fee_total_cents` falls by the same amount and `fee_balance_cents` does not move at all. A refund (withdrawal, or a fraud void) writes no rebuilder counter whatsoever: it simply zeroes `fee_escrowed_cents`, so `escrowed_fee_total_cents` falls and `fee_balance_cents` rises by the same amount purely because the invariant is re-derived, and the money becomes spendable again. And a return of the documentation package for correction disposes of nothing: the escrow is retained in full, with no refund and no capture.

### 3.7. Documentation Review, Scheduling and Inspection Capacity

A `StateTitleClerk` adjudicates the documentation package with a single `decision` field, optionally accompanied by a `review_note`, which is accepted and validated as a string but is kept on no resource and published in no response. A decision of `OPEN` takes a `SUBMITTED` application to `DOCS_UNDER_REVIEW` and refreshes `updated_at` and `version`. A decision of `ACCEPT` takes a `DOCS_UNDER_REVIEW` application to `DOCS_ACCEPTED`, at which point it becomes schedulable; acceptance additionally requires that `documentation_completeness_pct` be exactly 100.00 — otherwise `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE` — and that *every* one of the application's parts documents whose `part_class` is not `PHOTO_SET` be in `status` `VERIFIED`, a document short of that drawing the same `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE`. A decision of `RETURN` sends a `DOCS_UNDER_REVIEW` application back to `DRAFT` so the rebuilder can file corrected paperwork; as §3.6 states, `fee_escrowed_cents` is retained in full — a return for correction is not a refund. Calling the review operation on an application whose `status` is neither `SUBMITTED` nor `DOCS_UNDER_REVIEW` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE` (HTTP 409), and so is a decision paired with the wrong one of those two statuses — an `OPEN` against a `DOCS_UNDER_REVIEW` application, or an `ACCEPT` or a `RETURN` against a merely `SUBMITTED` one.

Scheduling a physical attempt is the busiest guarded operation in the program. The clerk supplies an `inspector_id`, a `station_id`, a `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` pair bounding the booked interval, and the mandatory `expected_application_version`. The application's `status` must be `DOCS_ACCEPTED`, `SCHEDULED` or `FAILED`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`. The version check described in §2.8 runs before every business gate. `attempts_completed` must be strictly below `attempt_limit`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409) — the base limit is three attempts, raised only by a granted appeal. The vehicle's `fraud_hold` must be `NONE`. The nominated inspector's `credential_status` must be `CURRENT`. No appeal of this application may sit in `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REMANDED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_PENDING_BLOCKS_SCHEDULING` (HTTP 409); an appeal in flight freezes the scheduling track until it is decided. Whenever the application already carries a `COMPLETED_FAIL` attempt — whichever of the three schedulable statuses the call is made from, a reschedule of a still-`SCHEDULED` attempt included — the body `inspector_id` must differ from the `inspector_id` of the most recent such attempt in `created_at` order, under the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.10. `scheduled_end_at` must be strictly after `scheduled_start_at`, and `scheduled_start_at` must be strictly after the logical clock — a slot at or before the current instant is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_SLOT_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422). When the inspected vehicle's `active_brand_code` is `FLOOD`, the nominated station's `supports_flood` must be true *and* the nominated inspector's `certifications` must contain `STRUCTURAL`; a violation of either half is refused with `ERR_STATION_NOT_FLOOD_CAPABLE` (HTTP 409).

Two capacity rules then apply, and they deliberately use different interval arithmetic. Each attempt publishes an `effective_end_at`, which equals `scheduled_end_at` for an ordinary vehicle and equals `scheduled_end_at` plus a thirty-minute flood-decontamination buffer when the inspected vehicle's `active_brand_code` is `FLOOD`. The two cases differ in string shape as well as in value: for an ordinary vehicle `effective_end_at` republishes the caller's own `scheduled_end_at` string verbatim, exactly as §2.4 requires of a caller-supplied instant, while the buffered form is a server-computed instant rendered in UTC with three fractional digits. The published value is fixed when the attempt is booked and is not re-derived afterwards even if the vehicle's `active_brand_code` later changes, whereas the interval arithmetic below always widens each attempt against its own vehicle's brand as that brand stands at the moment of the comparison. The inspector exclusivity rule compares the requested half-open interval running from `scheduled_start_at` up to but not including `effective_end_at` against the same half-open interval of every existing attempt of the same `inspector_id` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, where the existing attempt's end is likewise widened by the thirty-minute buffer when its own vehicle is flood-branded. Because the intervals are half-open, exactly touching endpoints do not collide: an existing attempt whose widened end equals the requested `scheduled_start_at` is not a conflict. Any true intersection is refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_INTERVAL_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). The station capacity rule instead counts, and it uses closed intervals with the raw `scheduled_end_at` rather than the widened end: the program counts the station's attempts whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` and whose closed interval from `scheduled_start_at` through `scheduled_end_at` intersects the requested closed interval — and here touching endpoints *do* count as intersecting — and that count must be strictly less than the station's `bay_count`. A count that has already reached `bay_count` is refused with `ERR_STATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).

A successful scheduling creates an attempt in `SCHEDULED`, computes its `effective_end_at`, assigns its `attempt_no`, moves the application's `status` to `SCHEDULED`, increments the application's `version` by 1, and increments both the inspector's `scheduled_inspection_count` and the station's `scheduled_occupancy_count`. The new attempt publishes `result`, `adequacy_score`, `checkpoint_results`, `vin_plate_reading`, `started_at`, `completed_at` and `cancellation_reason` as null, `blocking_findings_count` as 0, `forced_fail` as false, and `vin_verified` as false rather than null — no VIN having been transcribed yet. `attempt_no` is 1 for the first non-cancelled attempt on the application and rises by one for each subsequent non-cancelled attempt; cancelled attempts never consume a number. Three cases diverge in their side effects. From `DOCS_ACCEPTED` the new attempt simply gets `attempt_no` equal to `attempts_completed` plus one. From `SCHEDULED` the call is a reschedule and forms a self-loop `SCHEDULED → SCHEDULED` on the application: the prior attempt, which must still be in `SCHEDULED`, moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` set to `RESCHEDULED`, and because it was cancelled it releases its inspector interval and its station bay before the replacement is booked; the replacement carries the *same* `attempt_no` as the attempt it replaced, precisely because cancelled attempts do not consume a number. From `FAILED` the call is a re-inspection: `total_program_charge_cents` is recomputed with the 4,000-cent re-inspection fee now multiplied by the higher `attempts_completed`, `fee_escrowed_cents` moves by the delta between the new and the old charge — that application field being again the only escrow figure written, so the rebuilder's `fee_balance_cents` falls by exactly that delta while `escrowed_fee_total_cents` rises by it, both re-derived from the invariant of §3.6. A rebuilder whose `fee_balance_cents` cannot absorb the delta is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FEE_BALANCE`; that fiscal gate binds only when the delta is strictly positive, a recomputed charge that has fallen instead releasing escrow with nothing to absorb.

A clerk may also cancel a scheduled attempt outright, with an optional `cancellation_note`. Cancellation is admissible only from `SCHEDULED`; an attempt that is `IN_PROGRESS` or already terminal is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_SCHEDULED` (HTTP 409). The attempt moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` set to `CLERK_REQUEST`, and both the inspector's `scheduled_inspection_count` and the station's `scheduled_occupancy_count` are decremented. The application's fallback status is conditional on how far it had got: when the cancelled attempt was its only `SCHEDULED` attempt and `attempts_completed` is 0, the application falls back `SCHEDULED → DOCS_ACCEPTED`; when `attempts_completed` is at least 1, it falls back `SCHEDULED → FAILED` instead, because a previously failed attempt is still the application's most recent outcome.

### 3.8. Conducting the Attempt: Findings, Adequacy Scoring and Identity Verification

The assigned inspector — and only the assigned inspector, whose `X-User-ID` must equal the attempt's `inspector_id` — begins the attempt, optionally recording a `start_note`. Starting is admissible only from `SCHEDULED` — any other attempt status is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_SCHEDULED` (HTTP 409) — requires the inspector's `credential_status` to be `CURRENT`, requires the logical clock to have reached or passed `scheduled_start_at`, a clock still short of that instant being refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_SLOT_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422), and requires the vehicle's `fraud_hold` to be `NONE`. A successful start stamps `started_at` with the logical clock, moves the attempt `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`, and drives the application `SCHEDULED → IN_INSPECTION`, so that the application's `status` reads `IN_INSPECTION` for as long as the attempt is open.

While the attempt is `IN_PROGRESS` the inspector records findings. Each finding carries a `category` drawn from `STOLEN_COMPONENT`, `VIN_ALTERATION`, `STRUCTURAL_INADEQUACY`, `ODOMETER_DISCREPANCY` and `DOCUMENTATION_GAP`; a `severity` drawn from `ADVISORY`, `MAJOR` and `CRITICAL`; a narrative `description` of what was observed; and an optional `parts_document_id` naming the parts document the finding impeaches, left null when the finding is not tied to a specific document — only that document's existence is checked, and it is not required to belong to the inspected attempt's own application. Recording a finding on an attempt whose `status` is not `IN_PROGRESS` is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_IN_PROGRESS` (HTTP 409). The server writes `recorded_by_inspector_id` from the acting principal, sets `resolved` to false, and computes the finding's `blocking` flag, which is true exactly when `severity` is `CRITICAL` or `category` is `STOLEN_COMPONENT` or `VIN_ALTERATION`, and false otherwise. A blocking finding raises the attempt's `blocking_findings_count`, which counts this attempt's findings whose `blocking` is true and whose `resolved` is false. The first time `blocking_findings_count` reaches 1 or more, the attempt's `forced_fail` latches to true, and once latched it stays true for the remainder of that attempt and forces a `FAIL` result no matter what the adequacy arithmetic says.

A finding whose `category` is `STOLEN_COMPONENT` and which cites a `parts_document_id` sets off the longest cascade in the program, and every step of it must land. The cited parts document, if it was `VERIFIED`, moves `VERIFIED → REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` set to `STOLEN_COMPONENT` and with its `verified_by_inspector_id` left standing as the inspector who had verified it rather than overwritten by the cascading inspector, and because a verified amount has been withdrawn the inspected attempt's own application has its `documented_parts_cost_cents` recomputed, falling by that document's `amount_cents` whenever the cited document was one of that application's own. Because that document was impeached as stolen, a fraud referral is synthesised against the vehicle with `referral_type` of `IDENTITY_TAMPERING`, `origin` of `AUTO_CASCADE` and `status` of `OPEN`, whose `opened_by_user_id` is the cascading inspector's `X-User-ID`, and the vehicle's `fraud_hold` moves `NONE → UNDER_REFERRAL`. That synthesis is unconditional: it is not suppressed by an `IDENTITY_TAMPERING` referral that already stands `OPEN` or `INVESTIGATING` against the same vehicle, so a second stolen-component finding raises a second referral. Because that referral now exists and is attached to an application, the owning rebuilder's `open_referral_count` rises by one, and the moment that count reaches 2 the rebuilder's licence moves `ACTIVE → PROBATION` of its own accord. Unlike the odometer cascade of §3.11, this cascade cancels no inspection attempt: the attempt being conducted stays `IN_PROGRESS` and every other attempt of the vehicle keeps its booking despite the new fraud hold.

Completion closes the attempt and adjudicates it. The inspector supplies `checkpoint_results` and `vin_plate_reading`. `checkpoint_results` is an array that must contain exactly one entry per weighted checkpoint — five entries, no more and no fewer — each entry an object carrying a `code` and an `outcome`; a missing or duplicated checkpoint is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The array is stored verbatim on the attempt for audit. `vin_plate_reading` is the VIN the inspector transcribed off the public VIN plate; it is a mandatory non-blank string compared verbatim against the vehicle's `vin` and is never itself checked against the seventeen-character VIN alphabet. The attempt publishes `vin_verified`, which is true exactly when `vin_plate_reading` equals the inspected vehicle's `vin`. Completion is admissible only from `IN_PROGRESS`, or `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_IN_PROGRESS` results.

The structural repair adequacy score is a weighted sum over exactly those five submitted checkpoint outcomes — no other data enters the numerator. The checkpoint weights are 35 for `STRUCTURAL_FRAME`, 20 for `RESTRAINT_SYSTEM`, 20 for `VIN_INTEGRITY`, 15 for `SUSPENSION_STEERING` and 10 for `LAMPS_GLAZING`, which total 100. Each checkpoint's outcome contributes a factor: an outcome of `PASS` contributes the full weight, an outcome of `MARGINAL` contributes exactly half the weight, and an outcome of `FAIL` contributes nothing. The five contributions are added together and the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places to produce `adequacy_score`.

Three independent conditions constitute an automatic failure regardless of that score. The first is a `FAIL` outcome on either `STRUCTURAL_FRAME` or `VIN_INTEGRITY` — the two checkpoints the program treats as non-negotiable. The second is the attempt's own latched `forced_fail` flag. The third is a `vin_plate_reading` that differs from the vehicle's recorded `vin`. The attempt's `result` is `PASS` when none of those three conditions holds *and* `adequacy_score` is at least 70.00; the comparison is inclusive, so a score of exactly 70.00 passes. In every other case `result` is `FAIL`. A completion whose `vin_plate_reading` differs from the vehicle's `vin` is refused with `ERR_VIN_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) unless a finding with `category` of `VIN_ALTERATION` already stands on this attempt — in that case the mismatch is the documented defect the inspector came to record, and the completion is allowed to proceed to a `FAIL`. A completion that would be adjudicated `PASS` while an unresolved `STOLEN_COMPONENT` or `VIN_ALTERATION` finding stands against the application is refused with `ERR_STOLEN_COMPONENT_UNRESOLVED` (HTTP 409).

The two completion branches carry deliberately different side-effect sets, and both must be implemented in full. A passing completion moves the attempt `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED_PASS`, sets `result` to `PASS` and `completed_at` to the logical clock, recomputes the application's `attempts_completed` so that it rises by one, sets the application's `last_inspection_result` to `PASS`, drives the application `IN_INSPECTION → PASSED`, and releases the inspector interval and the station bay by decrementing the inspector's `scheduled_inspection_count` and the station's `scheduled_occupancy_count`. A failing completion moves the attempt `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED_FAIL`, sets `result` to `FAIL` and `completed_at` to the logical clock, likewise recomputes `attempts_completed` upward by one, sets `last_inspection_result` to `FAIL`, drives the application `IN_INSPECTION → FAILED`, and releases the same inspector interval and station bay with the same two decrements. The branches diverge in what they unlock rather than in what they release: a pass opens the rebuilt-title track and its escrow capture, while a fail opens the re-inspection and appeal tracks.

### 3.9. Failure, Appeal and Final Denial

A rebuilder whose attempt failed has two remedies: schedule a re-inspection, as §3.7 describes, or contest the failure. Filing an appeal names the `inspection_id` of the failed attempt, the statutory `grounds` — one of `SCORING_ERROR`, `PART_TRACEABILITY_DISPUTE` or `PROCEDURAL_DEFECT` — and a written `narrative` argument. The acting rebuilder must own the underlying application. The named attempt's `status` must be `COMPLETED_FAIL`, and the logical clock must be no later than that attempt's `completed_at` plus 30 days; a later filing, or a filing against an attempt that is not `COMPLETED_FAIL`, is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). No other appeal for the same attempt may already be in `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REMANDED`; one that is draws `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), which by the internal order of §2.5 is reported ahead of the `COMPLETED_FAIL` and window checks. A successful filing creates the appeal in `FILED` with `filed_at` stamped from the logical clock, a `remand_count` of 0 and a null `decision_note`, `decided_at` and `hearing_officer_user_id`, and drives the application `FAILED → APPEAL_PENDING`, which is what blocks re-inspection scheduling while the appeal is alive; that move is conditional on the application actually standing in `FAILED`, so an appeal filed against a `COMPLETED_FAIL` attempt whose application has meanwhile moved elsewhere — to `SCHEDULED` by a re-inspection, or into a terminal status — leaves the application's `status` exactly as it stands.

A `HearingOfficer` takes the appeal up, optionally recording a `review_note`, which is accepted and validated as a string but is kept on no resource and published in no response. That move is admissible from either `FILED` or `REMANDED` and lands the appeal in `UNDER_REVIEW`, recording the officer's `X-User-ID` as `hearing_officer_user_id`. Taking up an appeal that is in any other status is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

The officer then decides, supplying a `decision` and an optional `decision_note`, which is stored as the decision rationale and is null before the first decision. Deciding is admissible only from `UNDER_REVIEW`; anything else is `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID`. Any `HearingOfficer` may decide, not only the one who took the appeal up: the decision neither compares the acting `X-User-ID` against `hearing_officer_user_id` nor overwrites it, so that field keeps naming whoever last took the appeal up. Three decision values exist and their consequences are sharply different.

A decision of `GRANT` moves the appeal `UNDER_REVIEW → GRANTED`, so that its `status` reads `GRANTED`, and stamps `decided_at` from the logical clock. Because the appeal was granted, every finding of the appealed attempt has its `resolved` flag flipped to true — this is the only mechanism in the program that resolves a finding. Because those findings became resolved, the appealed attempt's `blocking_findings_count` falls to 0, while its `forced_fail` flag stays latched at true for audit and is never rewound. The application's `attempt_limit` rises by 1 whatever status the application is holding, restoring the attempt the vacated failure consumed, and an application standing in `APPEAL_PENDING` moves `APPEAL_PENDING → DOCS_ACCEPTED` so that it becomes schedulable again — an application that had moved elsewhere keeps its own `status` and gains only the extra attempt.

A decision of `DENY` splits on how many attempts the application has left, with the same actor and the same decision value producing two different outcomes. When `attempts_completed` is still strictly below `attempt_limit`, the appeal moves `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`, so that its `status` reads `DENIED`, with `decided_at` stamped, and the application returns `APPEAL_PENDING → FAILED`, unblocking the re-inspection track. When `attempts_completed` has reached or exceeded `attempt_limit`, the appeal likewise moves `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED` with `decided_at` stamped, but the application moves `APPEAL_PENDING → DENIED_FINAL` with `terminal_at` stamped from the logical clock, and the program captures the escrow: the rebuilder's `fees_consumed_cents` rises by the application's `fee_escrowed_cents`, `fee_escrowed_cents` is then set to 0, and because the escrow left the application the rebuilder's `escrowed_fee_total_cents` falls by the same amount while `fee_balance_cents` does not change at all. Unlike a grant, a denial writes the application's new `status` unconditionally rather than only out of `APPEAL_PENDING`. And because a `DENIED_FINAL` application has left the live set, the final-denial branch also recomputes the vehicle, whose `open_application_count` falls by one.

A decision of `REMAND` moves the appeal `UNDER_REVIEW → REMANDED`, raises its `remand_count` by 1, records the supplied `decision_note`, leaves `decided_at` null — only a grant or a denial ever stamps it — and leaves the application untouched, sitting in `APPEAL_PENDING`. Remands are bounded: an appeal may be remanded only while its `remand_count` is still below 1, so exactly one remand is ever permitted, and a second attempt to remand is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID`. A remanded appeal can be taken up again, which cycles it `REMANDED → UNDER_REVIEW` and re-records `hearing_officer_user_id`, and from there it can be granted or denied as above.

### 3.10. Issuing the Rebuilt Title, and Walking Away

Once an application stands in `PASSED`, a `StateTitleClerk` mints the rebuilt title, supplying the `issuing_state_code` of the issuing jurisdiction and the `owner_name` to print on the face. Four gates apply. The application's `status` must be exactly `PASSED`, or `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE` results. No finding on any of this application's attempts may have `category` of `STOLEN_COMPONENT` with `resolved` still false, or the issuance is refused with `ERR_STOLEN_COMPONENT_UNRESOLVED`. The vehicle's `fraud_hold` must be `NONE`. And the salvage title the application is converting must still have `status` of `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_TITLE_NOT_ACTIVE` results. Because a vehicle whose `brand_history` contains `NON_REPAIRABLE` can never reach this point legitimately, an attempt to mint a rebuilt title for such a vehicle is refused with `ERR_NON_REPAIRABLE_BRAND`.

A successful issuance mints a new title record whose `brand_code` is `REBUILT`, whose `predecessor_title_id` is the application's `salvage_title_id`, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `issued_at` is the logical clock, and whose `odometer_at_issue_miles` is taken from the vehicle's `last_odometer_miles`. Because a successor now exists, the old salvage title moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` and its `is_current` becomes false; if that title's `recognition_state` was `RECOGNITION_PENDING`, it becomes `STALE` and the pending interstate request against it moves to `SUPERSEDED`. Because the vehicle's current title changed, the vehicle's `active_brand_code` becomes `REBUILT` and its `brand_history` gains `REBUILT`, retaining the earlier `SALVAGE` or `FLOOD` entry forever. The program captures the escrow — `fees_consumed_cents` rises by `fee_escrowed_cents`, which is then zeroed, so `escrowed_fee_total_cents` falls by the same amount and `fee_balance_cents` is untouched. The rebuilder's `applications_titled_count` rises by 1, which is what eventually earns the volume discount of §3.6. And the application moves `PASSED → TITLE_ISSUED` with `terminal_at` stamped from the logical clock. The response body carries the newly minted title record rather than the application, under HTTP 201.

Withdrawal is the rebuilder's exit, and it is available from six statuses: `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `DOCS_UNDER_REVIEW`, `DOCS_ACCEPTED`, `SCHEDULED` and `FAILED`. The rebuilder supplies a free-text `withdrawn_reason`, mandatory and required to be a non-blank string, which is stored on the application. Withdrawing from a terminal status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`. A successful withdrawal refunds the escrow in full — the application's `fee_escrowed_cents` is set to 0 and no rebuilder counter is written, so `escrowed_fee_total_cents` falls by the refunded amount, `fee_balance_cents` rises by it as the invariant of §3.6 is re-derived, and the money becomes spendable again. Because the application is leaving the live set, any of its attempts still in `SCHEDULED` move `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` set to `APPLICATION_VOIDED`, each releasing its inspector interval and its station bay by decrementing the inspector's `scheduled_inspection_count` and the station's `scheduled_occupancy_count`. The application then moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `terminal_at` stamped, and the vehicle's `open_application_count` is recomputed downward, freeing the vehicle for a fresh application.

### 3.11. Conveyances, Brand Carry-Forward, Odometer Continuity and Interstate Recognition

A brand is only worth stamping if it survives the next sale, so recording a conveyance is the operation the whole program exists to protect. A `StateTitleClerk` records one by naming the `from_title_id` of the outgoing title being conveyed, the `buyer_name` of the acquiring owner to be printed on the successor, the `transfer_effective_at` instant at which the conveyance takes effect, the `odometer_reading_miles` disclosed at conveyance (at least 0), and the stated `sale_price_cents` (at least 0). The outgoing title must have `status` of `ACTIVE` with `is_current` true, or `ERR_TITLE_NOT_ACTIVE` results. The vehicle's `fraud_hold` must be `NONE`. Neither the vehicle's `brand_history` nor the outgoing title's `brand_code` may involve `NON_REPAIRABLE`, or the conveyance is refused with `ERR_NON_REPAIRABLE_BRAND`. And `transfer_effective_at` must be at or after the outgoing title's `issued_at`, and may not predate the latest already-recorded conveyance on the chain; an earlier instant is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_PREDATES_TITLE` (HTTP 422). A recorded conveyance carries `status` of `RECORDED`, and only ever becomes `VOID` when a fraud referral against its vehicle is substantiated — at which point *every* `RECORDED` conveyance of that vehicle is voided together, the cascade reasoning about the vehicle as a whole rather than about the voided title's own `predecessor_title_id` chain.

**Carrying the brand forward.** The program determines `carried_brand_code` by walking the outgoing title's ancestry: starting at the outgoing title itself and following `predecessor_title_id` from row to row until the root of the chain is reached, it collects the set of `brand_code` values encountered, discarding any occurrence of `NONE`. Every ancestor contributes regardless of its own `status` — `SUPERSEDED` and `VOID` ancestors still hand their brand down, which is precisely the point — while sibling titles of the same vehicle that are not part of this chain contribute nothing. If the collected set is empty, `carried_brand_code` is `NONE`. Otherwise `carried_brand_code` is the single member of the set with the highest brand severity; because the five severity values are distinct integers, that maximum is unique and no tie-break is required. The freshly minted successor title takes `carried_brand_code` as its own `brand_code`, takes the collected set sorted by brand severity descending as its `brand_history`, and takes the severity of the carried brand as its `brand_severity`. A conveyance that would carry a brand strictly less severe than the chain maximum is refused with `ERR_BRAND_ESCALATION_FORBIDDEN`.

**Replaying the odometer.** `odometer_flag` is derived by a chronological replay of everything the program knows about the vehicle's mileage. The program assembles one event per insurer damage report of the vehicle, carrying that report's `report_effective_at` and its `odometer_reading_miles`, and one event per conveyance of the vehicle whose `status` is `RECORDED`, carrying that conveyance's `transfer_effective_at` and its `odometer_reading_miles`; conveyances whose `status` is `VOID` are excluded entirely. The events are sorted by their effective instant ascending, with each event's `created_at` ascending as the sole tie-break — never the row identifier. The replay then keeps only those events whose effective instant is at or before the new conveyance's `transfer_effective_at`. If no such prior event exists, `odometer_flag` is `OK`. Otherwise the program takes the highest `odometer_reading_miles` among the retained prior events and compares the new disclosure against it: the tolerance is zero miles, so `odometer_flag` is `ROLLBACK_SUSPECTED` exactly when the new `odometer_reading_miles` is strictly less than that prior maximum, and `OK` when it equals or exceeds it.

A successful conveyance therefore does five things. It mints the successor title record with `predecessor_title_id` set to the outgoing title, `owner_name` taken from `buyer_name`, `issued_at` stamped from the logical clock, `odometer_at_issue_miles` taken from the conveyance's `odometer_reading_miles`, its `issuing_state_code` copied from the outgoing title, its `source_damage_report_id` left null, and the carried brand fields as computed above, and it writes that successor's identifier into the conveyance's `to_title_id` — which is null only during the instant the conveyance row is being created. It moves the outgoing title `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` with `is_current` driven to false, and if that title's `recognition_state` was `RECOGNITION_PENDING`, the pending interstate request moves to `SUPERSEDED` and the title's `recognition_state` becomes `STALE`. It recomputes the vehicle's `active_brand_code`, `brand_history` and `last_odometer_miles`. It publishes `odometer_flag`. And when `odometer_flag` comes out `ROLLBACK_SUSPECTED`, it synthesises a fraud referral with `referral_type` of `ODOMETER` and `origin` of `AUTO_CASCADE`, which drives the vehicle's `fraud_hold` to `UNDER_REFERRAL`, which in turn cancels every attempt still in `SCHEDULED` belonging to that vehicle's non-terminal applications, each with `cancellation_reason` set to `FRAUD_HOLD`. That synthesised referral leaves both `application_id` and `inspection_id` null, so it moves no rebuilder's `open_referral_count` and changes no licence standing.

**Interstate recognition.** A branded title can be offered to another jurisdiction. A `StateTitleClerk` submits one by naming the `title_record_id`, the `requesting_state_code` being asked to honour the brand, and the mandatory `expected_title_version` described in §2.8. The title's `status` must be `ACTIVE` with `is_current` true, or `ERR_TITLE_NOT_ACTIVE` results; the `requesting_state_code` must differ from the title's own `issuing_state_code`, because a state is never asked to recognise its own instrument; and the title's `brand_code` must not be `NONE`, because there is nothing to recognise on an unbranded title. Both of those last two refusals report `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and both are evaluated only after the version check and after the `ACTIVE`-and-current check rather than at the general position well-formedness occupies in §2.5. A successful submission creates the request in `PENDING`, snapshots the title's `brand_code` into `submitted_brand_code`, leaves `mapped_brand_code`, `outcome` and `decided_at` null, moves any prior request for the same title that was still `PENDING` to `SUPERSEDED`, drives the title's `recognition_state` to `RECOGNITION_PENDING`, and increments the title's `version` by 1.

A `ProgramAdmin` records the requesting state's answer by supplying a `mapped_brand_code` and an optional `decision_note`. The request must still be `PENDING`; one that is already `DECIDED` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_RECOGNITION_ALREADY_DECIDED` (HTTP 409). The brand-monotonicity rule of §2.6 binds here: the brand severity of `mapped_brand_code` must be at or below the brand severity of `submitted_brand_code`, and a strictly higher severity is refused with `ERR_BRAND_ESCALATION_FORBIDDEN`. The `outcome` follows mechanically from the comparison: it is `RECOGNIZED` when `mapped_brand_code` equals `submitted_brand_code`; it is `DOWNGRADED` when the mapped brand's severity is strictly lower than the submitted brand's but still above zero; and it is `REJECTED` when `mapped_brand_code` is `NONE`, meaning the state refuses to carry any brand at all. The request moves `PENDING → DECIDED` with `decided_at` stamped from the logical clock. The title's second dimension follows the outcome asymmetrically: `recognition_state` becomes `RECOGNIZED` when the outcome is `RECOGNIZED`, and becomes `DOWNGRADED` when the outcome is either `DOWNGRADED` or `REJECTED`. A pending request also ends as `SUPERSEDED` whenever the underlying title leaves `ACTIVE` for any reason, in which case the title's `recognition_state` becomes `STALE`.

### 3.12. Fraud Referrals, the Substantiation Cascade and Licence Standing

Either an `Inspector` or a `StateTitleClerk` may file a fraud referral, naming the `vehicle_id` whose identity or odometer is impeached, a `referral_type` drawn from `ODOMETER`, `IDENTITY_TAMPERING` and `DOCUMENT_FORGERY`, and an `evidence_summary` describing what supports the allegation. Two optional references narrow the scope: `application_id`, the application in which the irregularity surfaced, left null for referrals raised outside any application, and `inspection_id`, the attempt whose finding produced the referral, which a manual filing may name as well and which is null whenever it is not supplied. Filing is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) when another referral for the same vehicle *and the same* `referral_type` already sits in `OPEN` or `INVESTIGATING`. A manually filed referral gets `origin` of `MANUAL` and `status` of `OPEN`, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `opened_by_user_id`, leaves `investigator_user_id` and `resolved_at` null, and drives the vehicle's `fraud_hold` from `NONE` to `UNDER_REFERRAL`. When `application_id` is supplied, the owning rebuilder's `open_referral_count` rises by one, and reaching 2 moves the licence `ACTIVE → PROBATION`. Referrals synthesised by a cascade — the stolen-component cascade of §3.8 and the odometer-rollback cascade of §3.11 — are identical except that their `origin` is `AUTO_CASCADE`, that their `opened_by_user_id` is the cascading principal's identifier, and that the duplicate-type refusal above guards only the manually filed route: a cascade synthesises its referral unconditionally, even while a referral of the very same `referral_type` already stands `OPEN` or `INVESTIGATING` against that vehicle.

A `FraudInvestigator` takes a referral up, optionally recording an `investigation_note`, which is accepted and validated as a string but is kept on no resource and published in no response. That move is admissible only from `OPEN` and lands the referral in `INVESTIGATING`, recording the investigator's `X-User-ID` as `investigator_user_id`. Taking up a referral in any other status is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

Resolution takes an `outcome` and an optional `resolution_note`, is admissible only from `INVESTIGATING`, and is barred to the principal recorded in `opened_by_user_id` under the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.10. An outcome of `UNSUBSTANTIATED` is the quiet branch: `resolved_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the vehicle's `fraud_hold` returns to `NONE` provided no other referral of that vehicle is `OPEN`, `INVESTIGATING` or `SUBSTANTIATED`, and when the referral named an `application_id` the owning rebuilder's `open_referral_count` falls by one.

An outcome of `SUBSTANTIATED` is the most consequential cascade in the program, and all of it happens inside the one request. `resolved_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the vehicle's `fraud_hold` moves to `CONFIRMED`, which is sticky and never walked back. Because `fraud_hold` became `CONFIRMED`, every title record of the vehicle whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → VOID` with `is_current` driven to false while its `brand_history` is retained for audit; if such a title's `recognition_state` was `RECOGNITION_PENDING` it becomes `STALE` and the pending interstate request against it moves to `SUPERSEDED`. Because the whole vehicle's chain is impeached, every conveyance of that vehicle whose `status` is `RECORDED` moves `RECORDED → VOID` as §3.11 describes, whichever title of the vehicle it was recorded against. Because the vehicle's title was voided, every one of the vehicle's applications whose `status` is not already `TITLE_ISSUED`, `DENIED_FINAL`, `WITHDRAWN` or `VOID_FRAUD` moves to `VOID_FRAUD` with `terminal_at` stamped, and each of those refunds its escrow exactly as §3.6 defines a refund — the application's `fee_escrowed_cents` is zeroed and no rebuilder counter is written, so `escrowed_fee_total_cents` falls by the refunded amount and `fee_balance_cents` rises by it purely because the ledger invariant is re-derived. This void reaches every non-terminal status, not merely `SCHEDULED`. Because those applications were voided, each of their attempts sitting in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` set to `FRAUD_HOLD`, releasing its inspector interval and its station bay by decrementing the inspector's `scheduled_inspection_count` and the station's `scheduled_occupancy_count`. And when the referral names an `application_id`, the `substantiated_referral_count` of that application's rebuilder rises by one; the moment it reaches 1 that licence moves to `SUSPENDED`. A referral carrying a null `application_id` — the odometer-rollback cascade's synthesised referral of §3.11 among them — belongs to no rebuilder's ledger, so it moves no `substantiated_referral_count` and triggers no automatic suspension of its own, however many of that vehicle's applications the cascade has just voided. The vehicle row is written once as `fraud_hold` becomes `CONFIRMED` and written again at the end of the cascade, once the titles and applications have been voided and its `active_brand_code` and `open_application_count` have fallen, so its `version` rises by 2 whenever that closing pass still finds something to change.

**Licence standing.** A `ProgramAdmin` moves a rebuilder's `license_status` by supplying an `action` and an optional `note`; every licence action records the acting `X-User-ID` as `last_license_action_by_user_id`, recomputes both record-keeping score variants, and increments the rebuilder's `version` by 1. Five actions exist and each is admissible only from specific statuses; an action that is not a declared transition out of the rebuilder's current `license_status` — a `REINSTATE` on an `ACTIVE` licence, or any action at all on a `REVOKED` licence, which is terminal — is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_ACTION_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

An action of `PROBATION` moves the licence `ACTIVE → PROBATION`; the same move happens on its own whenever `open_referral_count` reaches 2. A rebuilder on `PROBATION` may still open and submit applications. An action of `CLEAR_PROBATION` moves the licence `PROBATION → ACTIVE`, and is admissible only while `open_referral_count` is exactly 0 — outstanding allegations must be cleared before probation is lifted, and a `CLEAR_PROBATION` against a non-zero count is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_ACTION_INVALID`. An action of `SUSPEND` moves the licence to `SUSPENDED` from either `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`, records the acting administrator's `X-User-ID` as `suspended_by_user_id` (as well as in `last_license_action_by_user_id`), and thereafter refuses new applications and new submissions from that rebuilder; the same move happens on its own once `substantiated_referral_count` reaches 1, and it takes precedence over the automatic probation above when a single request would satisfy both thresholds. An automatic move — whether to `PROBATION` or to `SUSPENDED` — records neither `suspended_by_user_id` nor `last_license_action_by_user_id`, so an automatically suspended rebuilder carries a null `suspended_by_user_id` and no administrator is barred from its later `REINSTATE` by the segregation rule. Both automatic thresholds are re-evaluated only inside a request that opens a referral naming one of that rebuilder's applications or that substantiates one — never on a read, never on a licence action, and never when a referral is resolved `UNSUBSTANTIATED` — so a `REINSTATE` leaves the licence `ACTIVE` even while `substantiated_referral_count` stands at or above 1, until some later such request re-applies the threshold and suspends it again. An action of `REINSTATE` moves the licence `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and is doubly gated: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `suspended_by_user_id`, and the rebuilder's `recordkeeping_score_rolling` must be at least 60.00, or the request is refused with `ERR_RECORDKEEPING_SCORE_BLOCKS_ACTION` (HTTP 422). A successful reinstatement sets `suspended_by_user_id` back to null. An action of `REVOKE` moves the licence `SUSPENDED → REVOKED` and is admissible only while the rebuilder's `recordkeeping_score_lifetime` is strictly below 40.00 — the program revokes for a demonstrated lifetime pattern, not for a recent dip — and a lifetime score at or above that ceiling draws the same `ERR_RECORDKEEPING_SCORE_BLOCKS_ACTION`. Revocation is deliberately unkind about money: every non-terminal application of that rebuilder keeps its escrow, because revocation refunds nothing.
