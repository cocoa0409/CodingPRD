# Product Sampling & Seeding API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Consumer brands seed physical product samples into the hands of selected panel members in order to manufacture three things at once: first trial, honest published reviews, and measurable awareness. The operational reality behind that simple idea is unforgiving. Kits must be costed component by component before a single unit leaves a warehouse; panel members must be screened for segment fit, account-age fraud signals and address clustering before they are trusted with free product; every incentivized review must carry a disclosure the retailer will actually accept; and the whole programme must be defensible after the fact against the accusation that the "lift" it reports is nothing more than the purchasing behaviour the audience would have shown anyway.

This document specifies the Product Sampling & Seeding API, the system of record for that programme. The API governs the full arc of a sampling campaign: objective and audience definition, kit composition and inventory receipt, panel recruitment and eligibility screening with deterministic holdout assignment, wave-based fulfilment under two independent capacity ceilings, delivery outcomes and per-sample cost accounting, disclosure-gated review collection with independent compliance moderation, syndication to retailer feeds under each retailer's own publication policy, diversion and resale investigation with a revocation cascade that reaches all the way out to already-published reviews, and finally conversion matching and lift measurement against the withheld holdout group.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. The first is **conservation**: physical kit units and budgeted cents are tracked in buckets that must always sum back to their totals, and every compensating action releases exactly the amount its forward action committed — never a recomputation of it. The second is **separation of authority**: the manager who defines a campaign cannot moderate its reviews, the moderator who asks a panelist for changes cannot be the one who later approves the result, and the moderator who opens a diversion case cannot be the one who substantiates it. Where these commitments collide with convenience, the specification below always resolves in favour of the commitment.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold across every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity and auditing fields, the global order in which requests are validated, numeric precision, the conserved quantities, version pinning, and the campaign-wide gates that govern whether child operations may proceed at all.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries its caller identity in two mandatory HTTP headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. Both headers must be present and non-empty, and `X-User-ID` must be a syntactically valid UUID; a request that violates any part of this is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The single exception is panel sign-up, which is deliberately reachable without an established identity so that a prospective panel member can recruit themselves. On that one endpoint an absent or empty `X-User-Role` is read as `Anonymous`, and an `Anonymous` caller needs no `X-User-ID` at all — whatever that header holds is ignored rather than checked for UUID syntax. A header naming a role outside the six is still `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` even here, and a sign-up that names a real role other than `Anonymous` must still supply a non-empty, syntactically valid UUID in `X-User-ID`.

The platform recognises exactly six roles, spelled precisely as follows: `CampaignManager`, `ComplianceModerator`, `FulfillmentOps`, `Panelist`, `PlatformAdmin` and `Anonymous`. A value in `X-User-Role` that is not one of these six is also rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. When the named role is well-formed but is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six roles divide authority as follows.

- A `CampaignManager` owns campaign definition and lifecycle — objective, audience, kit composition, budget, screening decisions, syndication queueing and lift measurement.
- A `ComplianceModerator` is an independent compliance function: review moderation and disclosure enforcement, fraud rescoring, diversion case handling and campaign suspension. This role can neither define campaigns nor allocate inventory.
- `FulfillmentOps` runs inventory, wave planning and outbound logistics: inventory receipts, write-offs, sample allocation, wave locking and release, delivery outcomes and retailer feed dispatch.
- A `Panelist` is a recruited panel member who acts on their own enrollments and their own reviews, and on nothing else.
- A `PlatformAdmin` operates the platform: the retailer registry, purchase-data ingestion, panelist administration, logical-clock control and full read access. This role deliberately holds no campaign, moderation or allocation authority.
- `Anonymous` covers unauthenticated self-service recruitment. Panel sign-up is the only endpoint reachable in this capacity; every other endpoint called anonymously returns `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.

Identity resolution differs by role, and the difference matters for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `Panelist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Panelist` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Panelist` compare that resource's `panelist_id` — for a review, the `panelist_id` of the `Enrollment` named by the review's `enrollment_id` — against `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `CampaignManager`, `ComplianceModerator`, `FulfillmentOps`, `PlatformAdmin` or `Anonymous`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records such identifiers where a workflow demands an audit trail — `launched_by_user_id` on a campaign, `opened_by_user_id` on a diversion case, `changes_requested_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a review — but never compares them to any entity's `id`.

A `Panelist`-role caller who targets an enrollment or a review that does not resolve to their own `Panelist` row is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership check guards exactly seven operations: enrollment creation, screener resubmission, review-draft creation, review submission, review revision, single-enrollment reads and single-review reads.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one timestamp in the system taken from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, so repositioning the clock never changes it.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Collection reads are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0; it may be no smaller than 0 and has no upper bound. A `limit` or `offset` outside its permitted range is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with rows that share a `created_at` — routine, since the logical clock only moves when it is told to — broken by insertion sequence, so the row written first is returned first. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any matching row remains beyond the returned page.

Most collections additionally accept optional equality filters, each named exactly after the field it filters. The campaign list accepts `status` and `objective_type`. The kit-line list accepts `campaign_id` and `status`. The fulfilment-wave list accepts `fulfillment_center_id` and `status`. The panelist list accepts `referred_by_panelist_id`, `integrity_status` and `segment_code`. The enrollment list accepts `campaign_id`, `panelist_id`, `screening_status`, `obligation_status` and `holdout_flag`. The sample-shipment list accepts `enrollment_id`, `fulfillment_wave_id`, `supersedes_shipment_id` and `status`. The review list accepts `enrollment_id` and `status`. The syndication-record list accepts `review_submission_id`, `retailer_id` and `status`. The diversion-case list accepts `panelist_id`, `sample_shipment_id` and `status`. The purchase-match list accepts `enrollment_id` and `qualified`. The lift-analysis list accepts `campaign_id` and `variant`. These filters are how a caller reaches child rows from a parent: there is no nesting in collection paths apart from the per-enrollment purchase-match read. Filters match by exact equality and are never checked for referential existence, so an id that names no row simply yields an empty page rather than a 404; the two boolean filters, `holdout_flag` and `qualified`, accept only the exact strings `true` and `false`; and a filter supplied with an empty value, like an unrecognised query parameter, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Auditing Fields and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID with no type prefix, assigned at creation and never reused. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written — read, like every other instant the server mints of its own accord, from the logical clock described at the end of this section rather than from wall-clock time. Every other instant the server stamps of its own accord is rendered in that same shape — ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional digits, even when the fractional part is zero. Alongside `created_at` this covers `updated_at` and every event or derived stamp the server writes: a campaign's `closed_at`, a shipment's `shipped_at` and `delivered_at`, an enrollment's `review_due_at`, a review's `first_submitted_at`, a syndication record's `syndicated_at` and `takedown_requested_at`, a diversion case's `substantiated_at`, a panelist's `last_risk_scored_at`, a lift analysis's `computed_at`, and the `current_utc` reported when the logical clock is repositioned. An instant that arrives in a request body is left alone instead: `sampling_window_start`, `sampling_window_end`, a wave's `window_start` and `window_end`, a panelist's `account_opened_at` and a purchase match's `purchased_at` are stored and echoed back exactly as the caller spelled them, never re-rendered into the three-digit shape.

Campaigns, kit lines, fulfilment centers, fulfilment waves, panelists, enrollments, sample shipments, review submissions, retailers, syndication records, diversion cases and lift analyses additionally carry `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed from the logical clock on every mutation of the row, and equal to `created_at` until the first mutation lands — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade or a counter refresh. Purchase-match rows are the one exception: a purchase match is immutable once ingested and therefore carries only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a purchase match.

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the 404 returned; a later segment is never consulted once an earlier one has failed. The identifiers that appear in paths are `campaign_id`, `kit_line_id`, `fulfillment_center_id`, `fulfillment_wave_id`, `panelist_id`, `enrollment_id`, `sample_shipment_id`, `review_submission_id`, `retailer_id`, `syndication_record_id`, `diversion_case_id`, `purchase_match_id` and `lift_analysis_id`, and each has its own not-found code: `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KIT_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FULFILLMENT_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FULFILLMENT_WAVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PANELIST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SYNDICATION_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DIVERSION_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PURCHASE_MATCH_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_LIFT_ANALYSIS_NOT_FOUND`. The same codes are used when a foreign key supplied in a request body fails to resolve; body foreign keys are checked in the order the fields appear in the endpoint's input list, and the members of `syndication_retailer_ids` are checked left to right within that array.

All time-dependent behaviour reads a single server-side **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Review deadlines, panelist cooldown windows, the review-velocity window, the retailer rate-limit window and the conversion window all resolve against it. The logical clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until it is explicitly moved it reads that value — it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A `PlatformAdmin` may reposition it through an administrative test-driving capability that takes a single field, `to_iso8601`, carrying the target ISO 8601 UTC instant, and returns the resulting clock reading as `current_utc`. Repositioning the clock mutates no entity.

### 2.5. The Validation Ladder

Every request is validated against one global, strictly ordered ladder of rules, and **the first rule that fails determines the response**: no later rule is evaluated, and no later rule's error code can appear once an earlier rule has rejected the request. The ladder is what makes error codes predictable when several things are wrong at once, so it is stated here once in full rather than restated per endpoint.

1. `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
3. The named role is among the roles authorised for the called endpoint — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
4. The request body carries every required input field, every supplied value matches its declared type, enum membership and permitted range, no unknown field is present, and `limit` and `offset` are inside their permitted ranges — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint. A field sent explicitly as JSON `null` counts as no value at all: on a required field that is the missing-field rejection, and on an optional field it selects that field's declared default. An endpoint that declares no body fields accepts only an absent or empty body, so any field whatsoever is an unknown field there. Collection reads reject an unrecognised query parameter on this same rung, while single-resource reads ignore query parameters altogether.
5. On campaign creation, `sampling_window_end` is strictly later than `sampling_window_start` — otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_INVALID` (400).
6. On fulfilment-wave creation, `window_end` is strictly later than `window_start` — otherwise `ERR_WAVE_WINDOW_INVALID` (400).
7. Every path identifier resolves to an existing row, checked left to right — otherwise the matching not-found code from §2.4 (404). Applies to every endpoint whose path contains an identifier.
8. Every foreign key in the request body resolves to an existing row, checked in input-list order — otherwise the matching not-found code from §2.4 (404). Applies to every endpoint with foreign keys in its input.
9. When `X-User-Role` is `Panelist`, `X-User-ID` equals the `panelist_id` of the enrollment behind the target resource — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to the seven ownership-scoped operations named in §2.1.
10. The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise the state code for that resource family (409): `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_TRANSITION`, `ERR_INVALID_KIT_LINE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_WAVE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_SHIPMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REVIEW_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_SYNDICATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DIVERSION_CASE_STATE` or `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_SCREENABLE`. Applies to every transition endpoint.
11. The owning campaign's `status` permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_ACCEPTING_ENROLLMENTS` (409). Enrollment creation and screening require `SCREENING_OPEN` or `SAMPLING_ACTIVE`; shipment allocation requires `SAMPLING_ACTIVE`; review-draft creation and review submission require `SAMPLING_ACTIVE` or `REVIEW_WINDOW`; lift analysis requires `REVIEW_WINDOW` or `CLOSED`. Applies to exactly those six operations.
12. The optimistic-concurrency field in the request body equals the parent row's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_ENROLLMENT_VERSION` (412), as described in §2.8. Applies to lift-analysis creation and shipment allocation.
13. The panelist behind the target resource does not have `integrity_status` equal to `BANNED` — otherwise `ERR_PANELIST_BANNED` (403). Applies to enrollment creation, screening, screener resubmission, shipment allocation, review-draft creation, review submission, review revision and syndication queueing.
14. When the operation publishes panelist content outside the platform, that panelist does not have `integrity_status` equal to `FLAGGED` — otherwise `ERR_PANELIST_INTEGRITY_HOLD` (403). Applies to syndication queueing and syndication dispatch.
15. Segregation of duties holds: the acting `X-User-ID` is not the user who performed the paired earlier step. A review's approver may not be the moderator who requested changes on it, and a diversion case's substantiator may not be the moderator who opened it — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to review moderation and diversion substantiation.
16. The campaign's `compliance_posture` is not `FROZEN` — otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_FROZEN` (409). Applies to screening, shipment allocation, campaign resume, syndication queueing and syndication dispatch.
17. When the operation publishes content to a retailer feed, the campaign's `compliance_posture` is exactly `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (409). Applies to syndication queueing and syndication dispatch.
18. `honest_opinion_attested` is true on the review being submitted or revised — otherwise `ERR_HONEST_OPINION_NOT_ATTESTED` (422).
19. `disclosure_statement` is present and contains one of the required disclosure phrases as a case-insensitive substring — otherwise `ERR_DISCLOSURE_STATEMENT_MISSING` (422). Applies to review submission and review revision.
20. The `retailer_id` being targeted is a member of the owning campaign's `syndication_retailer_ids` — otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_IN_CAMPAIGN` (422). Applies to syndication queueing.
21. The retailer's `feed_status` is `ACCEPTING` — otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_FEED_PAUSED` (409). Applies to syndication queueing and syndication dispatch.
22. The review's `word_count` is at least the campaign's `min_review_word_count` — otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_TOO_SHORT` (422). Applies to review submission and review revision.
23. No enrollment already exists for this campaign-and-panelist pair whose `screening_status` is anything other than `SCREEN_REVOKED` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT` (409). Applies to enrollment creation.
24. No syndication record already exists for this review-and-retailer pair whose `status` is anything other than `REJECTED` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_SYNDICATION_TARGET` (409). Applies to syndication queueing.
25. The enrollment being sampled has `holdout_flag` false — otherwise `ERR_HOLDOUT_ENROLLMENT_NOT_SAMPLEABLE` (422). Applies to shipment allocation.
26. The enrollment behind the review has `obligation_status` equal to `REVIEW_DUE` — otherwise `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_REVIEW` (422). Applies to review submission, where it is evaluated ahead of rungs 18, 19 and 22 in the pinned content-gate order of §3.7.
27. The campaign has at least one kit line whose `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_KIT_EMPTY` (422). Applies to opening screening and to shipment allocation.
28. The per-wave ceiling holds — otherwise `ERR_WAVE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to shipment allocation.
29. The center-aggregate ceiling holds — otherwise `ERR_CENTER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to shipment allocation.
30. Every `ACTIVE` kit line of the campaign has at least `quantity_per_kit` units available — otherwise `ERR_KIT_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Applies to shipment allocation.
31. The requested write-off count does not exceed the kit line's `units_available` — otherwise `ERR_WRITE_OFF_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE` (422).
32. `purchased_at` falls inside the campaign's conversion window — otherwise `ERR_PURCHASE_MATCH_OUT_OF_WINDOW` (422). Applies to purchase-match ingestion.
33. The campaign's `budget_available_cents` is at least the shipment's `committed_cost_cents` — otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` (422). Applies to shipment allocation.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis. Because the campaign-status gate sits at rung 11 and the posture gate at rung 16, a request against a campaign in the wrong lifecycle status reports `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_ACCEPTING_ENROLLMENTS` even when the posture is also unacceptable. And because version pinning sits at rung 12, a stale write is reported as a stale version even when the business rules further down the ladder would also have refused it.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

Money is never represented as a fractional amount. Every monetary field in the system — `budget_total_cents`, `budget_committed_cents`, `budget_available_cents`, `unit_cost_cents`, `extended_cost_cents`, `kit_assembly_unit_cost_cents`, `freight_cents_per_kit`, `surcharge_cents`, `committed_cost_cents`, `write_off_cost_cents`, `purchase_amount_cents`, both cost-per-sample figures and every penalty figure — is a whole number of cents. Wherever a monetary computation produces a fraction, the result is rounded to a whole cent using round-half-up, meaning that a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero.

Where a rounding happens matters as much as how. Kit assembly rounds **per line and then sums**: each line's extended cost is rounded to a whole cent first, and the campaign's per-kit assembly cost is the sum of those already-rounded line values. Summing the unrounded line values and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule, and produces a different figure whenever the discarded per-line fractions accumulate past half a cent. Both cost-per-sample variants, by contrast, sum whole-cent inputs and round exactly once, on the single final division.

Non-monetary precision is fixed as follows. Both fraud risk scores are carried to one decimal place with round-half-up and clamped so that they are never below 0.0 and never above 100.0. Conversion rates and the internal diversion rate are carried to four decimal places with round-half-up. The lift percentage is carried to two decimal places with round-half-up. The three client-supplied fractions — `holdout_pct`, `component_discount_pct` and `match_confidence` — accept at most four decimal places on input.

Interval semantics are deliberately not uniform, and each rule states its own. A campaign's sampling window is inclusive at both ends. The panelist cooldown interval is inclusive at both ends, so two intervals that touch at a single instant collide. A fulfilment wave's pick window is half-open — inclusive of `window_start`, exclusive of `window_end` — so two waves that abut at an instant do not overlap. All instant comparisons resolve to second granularity on ISO 8601 UTC values.

### 2.7. Conserved Quantities: Kit Units and Budget Cents

The system maintains two conserved quantities, and both must balance at every observable instant — not merely at the end of a workflow.

The first is physical kit units. For every kit line, the sum of `units_available`, `units_reserved`, `units_shipped` and `units_written_off` equals `units_received` exactly. Booking a receipt raises `units_received` and `units_available` together. Allocating a sample moves units from available to reserved. Releasing a wave moves them from reserved to shipped. Writing off damaged stock moves them from available to written-off. No operation is permitted to leave the four buckets out of balance, not even transiently between cascade hops.

The second is budget cents. For every campaign, the sum of `budget_committed_cents` and `budget_available_cents` equals `budget_total_cents` exactly. Allocation moves the shipment's `committed_cost_cents` from available to committed. Cancellation moves back **exactly the amount recorded on the shipment at allocation time**, read from the shipment's own `committed_cost_cents` snapshot, and never a fresh recomputation from the campaign's current kit cost. This distinction is what prevents a kit-cost change between allocation and cancellation from leaking cents into or out of the budget. The same snapshot discipline applies to inventory: cancellation returns exactly the per-line amounts recorded in the shipment's `reserved_line_units` map, so an allocate-then-cancel round trip restores every one of the four unit buckets to the value it held before the allocation.

### 2.8. Version Pinning on Parent Resources

Two write operations require the client to pin the version of a **parent** row that the write reinterprets. Both are non-optional inputs, and neither has a default: omitting the field is an invalid request.

Creating a lift analysis through `POST /lift-analyses` requires `expected_campaign_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Campaign`. If that value does not equal the campaign's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is that a new analysis legitimately supersedes the campaign's prior analysis, so a writer holding a stale view of the campaign would silently overwrite measurement that already reflects newer delivery, diversion and syndication counters.

Allocating a sample shipment through `POST /sample-shipments` requires `expected_enrollment_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Enrollment`. If that value does not equal the enrollment's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENROLLMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is that a reship carrying `supersedes_shipment_id` legitimately supersedes a lost shipment and returns the obligation axis to `NONE`; an allocation built on a stale view of the enrollment would double-commit budget and kit units against an obligation state that no longer holds. Allocation therefore also advances `Enrollment.version` as one of its side effects, which is what makes a second allocation built on the same pinned value fail.

No other endpoint accepts a version field of any kind. In particular, cancellation, delivery, loss reporting, wave locking, wave release, moderation and every diversion-case transition are all unpinned, and supplying a version field to them is an unknown field and therefore an invalid request.

### 2.9. Compliance Posture: the Campaign-Wide Trade Gate

Alongside its lifecycle `status`, every campaign carries a derived `compliance_posture` that is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. A newly created campaign starts `OPEN`. The posture is recomputed — never set directly by a client — whenever a diversion case is substantiated, whenever a shipment is confirmed delivered, and whenever a moderator rejects a review for disclosure failure.

The recomputation reads three campaign counters. First an internal **diversion rate** is derived: when `delivered_sample_count` is 0 the rate is 0.0, and otherwise it is `substantiated_diversion_count` divided by `delivered_sample_count`, rounded half-up to four decimal places. The posture then follows the first matching branch of this ordered test:

- the posture is `FROZEN` when the diversion rate is at least 0.2, or when `substantiated_diversion_count` is at least 5;
- otherwise the posture is `RESTRICTED` when the diversion rate is at least 0.05, or when `disclosure_rejected_review_count` is at least 3;
- otherwise the posture is `OPEN`.

Both rate comparisons are inclusive: a diversion rate of exactly 0.05 is already restricting, and a rate of exactly 0.2 is already freezing. Because the branches are ordered, a campaign that satisfies both the freezing and the restricting conditions is `FROZEN`.

The posture gates child work, and the two non-open postures gate different things. `RESTRICTED` withdraws only the ability to publish externally: queueing a review for syndication and dispatching a queued record are both refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`, while enrollment screening and sample allocation continue unaffected. `FROZEN` is broader: it additionally refuses enrollment screening, sample allocation and returning a suspended campaign to active sampling, all with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_FROZEN`, and it also refuses syndication queueing and dispatch with that same code.

### 2.10. Panel Integrity Standing and Segregation of Duties

Each panelist carries an `integrity_status` of `CLEAR`, `FLAGGED` or `BANNED`, which is fraud control's standing verdict on that person. `CLEAR` is the recruited default and the only standing under which a panelist can pass screening. `FLAGGED` marks a panelist whose audited fraud score has crossed the flagging threshold or who is under live investigation; a flagged panelist's content is held out of every outbound retailer feed, and any attempt to queue or dispatch it is refused with `ERR_PANELIST_INTEGRITY_HOLD`. `BANNED` is the outcome of substantiated diversion and blocks every panelist-facing write with `ERR_PANELIST_BANNED`.

Segregation of duties is enforced on two paired decisions, in both cases by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against the identifier the earlier step recorded. A review's approval is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the acting moderator's `X-User-ID` equals the review's `changes_requested_by_user_id`. A diversion case's substantiation is refused with the same code when the acting moderator's `X-User-ID` equals the case's `opened_by_user_id`. Both checks compare exact identifier equality, and neither is relaxed by elapsed time or by intervening revision cycles: a moderator who once asked for changes on a review can never approve that review, however many revision rounds follow.

### 2.11. Derived Counters and Cascading Consistency

A campaign row is the reporting surface for its whole programme, and every count and cost figure it exposes is server-maintained. `enrollment_count`, `holdout_enrollment_count`, `allocated_sample_count`, `delivered_sample_count`, `approved_review_count`, `syndicated_review_count`, `disclosure_rejected_review_count`, `substantiated_diversion_count`, `write_off_cost_cents`, `kit_assembly_unit_cost_cents`, `budget_committed_cents`, `budget_available_cents`, `cost_per_sample_provisional_cents` and `cost_per_sample_final_cents` are all computed, never accepted from a client, and each refresh is a mutation of the campaign row that refreshes its `updated_at` and increases its `version` by 1.

Some counters are monotone and some are not, and the difference is load-bearing. `enrollment_count` only ever rises: it is the campaign's total enrollment history and the source of each new enrollment's ordinal, so it is never decremented, not even when an enrollment is later revoked. `allocated_sample_count` falls again when an allocation is cancelled. `approved_review_count` and `syndicated_review_count` fall again when the revocation cascade retracts previously approved or previously syndicated content. `delivered_sample_count`, `disclosure_rejected_review_count` and `substantiated_diversion_count` only rise.

The system also performs multi-step propagation on its own initiative. Two such chains exist, and they differ in origin, depth and propagation pattern: cancelling an allocated shipment propagates three hops outward from the shipment through kit lines and the fulfilment wave to the campaign, while substantiating a diversion case propagates four hops from the panelist through their enrollments and reviews to their published syndication records. Each hop is caused by the previous hop's mutation rather than by the originating request, and each hop independently refreshes `updated_at` and increases `version` on every row it touches — including rows the client never named. Section 3 specifies both chains hop by hop. A state change the server performs as part of a chain is not a client-visible transition endpoint: the wave unlock that returns a wave from `LOCKED` to `PLANNED`, the enrollment revocation to `SCREEN_REVOKED`, the review retraction to `RETRACTED` and the syndication takedown to `TAKEDOWN_REQUESTED` all happen only as cascade hops and can never be requested directly.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Defining a Campaign and Composing Its Sample Kit

A `CampaignManager` opens a sampling programme by declaring what it is for. The declaration carries a `name` — non-empty and at most 160 characters — and an `objective_type` naming the primary sampling objective, which is exactly one of `TRIAL_GENERATION`, `REVIEW_ACQUISITION` or `AWARENESS`. Audience specification is explicit rather than inferred: `target_segment_codes` is an array of between one and eight non-empty strings with no duplicates, and only a panelist whose own `segment_code` appears in that array can pass screening. The sampling period is bounded by `sampling_window_start` and `sampling_window_end`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants and both inclusive; a window whose end is not strictly later than its start is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_INVALID`. Money enters as `budget_total_cents`, a whole-cent figure of zero or more.

Four further inputs are optional at definition time because each has a considered default. `syndication_retailer_ids` lists between zero and eight retailers the campaign is contractually cleared to publish to; every id in it must already exist, they are validated from left to right, and a miss returns `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_FOUND`. This array is a clearance list rather than a relationship: it is consulted when a review is queued for a retailer, and a retailer absent from it can never receive this campaign's reviews. Omitting it stores an empty array rather than leaving the field unset, and unlike `target_segment_codes` it carries no uniqueness constraint — a repeated id is accepted and simply clears the same retailer twice. `review_due_days` sets how long after delivery a panelist owes their review and defaults to three weeks — 21 days — with any value from 1 through 90 accepted. `min_review_word_count` sets the campaign-level floor on review length and defaults to 50 words, accepting 10 through 500. `holdout_pct` is the fraction of screened enrollments deliberately withheld from sampling so that lift can be measured against them; it defaults to one fifth, expressed as 0.2, and may be anywhere from 0 through 0.5 inclusive with at most four decimal places.

A campaign is born in `DRAFT` with `compliance_posture` `OPEN`, with `budget_available_cents` seeded to the full `budget_total_cents` and `budget_committed_cents` at zero, with every derived counter at zero, with `kit_assembly_unit_cost_cents` at zero, and with `launched_by_user_id` and `closed_at` both unset.

While the campaign is still `DRAFT`, its manager composes the sample kit line by line. Each line names its owning campaign through `campaign_id` and describes one component: a `component_sku` that must be non-empty and unique among that campaign's lines, a `component_role` drawn from `HERO_PRODUCT`, `ACCESSORY`, `INSERT_CARD` and `DISCLOSURE_INSERT`, a `unit_cost_cents` of zero or more, a `quantity_per_kit` of at least one unit consumed per assembled kit, and an optional supplier `component_discount_pct` that defaults to no discount at all and must be at least 0 and strictly below 1 with at most four decimal places. Lines may only be added while the campaign is `DRAFT`; once screening has opened, the kit is frozen and a further line is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_TRANSITION`. `component_sku` uniqueness is judged against every line the campaign already holds, `RETIRED` ones included, and a collision is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — checked after the campaign's existence and `DRAFT`-status gates rather than alongside the other rung-4 field checks, so a duplicate SKU aimed at a campaign that has left `DRAFT` reports the transition error instead. Every line is created `ACTIVE`, with all four of its unit buckets — `units_received`, `units_available`, `units_reserved` and `units_shipped` — and `units_written_off` at zero.

Kit costing is where the rounding discipline of §2.6 becomes concrete. Each line's `extended_cost_cents` is its `unit_cost_cents` multiplied by its `quantity_per_kit`, multiplied by one minus its `component_discount_pct`, and that product is rounded half-up to a whole cent **on the line**. The campaign's `kit_assembly_unit_cost_cents` is then the plain sum of those already-rounded line values, taken over the campaign's `ACTIVE` lines only and evaluated in order of `created_at` ascending with `component_sku` ascending as the tie-break. A `RETIRED` line contributes neither cost nor reservation. This figure is refreshed whenever a line is added, whenever a line is retired, and again when screening opens.

Retirement is the only kit-line transition: a `CampaignManager` moves a line from `ACTIVE` to `RETIRED`, which is terminal. Retiring a line that is already `RETIRED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_KIT_LINE_STATE`, and retiring any line of a `CLOSED` campaign is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_TRANSITION`; the line's own state is examined first, so an already-`RETIRED` line of a `CLOSED` campaign reports `ERR_INVALID_KIT_LINE_STATE`. Retirement recomputes the campaign's `kit_assembly_unit_cost_cents` — the retired line stops contributing — and increases both the line's and the campaign's `version`. It deliberately leaves `units_reserved` on the retired line untouched: those units are still owed back to `units_available` by the shipment that reserved them, and they are released by that shipment's own cancellation or by its wave's release, so the four-bucket balance of §2.7 continues to hold on a retired row. Retiring every line of a campaign therefore empties the reservation pool without unbalancing anything.

### 3.2. The Campaign Lifecycle

A campaign advances through `DRAFT`, `SCREENING_OPEN`, `SAMPLING_ACTIVE`, `REVIEW_WINDOW` and `CLOSED`, with a `SUSPENDED` detour available while sampling is live. `CLOSED` is the only terminal state.

Opening screening moves the campaign `DRAFT → SCREENING_OPEN` on a `CampaignManager`'s request. The campaign must currently be `DRAFT`, and it must have at least one `ACTIVE` kit line — a campaign with an empty kit is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_KIT_EMPTY`, since no kit could be assembled. Opening screening refreshes `kit_assembly_unit_cost_cents`, sets `budget_available_cents` to `budget_total_cents` less `budget_committed_cents`, and increases the campaign's `version` by 1.

Activating sampling moves the campaign `SCREENING_OPEN → SAMPLING_ACTIVE`, again on a `CampaignManager`'s request, and requires at least one enrollment of that campaign already in `SCREEN_PASSED`; a campaign with nobody screened in is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_TRANSITION`. Activation records `launched_by_user_id` from the acting manager's `X-User-ID` and increases `version`. Sample allocation is reachable only from `SAMPLING_ACTIVE`, which makes activation the gate on all outbound logistics.

Suspension and resumption form a repeatable cycle that belongs to compliance, not to campaign management. A `ComplianceModerator` moves a live campaign `SAMPLING_ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, which is not terminal, and later moves it `SUSPENDED → SAMPLING_ACTIVE` again; a campaign may suspend and resume any number of times. Resumption additionally requires that `compliance_posture` is not `FROZEN` — a frozen campaign stays suspended until the posture clears, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_FROZEN`. Each leg increases `version` by 1.

Opening the review window moves the campaign `SAMPLING_ACTIVE → REVIEW_WINDOW` and is the point at which allocation stops. It requires that the campaign has no shipment left in `ALLOCATED`: every planned kit must have shipped, been delivered, been reported lost or been cancelled first, and an outstanding allocation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_TRANSITION`. The transition refreshes `cost_per_sample_final_cents` and increases `version`.

Closing moves the campaign `REVIEW_WINDOW → CLOSED` and requires that no review of the campaign is still sitting in `SUBMITTED` awaiting a moderator; an undecided review is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_TRANSITION`. Closure forfeits the stragglers: every enrollment of the campaign whose `obligation_status` is still `REVIEW_DUE` moves to `FORFEITED`, each such enrollment's `version` increasing by 1. Closure then refreshes `cost_per_sample_final_cents`, stamps `closed_at` with the logical clock's current instant, and increases the campaign's `version`. Resuming, suspending, reopening or otherwise transitioning a `CLOSED` campaign is impossible, and any lifecycle request whose declared origin state does not match the campaign's current `status` — activating a `DRAFT` campaign, resuming a campaign that is not `SUSPENDED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_TRANSITION`.

### 3.3. Panel Recruitment and Fraud Risk Scoring

Recruitment is self-service. A prospective panel member signs up unauthenticated, and a `PlatformAdmin` may also create panelists administratively. Sign-up carries a login `email` that must be non-empty and globally unique across the panel, a `display_name` used as the public review byline, an `address_hash` that is a salted hash of the shipping address, an `account_opened_at` instant recording when the panel account was opened, and a `segment_code` naming the audience segment the person belongs to. Referral is optional: `referred_by_panelist_id` names the panelist who referred this one and defaults to unset for an organic signup; when supplied it must resolve to an existing panelist, or the request returns `ERR_PANELIST_NOT_FOUND`. An `email` that is already registered is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and that uniqueness check runs before the referral id is resolved, so a sign-up that both reuses an email and names a missing referrer reports the invalid request. A new panelist starts `CLEAR` with `diversion_strikes` at zero, `fraud_risk_score_screening` at 0.0, `fraud_risk_score_audit` unset until an audit rescore first runs, and `last_risk_scored_at` unset until the first scoring of any kind.

Fraud risk is a weighted score computed in two variants that share four terms and differ in two more. The shared terms are as follows.

- **Address clustering.** Equal address hashes across different panelists are the platform's strongest cheap fraud signal. The system counts the other panelists whose `address_hash` equals this panelist's, counting panelists in any `integrity_status` and excluding the panelist being scored. That collision count is capped at three, and the address term is 18 points multiplied by the capped count and divided by three — that is, 6 points for one collision, 12 for two, and the full 18 for three or more.
- **Account age.** The system takes the difference between the logical clock's current instant and `account_opened_at`, converts it to whole days by discarding any partial day, and adds 22 points when that whole-day age is strictly below 30 days. An account that is exactly 30 days old or older contributes nothing.
- **Diversion strikes.** The strike term is 25 points multiplied by `diversion_strikes` and divided by two, capped so that it never exceeds 25 points — 12.5 points for a single strike and the full 25 points from two strikes upward.
- **Tainted referral.** When `referred_by_panelist_id` is set and the referring panelist's own `integrity_status` is `FLAGGED` or `BANNED`, 15 points are added. An organic signup, or a referral from a `CLEAR` panelist, contributes nothing.

The **screening variant** is the sum of exactly those four terms, taken with the address term at its face weight. The result is capped at 100.0, rounded half-up to one decimal place, and written to `fraud_risk_score_screening`. It uses only signals observable before a sample ever ships.

The **audit variant** re-weights and extends. The address term is multiplied by 1.5 before summation, and two additional terms join.

- **Review velocity.** The system counts the reviews belonging to this panelist's enrollments whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `RETRACTED` and whose `first_submitted_at` is set and falls inside the trailing seven-day window ending at the logical clock's current instant, inclusive at both ends. When that count is at least 3, the velocity term is 20 points; otherwise it is nothing.
- **Live resale exposure.** The system counts this panelist's own diversion cases whose `status` is `INVESTIGATING` or `SUBSTANTIATED`. That count is capped at two, and the resale term is 30 points multiplied by the capped count and divided by two — 15 points for one live case and the full 30 for two or more.

The audit total is the 1.5-weighted address term plus the account-age term plus the strike term plus the referral term plus the velocity term plus the resale term, capped at 100.0, rounded half-up to one decimal place, and written to `fraud_risk_score_audit`. Because the audit variant strictly adds to the screening variant's terms and re-weights the address term upward, the same panelist row always audits at least as high as it screens, and strictly higher whenever any collision, velocity or live-case signal exists.

Scoring happens in three places. The screening variant runs automatically as part of eligibility screening. The audit variant runs automatically when a diversion case moves into investigation. And a `ComplianceModerator` may rescore a panelist on demand without touching any enrollment, supplying a `variant` field that is either `SCREENING` or `AUDIT` to select which score is refreshed. Every scoring, whatever its trigger, stamps `last_risk_scored_at` with the logical clock's current instant and increases the panelist's `version` by 1. An on-demand or investigation-triggered `AUDIT` rescore also applies the flagging rule: when the resulting `fraud_risk_score_audit` is at least 55.0 and the panelist is currently `CLEAR`, their `integrity_status` becomes `FLAGGED`. The threshold is inclusive — a score of exactly 55.0 flags — and the rule never downgrades a standing that is already `FLAGGED` or `BANNED`.

### 3.4. Enrollment, Eligibility Screening and Holdout Assignment

An enrollment is the join between a panelist and a campaign, and it is created either by the panelist themselves or by a `CampaignManager` on their behalf. The payload names the `campaign_id` and the `panelist_id` and carries `screening_answers`, a free-form object of screener responses — any JSON object, an empty one included, is accepted and stored verbatim without inspection of its keys. A `Panelist`-role caller whose `X-User-ID` is not the named `panelist_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Enrollment is only accepted while the campaign's `status` is `SCREENING_OPEN` or `SAMPLING_ACTIVE`; otherwise the response is `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_ACCEPTING_ENROLLMENTS`. A `BANNED` panelist cannot enroll at all, and the attempt returns `ERR_PANELIST_BANNED`. Duplication is blocked at the pair level: if the panelist already holds an enrollment on this campaign in any `screening_status` other than `SCREEN_REVOKED`, the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT`, which means a revoked enrollment is the one kind that may be replaced.

Creation assigns `enrollment_seq`, the enrollment's 1-based ordinal within its campaign, computed as the campaign's `enrollment_count` plus one. The campaign's `enrollment_count` then increases by 1 and its `version` by 1. The new row starts on both of its independent state axes at their initial values — `screening_status` at `PENDING_SCREEN` and `obligation_status` at `NONE` — with `holdout_flag` false, `screen_attempt_count` at zero, and `screening_failure_reason`, `review_due_at` and `fraud_score_at_screening` all unset.

These two axes are the heart of the enrollment model, and they evolve independently on the same row. The screening axis records eligibility: `PENDING_SCREEN`, `SCREEN_PASSED`, `SCREEN_FAILED` and the terminal `SCREEN_REVOKED`. The obligation axis records what the panelist owes the programme: `NONE`, `SAMPLE_SENT`, `REVIEW_DUE`, and the terminal `REVIEW_FULFILLED` and `FORFEITED`. A shipment that ships, is delivered, is lost and is then reshipped drives the obligation axis around a full loop while the screening axis sits unchanged at `SCREEN_PASSED` throughout.

Screening is a `CampaignManager` action and drives the screening axis out of `PENDING_SCREEN`. An enrollment not currently in `PENDING_SCREEN` is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_SCREENABLE`; a campaign not in `SCREENING_OPEN` or `SAMPLING_ACTIVE` yields `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_ACCEPTING_ENROLLMENTS`; a `FROZEN` posture yields `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_FROZEN`; and a `BANNED` panelist yields `ERR_PANELIST_BANNED`. Past those gates the operation always succeeds as an HTTP 200 — **a failed screen is not an error response.** It is a 200 whose returned enrollment carries `screening_status` `SCREEN_FAILED` and a `screening_failure_reason`.

Every evaluated screen increases `screen_attempt_count` by 1, refreshes the panelist's `fraud_risk_score_screening` and `last_risk_scored_at`, snapshots that freshly computed score onto the enrollment as `fraud_score_at_screening`, and increases the enrollment's `version` by 1 — and it does all of this whether the outcome is a pass or a fail. The decision itself walks four checks **in this pinned order, with the first failing check supplying `screening_failure_reason` and no later check evaluated**:

1. the panelist's `integrity_status` is not `CLEAR` — reason `INTEGRITY_HOLD`;
2. the panelist's `segment_code` is not one of the campaign's `target_segment_codes` — reason `SEGMENT_MISMATCH`;
3. the just-recomputed `fraud_risk_score_screening` is at least 55.0 — reason `FRAUD_SCORE_TOO_HIGH`, the threshold being inclusive so that a score of exactly 55.0 fails;
4. the panelist is inside a cooldown conflict with another campaign — reason `COOLDOWN_CONFLICT`.

If none of the four fires, the enrollment moves `PENDING_SCREEN → SCREEN_PASSED`. The ordering has visible consequences: a panelist who is both out of segment and scoring above the fraud threshold records `SEGMENT_MISMATCH`, because segment fit is checked before the score.

**Cooldown** exists to stop the panel's most enthusiastic members from being sampled continuously by overlapping campaigns. The resource being protected is the panelist. Every other enrollment of the same panelist whose `screening_status` is `SCREEN_PASSED` occupies its own campaign's sampling window extended by a two-week trailing exclusivity buffer — that is, from that campaign's `sampling_window_start` through its `sampling_window_end` plus 14 days, inclusive at both ends. Enrollments in `PENDING_SCREEN`, `SCREEN_FAILED` or `SCREEN_REVOKED` occupy nothing. The candidate campaign occupies its own `sampling_window_start` through `sampling_window_end`, also inclusive at both ends. A conflict exists when the candidate's window start is at or before an occupied interval's end **and** the candidate's window end is at or after that interval's start; prior enrollments are examined in `created_at` ascending order and the first conflict found ends the search. Because both intervals are closed, touching at a single instant counts as a collision: a candidate starting at exactly the prior campaign's window end plus 14 days conflicts, while a candidate starting one second later does not.

**Holdout assignment** runs only on the pass leg, and it is fully deterministic — no randomness is involved anywhere. An enrollment is placed in the holdout group when the whole-number part of its `enrollment_seq` multiplied by the campaign's `holdout_pct` is strictly greater than the whole-number part of one less than its `enrollment_seq` multiplied by that same `holdout_pct`. This staircase places exactly the whole-number part of the count-so-far multiplied by `holdout_pct` holdouts among the first however-many enrollments of a campaign, and it reads nothing but the enrollment's own ordinal and the campaign's configured fraction. When the flag comes out true, the campaign's `holdout_enrollment_count` increases by 1 and its `version` by 1. A holdout member is never sampled: they exist so that their purchasing behaviour can be compared against the treated group's.

A failed screen is recoverable, and the recovery path belongs to the panelist. The owning panelist replaces their screener answers, moving the enrollment `SCREEN_FAILED → PENDING_SCREEN`; the request carries a fresh `screening_answers` object that replaces the stored one wholesale rather than merging into it, and it clears `screening_failure_reason` and increases the enrollment's `version` by 1. The enrollment must currently be `SCREEN_FAILED`, the caller's `X-User-ID` must match the enrollment's `panelist_id`, and the panelist must not be `BANNED` — refusals are `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_SCREENABLE`, `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` and `ERR_PANELIST_BANNED` respectively. The cycle `SCREEN_FAILED → PENDING_SCREEN → SCREEN_PASSED` or `SCREEN_FAILED` is capped at three evaluated attempts: a resubmission is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_SCREENABLE` once `screen_attempt_count` has reached 3, and that attempt ceiling is checked after the state, ownership and ban gates. Resubmission consults neither the campaign's lifecycle `status` nor its `compliance_posture`, so a `FROZEN` posture or a campaign that has already left `SAMPLING_ACTIVE` does not block it — only the subsequent screen is gated on those.

### 3.5. Inventory Receipt, Wave Planning and Pick Capacity

Physical fulfilment is organised around centers and waves. `FulfillmentOps` registers a fulfilment center with a `name`, a `zone_code` naming the geographic zone it serves, a `daily_pick_capacity_units` of at least one unit that expresses how many kit units its pickers can pull in a day, and a `freight_cents_per_kit` of zero or more that is the outbound freight charged per kit dispatched from that center. Both numbers are policy about the building, not about any one shipment.

Inventory arrives against kit lines. A receipt names a `units` count, which increases both the line's `units_received` and its `units_available` by that amount and increases the line's `version` by 1; it touches no campaign field and so leaves the campaign's `updated_at` and `version` alone. Receipts are ungated beyond field validity: a `RETIRED` line still takes stock, and so does a line whose campaign has reached any lifecycle status including `CLOSED`. A write-off names a `units` count and a `reason` describing the damage or expiry, moves that many units out of `units_available` and into `units_written_off`, adds the units multiplied by the line's `unit_cost_cents` to the campaign's `write_off_cost_cents`, refreshes `cost_per_sample_final_cents`, and increases both the line's and the campaign's `version` by 1. A write-off may never exceed what is on hand: a request for more than `units_available` is refused with `ERR_WRITE_OFF_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE`. Write-offs are otherwise as ungated as receipts — the line's own `status` and the campaign's lifecycle `status` are both irrelevant. Written-off units never come back — the write-off bucket is a sink, and it is the reason the four-bucket identity of §2.7 counts `units_written_off` alongside available, reserved and shipped units.

A pick wave is a slice of a center's working time. Planning one names the `fulfillment_center_id` it runs at, a `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the slice, and a `planned_capacity_units` of at least one unit that is this wave's own ceiling. A wave whose `window_end` is not strictly after its `window_start` is refused with `ERR_WAVE_WINDOW_INVALID`. Waves are created `PLANNED` with `planned_unit_count` and `shipped_unit_count` at zero. As §2.6 sets out, a wave's interval is half-open — the start instant belongs to the wave and the end instant does not — so two waves that meet exactly, where one's `window_end` equals the next one's `window_start`, do not overlap and never compete for the same capacity.

Capacity is enforced at allocation time against **two independent ceilings**, and a candidate allocation must clear both.

- The **per-wave ceiling** compares the wave's own `planned_unit_count` plus the kit units the new shipment would consume against that wave's `planned_capacity_units`. Exceeding it is refused with `ERR_WAVE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Landing exactly on the ceiling is allowed; going one unit past it is not.
- The **center-aggregate ceiling** protects the building rather than the wave. The system collects every wave of the same `fulfillment_center_id` whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `LOCKED` and whose half-open interval overlaps the target wave's — that is, whose `window_start` is strictly before the target's `window_end` and whose `window_end` is strictly after the target's `window_start`, a set that always includes the target wave itself — sums their `planned_unit_count` values, adds the kit units the new shipment would consume, and compares the total against the center's `daily_pick_capacity_units`. Exceeding it is refused with `ERR_CENTER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A `RELEASED` wave is excluded from the overlap set, and because releasing a wave zeroes its `planned_unit_count`, a released wave's units stop counting against its neighbours. Two sibling waves at the same center that overlap in time therefore share one pool of picker capacity: each may be individually under its own `planned_capacity_units` and still be jointly refused.

A wave has three states. `FulfillmentOps` locks a `PLANNED` wave to `LOCKED`, closing it to further allocation; a wave that is not `PLANNED`, or one whose `planned_unit_count` is still zero because nothing was ever allocated into it, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WAVE_STATE`. Locking an empty wave is deliberately impossible — there would be nothing to release. `FulfillmentOps` then releases a `LOCKED` wave to `RELEASED`, which is terminal, handing the whole batch to the carrier. Releasing a wave that is not `LOCKED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WAVE_STATE`. There is also a return leg from `LOCKED` back to `PLANNED`, but as §2.11 states it has no request of its own: it fires only as a cascade hop when the last allocation leaves a locked wave.

### 3.6. Allocation, Shipping, Delivery and Per-Sample Cost Accounting

Allocation is the single busiest operation in the system: it is where a screened panelist, a planned wave, physical kit stock and campaign budget all have to agree at once. `FulfillmentOps` allocates by naming the `enrollment_id` receiving the kit, the `fulfillment_wave_id` it will be picked in, and `expected_enrollment_version` as the optimistic-concurrency token described in §2.8. Two optional fields follow: `surcharge_cents`, an extra per-shipment charge such as expedited handling, defaulting to zero; and `supersedes_shipment_id`, which names the earlier shipment this one replaces and is used only when reshipping after a loss.

The preconditions are checked in the global ladder order of §2.5, which means the transition-state family at rung 10 is examined before the campaign-status gate at rung 11, and both before the posture gate at rung 16. Within rung 10 the sub-order is pinned: the enrollment's `screening_status` must be `SCREEN_PASSED`, then its `obligation_status` must be either `NONE` or `FORFEITED`, then — when `supersedes_shipment_id` is supplied — the superseded shipment must be in `CANCELLED` or `LOST`, the two states that can be superseded, and finally the target wave's `status` must be `PLANNED`. The first three failures all report `ERR_INVALID_SHIPMENT_STATE`; a wave that is `LOCKED` or `RELEASED` reports `ERR_INVALID_WAVE_STATE`. Past rung 10 the campaign must be `SAMPLING_ACTIVE`, the posture must not be `FROZEN`, and the remaining business rungs apply in ladder order: the enrollment must not be a holdout member (`ERR_HOLDOUT_ENROLLMENT_NOT_SAMPLEABLE`), the campaign must still have at least one `ACTIVE` kit line (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_KIT_EMPTY`), both capacity ceilings must clear, every `ACTIVE` kit line must have stock, and the campaign's `budget_available_cents` must cover the shipment's cost. A holdout member is refused here and always: withholding them is the entire basis on which lift is later measured.

**What gets reserved** is decided by a fixed selector rather than by anything in the request. The candidate set is every kit line of the enrollment's campaign whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; `RETIRED` lines are never debited. If that set is empty the allocation is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_KIT_EMPTY`. The candidates are ordered by `created_at` ascending with `component_sku` ascending as the tie-break, and each contributes its own `quantity_per_kit` units — one kit needs one line's worth of every active component. The reservation is **all-or-nothing**: the lines are walked in that order and the first line whose `units_available` is below its `quantity_per_kit` refuses the whole allocation with `ERR_KIT_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT`, leaving every kit line untouched. No partial reservation is ever written, so a failed allocation is indistinguishable from one that never happened. On success the per-line debits are recorded on the shipment as `reserved_line_units`, a mapping from kit line to units taken, and their sum is recorded as `kit_unit_total` — the figure both capacity ceilings were tested against. Every debited line moves that many units out of `units_available` and into `units_reserved` and has its `version` increased by 1.

**What gets committed** is a snapshot, not a formula to be re-evaluated later. The shipment's `committed_cost_cents` is the campaign's current `kit_assembly_unit_cost_cents` plus the `freight_cents_per_kit` of the center that owns the shipment's wave. That amount moves out of the campaign's `budget_available_cents` and into its `budget_committed_cents`. If `budget_available_cents` is short of it, the allocation is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`; landing exactly on the available balance is permitted. The wave's `planned_unit_count` then grows by `kit_unit_total` and its `version` by 1; the campaign's `allocated_sample_count` grows by 1, its `cost_per_sample_provisional_cents` is refreshed and its `version` grows by 1; and the enrollment's `version` grows by 1, which is what makes a second allocation attempt with the same `expected_enrollment_version` fail as stale.

Cancellation is the compensating action and is available only while the shipment is `ALLOCATED`; a shipment in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SHIPMENT_STATE`. It runs the three-hop chain described in §2.11: the mapped units in `reserved_line_units` go back from `units_reserved` to `units_available` on each kit line; because those units are no longer reserved, the wave's `planned_unit_count` falls by `kit_unit_total`, and a `LOCKED` wave whose count thereby reaches zero returns to `PLANNED`; and because the wave's planned units changed, the campaign's `allocated_sample_count` falls by 1, exactly the shipment's snapshotted `committed_cost_cents` moves back from `budget_committed_cents` to `budget_available_cents`, and `cost_per_sample_provisional_cents` is refreshed. Because the release uses the snapshot rather than a fresh computation, a kit-cost change between allocation and cancellation — a line retired, a component discount renegotiated — cannot leak or invent a single cent. `CANCELLED` is terminal.

Releasing the wave ships everything in it at once. Every shipment of the released wave still in `ALLOCATED` moves to `SHIPPED` with `shipped_at` stamped from the logical clock; each such shipment's mapped units move from `units_reserved` to `units_shipped` on every kit line named in its `reserved_line_units`, with each line's `version` increased by 1; the wave's `shipped_unit_count` takes on the units that just shipped while its `planned_unit_count` returns to zero; and each affected enrollment's obligation axis advances `NONE → SAMPLE_SENT`. Shipping is a batch event by design, which is why there is no per-shipment ship request anywhere in the API.

Delivery and loss are the two outcomes of a `SHIPPED` shipment, and both are terminal. Confirming delivery moves the shipment `SHIPPED → DELIVERED`, stamps `delivered_at` from the logical clock, increases the campaign's `delivered_sample_count` by 1, refreshes `cost_per_sample_final_cents`, re-evaluates `compliance_posture`, and advances the enrollment's obligation axis `SAMPLE_SENT → REVIEW_DUE` while stamping `review_due_at` as the delivery instant plus the campaign's `review_due_days` days. Reporting a loss moves the shipment `SHIPPED → LOST` and refreshes `cost_per_sample_final_cents`; it advances the enrollment's obligation axis `SAMPLE_SENT → FORFEITED` **only when no other shipment of that enrollment is still in `ALLOCATED`, `SHIPPED` or `DELIVERED`** — a panelist with a live parallel shipment still owes their review, so the obligation stays where it is. A shipment that is not `SHIPPED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SHIPMENT_STATE` on either request.

The recovery path from a loss is a reship, and it is an ordinary allocation carrying `supersedes_shipment_id`. It returns the enrollment's obligation axis `FORFEITED → NONE`, which requires that the enrollment's `screening_status` is still `SCREEN_PASSED` and that the superseded shipment's own `status` is `LOST` or `CANCELLED` — those two alone are supersedable, and any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SHIPMENT_STATE`; the obligation axis then loops forward again through `SAMPLE_SENT` when the new wave releases. The reset of the obligation axis is the work of `supersedes_shipment_id` and of nothing else: an allocation accepted against a `FORFEITED` obligation without that field leaves the obligation reading `FORFEITED`, and since wave release only advances an obligation reading `NONE`, that enrollment's obligation never moves again. The full obligation cycle `NONE → SAMPLE_SENT → FORFEITED → NONE` can therefore be traversed any number of times on one enrollment while its screening axis never moves off `SCREEN_PASSED`.

**Cost per sample** is published in two variants that answer two different questions, and the difference between them is deliberate rather than incidental. A shipment's freight is always the `freight_cents_per_kit` of the center that owns that shipment's wave, so a campaign shipping from two centers mixes two freight rates.

The **provisional** variant answers "what is this campaign costing us per kit we have committed to?" and is refreshed on every allocation and every cancellation. Its population is every shipment of the campaign — that is, every shipment whose enrollment belongs to the campaign — whose `status` is `ALLOCATED`, `SHIPPED`, `DELIVERED` or `LOST`; `CANCELLED` shipments are excluded entirely. The numerator sums, once per shipment in that population, the campaign's `kit_assembly_unit_cost_cents` plus that shipment's freight, and it ignores `surcharge_cents` and `write_off_cost_cents` altogether. The result is that sum divided by the count of shipments in the population, rounded half-up to a whole cent in a **single** division at the end. When the population is empty the value is zero rather than undefined.

The **final** variant answers "what did each kit that actually reached a panelist cost us, all in?" and is refreshed on delivery, on loss, on write-off, on opening the review window and on close. Its numerator population is the campaign's shipments whose `status` is `DELIVERED` or `LOST`; the numerator sums, per shipment, the campaign's `kit_assembly_unit_cost_cents` plus that shipment's freight plus that shipment's `surcharge_cents`, and then adds the campaign's whole `write_off_cost_cents` once. Its denominator counts only the `DELIVERED` shipments. A `LOST` shipment therefore stays in the numerator and leaves the denominator: its cost was really incurred but it delivered nothing. When there are no delivered shipments the value is zero. For any given shipment set, the final figure is strictly larger than the provisional one whenever anything was lost, surcharged or written off, and the gap between the two figures is exactly the money the programme spent without putting product in a panelist's hands.

A dedicated cost read exposes both figures side by side together with the budget triple, returning `campaign_id`, `kit_assembly_unit_cost_cents`, `cost_per_sample_provisional_cents`, `cost_per_sample_final_cents`, `budget_total_cents`, `budget_committed_cents`, `budget_available_cents`, `allocated_sample_count`, `delivered_sample_count` and `write_off_cost_cents`, so that the budget conservation identity of §2.7 can be checked directly against the cost figures derived from it.

### 3.7. Review Collection, Disclosure Policy and Moderation

A review is the deliverable a sampled panelist owes back, and it belongs to their enrollment rather than to the campaign directly. The panelist drafts one by naming the `enrollment_id`, a `headline` of at most 120 characters, the `body_text` of the review, and a `star_rating` from 1 through 5. Two further fields are optional at draft time and mandatory in substance at submission time: `disclosure_statement`, the incentivized-review disclosure, which defaults to unset; and `honest_opinion_attested`, the panelist's attestation that the opinion is their own, which defaults to false. Drafting deliberately enforces almost nothing beyond field validity, ownership and the campaign being in `SAMPLING_ACTIVE` or `REVIEW_WINDOW` — **obligation eligibility is checked at submission, not at drafting**, so a panelist may compose a review before their sample is confirmed delivered. The draft is created in `DRAFT` with `revision_count` at zero, `first_submitted_at` unset, and both moderation audit fields unset.

`word_count` is derived, never supplied. It is the number of non-empty tokens obtained by splitting `body_text` on any run of whitespace, and it is recomputed from scratch whenever `body_text` changes — at draft creation and again on every revision. Leading, trailing and repeated whitespace therefore contribute nothing.

Submission moves the review `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is where the disclosure regime bites. Beyond the ladder's earlier rungs — the review must be `DRAFT` (`ERR_INVALID_REVIEW_STATE`), the caller must own it (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`), the panelist must not be `BANNED` (`ERR_PANELIST_BANNED`) and the campaign must be `SAMPLING_ACTIVE` or `REVIEW_WINDOW` (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_ACCEPTING_ENROLLMENTS`) — four content gates apply in this pinned order:

1. the enrollment's `obligation_status` is `REVIEW_DUE`, otherwise `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_REVIEW`, which is what stops a panelist reviewing a sample that was never delivered;
2. `honest_opinion_attested` is true, otherwise `ERR_HONEST_OPINION_NOT_ATTESTED`;
3. `disclosure_statement` is present and contains one of the two accepted disclosure phrases — "i received this product for free" or "sample provided by" — matched case-insensitively as a substring, otherwise `ERR_DISCLOSURE_STATEMENT_MISSING`; either phrase satisfies this gate;
4. `word_count` is at least the campaign's `min_review_word_count`, otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_TOO_SHORT`. The comparison is inclusive: a review whose word count exactly equals the campaign floor passes.

A first submission stamps `first_submitted_at` with the logical clock's current instant, and only a first submission does so — every later submission of the same review leaves that timestamp exactly as it was, which is what makes it a stable anchor for the audit variant's review-velocity window in §3.3. Submission increases the review's `version` by 1.

Moderation belongs to `ComplianceModerator` and takes a `decision` that is exactly one of `APPROVE`, `REQUEST_CHANGES` or `REJECT_DISCLOSURE`, plus an optional `moderator_note`. All three branches require the review to be `SUBMITTED` and all three record the note, writing it as `null` when the field is omitted, so a note left off a later decision erases whatever an earlier decision recorded rather than preserving it. Their side effects are deliberately disjoint.

- `APPROVE` moves the review `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, records `approved_by_user_id` from the acting moderator's `X-User-ID`, increases the campaign's `approved_review_count` by 1, and advances the enrollment's obligation axis `REVIEW_DUE → REVIEW_FULFILLED`, discharging the panelist's obligation. Approval is barred for the moderator who previously requested changes on this same review: when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the stored `changes_requested_by_user_id`, the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and a different moderator must take the decision.
- `REQUEST_CHANGES` moves the review `SUBMITTED → CHANGES_REQUESTED` and records `changes_requested_by_user_id` from the acting moderator. It touches no campaign counter, no posture and no obligation axis.
- `REJECT_DISCLOSURE` moves the review `SUBMITTED → DISCLOSURE_REJECTED`, which is terminal, increases the campaign's `disclosure_rejected_review_count` by 1 and re-evaluates `compliance_posture`. It is the branch for content whose disclosure is inadequate in substance even though it cleared the mechanical phrase check, and it is the only moderation branch that can move the campaign's posture. Note the asymmetry: approval moves an obligation axis and a counter but never the posture, rejection moves a counter and the posture but no obligation axis, and requesting changes moves neither.

Revision closes the loop. The owning panelist revises a review that is `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, supplying a fresh `headline`, `body_text`, `star_rating` and optionally `disclosure_statement` and `honest_opinion_attested`; the supplied content replaces the stored content wholesale, `word_count` is recomputed from the new `body_text`, `revision_count` increases by 1, `first_submitted_at` is left untouched, and the review returns to `SUBMITTED` with its `version` increased by 1. Omitting `disclosure_statement` or `honest_opinion_attested` leaves the stored value standing, whereas sending either as an explicit JSON `null` falls back to its declared default — an unset disclosure and a false attestation — which the gates below will then reject. The revision must clear the same content gates as a submission, in the same pinned order: the honest-opinion attestation first, then the disclosure phrase, then the word count. It does not re-check the obligation axis, so a revision lands even on an enrollment whose obligation has since moved off `REVIEW_DUE`. The cycle `SUBMITTED → CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` is repeatable without limit — a moderator may ask for changes as many times as the content warrants — and each pass through it leaves `revision_count` one higher while `first_submitted_at` still records the very first submission.

### 3.8. Retailer Syndication and Takedown

Reviews earn their keep by appearing on retailer product pages, and every retailer imposes its own publication policy. A retailer is registered with a `name` and four policy fields, all optional with defaults: `feed_status`, either `ACCEPTING` or `PAUSED` and defaulting to `ACCEPTING`; `requires_disclosure_badge`, defaulting to true, meaning this retailer insists on the badge wording rather than any disclosure; `min_star_rating_accepted`, from 1 through 5 and defaulting to 1, the lowest rating the feed will carry; and `max_reviews_per_panelist_per_month`, at least 1 and defaulting to 1, the retailer's anti-astroturfing rate limit on how often one panelist may appear.

Syndication proceeds in two steps, and the split is what makes retailer-side rejection observable rather than silent. First a `CampaignManager` or `FulfillmentOps` queues a record by naming the `review_submission_id` and the `retailer_id`. Queueing requires that the review is `APPROVED` (`ERR_INVALID_REVIEW_STATE`), that the panelist behind it is neither `BANNED` (`ERR_PANELIST_BANNED`) nor `FLAGGED` (`ERR_PANELIST_INTEGRITY_HOLD`), that the campaign's `compliance_posture` is neither `FROZEN` (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_FROZEN`) nor `RESTRICTED` (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`), that the named retailer is one of the owning campaign's cleared `syndication_retailer_ids` (`ERR_RETAILER_NOT_IN_CAMPAIGN`), that the retailer's `feed_status` is `ACCEPTING` (`ERR_RETAILER_FEED_PAUSED`), and that no record already exists for this same review-and-retailer pair in any `status` other than `REJECTED` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SYNDICATION_TARGET`). A rejected attempt may be re-queued after the underlying problem is fixed; a queued, syndicated or taken-down one may not be duplicated. The record is created `QUEUED` with `rejection_reason`, `syndicated_at` and `takedown_requested_at` all unset.

Dispatch then hands the queued record to the retailer's feed, and `FulfillmentOps` performs it. The record must be `QUEUED` (`ERR_INVALID_SYNDICATION_STATE`), the review must still be `APPROVED` (`ERR_INVALID_REVIEW_STATE`), the campaign posture must still be `OPEN` — `RESTRICTED` reports `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` and `FROZEN` reports `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_FROZEN` — and the retailer's `feed_status` must still be `ACCEPTING` (`ERR_RETAILER_FEED_PAUSED`). Past those gates the retailer's own three publication rules are evaluated **in this pinned order, with the first failure deciding the outcome and no later rule evaluated**:

1. the review's `star_rating` is below the retailer's `min_star_rating_accepted` — reason `BELOW_MIN_RATING`;
2. the retailer's `requires_disclosure_badge` is true and the review's `disclosure_statement` is either unset or does not contain the badge phrase "i received this product for free" as a case-insensitive substring — reason `DISCLOSURE_BADGE_MISSING`. **The retailer badge standard is stricter than the submission gate.** Submission accepts either accepted disclosure phrase, but a badge-requiring retailer accepts only the badge phrase, so a review that legitimately cleared submission with wording built on "sample provided by" still fails here;
3. the panelist has already appeared on this retailer's feed too often — reason `PANELIST_RATE_LIMIT`. The count is of syndication records for the same `retailer_id` whose `status` is `SYNDICATED` and whose underlying enrollment names the same panelist, restricted to those whose `syndicated_at` falls inside the trailing 30-day window ending at the logical clock's current instant, inclusive at both ends. Records in `REJECTED` or `TAKEDOWN_REQUESTED` are excluded from the count, so a taken-down placement frees the panelist's slot. The comparison is inclusive: when that count has already reached the retailer's `max_reviews_per_panelist_per_month`, the dispatch is rejected.

Dispatch always answers successfully at the HTTP level; what varies is which branch the record takes, and the two branches have disjoint side effects. When all three rules pass, the record moves `QUEUED → SYNDICATED`, `syndicated_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the campaign's `syndicated_review_count` increases by 1. When any rule fails, the record moves `QUEUED → REJECTED`, which is terminal, `rejection_reason` is set to the first failing rule's reason, and **no campaign counter moves at all** — a rejected placement is not a published one and must never inflate the campaign's syndication numbers.

A syndicated placement can also be pulled back. The move from `SYNDICATED` to the terminal `TAKEDOWN_REQUESTED` stamps `takedown_requested_at` and decreases the campaign's `syndicated_review_count` by 1, undoing exactly what dispatch added. As §2.11 states, it has no request of its own: it happens only as the final hop of the diversion-substantiation cascade, when the review behind it is retracted.

### 3.9. Diversion Monitoring, Conversion Matching and Lift Measurement

Free product attracts resellers, and a sampling programme that cannot police diversion cannot defend its numbers. A `ComplianceModerator` opens a case by naming the `panelist_id` under suspicion, the `sample_shipment_id` the suspicion attaches to, a `signal_type` that is exactly one of `RESALE_LISTING`, `ADDRESS_CLUSTER` or `BULK_REDEMPTION`, and an `evidence_note` recording what was observed. The case is created `OPEN` with `reopen_count` at zero, `substantiated_at` unset, and `opened_by_user_id` recorded from the opening moderator's `X-User-ID` — that recorded identifier is what the segregation-of-duties rule later reads. Opening is unconditional beyond field validity and the two foreign keys resolving: the named shipment need not belong to the named panelist, its `status` is not consulted, the panelist's `integrity_status` is not consulted — a `BANNED` panelist can be cased again — and nothing prevents any number of concurrent open cases against the same panelist or the same shipment.

A case has four states and its progression is deliberately narrow. Investigation moves it `OPEN → INVESTIGATING` and is the trigger for the audit fraud rescore: the panelist's `fraud_risk_score_audit` and `last_risk_scored_at` are refreshed, and a resulting score of at least 55.0 flags a `CLEAR` panelist. **The status write to `INVESTIGATING` happens before the rescore**, which means this very case is already counted by the audit variant's live-case term — moving a panelist's first case into investigation contributes 15 points to their own audit score in the same operation. Reopening moves the case `INVESTIGATING → OPEN` and increases `reopen_count` by 1; the cycle `OPEN → INVESTIGATING → OPEN` is repeatable, and the audit rescore re-runs on every re-entry into investigation. Dismissal moves the case `INVESTIGATING → DISMISSED`, which is terminal, and restores standing conditionally: when the panelist is currently `FLAGGED` and holds no other case in `INVESTIGATING` or `SUBSTANTIATED`, their `integrity_status` returns to `CLEAR` and their `version` increases by 1; a panelist with another live case stays flagged. Both investigation and reopening, and both dismissal and substantiation, require the case to be in the state their transition declares, and any mismatch is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DIVERSION_CASE_STATE`.

Substantiation moves the case `INVESTIGATING → SUBSTANTIATED`, which is terminal, and it is the heaviest single operation in the system. It is barred to the moderator who opened the case: when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the case's `opened_by_user_id`, the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, so opening and substantiating always require two different people. The operation stamps `substantiated_at` from the logical clock, and it produces one direct effect plus the four-hop cascade described in §2.11. The direct effect is on the campaign of the shipment's enrollment: its `substantiated_diversion_count` increases by 1 and its `compliance_posture` is re-evaluated. The cascade then runs: the panelist's `diversion_strikes` increases by 1 and their `integrity_status` becomes `BANNED`; because they are banned, every enrollment of theirs whose `screening_status` is `PENDING_SCREEN` or `SCREEN_PASSED` moves to the terminal `SCREEN_REVOKED` and its `obligation_status` becomes `FORFEITED` unless it is already `REVIEW_FULFILLED`; because those enrollments are revoked, every review of theirs in `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` moves to the terminal `RETRACTED`, with the campaign's `approved_review_count` decreasing by 1 for each review that was `APPROVED`; and because those reviews are retracted, every `SYNDICATED` syndication record of theirs moves to `TAKEDOWN_REQUESTED`, with the campaign's `syndicated_review_count` decreasing by 1 for each. The chain reaches from a single compliance decision all the way out to content already live on a retailer's site, and each hop is caused by the previous hop's mutation rather than by the substantiation request itself — an enrollment already `REVIEW_FULFILLED` keeps its fulfilled obligation, and a review already `DISCLOSURE_REJECTED` or `RETRACTED` is not touched again.

Conversion evidence arrives from outside. A `PlatformAdmin` ingests one matched purchase at a time, naming the `enrollment_id` the purchase is attributed to, a `retailer_channel_code` identifying the sales channel it came from, a `purchase_amount_cents` of zero or more, a `purchased_at` instant, and a `match_confidence` between 0 and 1 inclusive with at most four decimal places expressing how confident the matching process is that this purchase belongs to this panelist. The purchase must fall inside the campaign's conversion window — from the campaign's `sampling_window_start` through its `sampling_window_end` plus 30 days, inclusive at both ends — and one outside it is refused with `ERR_PURCHASE_MATCH_OUT_OF_WINDOW`. The stored `qualified` flag is derived, not supplied: it is true when the `match_confidence` is at least 0.8 and the `purchased_at` instant lies inside that same conversion window. The 0.8 floor is inclusive, so a match at exactly 0.8 qualifies. As §2.4 notes, a matched purchase is an immutable feed record: it carries `id` and `created_at` and neither an `updated_at` nor a `version`.

Lift measurement is the campaign's final accounting, and a `CampaignManager` requests it by naming the `campaign_id`, the `variant` to measure under, and `expected_campaign_version` as the optimistic-concurrency token of §2.8. The campaign must be in `REVIEW_WINDOW` or `CLOSED`, since measuring a campaign that is still sampling would count conversions that have not finished arriving.

The comparison is always between a treated group and the withheld holdout group. The **holdout group** is every enrollment of the campaign whose `holdout_flag` is true and whose `screening_status` is `SCREEN_PASSED`; its size is recorded as `holdout_count`. The **treated group** depends on the variant, and this is the whole point of having two.

- Under `ITT` — intent-to-treat — the treated group is every enrollment of the campaign whose `holdout_flag` is false and whose `screening_status` is `SCREEN_PASSED`, whether or not a sample ever reached them. It measures the effect of the decision to sample.
- Under `PER_PROTOCOL` the treated group is restricted further to those enrollments that have at least one shipment in `DELIVERED`. It measures the effect of actually receiving a sample.

Because the per-protocol denominator is a subset of the intent-to-treat one while the same matched purchases are counted, the same underlying data yields a different `treated_conversion_rate` and a different `lift_pct` under the two variants; the treated group's size is recorded as `treated_count`. An enrollment counts as converted when it has at least one matched purchase whose `match_confidence` is at least 0.8 and whose `purchased_at` falls inside the campaign's conversion window, and the converted tallies are recorded as `treated_converted_count` and `holdout_converted_count`. `treated_conversion_rate` is the treated converted count divided by `treated_count`, rounded half-up to four decimal places, and zero when the treated group is empty; `holdout_conversion_rate` is computed the same way against the holdout group. `lift_pct` is 100 multiplied by the difference between the two rates — treated minus holdout — rounded half-up to two decimal places, and it is legitimately negative when the holdout group converted better than the treated group. `power_status` is `INSUFFICIENT_POWER` when the holdout group is empty or the treated group holds fewer than 3 enrollments, and `MEASURED` otherwise; an underpowered analysis still reports all of its numbers, and the status is the caveat attached to them rather than a refusal to compute.

The cost side of the analysis charges diversion back against the programme, and its evaluation order is pinned because the figures look circular: the penalty depends on a rate, the rate depends on a tier of the cost per sample, and the adjusted cost per sample depends on the penalty. **The following order is the only valid reading.** First, `diverted_unit_count` is taken as the campaign's `substantiated_diversion_count` and `effective_sample_count` as the campaign's `delivered_sample_count` less that diverted count — the samples that actually did their job. Step one computes `base_cost_per_sample_cents`, the **pre-penalty** figure, as the campaign's `budget_committed_cents` divided by `effective_sample_count`, rounded half-up to a whole cent, and zero whenever `effective_sample_count` is zero or negative. Step two selects `penalty_rate_pct` **from that step-one figure and from nothing else**: 10 percent when the base cost per sample is below 500 cents, 20 percent when it is below 1500 cents, and 35 percent at 1500 cents or above. Step three computes `diversion_penalty_cents` as the base cost per sample multiplied by the penalty rate as a percentage and multiplied by `diverted_unit_count`, rounded half-up to a whole cent. Step four computes `adjusted_cost_per_sample_cents` as the campaign's `budget_committed_cents` plus `diversion_penalty_cents`, divided by `effective_sample_count` and rounded half-up to a whole cent, and zero when `effective_sample_count` is zero or negative. **Re-deriving the tier from the step-four figure is forbidden**: a campaign whose base cost sits just below a tier boundary and whose adjusted cost lands above it keeps the lower rate, because the tier was fixed at step two and the penalty is charged once.

Each analysis is a dated snapshot rather than a mutable record, so a new one retires its predecessors: every existing analysis of the same campaign whose `superseded` is false has it set to true before the new row is stored with `superseded` false and `computed_at` stamped from the logical clock. Requesting both variants in turn therefore leaves exactly one non-superseded analysis — the most recent one — while the earlier rows remain readable as history. The request also increases the campaign's `version` by 1, which is what makes a second request carrying the same `expected_campaign_version` fail with `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint listed below is served under the API root, and every one of them follows the envelope, pagination, validation-order and auditing rules of §2. An operation that brings a new resource into existence answers with HTTP 201 and returns the created resource; an operation that modifies or transitions an existing resource answers with HTTP 200 and returns the updated resource; every read answers with HTTP 200. The logical-clock endpoint returns 200 with the clock's new value. Error responses use the status code catalogued in §5 for the error the ladder in §2.5 selects.
