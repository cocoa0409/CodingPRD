# Defensible Space & Fuels Inspection API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A fire protection district operating in the wildland urban interface (WUI) carries a statutory obligation that no spreadsheet survives: it must know, parcel by parcel, how much fuel sits within striking distance of a habitable structure, whether the property owner has cleared it, and — when the owner has not — it must be able to prove that lawful notice was served before the district sends a contractor onto private land and bills the owner for the work. Today that obligation is discharged across disconnected tools. Inspection routes live in one inspector's notebook, compliance deadlines are computed by hand from mailing dates, abatement invoices are reconciled in a general ledger that has never heard of a defensible-space zone, and the insurance agent who calls asking whether 1420 Ridgeline Road is compliant gets an answer that is accurate only as of whenever someone last looked.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces that patchwork. The service owns a parcel inventory scored for wildfire risk and ranked into an inspection priority queue; a scheduling layer that pairs certified inspectors with parcels while honouring owner-notice lead times and access arrangements; a field-evaluation model that grades each visit against the three defensible-space zones and against a checklist of structure-hardening elements; an enforcement chain that runs from violation notice through compliance deadline, extension, abatement authority, contractor award, cost recovery and — where an owner never pays — a recorded lien; and a set of carrot-side programs (chipper days, curbside green-waste collection, and cost-share grants) that the district funds to make compliance affordable. Sitting above all of it are the two reporting surfaces the district is actually judged on: neighbourhood-level certification, and the correlation between a parcel's compliance posture and whether its structure survived a real incident.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read as load-bearing. First, **everything temporal is evaluated against a logical clock that the service itself holds**, not against wall time, because a compliance deadline that shifts under a test is a compliance deadline the district cannot defend. Second, **money and reserved capacity are conserved quantities**: every cent of a cost-recovery invoice is accounted for as paid, liened, or outstanding, and every dollar of grant budget is either unreserved or attached to exactly one enrollment. Implementations that treat these counters as derived-on-read conveniences rather than as invariants maintained on every mutation will diverge from this specification in ways the district's auditors notice.

All business resources described here are mounted under the `/fire-district` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller acts. Both must be present and non-blank on every endpoint; a request missing either header, carrying a blank value, or naming a role outside the seven declared below is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A syntactically valid role that is simply not permitted to invoke the requested operation is a different failure and is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `FireMarshal`, `Inspector`, `PropertyOwner`, `Contractor`, `CommunityOrganizer` and `TitleAgent`.

`ProgramAdmin` is district defensible-space program staff and owns the widest surface: the parcel inventory, cycle operation, inspector and contractor registration, inspection scheduling and noticing, support-program administration, certificate adjudication, and the logical clock. `FireMarshal` is the statutory abatement authority — it adjudicates community certification, voids notices, authorizes abatement, awards and cancels orders — and deliberately **cannot** schedule, notice, complete or verify inspections. `Inspector` is the field inspector who records access denials, zone measurements and hardening findings, closes out inspections, and verifies contractor abatement work. `PropertyOwner` is the parcel owner, who requests compliance certificates, enrolls in support programs, cancels its own enrollments, requests deadline extensions, and pays cost-recovery invoices. `Contractor` is a licensed abatement contractor reporting completed work on its own awarded orders. `CommunityOrganizer` is the volunteer neighbourhood organizer who submits and reopens a community certification application. `TitleAgent` is an insurer, lender or escrow agent that is read-only apart from opening a certificate request.

Four of the seven roles are **entity-backed**, which means `X-User-ID` is a real primary key and ownership is checked against it:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Inspector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Inspector`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Inspector` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — concretely, an inspection's `inspector_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PropertyOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Parcel`; ownership checks compare the resource's `parcel_id` to `X-User-ID`, either directly or transitively (a violation notice's `parcel_id`, a support enrollment's `parcel_id`, or — for an abatement order — the `parcel_id` of the violation notice the order was raised under).
- When `X-User-Role` is `Contractor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Contractor`; ownership checks compare an abatement order's `contractor_id` to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `CommunityOrganizer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Community`; ownership checks compare the target community's `id` to `X-User-ID`.

The remaining three roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `FireMarshal` or `TitleAgent`, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may read it — the segregation-of-duties rule described later compares a `FireMarshal`'s `X-User-ID` against a recorded `verified_by_inspector_id`, for instance — but it never resolves it to a stored row's `id` and never requires it to exist.

Ownership enforcement is a distinct failure from role membership. A caller whose role is permitted on the endpoint but whose `X-User-ID` does not match the resource it is reaching for is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This check guards twelve operations: recording an access denial, recording a zone evaluation, recording a hardening finding, completing an inspection, extending a violation notice, reporting completed abatement work, recording a cost-recovery payment, creating a support enrollment, cancelling a support enrollment, opening a compliance-certificate request on a parcel, submitting a community certification, and reopening a community for organizing.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position and defaults to 0; it may not be negative. A `limit` below 1, a `limit` above 100, or a negative `offset` is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states a different ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending — oldest record first. The single documented exception is the cycle priority queue, which is ordered by ascending inspection priority rank so that the highest-risk parcel appears first.

List responses populate four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used to build the page, `total_count` reports the number of records matching the filter before pagination was applied, and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every resource is identified by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded semantics; clients must treat them as opaque strings and must never attempt to derive one identifier from another.

Most resources also carry the full set of audit columns: `created_at`, recording when the row was first written; `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation; and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly 1 on every successful mutation of that resource. The `version` counter is the anchor of the stale-read protection described in §2.8, and it also advances when a resource is mutated as a *consequence* of an operation aimed at some other resource — a parcel whose compliance posture changes because a notice was issued against it has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed just as surely as if it had been edited directly.

Three record kinds are immutable append-only evidence and deliberately do **not** carry the full audit set. A zone evaluation, a structure-hardening finding, and a cost-recovery payment each carry only `id` and `created_at`: they have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, because nothing in this specification ever edits one after it is written. Every other resource kind — inspection cycles, communities, parcels, inspectors, contractors, inspections, violation notices, abatement orders, support programs, support enrollments and compliance certificates — carries `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

All timestamps, whether audit columns or domain instants, are ISO 8601 UTC strings with exactly three fractional-second digits, for example `2026-08-14T09:30:00.000Z`. The one systematic exception is a class of *end-of-day* domain deadlines — compliance deadlines, invoice due dates and certificate expiries — which by construction always carry the time component 23:59:59 UTC with zero fractional seconds.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The service does not read wall-clock time. Instead it maintains a **logical clock** that begins at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` on a freshly started server, and every deadline comparison, window test, expiry check and staleness measurement in this document is evaluated against that clock. An administrative capability, available to `ProgramAdmin` only, sets the clock to a supplied instant carried in the request as `to_iso8601`; the response reports the new value as `current_utc`. Setting the clock may move it forward or backward and performs no state transitions of its own — no notice becomes overdue and no certificate expires merely because the clock moved. Time-driven consequences are applied only by the cycle-advance checkpoint described in §3.1.

### 2.6. Resource Resolution and Reference Integrity

Path segments are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. A request naming an inspection under a parcel that does not exist reports the missing parcel, not the missing inspection. Once every path segment resolves, foreign keys supplied in the request body are validated in a fixed declaration order: `cycle_id`, then `parcel_id`, then `community_id`, then `inspector_id`, then `contractor_id`, then `inspection_id`, then `violation_notice_id`, then `support_program_id`. Each missing referent has its own error identifier and all of them answer with HTTP 404: `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARCEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMUNITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ZONE_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ABATEMENT_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`. The path variables the reader will encounter are `cycle_id`, `community_id`, `parcel_id`, `inspector_id`, `contractor_id`, `inspection_id`, `zone_evaluation_id`, `hardening_finding_id`, `notice_id`, `order_id`, `payment_id`, `support_program_id`, `enrollment_id` and `certificate_id`; `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND` additionally covers a `supersedes_notice_id` filter value that names no notice.

### 2.7. Precision, Rounding and Money

Every rounding operation in this specification uses **round half up**: a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero. There is no banker's rounding anywhere in the system.

Scores and percentages are carried to **2 decimal places**. This covers `risk_score`, `compliance_rate_pct`, `measured_veg_points`, `hardening_credit_points`, the per-zone `points`, and the survival-correlation percentages. Two quantities are exceptions with their own precision: a zone's `required_clearance_ft` and `deficit_ft` are carried to **1 decimal place**, and a zone's `deficit_ratio` is carried to **4 decimal places** because it is the input to a banded severity comparison where the fourth digit decides the band.

All monetary values are integers denominated in **whole cents** — there are no fractional cents anywhere in the system, and every field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer. Where a monetary value is produced by multiplication, the product is rounded half up to a whole cent **at the step in which it appears**, before it participates in any later sum. This "round-per-line, then add" discipline is not cosmetic: rounding each line independently and summing the rounded lines can differ by a cent or two from summing the raw products and rounding once, and the former is what this specification requires. Subsidy and match fractions such as `subsidy_pct` and `bonus_pct` are exact two-decimal fractions.

Crew-hour quantities — `crew_hours`, `actual_crew_hours`, `committed_crew_hours` and `completed_crew_hours` — are decimal numbers carried to one decimal place, and the two client-supplied ones must be strictly positive multiples of one half hour. Green-waste tonnage in `green_waste_tons` is a non-negative number with at most 2 decimal places.

### 2.8. Stale-Read Protection on Parcel and Program Writes

Two write paths in this system read a parent record, make a decision on the strength of what they read, and then mutate both parent and child. Because both writes are legitimately *repeatable* — a second violation notice on the same parcel is permitted by the supersession rule, and a second enrollment in the same program is permitted for a different parcel — a plain state precondition cannot protect them. Instead each requires the client to declare the parent revision it based its decision on, using optimistic concurrency control.

**Issuing a discretionary violation notice against a parcel** requires a non-optional `expected_parcel_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Parcel`. If that value does not equal the parcel's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARCEL_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no notice is created. The rule exists because a discretionary notice both voids any still-open notice on the parcel and rewrites `open_violation_count` and `compliance_status`; a client working from a parcel snapshot that another writer has already superseded would otherwise overwrite a picture it never observed. The same `expected_parcel_version` field, with the same rejection code and status, is required when updating a parcel's attributes.

**Creating a support enrollment** requires a non-optional `expected_program_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `SupportProgram`. A mismatch with the program's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Enrollment admission reads `budget_remaining_cents` and `reserved_slots`, both of which any prior enrollment writer will have moved; without the version check a later client would be admitted against budget that is already reserved.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, none of the inspection, notice, abatement-order, certificate or community transitions accept `expected_parcel_version`, `expected_program_version`, or any other expected-version input; those operations are protected by state preconditions instead.

### 2.9. The Request-Screening Ladder

Requests are screened by a fixed ladder of gates, and the ladder is **strictly ordered**: the first gate that fails determines the response, and no later gate is evaluated. This matters constantly in practice. An unlicensed contractor named on an order that is not in the awardable state answers with the state error, not the licensing error, because the state gate sits higher. A stale parcel version on a request whose enum values are also malformed answers with the malformed-request error, because request-shape screening sits above concurrency screening. Implementations that evaluate these checks in a different order will return defensible-looking but wrong error identifiers.

The gates, in ascending order of evaluation:

1. **Authentication** — both auth headers present, and `X-User-Role` one of the seven declared roles. Applies to every endpoint. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role membership** — the acting role appears in the target endpoint's authorized-role list in §4. Applies to every endpoint. Failure: `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. **Existence** — every path referent resolves left to right, then every body foreign key resolves in the declaration order given in §2.6. Applies to every endpoint. Failure: the matching not-found identifier (404).
4. **Resource ownership** — for entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` matches the owning key as described in §2.1. Applies to the twelve owner-scoped operations listed in §2.1. Failure: `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
5. **Request shape** — required body fields present and non-null; enum fields carrying declared members only; numeric fields inside their declared ranges (`slope_pct` between 0 and 100 inclusive, `crew_hours` and `actual_crew_hours` positive multiples of 0.5, `amount_cents` at least 1); datetimes parsing as ISO 8601 UTC. Applies to every endpoint. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
6. **Window orientation** — `scheduled_end_utc` strictly later than `scheduled_start_utc` on inspection scheduling and rescheduling, `work_end_utc` strictly later than `work_start_utc` on abatement award, and `service_window_end_utc` strictly later than `service_window_start_utc` on support-program creation. An end equal to its start is a violation. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` (422).
7. **Cost-share invoice presence** — `vendor_invoice_cents` must be present when the target program's `program_type` is `COST_SHARE_GRANT` and must be absent otherwise. Applies to support-enrollment creation. Failure: `ERR_MISSING_VENDOR_INVOICE` (422).
8. **Natural-key uniqueness** — `Parcel.apn`, `Contractor.business_name`, `Community.name` and `InspectionCycle.season_year` are each unique across their collection. Applies to parcel creation, contractor creation, community creation and inspection-cycle creation. Failure: `ERR_APN_ALREADY_REGISTERED` (409) for all four collections.
9. **Optimistic concurrency** — `expected_parcel_version` matches the current `Parcel.version` on parcel update and on discretionary notice issuance; `expected_program_version` matches the current `SupportProgram.version` on enrollment creation. Failure: `ERR_STALE_PARCEL_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (412).
10. **State gate** — the target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, including any attempt to leave a terminal state. Applies to every state-changing endpoint. The general failure is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), with five specialised identifiers at the same rung: `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_OVERDUE` when abatement authority is exercised on a notice that has not lapsed, `ERR_CYCLE_ALREADY_ACTIVE` when a second cycle activation is attempted, `ERR_ZONE_ALREADY_EVALUATED` when a zone already carries an evaluation on the same inspection, `ERR_ACCESS_DENIAL_LIMIT_REACHED` on a third denial attempt, and `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT` for a repeat non-cancelled enrollment of the same parcel in the same program.
11. **Cycle activity** — the governing inspection cycle has status `ACTIVE`. Applies to nine operations: inspection scheduling, owner-notice service, zone-evaluation recording, hardening-finding recording, inspection completion, support-program creation, support-enrollment creation, cycle advance and cycle close. Failure: `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
12. **Service window open** — the logical clock lies within the program's half-open service window, at or after `service_window_start_utc` and strictly before `service_window_end_utc`. Applies to support-enrollment creation. Failure: `ERR_PROGRAM_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409).
13. **Inspector certification** — a parcel whose `hazard_zone` is `VERY_HIGH` may only be inspected by an inspector whose `certification_level` is `WUI_SPECIALIST`. Applies to inspection scheduling and rescheduling. Failure: `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED` (422).
14. **Inspector standing** — the assigned or acting inspector's `status` is `ACTIVE`. Applies to inspection scheduling, inspection rescheduling and abatement verification. Failure: `ERR_INSPECTOR_SUSPENDED` (409).
15. **Segregation of duties** — the inspector verifying an abatement order's completion must not be the inspector recorded on the inspection that substantiated that order's violation notice. Applies to abatement verification. Failure: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
16. **Notice lead time** — at least `notice_lead_days` whole days separate the logical clock from `scheduled_start_utc` when the owner notice is served. Applies to owner-notice service. Failure: `ERR_NOTICE_LEAD_TIME_VIOLATION` (422).
17. **Zone coverage and result agreement** — completion requires exactly one zone evaluation for each of `ZONE_0_EMBER`, `ZONE_1` and `ZONE_2` on the inspection, and the submitted `result` must agree with the findings. Applies to inspection completion. Failure: `ERR_ZONE_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE` (422).
18. **Certificate eligibility** — issuance requires the parcel's `compliance_status` to be `COMPLIANT` or `EXEMPT` and requires that no violation notice on the parcel is in `ISSUED`, `EXTENDED`, `OVERDUE` or `ABATEMENT_AUTHORIZED`. Applies to the certificate decision. Error selection is unambiguous: `ERR_PARCEL_NOT_INSPECTED` when `compliance_status` is `PENDING_INSPECTION`, and `ERR_OPEN_VIOLATION_EXISTS` in every other failing case (409).
19. **Community thresholds** — certification submission requires the member-parcel floor and the outreach floor; the certify decision additionally requires the community compliance rate floor. Applies to certification submission and the certification decision. Failure: `ERR_COMMUNITY_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET` (422).
20. **Inspector occupation conflict** — the new half-open inspection window must not intersect another live window of the same inspector. Applies to inspection scheduling and rescheduling. Failure: `ERR_INSPECTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
21. **Contractor occupation conflict** — the new closed work window must not intersect another live awarded window of the same contractor. Applies to abatement award. Failure: `ERR_CONTRACTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
22. **Contractor licensing and insurance** — `license_status` is `ACTIVE`, and `work_start_utc` falls strictly before `insurance_expiry_utc`. Applies to abatement award. Failure: `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_LICENSED` or `ERR_CONTRACTOR_INSURANCE_LAPSED` (409).
23. **Extension limit** — `extension_count` is below the single-extension ceiling. Applies to notice extension. Failure: `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_REACHED` (409).
24. **Contractor daily capacity** — no UTC calendar date covered by the new work window may push the contractor past `max_daily_orders` live orders. Applies to abatement award. Failure: `ERR_CONTRACTOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
25. **Abatement estimate cap** — `estimated_cost_cents` may not exceed the district's per-order abatement ceiling. Applies to abatement authorization. Failure: `ERR_ABATEMENT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422).
26. **Cost-share invoice cap** — `vendor_invoice_cents` may not exceed the district's cost-share invoice ceiling. Applies to support-enrollment creation. Failure: `ERR_COST_SHARE_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422).
27. **Payment ceiling** — `amount_cents` may not exceed the order's outstanding balance. Applies to cost-recovery payment recording. Failure: `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING` (422).

Note the deliberate interaction between rungs 10 and 23. The extension operation accepts **both** an `ISSUED` and an already-`EXTENDED` notice as legitimate from-states, so an already-extended notice clears the state gate at rung 10 and is refused further down at rung 23 with `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_REACHED` rather than with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. When that refusal happens, `deadline_utc` and `extension_count` are left exactly as they were.

### 2.10. Conserved Quantities and Cascade Discipline

Several counters in this system are **conservation identities** rather than convenience fields, and they must hold after every single mutation — not merely eventually.

An inspector's `assigned_open_count` must always equal the number of inspections carrying that `inspector_id` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `NOTICED` or `ACCESS_DENIED`. A contractor's `committed_crew_hours` must always equal the sum of `crew_hours` across that contractor's orders whose `status` is `AWARDED` or `WORK_COMPLETE`; its `completed_crew_hours` is a lifetime tally that only ever grows. A support program's `budget_remaining_cents` must always equal `budget_total_cents` minus the sum of `reserved_amount_cents` over that program's enrollments whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `COMPLETED` or `REIMBURSED` — enrollments in `WAITLISTED`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing. A program's `reserved_slots` counts enrollments in `APPROVED`, `COMPLETED` or `REIMBURSED`. A parcel's `open_violation_count` counts that parcel's notices whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `EXTENDED`, `OVERDUE` or `ABATEMENT_AUTHORIZED`. For an invoiced abatement order, `total_recoverable_cents` always equals `paid_cents` plus `lien_amount_cents` plus the outstanding remainder.

Because these are identities, every **compensating** operation must restore the counter to exactly its pre-operation value, not approximately and not by recomputation drift. Cancelling an awarded order returns `committed_crew_hours` to what it was before the award. Cancelling an approved enrollment returns `budget_remaining_cents` to what it was before approval. Voiding a notice decrements `open_violation_count` by exactly 1.

Cascades in this system are **immediate and transitive**. When an operation changes a parcel's compliance posture, the community rate that reads that posture is recomputed in the same request, and a community whose recomputed rate falls below the lapse floor is transitioned in the same request. There is no deferred settlement, no background reconciliation, and no eventual consistency window. Each entity touched by a cascade has its own `version` incremented by 1 and its own `updated_at` refreshed — a single inspection completion can therefore advance the `version` of an inspection, a parcel, one or more violation notices, one or more compliance certificates, and a community.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Fire-Season Cycle and its Ops-Run Checkpoint

Everything the district does in the field happens inside a **fire-season inspection cycle**. A `ProgramAdmin` opens one by declaring the `season_year` it governs, which must be a whole year no earlier than 2020 and no later than 2100 and which no other cycle may already claim — a duplicate year is refused with `ERR_APN_ALREADY_REGISTERED`, the shared natural-key conflict identifier. Two policy dials are set at the same moment and govern the whole season. `notice_lead_days` is the minimum number of whole days that must separate an owner notice from the visit it announces, and it defaults to 10 when the request omits it. `invoice_due_days` is the number of whole days an owner has to settle an abatement invoice before the unpaid remainder becomes a lien, and it defaults to 45; it must be at least 1, whereas `notice_lead_days` may legitimately be 0 for a district that wants no lead-time floor at all. A newly created cycle opens in `PLANNING` with its `epoch` counter at 0 and its `activated_at_utc` and `closed_at_utc` both null.

A cycle becomes operational when a `ProgramAdmin` moves it `PLANNING → ACTIVE`, which stamps `activated_at_utc` with the logical clock. The district runs exactly one season at a time: if any other cycle is already `ACTIVE`, the activation is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_ALREADY_ACTIVE`, and a cycle that is not sitting in `PLANNING` cannot be activated at all. Field work is gated on this state — scheduling an inspection, serving its notice, recording zone or hardening findings, closing out an inspection, opening a support program and enrolling a parcel all require the governing cycle to be `ACTIVE`, and a request against a `PLANNING` or `CLOSED` cycle is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_ACTIVE`.

The most consequential operation in the whole system is the **cycle advance**, an operations checkpoint that a `ProgramAdmin` runs against an `ACTIVE` cycle to let the passage of logical time take effect. Nothing in this system expires on its own; deadlines lapse, liens attach, waitlists move and communities lose their certification only when an advance is run. The advance executes eight steps, strictly in this order, and later steps consume the results of earlier ones:

1. Every violation notice standing in `ISSUED` or `EXTENDED` whose `deadline_utc` is strictly earlier than the logical clock transitions to `OVERDUE`. This sweep is district-wide and does not care whether the cited parcel belongs to an organized community or to none.
2. Because notices became overdue in step 1, each affected parcel's `compliance_status` is set to `NON_COMPLIANT`; a parcel already carrying that posture simply stays there, and the write is idempotent, but its `updated_at` and `version` still advance.
3. Every abatement order standing in `INVOICED` whose `invoice_due_utc` is strictly earlier than the logical clock transitions `INVOICED → LIEN_FILED`, and its `lien_amount_cents` is set to the recoverable total less whatever has been paid.
4. For each support program belonging to this cycle, waitlisted enrollments are promoted in `created_at` ascending order for as long as the program has an unused slot and enough remaining budget to fund the candidate's full award; each promotion reserves that award against the program's budget, and the enrollments that remain waitlisted are renumbered from 1 in `created_at` ascending order.
5. Every parcel that is not exempt has its `inspection_priority_rank` recomputed from scratch.
6. Every community has its `compliance_rate_pct` recomputed under the community-scoped metric.
7. Because step 6 moved those rates, any community standing in `CERTIFIED` whose freshly recomputed rate has fallen below the lapse floor of 60.00 percent transitions `CERTIFIED → LAPSED` and has its `lapse_count` incremented by 1. A community whose recomputed rate is null — meaning it has no eligible member parcels at all — does not lapse.
8. Finally the cycle's `epoch` is incremented by exactly 1 and its `version` is incremented by 1.

Step 2 is a consequence of step 1 and step 7 is a consequence of step 6; an implementation that reorders them, or that recomputes community rates before expiring notices, will produce different `epoch`-to-`epoch` state than this specification requires. The `epoch` counter is the district's way of numbering checkpoints, and it is exposed on cycle reads precisely so that an operator can tell whether a given consequence has been applied yet.

Closing a season moves the cycle `ACTIVE → CLOSED` and stamps `closed_at_utc`. The close is refused while any inspection belonging to that cycle still stands in `SCHEDULED`, `NOTICED` or `ACCESS_DENIED`: the district will not close a season with unfinished field work on the books, and such an attempt answers with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. `CLOSED` is terminal — a closed cycle can never be reactivated, advanced or closed again.

Two read surfaces hang off the cycle. The **priority queue** returns the district's non-exempt parcels ordered by `inspection_priority_rank` ascending, so rank 1 — the parcel most urgently in need of a visit — appears first; it accepts `limit` and `offset` like any other list. The **district metrics** read computes its numbers fresh at read time rather than serving stored values, and returns `eligible_parcel_count`, `compliant_parcel_count`, `compliance_rate_pct`, `survived_compliant_pct`, `survived_noncompliant_pct`, `survival_delta_pp` and the cycle's current `epoch`.

### 3.2. The Parcel Inventory, Risk Classification and the Priority Queue

Parcel intake is a `ProgramAdmin` responsibility. Each parcel enters the inventory with its assessor parcel number in `apn` — non-empty and unique across the whole inventory, with a collision refused as `ERR_APN_ALREADY_REGISTERED` — together with the `owner_name` of record, the `owner_email` that will be used whenever a notice is served electronically, and the physical `situs_address`. A parcel that belongs to an organized neighbourhood carries that neighbourhood's identifier in `community_id`; parcels outside any organized community leave it null. `structure_present` records whether a habitable structure exists on the land and defaults to true when omitted. The recorded lot size goes into `lot_area_sqft`, which must be at least 1 square foot.

Five intake attributes drive the risk model. `roof_class` is the roof assembly's fire rating and is one of `CLASS_A`, `CLASS_B`, `CLASS_C` or `NONE`. `vegetation_type` is the dominant fuel model the owner reports and is one of `IRRIGATED_LANDSCAPE`, `GRASS`, `BRUSH` or `MIXED_CONIFER`. `slope_pct` is the average parcel slope expressed in percent, between 0 and 100 inclusive. `hazard_zone` is the state-mapped fire hazard severity zone and is one of `MODERATE`, `HIGH` or `VERY_HIGH`. `access_constraint` records the field-access impediment an inspector should expect and is one of `NONE`, `GATED`, `LOCKED_GATE` or `DOG_ON_SITE`. Two further flags matter later rather than now: `owner_hardship_flag`, which records an income or senior hardship determination and defaults to false, and `exemption_flag`, which records a district-granted program exemption and also defaults to false.

**Risk scoring.** A parcel's `risk_score` is a 0-to-100 figure assembled from four weighted subscores. The slope subscore is the parcel's `slope_pct` multiplied by two, capped at 100 — so a 60 percent slope scores the full 100 while a 30 percent slope scores 60. The vegetation subscore depends on the scoring variant and is discussed below. The structure subscore is read from the roof rating: a `CLASS_A` roof contributes 10 points, `CLASS_B` contributes 45, `CLASS_C` contributes 80, and an unrated roof (`NONE`) contributes the full 100. The access subscore is read from the access impediment: `NONE` contributes 0, `GATED` contributes 25, `DOG_ON_SITE` contributes 40, and `LOCKED_GATE` — the impediment that most often turns a visit into a wasted trip — contributes 60. These four subscores are combined into a weighted subtotal in which slope carries a weight of 0.30, vegetation carries 0.30, structure carries 0.25 and access carries 0.15.

The **order of the final two operations is pinned and is a frequent source of error**. From the weighted subtotal the system first *subtracts* the structure-hardening credit, and only then *multiplies* the difference by the hazard-zone multiplier — 1.00 for a `MODERATE` zone, 1.15 for `HIGH`, and 1.30 for `VERY_HIGH`. Multiplying first and subtracting the credit afterwards produces a different number and is incorrect. The product is then clamped so that it can never exceed 100.00, and the clamped value is rounded half up to two decimal places to give `risk_score`. Clamping happens before rounding, not after.

`risk_class` bands the resulting score, with every boundary inclusive at its lower edge: a score of 75.00 or above is `EXTREME`, a score of 55.00 or above but below 75.00 is `HIGH`, a score of 35.00 or above but below 55.00 is `MODERATE`, and anything below 35.00 is `LOW`. A score of exactly 55.00 is therefore `HIGH`, never `MODERATE`.

Two variants of this computation exist, and `risk_score_basis` records which one produced the current figure. The **intake-provisional** variant runs at parcel creation and on any attribute update that changes a scoring field while the parcel has not yet been verified in the field; it takes the vegetation subscore from the owner's self-reported `vegetation_type` — 15 points for `IRRIGATED_LANDSCAPE`, 40 for `GRASS`, 75 for `BRUSH` and 90 for `MIXED_CONIFER` — and it uses a hardening credit of exactly 0.00, because no structure-hardening findings exist yet. It sets `risk_score_basis` to `INTAKE_PROVISIONAL`. The **post-inspection-verified** variant runs whenever an inspection is closed out, whether the result was a pass or a failure; it replaces the self-reported vegetation subscore with the inspection's own `measured_veg_points`, and it computes a real hardening credit worth 4.00 points for each structure-hardening element the inspector found compliant on that inspection, capped at 20.00 points however many elements were checked. It sets `risk_score_basis` to `POST_INSPECTION_VERIFIED`. Only the findings attached to the inspection being completed enter the calculation; findings recorded on some earlier inspection of the same parcel are excluded. For identical parcel attributes the two variants routinely produce different scores and can land the parcel in different bands.

`compliance_status` is the parcel's derived defensible-space posture, initialised at intake to `PENDING_INSPECTION` and thereafter moving among `COMPLIANT`, `NON_COMPLIANT`, `ABATEMENT_PENDING` and `EXEMPT` as inspections, notices and abatement orders move. Alongside it the parcel keeps `open_violation_count`, initialised to 0, and `last_passed_inspection_at_utc`, which holds the completion instant of the most recent passing inspection on the parcel and stays null until one exists. `inspection_priority_rank` is null at intake and remains null until the first cycle advance ranks the inventory.

**Priority ranking.** The ranking considers only parcels whose `exemption_flag` is false; an exempt parcel is not ranked last, it is excluded from the ordering entirely and keeps `inspection_priority_rank` null. Candidates are ordered by four keys. The first key is the risk band, treated as an ordinal — `EXTREME` counts as 4, `HIGH` as 3, `MODERATE` as 2 and `LOW` as 1 — sorted from highest to lowest. The second key is `open_violation_count`, sorted from highest to lowest. The third key is the parcel's inspection staleness in whole days, sorted from highest to lowest: staleness is the number of whole days from `last_passed_inspection_at_utc` to the logical clock, computed by taking the elapsed seconds, dividing by the 86,400 seconds in a day and discarding any fractional remainder; a parcel that has never passed an inspection is treated as having a staleness of 100,000 days, which guarantees it sorts ahead of any parcel that has ever passed. The fourth and final key breaks any remaining tie by `created_at` ascending, oldest parcel first — the parcel's `id` is **never** used as a tie-break. Each candidate's `inspection_priority_rank` is its 1-based position in that ordering; ranks are consecutive integers starting at 1 with no gaps.

**Updating a parcel.** A `ProgramAdmin` may revise `access_constraint`, `roof_class`, `vegetation_type`, `slope_pct`, `hazard_zone`, `owner_email`, `community_id`, `owner_hardship_flag` and `exemption_flag`; every such request carries `expected_parcel_version` and is refused with `ERR_STALE_PARCEL_VERSION` when that value does not match the parcel's current `version`. When any scoring attribute changes, the score and band are recomputed — under the intake-provisional variant unless a completed inspection has already pinned the basis to `POST_INSPECTION_VERIFIED`, in which case the verified basis is retained. Setting `exemption_flag` to true has two immediate consequences beyond the flag itself: `compliance_status` becomes `EXEMPT` and `inspection_priority_rank` is cleared to null. Every successful update increments the parcel's `version` by 1.

**Recording an incident outcome.** After a real fire, a `ProgramAdmin` records what happened to the structure by supplying `incident_name`, an `outcome` of either `SURVIVED` or `DESTROYED`, and the `incident_date_utc` on which the incident occurred. These land on the parcel as `survival_incident_name`, `structure_survival_outcome` and `survival_incident_date_utc` respectively, all three null until an outcome is recorded, and the parcel's `version` advances by 1. The recorded outcome feeds the survival-correlation figures described in §3.9 and nothing else — it does not alter the parcel's compliance posture, its score or its rank. Parcel listings can be narrowed by `community_id`, by `compliance_status` and by `risk_class`, and paged with `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.3. The Inspector Roster and Booking a Visit

An inspector enters the roster with a `full_name` and a `certification_level` of `BASIC`, `ADVANCED` or `WUI_SPECIALIST`, plus a `status` that defaults to `ACTIVE` and may be set to `SUSPENDED`. Inspector reads expose `assigned_open_count`, which starts at 0 and thereafter tracks the inspector's live workload exactly as described in §2.10.

Booking a visit is a `ProgramAdmin` operation that names the `parcel_id` being inspected, the `cycle_id` under which the work is authorized, the `inspector_id` being assigned, an `inspection_type` of `INITIAL`, `REINSPECTION` or `COMPLAINT`, the `scheduled_start_utc` and `scheduled_end_utc` bounding the visit, and the `access_arrangement` agreed with the owner — `EXTERIOR_ONLY` where the inspector will work from outside the fence, `OWNER_PRESENT` where the owner will be on site to open up, or `GATE_CODE` where the owner has supplied a code. A newly booked inspection opens in `SCHEDULED` with `access_denial_count` at 0, `result`, `notice_method` and `notice_sent_at_utc` all null, and the assigned inspector's `assigned_open_count` incremented by 1. Note that `inspection_type` is what distinguishes a routine first look from the return visit that can lift a citation: only a `REINSPECTION` clears outstanding violation notices when it passes.

Three staffing constraints guard the booking, and they also guard rebooking. First, a parcel sitting in a `VERY_HIGH` hazard zone may only be assigned to an inspector holding `WUI_SPECIALIST` certification; a `BASIC` or `ADVANCED` inspector on such a parcel is refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED`. Second, a `SUSPENDED` inspector cannot be assigned at all, and the attempt answers `ERR_INSPECTOR_SUSPENDED`. Third, an inspector cannot be in two places at once: the requested window is treated as **half-open**, occupying every instant from `scheduled_start_utc` inclusive up to `scheduled_end_utc` exclusive, and it must not intersect the half-open window of any other inspection assigned to the same inspector that still stands in `SCHEDULED`, `NOTICED` or `ACCESS_DENIED`. Because the window is half-open, exact adjacency is fine — a new visit starting at the precise instant an existing one ends, or ending at the precise instant an existing one begins, does not conflict. Any real overlap is refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Inspections that have reached `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `ABANDONED` release their window and no longer block anything. Inspection listings can be filtered by `parcel_id`, by `cycle_id`, by `inspector_id` and by `status`.

### 3.4. Serving Notice, Denied Access, and Rebooking

California-style defensible-space enforcement rests on lawful notice, so an inspection cannot simply be walked. A `ProgramAdmin` serves the owner notice by declaring the `notice_method` used — `IN_PERSON`, `CERTIFIED_MAIL` or `EMAIL` — which advances the inspection `SCHEDULED → NOTICED` and stamps `notice_sent_at_utc` with the logical clock. The service is refused unless the whole days remaining between the logical clock and `scheduled_start_utc` are at least the governing cycle's `notice_lead_days`; falling short answers `ERR_NOTICE_LEAD_TIME_VIOLATION`. With the district default of 10 days, an inspector cannot be sent to a gate nine days after the letter went out.

Field reality intrudes next. When an inspector arrives and cannot get in — a locked gate nobody opened, a dog loose in the yard, an owner who refuses entry — the assigned inspector records the failed attempt with a `reason_code`. The acting inspector must be the one assigned to that inspection, and a different inspector's attempt is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. What the recorded denial does depends on how many have already accumulated, and the two outcomes are branches of the same operation selected purely by `access_denial_count`:

- **First denial.** With `access_denial_count` standing at 0, the count is incremented to 1 and the inspection moves `NOTICED → ACCESS_DENIED`. The visit is not dead; it is waiting to be rebooked.
- **Second denial.** With `access_denial_count` already standing at 1 — one short of the district's ceiling of two denials per inspection — the count is incremented to 2 and the inspection moves `NOTICED → ABANDONED` instead of to `ACCESS_DENIED`. Abandonment is not a neutral outcome: refusing the district twice is itself a violation. The system therefore raises a violation notice against `parcel_id` with `severity` forced to `CRITICAL` and `notice_method` forced to `CERTIFIED_MAIL`, resolving `supersedes_notice_id` exactly as the notice-creation rules in §3.6 prescribe. Because a notice now exists on the parcel, the parcel's `compliance_status` becomes `NON_COMPLIANT` and its `open_violation_count` rises by 1. The abandoned inspection also releases the assigned inspector's `assigned_open_count` by 1.

A third denial attempt has nowhere to go and is refused with `ERR_ACCESS_DENIAL_LIMIT_REACHED`, as is any denial recorded against an inspection already sitting in `ABANDONED`. `ABANDONED` is terminal.

Rebooking is how the access cycle closes. A `ProgramAdmin` rebooks an inspection standing in `ACCESS_DENIED` by supplying a fresh `scheduled_start_utc` and `scheduled_end_utc` and, optionally, a different `inspector_id` — a locked gate is often a good reason to send someone else. The inspection returns `ACCESS_DENIED → SCHEDULED`, its `notice_method` and `notice_sent_at_utc` are cleared back to null so that a fresh notice must be served before the new visit, and the new window is subjected to the same certification, standing and occupation checks the original booking faced. Where the inspector changed, the previous inspector's `assigned_open_count` decreases by 1 and the new inspector's increases by 1. Crucially, **`access_denial_count` is not reset** — the parcel that denied access once starts the next round with one denial already banked, so the very next denial abandons the inspection. Rebooking anything that is not in `ACCESS_DENIED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. This closes the loop `ACCESS_DENIED → SCHEDULED → NOTICED → ACCESS_DENIED`, which may run at most twice before abandonment ends it.

Cancellation is the escape hatch. A `ProgramAdmin` may cancel an inspection standing in either `SCHEDULED` or `NOTICED` by recording a `reason`, moving it `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` or `NOTICED → CANCELLED`. Cancellation releases the inspector's half-open window so that a later inspection may occupy it and decrements that inspector's `assigned_open_count` by 1. A `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `ABANDONED` inspection cannot be cancelled — all three are terminal — and the attempt answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.5. Field Evaluation: Zones, Hardening, and Closing Out the Visit

Once an inspector is on site, the evaluation is recorded finding by finding. Two kinds of finding exist, both created by the assigned inspector, both immutable once written, and both carrying only `id` and `created_at` for audit metadata.

**Zone evaluations.** Defensible space is regulated in three concentric bands, and the inspector records one measurement per band: `ZONE_0_EMBER` is the ember-resistant zone hugging the structure, `ZONE_1` is the lean-clean-and-green zone beyond it, and `ZONE_2` is the reduced-fuel zone reaching to the property line. A zone evaluation carries the `zone` it covers, the `measured_clearance_ft` the inspector actually paced out in feet (never negative), and `ladder_fuel_present`, a flag recording whether continuous ladder fuels were observed carrying flame from ground to canopy; it defaults to false. At most one evaluation may exist per zone per inspection, and a second attempt on a zone already measured is refused with `ERR_ZONE_ALREADY_EVALUATED`.

The system grades the measurement immediately. First it works out how much clearance the zone actually required, which depends on the parcel's slope: on gentle ground, where `slope_pct` is below 20, the slope factor is 1.00; on moderate ground, where `slope_pct` is at least 20 but below 40, the factor is 1.25; on steep ground, where `slope_pct` is 40 or more, the factor is 1.50. That factor multiplies the zone's base requirement — 5 feet for the ember zone, 30 feet for zone one, and 100 feet for zone two — and the product, rounded half up to one decimal place, becomes `required_clearance_ft`. A zone-two requirement on a 45 percent slope is therefore 150.0 feet rather than 100.

`deficit_ft` is how far short the owner fell: the required clearance minus the measured clearance, floored at 0 so that clearing more than required never produces a negative deficit, carried to one decimal place. `deficit_ratio` expresses that shortfall as a fraction of the requirement — the deficit divided by the required clearance, rounded half up to four decimal places. `deficiency_severity` then bands the ratio, with **the upper edge of each band inclusive**: a ratio of exactly 0.0000 is `NONE`, a ratio at or below 0.2500 is `MINOR`, a ratio at or below 0.6000 is `MAJOR`, and anything larger is `CRITICAL`. A ratio of exactly 0.2500 is `MINOR`, and a ratio of exactly 0.6000 is `MAJOR`.

The zone's contribution to the parcel's fuel load is `points`. The ratio is first scaled to a percentage by multiplying by 100 and weighted by the zone's importance — the ember zone carries a weight of 1.50 because embers landing against the wall are what actually ignite houses, zone one carries 1.00, and zone two carries 0.60 — and that product is rounded half up to two decimals and capped at 100.00. If ladder fuels were present, a flat penalty of 8.00 points is added on top, and the sum is again rounded half up to two decimals and capped at 100.00 to give `points`.

**Structure-hardening findings.** Separately the inspector works through a hardening checklist, recording one finding per element with an `item_code` of `EAVE_VENTS`, `ROOF_ASSEMBLY`, `DECK_UNDERSIDE`, `WINDOW_GLAZING`, `SIDING` or `GUTTER_DEBRIS` and a `status` of `COMPLIANT`, `DEFICIENT` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`. Each finding is worth `credit_points` of exactly 4.00 when its `status` is `COMPLIANT` and exactly 0.00 otherwise — a `DEFICIENT` element and a `NOT_APPLICABLE` element earn the owner the same nothing. At most one finding may exist per `item_code` per inspection, and a duplicate is refused by the state gate with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Findings and evaluations are both readable individually and as lists filtered by `inspection_id`, by `zone` for evaluations and by `item_code` for findings.

**Closing out the visit.** The assigned inspector closes an inspection standing in `NOTICED` by submitting a `result` of `PASS` or `FAIL`, optionally accompanied by a `violation_notice_method` that names how any resulting citation should be served. Completion refuses to run unless all three zones — `ZONE_0_EMBER`, `ZONE_1` and `ZONE_2` — carry exactly one evaluation each on this inspection, and unless the submitted result agrees with those findings: a `PASS` requires every zone's `deficiency_severity` to be `NONE`, and a `FAIL` requires at least one zone whose severity is not `NONE`. Any of these failures answers `ERR_ZONE_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE`. An inspection in any state other than `NOTICED` cannot be completed.

Both branches begin by rolling the findings up onto the inspection. `measured_veg_points` is the sum of the three zones' `points` values, and the **rounding discipline here is pinned**: each zone's `points` is already rounded to two decimals, and it is those rounded values that are added; the sum is then rounded half up to two decimals and capped at 100.00. Summing the unrounded per-zone products and rounding only at the end is incorrect. `hardening_credit_points` is 4.00 for every hardening finding on this inspection whose status is `COMPLIANT`, capped at 20.00. `max_deficiency_severity` is the worst severity among the three zone evaluations, ranked `NONE`, then `MINOR`, then `MAJOR`, then `CRITICAL`, and it is `NONE` when every zone came in compliant. Only rows attached to *this* inspection are considered; findings from an earlier visit to the same parcel are excluded. `completed_at_utc` is stamped with the logical clock, the submitted `result` is stored, the parcel is rescored under the post-inspection-verified variant with `risk_score_basis` set to `POST_INSPECTION_VERIFIED` and `risk_class` re-banded, the inspection moves `NOTICED → COMPLETED`, the assigned inspector's `assigned_open_count` drops by 1, and both the inspection's and the parcel's `version` advance by 1.

From there the two branches diverge sharply.

**A passing inspection** sets the parcel's `last_passed_inspection_at_utc` to the same instant as `completed_at_utc` and sets `compliance_status` to `COMPLIANT` — unless the parcel's `exemption_flag` is true, in which case the `EXEMPT` posture is retained rather than overwritten. When the completing inspection's `inspection_type` is `REINSPECTION`, every violation notice on that parcel standing in `ISSUED`, `EXTENDED` or `OVERDUE` transitions to `CLEARED` with `cleared_at_utc` stamped at the logical clock; because those notices cleared, the parcel's `open_violation_count` is recomputed and each cleared notice's `version` advances by 1. A passing `INITIAL` or `COMPLAINT` inspection clears nothing — the owner must be re-inspected under a `REINSPECTION` for a citation to lift. Finally, because the parcel's `compliance_status` changed, the `compliance_rate_pct` of the parcel's community is recomputed whenever `community_id` is not null; a rate that has just risen cannot push a community below the lapse floor, so no lapse follows a pass.

**A failing inspection** fires the deepest cascade in the system, six linked consequences that must all land in the same request:

1. The rollup described above completes with `result` set to `FAIL`.
2. Because that rollup produced verified measurements, the parcel is rescored under the post-inspection-verified variant, `risk_score_basis` becomes `POST_INSPECTION_VERIFIED`, `risk_class` is re-banded, and `compliance_status` becomes `NON_COMPLIANT`.
3. Because the parcel is now non-compliant, a violation notice is created against it, citing this inspection as the substantiating evidence. Its `severity` is copied from the inspection's `max_deficiency_severity`, its `notice_method` is the `violation_notice_method` supplied on the completion request and defaults to `CERTIFIED_MAIL` when that field is omitted, and its `delivered_at_utc`, `compliance_days` and `deadline_utc` are computed by the deadline rules in §3.6. The parcel's `open_violation_count` rises by 1.
4. Because a violation notice now exists on the parcel, every compliance certificate on that parcel standing in `ISSUED` whose `expires_at_utc` is at or after the logical clock transitions `ISSUED → REVOKED` with `revoked_at_utc` stamped at the logical clock. An already-expired certificate is left alone. This is the consequence most often missed: a failing inspection silently invalidates the clean paperwork an escrow may already be relying on.
5. Because the parcel's `compliance_status` changed in link 2, the `compliance_rate_pct` of the parcel's community is recomputed whenever `community_id` is not null.
6. Because link 5 lowered that rate, a community standing in `CERTIFIED` whose recomputed rate is not null and has fallen below the lapse floor of 60.00 percent transitions `CERTIFIED → LAPSED` and has its `lapse_count` incremented by 1.

Every entity touched along that chain — inspection, parcel, the new notice, each revoked certificate, and the community — has its own `version` incremented by 1.

### 3.6. Violation Notices: Service, Deadlines, Extensions and Supersession

A violation notice is the district's legal instrument. Every notice records the cited `parcel_id` and the `inspection_id` of the substantiating visit — either the completed failing inspection that documented the deficiency, or the abandoned inspection that documents an access refusal. It opens in `ISSUED` with `issued_at_utc` stamped at the logical clock, `extension_count` at 0, and `cleared_at_utc` and `void_reason` both null. Its `severity` is `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`, copied from the substantiating inspection's `max_deficiency_severity`, or forced to `CRITICAL` when the notice documents an access refusal rather than a measured deficiency.

**Constructive service and the compliance deadline** are computed in four steps, and the choice of `notice_method` matters more than owners expect. Where the notice was handed over in person or sent by email, `delivered_at_utc` equals `issued_at_utc` — service is instantaneous. Where it went by certified mail, the district takes constructive service to occur five days after issuance, so `delivered_at_utc` is `issued_at_utc` plus 5 days. That single choice moves every downstream date.

From there the severity of the finding sets the base allowance: a `MINOR` deficiency earns the owner 60 days, a `MAJOR` deficiency 30 days, and a `CRITICAL` deficiency only 14. Parcels in the worst hazard zone get less time. When the cited parcel's `hazard_zone` is `VERY_HIGH`, the base allowance is multiplied by three quarters and rounded **up** to a whole day, then floored at a statutory minimum of 7 days so that no owner is ever given less than a week — a `CRITICAL` finding on a `VERY_HIGH` parcel therefore yields 11 days (14 multiplied by three quarters is 10.5, rounded up to 11), a `MAJOR` finding yields 23, and a `MINOR` finding yields 45. Parcels in `MODERATE` or `HIGH` zones keep the base allowance untouched. The result is stored as `compliance_days`, which is always a whole number and never below 7.

`deadline_utc` is then the calendar date reached by adding `compliance_days` days to `delivered_at_utc`, expressed at 23:59:59 UTC on that date — owners get the whole final day. The comparison that decides lateness is strict: a notice is past due only when `deadline_utc` is strictly earlier than the logical clock, so a clock reading exactly equal to `deadline_utc` is still inside the compliance window.

**Extensions.** Either a `ProgramAdmin` or the cited `PropertyOwner` may request the district's single permitted extension by supplying a `reason`; when the requester is the owner, `X-User-ID` must equal the notice's `parcel_id`. A granted extension moves the notice `ISSUED → EXTENDED`, adds 30 whole days to `deadline_utc` while preserving the 23:59:59 UTC end-of-day form, and increments `extension_count` by 1. Two limits apply. The request must arrive while the logical clock is at or before the existing `deadline_utc` — an extension asked for after the deadline has already lapsed is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, as is any request against a notice that has reached `CLEARED`, `VOIDED` or `ABATEMENT_AUTHORIZED`. And `extension_count` must still be below the ceiling of one extension per notice: as noted in §2.9, an already-`EXTENDED` notice is an accepted target for the state gate and is refused further down the ladder with `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_REACHED`, leaving `deadline_utc` and `extension_count` untouched.

**Lapsing.** Nothing marks a notice late on its own. At the next cycle advance, a notice standing in `ISSUED` or `EXTENDED` whose `deadline_utc` has passed transitions to `OVERDUE` — `ISSUED → OVERDUE` and `EXTENDED → OVERDUE` behave identically — and the cited parcel's `compliance_status` is set to `NON_COMPLIANT`, with both the notice's and the parcel's `version` advancing by 1.

**Clearing.** Late compliance still counts. A passing reinspection on the cited parcel clears a notice from `ISSUED`, from `EXTENDED`, or from `OVERDUE`; all three transitions stamp `cleared_at_utc` at the logical clock, decrement the parcel's `open_violation_count` by 1, recompute the parcel's `compliance_status`, and advance the notice's `version` by 1. `OVERDUE` is a detour, not a dead end — voluntary compliance after the deadline is still recognised. A notice may also clear out of `ABATEMENT_AUTHORIZED` when the district's own contractor finishes the work, which §3.7 describes. `CLEARED` and `VOIDED` are the notice's two terminal states.

**Voiding.** A `FireMarshal` who concludes a notice was issued in error voids it with a `reason`, which is recorded as `void_reason`. Voiding is available from `ISSUED`, `EXTENDED` and `OVERDUE` alike, and all three paths behave identically: the parcel's `open_violation_count` drops by 1 and its `compliance_status` is recomputed — restored to `COMPLIANT` when the count has reached 0 and the parcel has a passing inspection on record, and set back to `PENDING_INSPECTION` when the count has reached 0 but no passing inspection exists — with both the notice's and the parcel's `version` advancing by 1. One integrity guard applies: the acting marshal's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `verified_by_inspector_id` recorded on any abatement order raised under that notice, a condition that is vacuously satisfied while no such order exists; a marshal who is also the recorded verifier of the abatement work is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

**Discretionary issuance and supersession.** A `ProgramAdmin` may also issue a notice directly against a parcel — the path used when a complaint or a re-measured deficiency justifies a fresh citation without waiting for a new inspection to close. The request names the substantiating `inspection_id`, the `notice_method` to serve it by, and `expected_parcel_version`, and a version mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_PARCEL_VERSION`. Because a parcel may hold only one live citation, issuing this notice **supersedes** whatever was open: any existing notice on the same parcel standing in `ISSUED`, `EXTENDED` or `OVERDUE` is driven to `VOIDED` with its `void_reason` recorded as `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_NOTICE`, and the new notice's `supersedes_notice_id` is set to the identifier of the notice it replaced. A notice that supersedes nothing leaves `supersedes_notice_id` null. The new notice's `delivered_at_utc`, `compliance_days` and `deadline_utc` are computed by the rules above, the parcel's `open_violation_count` is recomputed, its `compliance_status` is set to `NON_COMPLIANT`, and its `version` advances by 1. The same supersession resolution applies to the citation raised automatically when an inspection is abandoned after a second access refusal.

Notices are readable individually — reads expose `deadline_utc`, `compliance_days` and `extension_count` — and as a list filtered by `parcel_id`, by the originating `inspection_id`, by `supersedes_notice_id` to find what replaced what, and by `status`.

### 3.7. Abatement Authority: Authorizing, Awarding and Performing the Work

When an owner lets a deadline lapse, the district may clear the fuel itself and bill for it. That authority is deliberately narrow: it attaches only after the compliance deadline has actually passed. A `FireMarshal` exercises it against a notice standing in `OVERDUE`, and an attempt against a notice in any other state — including one still inside its compliance window — is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_OVERDUE`. The marshal supplies `estimated_cost_cents`, the district's own estimate of the job, which may not be negative and may not exceed the per-order abatement ceiling of 750,000 cents, that is 7,500 dollars; a larger estimate answers `ERR_ABATEMENT_CAP_EXCEEDED`. A notice that already carries a live order — one standing in `AUTHORIZED`, `AWARDED`, `WORK_COMPLETE` or `INVOICED` — cannot be authorized a second time, and the attempt answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Authorization has three effects at once. A new abatement order is created in `AUTHORIZED` carrying the supplied `estimated_cost_cents`, with `paid_cents` and `lien_amount_cents` both initialised to 0 and its `contractor_id`, `crew_hours`, `work_start_utc`, `work_end_utc` and every cost field still null. The notice moves `OVERDUE → ABATEMENT_AUTHORIZED`. And the cited parcel's `compliance_status` becomes `ABATEMENT_PENDING`, which is neither compliant nor merely non-compliant but a third posture meaning "the district is handling it". Both the notice's and the parcel's `version` advance by 1.

**The contractor roster.** A contractor is registered with a `business_name` that must be non-empty and unique across the roster, an `hourly_rate_cents` crew labour rate of at least 1 cent per crew-hour, a `mobilization_fee_cents` flat mobilization charge that may be 0, a `license_status` of `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED` that defaults to `ACTIVE`, an `insurance_expiry_utc` liability-insurance expiry, and a `max_daily_orders` ceiling on how many jobs the crew can hold on any one calendar day, which must be at least 1 and defaults to 2. `committed_crew_hours` and `completed_crew_hours` both start at 0. A `ProgramAdmin` may later revise `license_status`, `insurance_expiry_utc`, `hourly_rate_cents`, `mobilization_fee_cents` and `max_daily_orders`; each revision advances the contractor's `version` by 1.

**Awarding the work.** A `FireMarshal` awards an order standing in `AUTHORIZED` by naming the `contractor_id`, the `crew_hours` being reserved — a positive number in half-hour multiples — and the `work_start_utc` and `work_end_utc` bounding the job. Four eligibility tests run. The contractor's `license_status` must be `ACTIVE`, and a `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED` licence answers `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_LICENSED`. The `work_start_utc` must fall strictly before the contractor's `insurance_expiry_utc`; a start at or after that instant answers `ERR_CONTRACTOR_INSURANCE_LAPSED`. The work window is treated as **closed** — it occupies every instant from `work_start_utc` through `work_end_utc` inclusive — and it must not intersect the closed window of any other order of the same contractor standing in `AWARDED` or `WORK_COMPLETE`; unlike the inspector rule, **touching endpoints do conflict here**, so a new job starting at the exact instant an existing job ends is refused with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Finally, for every UTC calendar date the new closed window touches, the count of that contractor's `AWARDED` and `WORK_COMPLETE` orders whose closed windows cover that same date must remain at or below `max_daily_orders`; exceeding it on any single date answers `ERR_CONTRACTOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. This is an aggregate-per-day test, not a pairwise one: with the default cap of 2, a third overlapping job on the same Tuesday is refused even though no two of the three windows are identical.

A successful award stores `contractor_id`, `crew_hours`, `work_start_utc` and `work_end_utc`, moves the order `AUTHORIZED → AWARDED`, and increases the contractor's `committed_crew_hours` by the reserved `crew_hours`. Both the order's and the contractor's `version` advance by 1.

**Reporting the work done.** The awarded `Contractor` — and only that contractor, matched by `X-User-ID` against the order's `contractor_id` — reports completion by supplying `actual_crew_hours`, again a positive multiple of half an hour, and `green_waste_tons`, the non-negative tonnage hauled off carried to two decimals. The report is refused unless the logical clock has reached `work_start_utc`; a crew cannot report finishing a job it has not started. The order moves `AWARDED → WORK_COMPLETE`, and note that the reservation **is not released here**: `committed_crew_hours` stays committed through `WORK_COMPLETE` and is only unwound at invoicing or cancellation.

**Cancelling.** A `FireMarshal` may cancel an order standing in `AUTHORIZED` or in `AWARDED` by recording a `reason`, stored as `cancel_reason`. Both paths revert the enforcement chain: the referenced notice returns `ABATEMENT_AUTHORIZED → OVERDUE` and the parcel's `compliance_status` reverts to `NON_COMPLIANT`, exactly undoing what authorization did. The `AWARDED` path does one thing more — it decreases the contractor's `committed_crew_hours` by the order's `crew_hours`, restoring that counter to precisely its pre-award value — whereas the `AUTHORIZED` path has no reservation to unwind. Cancelling from `AUTHORIZED` advances the order's, the notice's and the parcel's `version` by 1; cancelling from `AWARDED` advances those three plus the contractor's. `CANCELLED`, `PAID` and `LIEN_FILED` are the order's three terminal states.

#### Cost Recovery: Verification, Invoicing, Payment and Liens

Verification is where the money is decided, and it is deliberately assigned to a *different* inspector than the one who wrote the citation. An `Inspector` verifies an order standing in `WORK_COMPLETE`, optionally attaching a `verified_note`. Two guards apply beyond the state gate. The acting inspector's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, and a suspended inspector is refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_SUSPENDED`. And the acting inspector's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `inspector_id` recorded on the inspection that substantiated this order's violation notice — the district will not let the officer who found the violation also sign off on the paid remedy. Self-verification is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The invoice is assembled in a pinned sequence, and the sequence resolves what looks like a circularity: **the subsidy tier is keyed on the pre-subsidy total, not on the final recoverable amount.**

1. The **labour line** is the reported `actual_crew_hours` multiplied by the contractor's `hourly_rate_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent, and stored as `labor_line_cents`.
2. The **disposal line** is `green_waste_tons` multiplied by the district's tipping rate of 9,200 cents per ton — 92 dollars a ton — rounded half up to a whole cent, and stored as `disposal_line_cents`.
3. The **mobilization line**, `mobilization_line_cents`, is simply a copy of the contractor's `mobilization_fee_cents`.
4. **Administrative overhead** is charged at 12.5 percent, and it is charged **per line and then summed**: the labour line times 0.125 rounded half up to a whole cent, plus the disposal line times 0.125 rounded half up to a whole cent, plus the mobilization line times 0.125 rounded half up to a whole cent. The total is stored as `admin_overhead_cents`. Applying 12.5 percent to the combined lines and rounding once is a different number and is incorrect.
5. `pre_subsidy_total_cents` is the labour line plus the disposal line plus the mobilization line plus the administrative overhead.
6. `subsidy_pct` is selected from `pre_subsidy_total_cents` on a three-step ladder: a pre-subsidy total below 100,000 cents draws the most generous 0.30 subsidy, a total from 100,000 cents up to but not including 300,000 cents draws 0.20, and a total of 300,000 cents or more draws 0.10 — small jobs are subsidised most heavily because the district would rather clear them than argue about them. `district_subsidy_cents` is `pre_subsidy_total_cents` multiplied by `subsidy_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent.
7. `total_recoverable_cents` — the amount the owner actually owes — is `pre_subsidy_total_cents` minus `district_subsidy_cents`. `invoice_due_utc` is the calendar date reached by adding the governing cycle's `invoice_due_days` to the logical clock, expressed at 23:59:59 UTC on that date.

Two variants of this computation exist, distinguished by whether the owner had already tried to help themselves, and `recovery_basis` records which one ran. The qualifying signal is a support enrollment on the same parcel whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `COMPLETED` or `REIMBURSED` and whose `created_at` is strictly earlier than the substantiating notice's `issued_at_utc`. Where **no** such earlier enrollment exists, the **district-contracted** variant applies: administrative overhead is charged in full per step 4 and the subsidy tier is used exactly as looked up, with `recovery_basis` set to `DISTRICT_CONTRACTED`. Where **at least one** such earlier enrollment exists, the **voluntary-precompliance** variant applies: `admin_overhead_cents` is forced to 0, so `pre_subsidy_total_cents` equals just the three raw cost lines, and `subsidy_pct` is stepped exactly one position toward more generous along the ladder whose rungs are 0.10, then 0.20, then 0.30 — a job that would have drawn 0.10 draws 0.20 instead, one that would have drawn 0.20 draws 0.30, and one already at 0.30 stays at 0.30 because the ladder saturates there. `recovery_basis` is set to `VOLUNTARY_PRECOMPLIANCE`. For identical cost lines the voluntary variant always yields a strictly smaller `total_recoverable_cents` than the district-contracted one; an owner who showed up to a chipper day before the citation pays less.

Verification is also a five-link cascade, all of it landing in the same request:

1. The cost fields above are computed, `verified_by_inspector_id` records the acting inspector, `invoice_due_utc` and `recovery_basis` are set, and the order moves `WORK_COMPLETE → INVOICED`.
2. Because the order became invoiced, the reservation finally unwinds: the contractor's `committed_crew_hours` decreases by the order's `crew_hours` and its `completed_crew_hours` increases by the order's `actual_crew_hours`, restoring the conservation identity of §2.10. Note that these are different quantities — reserved hours and actually-worked hours need not match — and `completed_crew_hours` is never decremented by anything.
3. Because the order became invoiced, the referenced violation notice transitions `ABATEMENT_AUTHORIZED → CLEARED` with `cleared_at_utc` stamped at the logical clock. The work has been done, even if the district paid for it.
4. Because that notice cleared, the parcel's `open_violation_count` decreases by 1 and its `compliance_status` becomes `COMPLIANT` — but only when no other open notice remains on the parcel.
5. Because the parcel's `compliance_status` changed in link 4, the `compliance_rate_pct` of the parcel's community is recomputed whenever `community_id` is not null.

The order's, the contractor's, the notice's and the parcel's `version` each advance by 1.

**The payment waterfall.** An owner's payment does not simply reduce a single balance; it is allocated across four named cost buckets in a fixed order, so the district can report which portion of a recovery has actually been collected. The bucket order is `ADMIN_OVERHEAD` first, then `MOBILIZATION`, then `DISPOSAL`, and `LABOR` last, and each bucket's capacity is the corresponding line amount — `admin_overhead_cents`, `mobilization_line_cents`, `disposal_line_cents` and `labor_line_cents` respectively. The recoverable amount is poured into the buckets in that order, each filled to its capacity before the next receives anything; because `total_recoverable_cents` is smaller than the sum of the caps by exactly the subsidy, **the subsidy shortfall lands entirely in the last bucket, `LABOR`**. The sum of the filled bucket amounts always equals `total_recoverable_cents`.

Each recorded payment then consumes that filled allocation in the same bucket order, net of whatever earlier payments already consumed: for each bucket in turn, the payment contributes the lesser of what remains of the payment and what remains unpaid in that bucket. The result is stored on the payment as `allocation`, an object with integer-cent values under the four keys `ADMIN_OVERHEAD`, `MOBILIZATION`, `DISPOSAL` and `LABOR`. The payment also records `outstanding_after_cents`, which is `total_recoverable_cents` minus the order's `paid_cents` as it stands immediately after this payment is applied.

A payment is recorded by a `ProgramAdmin` or by the owning `PropertyOwner` against an order that has reached `INVOICED` or `LIEN_FILED`, supplying `amount_cents` — at least 1 cent — and a `method` of `CHECK`, `CARD`, `ACH` or `WRITE_OFF`. A `WRITE_OFF` is a district write-off rather than money received, but it consumes outstanding balance exactly like a real tender does. The payment may not exceed the outstanding balance, that is `total_recoverable_cents` minus `paid_cents`, and an over-payment is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`. Each accepted payment increases the order's `paid_cents` by `amount_cents` and advances the order's `version` by 1. When the order stands in `INVOICED` and the payment brings `paid_cents` to exactly `total_recoverable_cents`, the order moves `INVOICED → PAID`. A payment against a `LIEN_FILED` order is recorded and allocated but does not move the order's state, because `LIEN_FILED` is terminal. Payments themselves are immutable and are readable individually or as a list filtered by `abatement_order_id` and by `method`.

**Liens.** An owner who lets the invoice go unpaid past its due date has the balance recorded against the property. At the cycle advance following `invoice_due_utc`, an order standing in `INVOICED` whose `invoice_due_utc` is strictly earlier than the logical clock moves `INVOICED → LIEN_FILED` and its `lien_amount_cents` is set to `total_recoverable_cents` minus `paid_cents` — before that moment `lien_amount_cents` is 0. The conservation identity of §2.10 must hold after every payment and after every lien filing: `total_recoverable_cents` always equals `paid_cents` plus `lien_amount_cents` plus the outstanding remainder. Abatement orders are readable individually — reads expose the labour, overhead, subsidy and recoverable totals — and as a list filtered by `violation_notice_id`, by `contractor_id`, by `verified_by_inspector_id` and by `status`.

### 3.8. Compliance-Support Programs and Cost-Share Awards

Enforcement alone does not clear brush; the district also funds the carrot side. A `ProgramAdmin` opens a support program inside a cycle by naming the `cycle_id`, a `program_type` of `CHIPPER_DAY`, `CURBSIDE_COLLECTION` or `COST_SHARE_GRANT`, a `budget_total_cents` reimbursement budget that may be 0, a `capacity_slots` ceiling of at least 1 simultaneously approved enrollment, and the `service_window_start_utc` and `service_window_end_utc` bounding the period in which enrollment is accepted. Only `COST_SHARE_GRANT` programs actually reimburse money — a chipper day and a curbside collection consume a slot but never a cent. At creation `budget_remaining_cents` is initialised to `budget_total_cents` and `reserved_slots` to 0. Programs are readable individually and as a list filtered by `cycle_id` and by `program_type`.

Enrolling is available to a `ProgramAdmin` or to the owning `PropertyOwner`, and the request names the `support_program_id`, the enrolling `parcel_id`, the `expected_program_version` described in §2.8, and — for a cost-share grant only — the `vendor_invoice_cents` the owner is claiming against. Presence of that invoice is strictly conditional: it must be supplied when the program's `program_type` is `COST_SHARE_GRANT` and must be omitted otherwise, with a violation either way answering `ERR_MISSING_VENDOR_INVOICE`. An invoice above the district's cost-share invoice ceiling of 1,000,000 cents — four times the per-parcel award cap — is refused outright with `ERR_COST_SHARE_CAP_EXCEEDED` rather than being clamped down to the cap. Enrollment is accepted only while the logical clock lies inside the program's **half-open** service window, at or after `service_window_start_utc` and strictly before `service_window_end_utc`; a clock before the start or at or after the end answers `ERR_PROGRAM_WINDOW_CLOSED`. And a parcel may hold only one live enrollment per program: a second attempt while an enrollment for the same program and parcel already stands in `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `WAITLISTED`, `COMPLETED` or `REIMBURSED` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT`, while a `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW` enrollment leaves the parcel free to try again.

**Sizing a cost-share award** runs in a pinned sequence, and as with cost recovery the sequence resolves an apparent circularity: **the hardship bonus tier is keyed on the pre-bonus award, not on the final award.**

1. The eligible cost is the lesser of the submitted `vendor_invoice_cents` and the per-parcel award cap of 250,000 cents, that is 2,500 dollars.
2. The base match rate is read from the parcel's `risk_class` **as it stands at decision time**: an `EXTREME` parcel is matched at 0.75, a `HIGH` parcel at 0.60, a `MODERATE` parcel at 0.45 and a `LOW` parcel at 0.30. Riskier ground earns a bigger share of the bill.
3. `award_before_bonus_cents` is the eligible cost multiplied by that base match rate, rounded half up to a whole cent.
4. `bonus_pct` is then selected from `award_before_bonus_cents` on a three-step ladder whose **boundaries are inclusive at the lower edge**: a pre-bonus award below 50,000 cents draws 0.15, a pre-bonus award from 50,000 cents up to but not including 120,000 cents draws 0.10, and a pre-bonus award of 120,000 cents or more draws 0.05 — a pre-bonus award of exactly 120,000 cents therefore draws 0.05, not 0.10. `hardship_bonus_cents` is `award_before_bonus_cents` multiplied by `bonus_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent, **but only when the parcel's `owner_hardship_flag` is true**; where the flag is false the bonus is exactly 0.
5. `award_cents` is `award_before_bonus_cents` plus `hardship_bonus_cents`.

For a program that is not a cost-share grant, `award_before_bonus_cents`, `bonus_pct` and `award_cents` are all null and no money is ever reserved.

**Admission is decided inside the enrollment request itself**, and the two branches are mutually exclusive. The enrollment is **approved** when the program still has an unused slot — `reserved_slots` strictly below `capacity_slots` — and, for a cost-share grant, when `budget_remaining_cents` is at least the computed `award_cents`. Approval sets `reserved_amount_cents` to `award_cents` for a grant, debits the program's `budget_remaining_cents` by that same amount, increments `reserved_slots` by 1, leaves `waitlist_position` null, and advances the program's `version` by 1. The enrollment is instead **waitlisted** when the program's `reserved_slots` has reached `capacity_slots`, or when the program is a cost-share grant whose `budget_remaining_cents` falls short of the full `award_cents`. A waitlisted enrollment sets `reserved_amount_cents` to 0, takes a `waitlist_position` of one more than the count of enrollments already waitlisted on that program, and leaves both `budget_remaining_cents` and `reserved_slots` untouched. **Partial funding is never granted** — an under-funded enrollment waits for its full award rather than accepting what is available. `REQUESTED` is the enrollment's declared initial state and the admission decision resolves it to `APPROVED` or `WAITLISTED` before the response is returned. A newly created enrollment carries `reimbursed_cents` of 0 — an integer zero rather than null — for every `program_type`, grant or not, and it stays 0 until a reimbursement writes to it.

Waitlists move only at a cycle advance. For each program in the advancing cycle, the earliest remaining waitlisted enrollment by `created_at` ascending that still fits **both** an unused slot and — for a grant — enough remaining budget for its full `award_cents` is promoted `WAITLISTED → APPROVED`: its `reserved_amount_cents` becomes its `award_cents`, its `waitlist_position` is cleared to null, the program's `budget_remaining_cents` is debited and its `reserved_slots` incremented, and both the enrollment's and the program's `version` advance by 1. Promotion continues down the queue while both conditions hold, and the enrollments still waitlisted afterwards are renumbered from 1 in `created_at` ascending order. Note that a candidate too expensive for the remaining budget does not permanently block the queue in this specification's ordering — promotion walks the queue in creation order and admits each candidate that fits.

**Closing out an enrollment** is a `ProgramAdmin` operation on an enrollment standing in `APPROVED`, and the single `attended` flag it carries forks into three distinct outcomes:

- `attended` true on a `CHIPPER_DAY` or `CURBSIDE_COLLECTION` program moves the enrollment `APPROVED → COMPLETED`. Nothing financial happens: `reserved_amount_cents` and the program's `budget_remaining_cents` are both left exactly as they were, and only the enrollment's `version` advances by 1.
- `attended` true on a `COST_SHARE_GRANT` program moves the enrollment `APPROVED → REIMBURSED`. `reimbursed_cents` is set to `award_cents`, and `reserved_amount_cents` deliberately **stays** at `award_cents` so that `budget_remaining_cents` does not move — the money was always going to be spent, and reimbursement merely disburses what was reserved. Only the enrollment's `version` advances by 1.
- `attended` false moves the enrollment `APPROVED → NO_SHOW` regardless of program type. `reserved_amount_cents` is zeroed, the program's `budget_remaining_cents` is credited back by the amount that had been reserved, and `reserved_slots` is decremented by 1 so the slot returns to the pool. Both the enrollment's and the program's `version` advance by 1.

**Cancelling an enrollment** is available to a `ProgramAdmin` or, with `X-User-ID` matching the enrollment's `parcel_id`, to the owning `PropertyOwner`, and it accepts a `reason`. From `APPROVED` it exactly compensates the approval: `reserved_amount_cents` is zeroed, the program's `budget_remaining_cents` is credited back by the previously reserved amount so it returns to precisely its pre-approval value, `reserved_slots` is decremented by 1, and both `version` counters advance. From `WAITLISTED` there is nothing to compensate — `budget_remaining_cents` and `reserved_slots` are left untouched because nothing was ever reserved — but the remaining waitlisted enrollments on that program are renumbered from 1 in `created_at` ascending order, and the enrollment's `version` advances by 1. `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW`, `REIMBURSED` and `CANCELLED` are all terminal. Enrollment reads expose `award_cents`, `reserved_amount_cents` and `waitlist_position`, and enrollment listings can be filtered by `support_program_id`, by `parcel_id` and by `status`.

### 3.9. Documentation, Neighbourhood Certification and Program Metrics

**Compliance certificates** are what insurers, lenders and escrow officers actually want from the district. Either a `TitleAgent` or the owning `PropertyOwner` opens a request against a parcel, declaring a `requester_kind` of `INSURANCE`, `REAL_ESTATE` or `LENDER` and a `transaction_reference` — the policy number, escrow number or loan number the certificate will be filed under. The `requester_kind` drives no logic and is retained purely for program reporting. A newly opened request stands in `REQUESTED` with `decision_note`, `issued_at_utc`, `expires_at_utc`, `snapshot_risk_class`, `snapshot_compliance_status` and `revoked_at_utc` all null.

Adjudication belongs to a `ProgramAdmin`, who supplies a `decision` of `ISSUE` or `DENY` and an optional `note`. Issuance is only available on a parcel with a clean posture: its `compliance_status` must be `COMPLIANT` or `EXEMPT`, and it must hold no violation notice standing in `ISSUED`, `EXTENDED`, `OVERDUE` or `ABATEMENT_AUTHORIZED`. Error selection between the two failure modes is unambiguous — a parcel whose `compliance_status` is `PENDING_INSPECTION` answers `ERR_PARCEL_NOT_INSPECTED`, and every other failing case, including a `NON_COMPLIANT` or `ABATEMENT_PENDING` posture and the open-notice case, answers `ERR_OPEN_VIOLATION_EXISTS`.

An `ISSUE` decision moves the certificate `REQUESTED → ISSUED` and stamps four fields: `issued_at_utc` at the logical clock; `expires_at_utc` at the calendar date 90 days after issuance, expressed at 23:59:59 UTC on that date; `snapshot_risk_class` pinned to the parcel's `risk_class` at that moment; and `snapshot_compliance_status` pinned to the parcel's posture, which will therefore be either `COMPLIANT` or `EXEMPT`. Those snapshots are frozen evidence — later rescoring of the parcel never rewrites them. A `DENY` decision moves the certificate `REQUESTED → DENIED`, records the supplied text as `decision_note`, and leaves every snapshot field null. Either way the certificate's `version` advances by 1. `DENIED` and `REVOKED` are terminal; the revocation path is the one described in §3.5, where a failing inspection on the parcel drives an unexpired issued certificate `ISSUED → REVOKED` with `revoked_at_utc` stamped and the original snapshots preserved exactly as issued. Certificates are readable individually and as a list filtered by `parcel_id`, by `requester_kind` and by `status`.

**Neighbourhood certification** is the district's community-organizing programme. A `ProgramAdmin` registers a neighbourhood with a `name` that must be non-empty and unique across all communities, the `organizer_name` of the volunteer of record, and the `target_certification_year` the neighbourhood is working toward, which must fall between 2020 and 2100 inclusive. A new community opens in `ORGANIZING` with `compliance_rate_pct` null, `parcels_outreached_count` at 0, `certified_at_utc` null and `lapse_count` at 0. Parcels join by carrying that community's identifier in their `community_id`.

The organizer submits the application, supplying `parcels_outreached_count` — how many doors the volunteers actually knocked on. Three conditions must hold: the community must stand in `ORGANIZING`, the acting `CommunityOrganizer`'s `X-User-ID` must equal the community's `id`, and the neighbourhood must have at least 3 member parcels while the supplied `parcels_outreached_count` is at least as large as that member-parcel count — a neighbourhood cannot claim to have reached more households than it contains, nor fewer. Falling short of either the member-parcel floor or the outreach floor answers `ERR_COMMUNITY_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`. A successful submission records `parcels_outreached_count`, recomputes `compliance_rate_pct` under the community-scoped metric, moves the community `ORGANIZING → SUBMITTED`, and advances its `version` by 1.

A `FireMarshal` then adjudicates a `SUBMITTED` community with a `decision` of `CERTIFY` or `DEFER` plus an optional `note`, and the two branches diverge in both precondition and effect. `CERTIFY` requires the community's `compliance_rate_pct` to be non-null **and** at or above the certification floor of 75.00 percent; a null rate or a rate below that floor answers `ERR_COMMUNITY_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`. A successful certify moves the community `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED` and stamps `certified_at_utc` at the logical clock. `DEFER` carries no rate precondition at all, moves the community `SUBMITTED → DEFERRED`, and leaves `certified_at_utc` exactly as it was — null for a community that has never been certified, or the earlier instant for one that has. Both branches advance the community's `version` by 1.

Certification is not permanent. As §3.1 describes, a cycle advance recomputes every community's rate and then lapses any `CERTIFIED` community whose recomputed rate has dropped below 60.00 percent, moving it `CERTIFIED → LAPSED` and incrementing `lapse_count` by 1; the same lapse fires outside a cycle advance as the last link of the inspection-failure cascade. Both a `DEFERRED` and a `LAPSED` community can go round again: the acting organizer, matched by `X-User-ID` against the community's `id`, reopens it for another round, which moves the community `DEFERRED → ORGANIZING` or `LAPSED → ORGANIZING`, resets `parcels_outreached_count` to 0 so the next submission must count doors afresh, and advances the `version` by 1. There is no cap on how many rounds a neighbourhood may run — the community lifecycle has no terminal state, and `lapse_count` accumulates across all of them as the district's memory of how often certification has slipped.

**Program metrics** are computed fresh at read time rather than served from stored columns, and both scopes share one definition of eligibility. A parcel is **eligible** when its `compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT`, `NON_COMPLIANT` or `ABATEMENT_PENDING`. Parcels sitting in `PENDING_INSPECTION` or `EXEMPT` are excluded from **both** the numerator and the denominator — they are not counted as failures, they are simply not counted. `compliant_parcel_count` is the number of eligible parcels whose posture is `COMPLIANT`, `eligible_parcel_count` is the size of the eligible set, and `compliance_rate_pct` is one hundred times the compliant count divided by the eligible count, rounded half up to two decimals. Where the eligible set is empty the rate is **null**, which is a distinct value from 0.00 and must be reported as null rather than as a zero.

The two scopes differ only in which parcels they consider. The **district scope**, served by the cycle metrics read, spans every parcel in the district including those whose `community_id` is null. The **community scope**, served by the community metrics read and used at both the cycle-advance recomputation and the inspection-failure cascade, spans only the parcels whose `community_id` equals the target community; this is the value persisted to `Community.compliance_rate_pct` and the value compared against the 75.00 percent certification floor and the 60.00 percent lapse floor. The community metrics read returns `eligible_parcel_count`, `compliant_parcel_count`, `compliance_rate_pct` and `member_parcel_count`, the last being the raw count of parcels carrying that `community_id` regardless of posture.

The district scope additionally reports the **survival correlation** the district's board cares most about — whether defensible space actually saved houses. The correlation is computed over parcels that both carry a recorded `structure_survival_outcome` of `SURVIVED` or `DESTROYED` and are eligible by the posture rule above. Within that set, `survived_compliant_pct` is one hundred times the number of parcels that were `COMPLIANT` and `SURVIVED` divided by the number that were `COMPLIANT`, rounded half up to two decimals, or null when no compliant parcel carries an outcome. `survived_noncompliant_pct` is one hundred times the number of parcels that were **not** `COMPLIANT` and `SURVIVED` divided by the number that were not `COMPLIANT`, rounded half up to two decimals, or null when no non-compliant parcel carries an outcome. `survival_delta_pp` is the compliant survival percentage minus the non-compliant one, rounded half up to two decimals and expressed in percentage points, and it is null whenever either component is null.
