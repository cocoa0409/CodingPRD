# Restaurant-Attached Gift Shop API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A Cracker Barrel-style store is two businesses sharing one roof, one payroll, one backroom and one cash register. A guest waiting forty minutes for a table wanders the retail floor and buys a nostalgic tin sign; the same ticket that carries the pot roast also carries the tin sign. Today that commerce is run on spreadsheets. This API replaces them with a single system of record mounted under `/retail-ops`, covering the retail assortment, the dining check, the combined transaction, the seasonal markdown cadence, the shared backroom and the labor pool that crosses between dining room and sales floor.

Three pressures shape the requirements. First, revenue must be recognized twice over — once provisionally when the retail portion is first posted against a dining check, and again finally when the business day closes — and the two numbers legitimately differ, so every downstream figure (brand revenue, retail sales per guest, the liquidation trigger) must be pinned to a stated variant rather than left to the reader's judgement. Second, retail merchandising on this format is seasonal and imported: assortment decisions are made a quarter ahead against a season window, and post-holiday clearance has to run on a fixed weekly cadence that walks a price down in stages rather than in one jump. Third, the space is genuinely shared: the backroom corridor reserved for short-dated food is not available to retail receiving, and a labor force that crosses between tips-eligible and non-tip-eligible workcodes has to be adjudicated precisely, because a misclassified shift silently mis-states the retail conversion index.

The result is a system where a store day is opened, trafficked, closed; where a check is opened, decorated with retail lines, tipped, finalized, settled; where a clearance window is activated, walked down through four stages, extended, reactivated, liquidated; where a transfer is dispatched, received, reconciled or voided; and where every one of those movements is reflected in a small number of conserved quantities — backroom occupancy plus committed units, brand recognition totals, the store day's conversion index — that must never drift.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The rules in this section apply to every resource and every operation in the API. They cover identity and authorization, the shape of every HTTP response, pagination, the common system-managed attributes carried by all resources, the logical clock that replaces wall time, arithmetic precision, optimistic concurrency, and the order in which the server resolves and rejects requests.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the caller and `X-User-Role` declares the caller's operational capacity. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the eight declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Presenting a well-formed role that is not permitted for the requested operation is a different failure and is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `AssortmentPlanner`, `BoutiqueManager`, `RetailLead`, `RetailCashier`, `Cashier`, `Server` and `Anonymous`. `Anonymous` exists only so that the unauthenticated-rejection rule can be exercised; it holds no operations and no exclusive rights.

Two of the roles are backed by persisted entities and six are not. When `X-User-Role` is `Cashier` or `Server`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StaffShift`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `StaffShift` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — in practice this means the `presented_shift_id` carried on a `GuestCheck` is checked against the caller, and the `crossover_authorized_by_shift_id` carried on a `StaffShift` is likewise a real `StaffShift` reference. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `AssortmentPlanner`, `BoutiqueManager`, `RetailLead`, `RetailCashier` or `Anonymous`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it, for example against an audit field, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Operation ownership is split between exclusive and shared rights. A `PlatformAdmin` alone creates and updates the chain-level configuration — brand creation and brand update, location creation, and the clock-setting capability — and shares read access to brands, locations and every computed read with the other internal roles. An `AssortmentPlanner` alone creates items, creates and updates planogram versions, publishes and retires them, and creates and reshapes planogram slots; the planner additionally shares clearance-schedule creation, the import-deadline check, the import-plan read and the region-fit read. A `BoutiqueManager` alone opens and closes store days, voids guest checks, disputes and resolves receipts, voids a receiving, starts, completes, reverts and cancels staff shifts, verifies a planogram slot, and records manual capacity readings; the manager shares guest-count posting, clearance extension, reactivation and liquidation, and staff-shift scheduling. A `RetailLead` alone rings receipts, reconciles and settles them, and runs the transfer pipeline from creation through dispatch, receiving, reconciliation and redispatch; the lead shares guest-count posting, receipt editing, attaching a receipt to a check, and applying a planogram slot. A `RetailCashier` holds no exclusive operation and shares receipt creation, receipt editing and receipt reads with the lead. A `Cashier` alone finalizes and settles combined checks, and shares tip posting, check attachment and check reads. A `Server` alone opens dining checks, and shares tip posting and check reads.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` as query parameters. `limit` caps how many items the endpoint will return; it defaults to 10, and legal values run from a minimum of 1 to a maximum of 100 inclusive — a `limit` below 1 or above 100 is not legal. `offset` states how many items to skip from the front of the result set and defaults to 0. Whenever a list response is produced, the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in addition to the envelope's standard identifiers, so a client can see both the page it received and how much more exists.

Every list endpoint orders its results by the resource's `created_at` timestamp in ascending order unless a subsection of §3 states otherwise. The relative order of two resources created at the same recorded instant is not specified; clients must not depend on it.

### 2.4. Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource in this API — `Brand`, `Location`, `StoreDay`, `Item`, `PlanogramVersion`, `PlanogramSlot`, `GuestCheck`, `Receipt`, `ClearanceSchedule`, `StaffShift`, `Transfer` and `BackroomCapacityReading` — carries a system-managed `id`. This `id` is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix, no entity name embedded in it, and no human-readable component; clients treat it as opaque and address resources by it. Each resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits (for example `2026-09-01T00:00:00.000Z`), stamped when the resource was first created.

Every one of those twelve resources carries `common_fields: true` semantics: each also has an `updated_at` and a `version`. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the most recent moment the resource's properties changed, including changes to derived fields. `version` is an integer that begins at its initial value and increments by one on every mutation of the resource, whether the mutation was requested by a client or performed by the server on the server's own initiative. No resource in this API is exempt from carrying `updated_at` and `version`; there is no immutable-only entity here.

The server does not read wall-clock time. It reads a **logical clock** that starts at exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` when the process starts and advances only when the clock-setting capability or the store-day advance capability is invoked. Every timestamp the server stamps — `closed_at`, `reconciled_at`, `settled_at`, `reconciliation_posted_at`, `reversal_posted_at`, `dispatched_at`, `received_at`, `activated_at`, `completed_at`, `published_at`, `applied_at`, `verified_at` — is taken from this logical clock, not from the host's system time, and no operation ever silently falls back to real time. Two requests that arrive at the same logical instant therefore stamp the same value, and the relative ordering of events is decided by the clock's actual value rather than by arrival order.

### 2.5. Arithmetic, Precision and Per-Line Summation

All monetary amounts in this API are integer cents. A monetary output is computed with exact decimal arithmetic and then rounded to 0 decimal places using round-half-up before it is stored or returned; there is no field in this API that stores fractional cents. Percentage-valued outputs — `tip_pct`, `markdown_pct`, `current_markdown_pct` — are rounded to 4 decimal places using round-half-up. Basis-point outputs — `variance_bps`, `waiting_area_conversion_index_bps`, `drift_bps` — are integer basis points rounded to 0 decimal places with round-half-up.

One ordering rule binds every aggregation in this document: whenever a calculation sums per-line or per-bucket values, each component is rounded first and the rounded components are then summed. Summing raw values and rounding the total afterwards is never the intended behaviour anywhere in this API, because it produces a different integer.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Versions

Three write operations require the client to present the version it last observed for a parent resource, and are rejected when that observation is stale. The field is named `expected_<parent>_version` and is non-optional on its endpoint; there is no mode in which the check is skipped.

A retail ticket may be rung with a dining check as one combined transaction. Attaching that ticket requires the client to supply `expected_guest_check_version`, carrying the `version` of the target `GuestCheck` it last read. If the check's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the write is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The refusal is specifically version-driven: the combined ticket is legitimate product behaviour whether or not another ticket was added to the check first, so the check's `check_status` alone would not reject the write — the stale observation is what does.

Reshaping a planogram slot — changing its `facings` or its `slot_position` — requires `expected_planogram_version_version`, carrying the `version` of the slot's `PlanogramVersion` that the client last read. If the version has advanced, the reshape is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Again this is version-driven: reshaping is legitimate whenever the slot is still `PLANNED`, including immediately after a sibling slot in the same version was reshaped, so a state field would not reject the write.

Posting guest counts against a store day requires `expected_store_day_version`, carrying the `version` of the `StoreDay` the client last read. If the store day's `version` has advanced, the post is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Posting guest counts is legitimate at any point while `day_status` is `OPEN`, including immediately after another count was posted, so the day's status alone would not reject the write.

These three are the only endpoints that reject a write on parent-version grounds. A fourth endpoint — the business-day close — accepts `expected_store_day_version` in its request body, but the API declares no stale-version rejection for it, so a mismatch on that field at close time is not a rejection condition and the close proceeds; the field is accepted and recorded rather than enforced. No endpoint outside these four accepts an `expected_*_version`-shaped parameter at all.

### 2.7. Left-to-Right Resource Resolution

For any request whose path names a resource, the server resolves each path segment from left to right and confirms that the named resource exists before evaluating anything else about the request. When a typed path segment resolves to nothing, the server reports a type-specific identifier: a `Brand` segment yields `ERR_BRAND_NOT_FOUND`, a `Location` segment yields `ERR_LOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, a `StoreDay` segment yields `ERR_STORE_DAY_NOT_FOUND`, an `Item` segment yields `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, a `GuestCheck` segment yields `ERR_CHECK_NOT_FOUND`, a `Receipt` segment yields `ERR_RECEIPT_NOT_FOUND`, a `Transfer` segment yields `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`, a `StaffShift` segment yields `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_FOUND`, a `ClearanceSchedule` segment yields `ERR_CLEARANCE_NOT_FOUND`, and a `PlanogramVersion` or `PlanogramSlot` segment yields `ERR_PLANOGRAM_NOT_FOUND`. A read that matches no resource at all and carries no typed path segment — a top-level collection that does not exist — reports the generic `ERR_NOT_FOUND`. All ten of these carry HTTP 404.

The same left-to-right discipline governs identifiers carried in a request body rather than a path. A request that references an `item_id` for which no `Item` exists reports `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and a request that references an unallocated `planogram_version_id` reports `ERR_PLANOGRAM_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) from the same resolution pass.

### 2.8. Conserved Quantities and Cascade Bookkeeping

Two invariants must hold at every observable moment, and operations that touch them are described in §3 in terms of the invariant rather than as ad-hoc adjustments.

The first is the shared backroom. A location's `backroom_occupancy_units` — the physical retail units measured in the narrow backroom — plus its `backroom_committed_units` — units promised to transfers that have been dispatched but not yet received — plus the standing food corridor is the burden the space carries, and whatever remains of `backroom_capacity_units` after those deductions is `staging_free_units`, the units genuinely available for a new commitment. Any operation that moves goods must leave `backroom_occupancy_units + backroom_committed_units` at a value consistent with the goods actually in or promised to the space.

The second is the brand revenue ledger. `provisional_retail_revenue_cents` and `recognized_retail_revenue_cents` on a `Brand` are recomputed — never incremented in place — from the current inclusion set of that brand's receipts, so a receipt entering, leaving or re-entering the set is reflected faithfully. `brand_retail_sales_per_guest_cents` is likewise recomputed as a guest-count-weighted roll-up rather than accumulated.

Whenever the server mutates a resource as a consequence of an operation on a different resource, the mutated resource's `version` is incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed exactly as if the client had written it directly. Cascades are therefore never invisible: a store-day close that bubbles receipts to `CLOSED` leaves each bubbled receipt with an advanced `version`, and a receiving that appends a capacity reading leaves the location with an advanced `version`.

### 2.9. Retail Price, Markdown Stage and the Dual Price Variants

An `Item` carries an everyday price, `unit_retail_cents`, which must be greater than zero, and a landed cost, `unit_cost_cents`, which is never negative. It also carries three computed price-related fields that must be read together. `current_markdown_pct` is the markdown percent applied by the latest non-reversed clearance markdown event. `effective_price_cents` is the **highest-of-stacked** price: it applies each overlapping active schedule's markdown to the everyday price in turn and never moves upward, so re-applying a stage's markdown to an already-marked item leaves the settled price where it was. `compounded_price_cents` is the **compounded-chain** price: it applies the same overlapping schedules in order, multiplying each previous result, so the same two fifty-percent stages that leave the effective price at half of retail leave the compounded price at a quarter of retail. The read of a clearance schedule exposes the difference between the two as a discount delta, `delta_cents`, equal to the effective price minus the compounded price rounded down to a whole cent.

A fourth price notion is the **liquidation tier price**, `liquidation_price_cents`, which is set only when a schedule liquidates and is not a price the item trades at during the ordinary clearance run. A fifth is the **blended liquidation price**, `liquidation_blended_cents`, which averages the liquidation buckets by unit.

A clearance schedule's `stage` is one of `S1`, `S2`, `S3`, `S4`, and the stage determines the schedule's `markdown_pct` through a fixed table: `S1` is 0.25, `S2` is 0.40, `S3` is 0.55 and `S4` is 0.70. The cadence advances exactly one stage for each completed whole seven-day week the window has spent on clearance, and it never advances past `S4`.

### 2.10. Store-Day Posture and the Conversion Gate

A store day carries two independent state axes. `day_status` is `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, and it governs whether the day may still be mutated at all: once `day_status` has reached `CLOSED` the day is terminal and is never returned to `OPEN`, and a store-day-scoped mutation against a closed day is rejected as an invalid state, reported with `ERR_STORE_DAY_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). The second axis is the pair of `traffic_state` and `conversion_gate`, which is re-derived from the day's guest count and its settlement lag on every event and governs which retail-side operations are permitted.

`traffic_state` is `QUIET`, `STEADY` or `RUSH`, and it is derived purely from `restaurant_guest_count`: at or above 120 guests the day is `RUSH`, at or above 40 but below 120 it is `STEADY`, and below 40 it is `QUIET`.

`conversion_gate` is `OPEN`, `GATED_RESTRICTED` or `SUSPENDED`. An `OPEN` gate admits every retail-side write in this document. A `SUSPENDED` gate refuses every retail-side write it guards, and such a refusal is reported with `ERR_STORE_DAY_GATE_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 409). A `GATED_RESTRICTED` gate still admits sales-floor writes but refuses new backroom load, and a receiving refused on that ground — as opposed to on the suspended ground — is reported with `ERR_STORE_DAY_GATE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409) instead. The two codes are not interchangeable and the gate's own value decides which one applies: a suspended gate reports the suspended identifier even where the restricted identifier might also have described the situation, because the suspended check is evaluated strictly earlier in the cascade of §2.12.

A third posture condition is independent of the gate. A location whose `staging_free_units` has been driven to zero or below, or whose day posture otherwise blocks a retail-side operation, refuses the operation with `ERR_POSTURE_LOCKOUT` (HTTP 409). This identifier exists because the underlying condition is a spatial or capacity fact about the location rather than a state fact about the resource being written.

### 2.11. Labor Pools, Workcodes and Tip Eligibility

A `StaffShift` is scheduled into exactly one `workcode`, drawn from the six values `SERVER`, `COOK`, `CASHIER`, `RETAIL_STOCK`, `RETAIL_CASHIER` and `RETAIL_GIFT`. These divide into two pools: the restaurant pool is `SERVER`, `COOK` and `CASHIER`, and the retail pool is `RETAIL_STOCK`, `RETAIL_CASHIER` and `RETAIL_GIFT`. The retail pool's three workcodes are front-of-house retail positions that are never tip-eligible, and within the restaurant pool only `SERVER` and `CASHIER` are tip-eligible — `COOK` is a restaurant position that is not. The workcodes `SERVER` and `CASHIER` are therefore the only tip-eligible positions in the chain.

A shift's `tip_eligible` is not a free field. It is derived, and the derivation is the only way the field acquires a truthy value: the workcode must be tip-eligible, the shift must have worked at least the brand's minimum tip-eligible minutes, and the store day's `conversion_gate` must be exactly `OPEN`. A shift whose `tip_eligible_claim` is asserted against a non-tip-eligible workcode is refused at scheduling time rather than silently recorded as ineligible, and a claim that cannot reach the minimum minute bar is likewise refused at scheduling time rather than quietly downgraded.

Tip eligibility propagates outward. A `GuestCheck` records the `StaffShift` that presented it in `presented_shift_id`, and the check's own `tip_eligible` is true only when **every** shift that presented it is tip-eligible and the check is `FINALIZED`. Because a check can be presented by more than one shift across its life, the check's eligibility is a conjunction over all presenting shifts, not the eligibility of the first or the last one. This is the mechanism by which a shift's tip status reaches the store day's commercial metrics: the day's `waiting_area_conversion_index_bps` is computed in part from the tips earned on checks whose `tip_eligible` is true and whose `check_status` is `FINALIZED`, so a shift whose eligibility changes can move the day's index.

### 2.12. Validation Precedence

Every endpoint evaluates its validations in a fixed total order, and when a request violates more than one rule the rule with the lowest priority number wins and is the only identifier the client sees. The order is literal: the server does not report a set of violations, and it does not report a later-priority violation because it happens to be easier to detect.

Authentication comes first. The presence of both headers and the well-formedness of the role is priority 1 and reports `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Permission for the operation is priority 2 and reports `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Existence of the named resources is priority 3 and reports one of the ten type-specific 404 identifiers, or the generic `ERR_NOT_FOUND` for an untyped target. Duplicate store-day creation is priority 4 and reports `ERR_STORE_DAY_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409). The fiscal-period openness check is priority 5 and reports `ERR_FISCAL_PERIOD_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). The food-storage-zone rule is priority 6 and reports `ERR_FOOD_STORAGE_ZONE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 409). State-machine membership — whether the target resource is in a state from which the requested operation is defined — is priority 7 and reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), with the closed-day case surfacing as `ERR_STORE_DAY_CLOSED` and the location-posture case as `ERR_POSTURE_LOCKOUT`. The suspended-gate rule is priority 8 and reports `ERR_STORE_DAY_GATE_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 409). Reference validity of item identifiers is priority 9 and reports `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Receipt-number uniqueness is priority 10 and reports `ERR_RECEIPT_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409). The gift-card line rule is priority 11 and reports `ERR_GIFT_CARD_LINE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The check/store-day agreement rule is priority 12 and reports `ERR_CHECK_STORE_DAY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The clearance window buffer rule is priority 13 and reports `ERR_CLEARANCE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). The active-clearance slot limit is priority 14 and reports `ERR_CLEARANCE_SLOT_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). The liquidation-finality rule is priority 15 and reports `ERR_CLEARANCE_NOT_FINAL` (HTTP 409). The combined-check ceiling is priority 16 and reports `ERR_CHECK_TOTAL_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). The staging-free-units floor is priority 17 and reports `ERR_BACKROOM_OVER_CAPACITY` (HTTP 409). The tip-workcode rule is priority 18 and reports `ERR_TIP_INELIGIBLE_WORKCODE` (HTTP 422). The shift-overlap rule is priority 19 and reports `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). The crossover-authorization rule is priority 20 and reports `ERR_CROSSOVER_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The slot-position and theme-carry rule is priority 21 and reports `ERR_PLANOGRAM_SLOT_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). The publish-readiness rule is priority 22 and reports `ERR_PLANOGRAM_VERSION_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). The regional-assortment rule is priority 23 and reports `ERR_NO_REGIONAL_ASSORTMENT` (HTTP 403). The restricted-gate receiving rule is priority 24 and reports `ERR_STORE_DAY_GATE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409) — it is deliberately later than the suspended-gate rule so that a suspended gate never reports this identifier. The reactivation-count rule is priority 25 and reports `ERR_CLEARANCE_WINDOW_REACTIVATION_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). The shift-too-short rule is priority 26 and reports `ERR_TIP_SHIFT_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422). The receiving-tolerance rule is priority 27 and reports `ERR_TRANSFER_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

Two adjacencies in that ladder are load-bearing and are called out in §3 where they are observable. A receiving that is both in the wrong state and posted on a suspended day reports the state failure, because priority 7 precedes priority 8. A retail ticket that both names an unknown item and is posted on a suspended day reports the gate failure, because priority 8 precedes priority 9.

A malformed request — a body that will not parse, a missing required field, a violated numeric bound such as a `facings` below one or a `declared_units` at or below zero, or a failed generic type constraint — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

### 2.13. Assortment Tiers and Regional Carry

A `Location` is assigned an `assortment_tier` of `FULL`, `REGIONAL_TWEAK` or `MINIMAL`, and a `region_code` drawn from `NORTHEAST`, `SOUTHEAST`, `MIDWEST`, `SOUTHWEST` and `WEST`. The tier decides which themes a store is allowed to merchandise. A `FULL` store carries every theme. A `REGIONAL_TWEAK` store carries the region's core themes plus whatever codes the location lists in `local_event_codes`. The region's core themes are `HARVEST`, `WINTER_HOLIDAY` and `NOSTALGIA_CLASSIC` for `NORTHEAST`; those three plus `SPRING` for `SOUTHEAST`; those three plus `SUMMER` for `MIDWEST`; `HARVEST`, `SUMMER` and `NOSTALGIA_CLASSIC` for `SOUTHWEST`; and `SPRING`, `SUMMER` and `NOSTALGIA_CLASSIC` for `WEST`. A `MINIMAL` store carries exactly the codes in `local_event_codes` and nothing else — where `local_event_codes` is empty, a `MINIMAL` store carries no theme at all and therefore no ordinary item. Attempting to merchandise a theme the tier does not carry is rejected with `ERR_NO_REGIONAL_ASSORTMENT` (HTTP 403), and the same refusal surfaces through the slot-conflict ladder as `ERR_PLANOGRAM_SLOT_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409) when it is raised by the slot-position rule.

An item's theme is drawn from `HARVEST`, `WINTER_HOLIDAY`, `SPRING`, `SUMMER` and `NOSTALGIA_CLASSIC`. A `nostalgia_flag` on an item marks it a year-round nostalgia-assortment member rather than a seasonal one; the flag does not change the theme, it changes how the item is treated by the clearance cadence, since two overlapping windows on one item — a seasonal-decor window and a nostalgia-assortment window whose date ranges intersect — are the ordinary case in which the dual price variants diverge.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening the Day and Reading the Waiting Room

A `BoutiqueManager` opens a business day by presenting a `location_id` and a `business_date`, the latter an ISO 8601 instant at 00:00:00Z. A location may have only one store day per business date: opening a second day for the same location and date is refused with `ERR_STORE_DAY_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409), and that check sits at priority 4, ahead of every fiscal and business rule. A freshly opened day carries `day_status` of `OPEN`, `traffic_state` of `QUIET`, `conversion_gate` of `OPEN`, and every counter at zero — `restaurant_guest_count`, `restaurant_sales_cents`, `waiting_area_shopper_count`, `waiting_area_converted_count` and `retail_transaction_count` all start at zero. Its `closed_at` is null, and it stays null for as long as `day_status` is `OPEN`.

Traffic is posted, not inferred from the dining room's own records. A `BoutiqueManager` or a `RetailLead` posts a guest count by supplying `expected_store_day_version` alongside four readings: `restaurant_guest_count`, `restaurant_sales_cents`, `waiting_area_shopper_count` and `waiting_area_converted_count`. The `expected_store_day_version` is mandatory and carries the `version` of the store day the caller last read; if the day has moved on since that read the post is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — §2.6 gives the full reasoning for why this write, unlike most, cannot be protected by a state check. A post against a day whose `day_status` is `CLOSED` is refused earlier in the ladder as `ERR_STORE_DAY_CLOSED` (HTTP 409).

Posting a count is a transition even when the day neither opens nor closes, and it has four distinct outcomes depending on where the count lands. A count at or above 120 guests moves `traffic_state` to `RUSH` and simultaneously moves `conversion_gate` to `GATED_RESTRICTED` — a full dining room restricts the retail floor's backroom intake without shutting the floor itself. A count at or above 40 and below 120 moves `traffic_state` to `STEADY` and sets `conversion_gate` to `OPEN` when no receipt of that store day is in `DISPUTED` status and to `SUSPENDED` when at least one is. A count below 40 moves `traffic_state` to `QUIET`; the gate then follows the day's disputes the same way, becoming `SUSPENDED` when at least one receipt of that store day is in `DISPUTED` status, and returning to `OPEN` when none is. In every one of the four cases the day's `waiting_area_conversion_index_bps` is recomputed, its final and provisional retail-sales-per-guest figures are recomputed, and its `version` is incremented by one.

The same guest count arriving at another value re-derives the posture again; there is no stuck state. A day that has been driven to `RUSH` by a 130-guest count and then receives a 30-guest count returns to `QUIET`, and with no disputed receipt outstanding its gate returns to `OPEN` in the same movement.

`retail_sales_cents` is a stored aggregate, not a live derivation. It is the sum of the stored `provisional_retail_cents` values of the store day's receipts whose status is one of `DRAFT`, `RECONCILED`, `SETTLED` or `CLOSED`; a receipt in `DISPUTED` status is outside that set. Because it sums stored values rather than re-deriving them from each item's `effective_price_cents`, a later markdown advance cannot retroactively move a store day's recorded retail sales — the number a day reports is the number its receipts were written with. Posting guest counts and settling a receipt both recompute this field; the settlement and close operations state the same inclusion behaviour rather than a different one.

Two different per-guest figures sit on the day, and they answer different questions. The **final** figure, `retail_sales_per_guest_cents`, divides the day's `retail_sales_cents` by the day's `restaurant_guest_count`, then rounds half-up to a whole cent. If the day's `restaurant_guest_count` is zero the figure is pinned to 0 — the empty set has the value zero, and the server never lets a division by zero escape. The **provisional** figure, `provisional_retail_sales_per_guest_cents`, uses the identical numerator but swaps the denominator to the count of guests who entered the waiting area, that is `waiting_area_shopper_count`; it too rounds half-up to a whole cent and is pinned to 0 when that denominator is zero. The two denominators are genuinely different populations — everyone seated in the dining room, versus those who stood in the retail waiting area — so the two figures are not interchangeable and the day carries both.

### 3.2. The Dining Check, Its Combined Total and Its Service Fee

A `Server` opens a dining check with a `location_id`, a `store_day_id`, a `table_number`, a `restaurant_guest_count` of at least one, a `restaurant_subtotal_cents` that is never negative, and a `presented_shift_id` naming the shift that presented the check. The check starts with `check_status` of `PENDING`, `settlement_status` of `UNSETTLED`, `retail_subtotal_cents` of zero and `tip_cents` of zero, with `tip_eligible` false and with `service_fee_cents` and `total_cents` immediately recomputed so that a freshly opened check already reports a fee and a total rather than placeholders.

The combined total is the single most involved piece of arithmetic in the system because it is recomputed on five different occasions — when the check is opened, when it is edited, when it is finalized, when a retail ticket attaches to it, and when a retail ticket attached to it is disputed — and because it deliberately treats the tip as visible to the tier walk but invisible to the fee basis. Its evaluation order is fixed and must be followed literally rather than reordered for convenience.

The retail side is gathered first. `retail_subtotal_cents` is the sum of the `provisional_retail_cents` values of every receipt attached to this check whose status is one of `DRAFT`, `RECONCILED`, `DISPUTED`, `SETTLED` or `CLOSED`. Next, a provisional total is formed by adding the check's `restaurant_subtotal_cents`, its `retail_subtotal_cents` and its `tip_cents`; this provisional total exists only to establish which fee tier the check has reached, and it is the retail side attaching to the check that moves a check across a tier boundary, since the food-and-beverage subtotal is fixed when the check is opened.

The fee itself is banded and marginal, not flat. The basis for the fee is the sum of `restaurant_subtotal_cents` and `retail_subtotal_cents` — the tip is counted when deciding which tier the check has reached, but it never forms part of the amount the fee percentages multiply, so fee is transparent to the tip while the total is not. Four bands cover the basis in ascending order: from 0 up to but not including 4000 cents, from 4000 up to but not including 8000 cents, from 8000 up to but not including 12000 cents, and from 12000 cents upward without an upper limit. The percentages are 0.00, 0.15, 0.18 and 0.20 respectively, and each percentage applies only to the portion of the basis that falls inside its own band. Band edges are inclusive at the lower edge and exclusive at the upper edge. Each band's contribution is computed by multiplying the portion of the basis inside that band by the band's percentage and rounding the product half-up to a whole cent, and the four rounded contributions are then summed; the fee is never computed by rounding a single product spanning several bands. A basis that stops before the second band produces a fee of zero.

The final total is then formed, and it is here that the tip re-enters: `total_cents` is the check's `restaurant_subtotal_cents` plus its `retail_subtotal_cents` plus its `tip_cents` plus its `service_fee_cents`. The freshly computed `service_fee_cents` must not be fed back into the tier walk to select a higher tier — the walk is performed once, against the provisional total above, and the resulting fee is added afterwards. Because the fee is banded, a check whose basis crosses a band edge does not jump its whole basis to the higher percentage; only the portion above the edge is charged at the higher rate.

A check may also carry an explicit restaurant share, and how that share was arrived at is recorded in `restaurant_share_basis`, one of `CHECK_TOTAL` or `ALLOCATED`. Under the ordinary `CHECK_TOTAL` treatment the restaurant side is simply the restaurant subtotal it was opened with. Under `ALLOCATED`, the restaurant portion is derived inside the one combined ticket rather than read off the check: the restaurant share is the check's `total_cents` multiplied by `restaurant_subtotal_cents` and divided by the sum of `restaurant_subtotal_cents` and `retail_subtotal_cents`, rounded half-up to a whole cent, and the retail share is whatever remains of `total_cents` after the restaurant share is taken. That derived restaurant figure is what a receipt carries in `restaurant_share_cents`, and the corresponding `restaurant_share_cents` and `retail_share_cents` are both exposed by the day's revenue-attribution read.

A gratuity is posted by a `Cashier` or a `Server` and is supplied as non-negative `tip_cents`. Posting a tip writes the amount, recomputes the check's `tip_pct` as the tip divided by the sum of `restaurant_subtotal_cents` and `retail_subtotal_cents` rounded half-up to four decimal places, recomputes `total_cents` — which, per the fixed evaluation order above, means the tip is now part of the provisional total used for tier selection and is also added in the final total — and increments the check's `version`. Tip posting does not by itself finalize the check, and it does not by itself make the check tip-eligible.

A check is finalized by a `Cashier`. Finalization recomputes `retail_subtotal_cents` once more from the receipts then attached, recomputes `service_fee_cents` through the banded fee function described above, recomputes `total_cents`, recomputes the check's `tip_eligible` from its presenting shift, and moves `check_status` to `FINALIZED`. Only a check in `PENDING` may be edited through the check-edit capability, which lets a `BoutiqueManager` or a `Cashier` revise `restaurant_guest_count` and `restaurant_subtotal_cents` and re-derive the fee and the total; the edit capability does not touch the retail side, whose contents follow the attached receipts.

Settlement is independent of finalization. A `Cashier` settles a check with a `payment_method` and, optionally, a `restaurant_share_basis`, moving `settlement_status` to `SETTLED`; the two status axes move separately, so a check can be `FINALIZED` while still `UNSETTLED`, and the settlement status has a third value `REFUNDED` for tender that has been given back.

A `BoutiqueManager` may void a check outright. Voiding moves `check_status` to `VOIDED` and cascades: every receipt attached to that check whose status is `RECONCILED` or `SETTLED` is moved to `DISPUTED` with `reversal_posted_at` stamped, and the check's store day has its `retail_sales_cents`, its `retail_sales_per_guest_cents` and its `retail_transaction_count` recomputed. The void increments both the check's `version` and the store day's `version`.

Two ceilings constrain the check. A check that has already reached `FINALIZED` or `VOIDED` will not accept a further retail line; an attempt to attach one is refused with `ERR_CHECK_ALREADY_FINALIZED` (HTTP 409). The same terminal-state refusal, carrying the same identifier, rejects a finalize attempt on a check that has already reached `FINALIZED` or `VOIDED`. And the combined check is capped in aggregate: a receipt that would push a check's `total_cents` above the brand's `max_check_total_cents` is refused with `ERR_CHECK_TOTAL_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), a rule that sits at priority 16 and applies both when a ticket is first rung against a check and when an existing ticket's lines are edited. Posting a gratuity is checked against the same ceiling: a tip that would carry the check's `total_cents` past the brand's `max_check_total_cents` is likewise refused with `ERR_CHECK_TOTAL_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), and the tip is left unwritten.

### 3.3. Ringing the Retail Ticket and Attaching It to a Check

Type-specific reads address a single resource by the identifying path segment — a brand by its `brandId` segment, a location by its `locationId` segment, and so on for the other ten entity types — while their list forms take the filter parameters named in §3.9.

A `RetailLead` or a `RetailCashier` rings a retail ticket by supplying a `location_id`, a `store_day_id`, an optional `guest_check_id`, a `receipt_number`, a `lines` array, an optional `restaurant_share_cents`, an optional `restaurant_share_basis`, a `payment_method` of `CASH`, `CARD`, `GIFT_CARD` or `SPLIT`, and an optional `expected_guest_check_version`. Each element of `lines` describes one ticket line and carries an `item_id`, a `quantity`, a `unit_price_cents`, a `discount_pct` and a `line_kind`. A ticket that names no check is a standalone retail sale recorded against the store day; a ticket that names a check is the retail half of a combined transaction. The new ticket begins in `DRAFT` status, and its `provisional_retail_cents` is computed immediately.

The ticket's provisional revenue is computed line by line and then aggregated, with the rounding performed per line before any summation. For each line, a gross amount is the item's `effective_price_cents` multiplied by the line's `quantity`. A net amount is then the gross amount minus the gross amount multiplied by the line's `discount_pct`, that product rounded half-up to two decimal places before it is subtracted. The base block is the sum of those net amounts. A sale-staging block is then formed by taking each net amount, multiplying it by the staging factor of 0.36, and rounding that product half-up to two decimal places, and summing the rounded results. On a ticket that rides on a dining check the provisional figure is the base block minus the staging block divided by seven, that quotient rounded half-up to two decimal places. On a standalone retail ticket — one with no `guest_check_id` — no staging block applies at all and the provisional figure is simply the sum of the net line amounts. Only lines whose `line_kind` is one of `RETAIL`, `SEASONAL_DECOR` or `NOSTALGIA` enter either block; a line whose `line_kind` is `GIFT_CARD` is excluded from the base block entirely and therefore contributes nothing to the provisional figure.

Several referential rules guard the ticket. Its `receipt_number` must be unique within the location and the business date of the store day it names, and a collision is refused with `ERR_RECEIPT_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409) at priority 10. A ticket may attach only to a check whose `store_day_id` equals the ticket's own `store_day_id`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_CHECK_STORE_DAY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) at priority 12, and the same rule guards editing an existing ticket. A `GIFT_CARD` line may appear at most once on a ticket and only when the ticket's `payment_method` is `GIFT_CARD`; a line that appears twice, or appears under any other tender, is refused with `ERR_GIFT_CARD_LINE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) at priority 11. And every `item_id` named by the ticket's lines must resolve to an existing item, with a failure reported as `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) at priority 9.

The attach itself is version-pinned. When a ticket is rung with a `guest_check_id`, the caller must also supply `expected_guest_check_version` carrying the version of that check it last read. If the check has advanced since — because another ticket was attached, or a tip was posted, or the check was finalized — the attach is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This is exactly the situation §2.6 describes: nothing about the check's own status makes the second ticket illegitimate, so only the stale observation rejects it. A ticket may also be moved onto a check after it has been rung, through a separate attach capability shared by the `RetailLead` and the `Cashier`, which likewise requires `expected_guest_check_version` and returns the ticket.

Ringing a ticket has a wide fan-out. The ticket increments the store day's `retail_transaction_count` and triggers a recomputation of the store day's `retail_sales_cents`. When it is attached to a check, the check's `retail_subtotal_cents`, `service_fee_cents` and `total_cents` are all recomputed per §3.2, which is precisely why the retail side can move a check across a fee band. A newly created ticket increments the store day's `version` and the location's `version`, and it increments the check's `version` when a check was named. Editing a `DRAFT` ticket through the ticket-edit capability, which lets a `RetailLead` or a `RetailCashier` revise `lines`, `restaurant_share_cents`, `restaurant_share_basis` or `payment_method`, recomputes the ticket's provisional figure and every attached check's totals and increments the ticket's `version`.

One asymmetry is worth stating explicitly, because it is easy to assume away. Attaching a ticket to a check through the post-rung attach capability sets the ticket's `guest_check_id` and makes the ticket's `store_day_id` follow the check; it recomputes the check's `retail_subtotal_cents`, `service_fee_cents` and `total_cents`, and it increments the ticket's `version`, the check's `version` and the store day's `version`. Its effect on the ticket's own `provisional_retail_cents` is the same base-versus-retail-only switch described above, because a ticket that has just acquired a `guest_check_id` has crossed from the standalone computation to the base computation. And like every other retail-side write a ticket participates in, the attach is gated on the store day's posture per §2.10, with a `SUSPENDED` gate refusing it and reporting `ERR_STORE_DAY_GATE_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 409).

### 3.4. Recognizing Retail Revenue and Disputing It

A ticket's retail revenue is recognized in two stages, and the brand decides how many of those stages are actually run. A `Brand` carries `retail_recognition_policy`, one of `FINAL_ONLY` or `PROVISIONAL_THEN_FINAL`, defaulting to `FINAL_ONLY`. Under `FINAL_ONLY` the provisional posting is skipped and the final figure is written straight to `recognized_retail_cents`, with the difference figure `recognition_delta_cents` left at zero because there is only ever one posting. Under `PROVISIONAL_THEN_FINAL` the provisional figure is written at reconciliation, the final figure overwrites the recognized amount at store-day close, and `recognition_delta_cents` carries the difference between the two postings, equal to `recognized_retail_cents` minus `provisional_retail_cents`. The delta field exists on every ticket in both policies; under `FINAL_ONLY` it is written as zero — there is only ever the one posting — so it reads zero even though the recognized and provisional figures are themselves the two different variants.

Reconciliation is the act that posts retail revenue, and it is performed by a `RetailLead`. A ticket moves from `DRAFT` to `RECONCILED` only when it is currently in `DRAFT` and either it names no check at all or the check it names has a `check_status` that is not `VOIDED`. Reconciliation recomputes both `provisional_retail_cents` and `recognized_retail_cents`, writes `recognition_delta_cents` under the brand's recognition policy — the difference between the recognized and provisional figures under `PROVISIONAL_THEN_FINAL` and zero under `FINAL_ONLY` — stamps `reconciliation_posted_at`, stamps `reconciled_at` from the logical clock, and moves the status to `RECONCILED`. It also recomputes the brand's `provisional_retail_revenue_cents` and `recognized_retail_revenue_cents` over the brand's current inclusion set, and it increments the ticket's `version`, the brand's `version` and the store day's `version`.

The **final** figure that a `PROVISIONAL_THEN_FINAL` brand writes at store-day close is a different number for byte-identical inputs, and the difference is structural rather than incidental. It is computed from the same per-line net amounts and the same staging factor of 0.36 as the provisional figure, and it uses the same per-line rounding discipline, but the divisor on the staging block is three rather than seven. Concretely, the final recognized amount is the base block — the sum of the per-line net amounts — minus the staging block divided by three, that quotient rounded half-up to two decimal places, where the staging block is the sum of each line's net amount multiplied by 0.36 and rounded half-up to two decimal places. Only lines whose `line_kind` is one of `RETAIL`, `SEASONAL_DECOR` or `NOSTALGIA` enter the final computation, exactly as in the provisional one. Because seven and three are not proportional, a ticket reconciled and then closed reports two different recognized amounts, and that divergence is the intended behaviour rather than a bug to be smoothed over.

The brand's two revenue totals are recomputed rather than incremented, and their inclusion set is the set of that brand's receipts whose status lies in `RECONCILED`, `SETTLED` or `CLOSED`. A receipt entering that set raises both totals; a receipt whose status leaves that set lowers them. Two operations move a receipt out of the set and two move it back in, and all four recompute the brand totals from scratch rather than applying a signed adjustment.

The first is a dispute raised by a `BoutiqueManager` against a `RECONCILED` ticket. A dispute requires the ticket to be in `RECONCILED` status and requires the logical clock's current instant to be no later than the ticket's `reconciled_at` plus the brand's `open_receipt_grace_hours`. Meeting those conditions, the dispute stamps `reversal_posted_at` — which is what makes the ticket's posting eligible for reversal — moves the status to `DISPUTED`, removes the ticket from the brand's inclusion set, recomputes both brand totals, and increments the ticket's `version`, the brand's `version` and the store day's `version`. A ticket that has sat unreconciled past the grace window can no longer be disputed at all, and the brand's `open_receipt_grace_hours` defaults to 24.

The second is a dispute raised against a `SETTLED` ticket, which is the manager's reversal before the day is put to bed. It requires the ticket to be `SETTLED` and requires the referenced `StoreDay` to still have `day_status` of `OPEN` — a retail-only ticket names no check, so the guard is the day's own status rather than a check's. This dispute stamps `reversal_posted_at`, removes the ticket from the store day's `retail_sales_cents` aggregation, recomputes the day's `retail_sales_cents` and `retail_sales_per_guest_cents`, decrements the day's `retail_transaction_count` by one, recomputes the brand's `recognized_retail_revenue_cents` over the inclusion set, and increments the ticket's `version`. Disputing a settled ticket is where the day's transaction count moves downward, so a test that settles then disputes should see the count fall back.

Resolution is the way back, and it is also a `BoutiqueManager` action. A disputed ticket returns to `RECONCILED` when it is currently `DISPUTED` and the logical clock is still no later than the ticket's `reconciled_at` plus the brand's `open_receipt_grace_hours`. Resolution clears `reversal_posted_at`, recomputes the provisional and recognized amounts on the ticket's current line set, re-enters the ticket into the brand's inclusion set, recomputes both brand totals, and increments the ticket's `version`, the brand's `version` and the store day's `version`. A ticket that was disputed inside the grace window but whose window has since elapsed cannot be resolved; the clock is re-checked at resolution time, not merely at dispute time.

Settlement is a `RetailLead` action on a reconciled ticket. A ticket may settle only when it is in `RECONCILED` status and the logical clock's current instant is at or after the ticket's `reconciled_at`; settling stamps `settled_at`, recomputes the store day's `retail_sales_cents` over its inclusion set, and increments the ticket's `version` and the store day's `version`. Settlement does not change the recognized amount, does not move the ticket out of the brand's inclusion set — `SETTLED` is inside that set — and does not touch the ticket's `provisional_retail_cents`. A ticket reaches `CLOSED` only when its store day closes, and `CLOSED` is the ticket's terminal status: a closed ticket accepts no dispute, resolution, settle or edit, and re-entering the lifecycle from it is not possible.

Recognition is fiscally bounded. Any action that posts revenue into a brand period — reconciliation and store-day close are the two — checks that the target business date's fiscal period, named by the brand's `fiscal_period` and carrying a `fiscal_period_status` of `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, is still `OPEN`. Once the period has been closed, recognition is refused with `ERR_FISCAL_PERIOD_CLOSED` (HTTP 422), a check that sits at priority 5, ahead of every state and safety rule. Closing a period is done by updating the brand, and reopening it is done the same way; there is no dedicated period-closing operation.

### 3.5. Closing the Day, the Waiting-Area Conversion Index, and the Clearance Trigger

Closing the business day is a `BoutiqueManager` action and the widest single cascade in the system. It requires the day to still be `OPEN` and requires that every receipt carrying that store day's identifier has a status other than `DRAFT` — a day with an un-reconciled ticket still sitting in draft cannot be closed. Closing accepts `expected_store_day_version` in its body, but §2.6's three stale-version rejections do not include this endpoint, so a mismatch on it is accepted and the close proceeds.

The close sweeps in a fixed order. Every receipt of that store day currently in `SETTLED` status is bubbled to `CLOSED` with `closed_at` stamped from the logical clock, and each bubbled receipt has its `recognized_retail_cents` written with the final variant — the base block less the staging block divided by three — under both recognition policies, since the final figure is what `recognized_retail_cents` holds once the day is closed, while its `recognition_delta_cents` is written under the brand's policy exactly as reconciliation writes it; a receipt reaches `CLOSED` only by this bubble-up, since the receipt's own settled-to-closed transition is the system's, not a client's. The store day's `retail_sales_cents`, its final `retail_sales_per_guest_cents` and its provisional `provisional_retail_sales_per_guest_cents` are recomputed. Then `day_status` moves to `CLOSED` and `closed_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The brand's `provisional_retail_revenue_cents` and `recognized_retail_revenue_cents` are recomputed over the brand's inclusion set, and the brand's `brand_retail_sales_per_guest_cents` is recomputed as a guest-count-weighted roll-up across the closed store days of the brand's `fiscal_period` — the day just closed being one of them. Then the clearance consequence fires, comparing the freshly computed `retail_sales_per_guest_cents`, and finally the store day's `version` is incremented by one.

The clearance consequence is the reason a retail metric reaches into merchandising. If the closing day's freshly computed `retail_sales_per_guest_cents` is below the brand's `liquidation_trigger_rpg_cents`, then every `ClearanceSchedule` of that location currently in `ACTIVE` status whose `end_date` lies before the close-time instant is moved to `EXTENDED`, its `extension_count` is increased by one, and its `end_date` is pushed out by 14 days. The threshold defaults to 700 cents — that is, seven dollars of retail sales per restaurant guest. The comparison is strict: a day whose figure exactly equals the threshold does not trigger, and a day one cent below it does. A day that has just been extended by this rule occupies its clearance slot exactly as before, because an `EXTENDED` schedule counts against the location's active limit every bit as much as an `ACTIVE` one does. The close also increments the `version` of every `ClearanceSchedule` it extends.

The waiting-area conversion index is the day's commercial score, and it is recomputed on four occasions: when a guest count is posted, when a check is finalized, and when a shift is completed or cancelled. It combines two very different signals — whether waiting-area traffic actually converted into purchases, and how much of the retail take was earned by staff working for tips — and the index deliberately dilutes the first as the day racks up transactions.

The raw conversion rate is expressed in basis points of waiting-area shoppers. It is the day's `waiting_area_converted_count` multiplied by ten thousand, divided by the day's `waiting_area_shopper_count` plus one, rounded half-up to a whole basis point. The plus-one in the denominator is intentional and is not a guard against an empty waiting area; it is part of the formula. A dilutive decay is then applied: each retail transaction the day has already recorded drags the raw rate down by ten percent of itself, and that cumulative drag is capped at fifty percent. The decayed rate is the raw rate multiplied by one minus the decay, rounded half-up to a whole basis point. If the day has recorded no retail transactions the decay is zero and the decayed rate equals the raw rate; if it has recorded five or more, the decay is at its fifty-percent cap.

The second signal is the tip share. If the day's `retail_sales_cents` is zero the tip share is pinned to 0; otherwise it is the day's earned tips multiplied by ten thousand and divided by the day's `retail_sales_cents`, rounded half-up to a whole basis point. Earned tips are the sum of `tip_cents` across the checks of that store day whose `tip_eligible` is true and whose `check_status` is `FINALIZED` — both conditions are required, so a generous tip on a check that is not tip-eligible, or a tip-eligible check that has not been finalized, contributes nothing. The index then blends the two signals by the brand's `waiting_area_retail_weight_bps`: the index is the weight multiplied by the decayed conversion rate, plus ten thousand minus the weight multiplied by the tip share, that sum divided by ten thousand and rounded half-up to a whole basis point. At the default weight of six thousand basis points the conversion rate carries sixty percent of the index and the tip share carries forty.

The index is therefore the loop that closes back on labor. A shift's tip eligibility determines which checks are tip-eligible, the tip-eligible finalized checks determine the day's earned tips, earned tips determine the tip share, and the tip share determines the index. §3.6 describes the step that starts this loop.

### 3.6. Scheduling, Working and Correcting Shifts Across Two Labor Pools

A `BoutiqueManager` schedules a shift by supplying a `location_id`, an opaque `employee_id`, a `start_utc`, an `end_utc`, a `workcode`, an optional `tip_eligible_claim` and an optional `crossover_authorized_by_shift_id`. A new shift starts with `status` of `SCHEDULED`, `tip_eligible` false and `tip_eligible_minutes` of zero. The worked interval is half-open, running from `start_utc` inclusive to `end_utc` exclusive.

Five rules guard scheduling, and they are not all about the same thing. A shift claiming tip eligibility must be scheduled into one of the two tip-eligible workcodes, `SERVER` or `CASHIER`; a claim asserted against a front-of-house retail workcode is refused with `ERR_TIP_INELIGIBLE_WORKCODE` (HTTP 422) at priority 18 rather than being quietly recorded as ineligible. A shift that asserts tip eligibility may also not be shorter than the brand's tip minimum — a claim that cannot reach the bar is refused with `ERR_TIP_SHIFT_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422) at priority 26, again rather than being downgraded silently. The brand's minimum defaults to 240 minutes.

A shift may not double-book an employee. Within one location and for one `employee_id`, no second shift's interval may overlap the first by a minute or more; a back-to-back pair that touches at a single instant is permitted, because the intervals are half-open and the earlier shift's `end_utc` does not include the later shift's `start_utc`. An overlap is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) at priority 19. Note that this rule is scoped to the location and the employee together, so the same employee may hold non-overlapping shifts at different locations.

The crossover rule is what keeps the two labor pools honest. For this rule the restaurant pool is exactly `SERVER` and `CASHIER` and the retail pool is exactly `RETAIL_STOCK`, `RETAIL_CASHIER` and `RETAIL_GIFT`. A single shift may not cover work in both pools unless it carries `crossover_authorized_by_shift_id` naming a manager shift that is currently in progress and authorizes the crossover. A shift that spans both pools without that authorizing reference is refused with `ERR_CROSSOVER_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403) at priority 20. The authorizing reference is a real `StaffShift` reference recorded on the shift, and it is the only thing that makes a cross-pool shift legitimate.

Starting a shift is a `BoutiqueManager` action that requires the shift to be `SCHEDULED`, requires the logical clock's current instant to be at or after the shift's `start_utc`, and requires the referenced store day's `conversion_gate` to be anything other than `SUSPENDED` — a suspended gate blocks the retail side of the operation even when the shift itself is a restaurant position. Starting derives the shift's tip eligibility in full.

The derivation is threshold-based and has a rounding step that is easy to get wrong in one direction only. Tip eligibility requires the shift's workcode to be one of `SERVER` or `CASHIER`. The clocked minute count, `tip_eligible_minutes`, is not the exact interval; the shift's length in hours is rounded **up** to a whole number of hours and then multiplied by sixty to yield minutes, and a shift shorter than a full hour yields a minute count of zero rather than the sixty a plain ceiling would give. A shift of three hours and five minutes therefore counts as four hours, that is 240 minutes, and does cross a 240-minute bar; the ceiling is applied before the minute conversion, and flooring first would lose exactly the boundary cases that matter. The shift is then tip-eligible only when all three conditions hold together: the workcode is tip-eligible, the rounded-up minute count is at least the brand's `tip_minimum_minutes`, and the referenced store day's `conversion_gate` is exactly `OPEN`. A shift whose rounded-up length is zero hours — one shorter than an hour — has a minute count of zero and is ineligible regardless of its workcode. At the default minimum of 240 minutes, a four-hour shift meets the bar exactly and a three-hour-fifty-nine-minute shift that rounds up to four hours also meets it.

Starting a shift then cascades outward into the tip loop that §3.5 closes. Every `GuestCheck` that this employee presented — recorded on the check as `presented_shift_id` — has its `tip_eligible` recomputed as the conjunction over all shifts that reference it: a check is tip-eligible only when **every** presenting shift is tip-eligible, so a second, ineligible presenter can withdraw eligibility from a check that a first presenter established. Each recomputed check has its `tip_pct` recomputed. The store day's `waiting_area_conversion_index_bps` is recomputed over the day's presenting shifts. The shift's own `version` is incremented.

Completion is the counterpart. A `BoutiqueManager` completes a shift that is currently `IN_PROGRESS` and whose `start_utc` the logical clock has reached; completion stamps `completed_at`, recomputes `tip_eligible_minutes` over the worked interval and recomputes `tip_eligible` through the same three-condition derivation, recomputes every presented check's `tip_eligible` across all presenting shifts, and recomputes the store day's conversion index. Completion additionally has a spatial consequence: the shift's location becomes eligible for the next receiving window. This is the hop by which a labor event reaches the backroom — closing a crossover shift is what lets the location take goods again. `COMPLETED` is one of the shift's two terminal states and a shift that has reached it accepts no further transition.

Reverting and cancelling are the correction paths and they behave differently, which is deliberate. Reverting a shift — permitted while it is `IN_PROGRESS` and while the logical clock is no later than the shift's `end_utc` plus the brand's `open_receipt_grace_hours`, the same grace constant the dispute window uses — returns the status to `SCHEDULED`, sets `tip_eligible` false and `tip_eligible_minutes` to zero, and recomputes every presented check that is `FINALIZED`. Cancelling a shift — permitted while it is `SCHEDULED` and either the shift does not claim tip eligibility or the brand's `fiscal_period_status` is still `OPEN` — moves the status to `CANCELLED`, leaves `completed_at` unstamped, and recomputes every presented check whose `check_status` is `FINALIZED` to `tip_eligible` false. `CANCELLED` is the shift's other terminal state, so a cancelled shift is as final as a completed one. Both paths recompute the store day's conversion index and increment the shift's `version`, because both remove a tip-eligible presenter from the day's arithmetic.

A note on the fiscal guard in cancellation: a shift that claims tip eligibility cannot be cancelled once the brand's period has been closed, because the tip liability it carries has already been recognized. A shift that claims nothing may be cancelled at any time while it is still `SCHEDULED`.

### 3.7. Receiving Into the Shared Backroom

A chain begins as a `Brand`, created by a `PlatformAdmin` with a `brand_code` unique across the fleet and immutable once created, together with a `legal_name` recording the registered entity, a `fiscal_period` label such as `2026-Q3`, and an optional `retail_recognition_policy`. The remaining brand inputs all carry defaults and may be omitted: `liquidation_trigger_rpg_cents` defaults to 700 cents, `open_receipt_grace_hours` to 24, `transfer_variance_tolerance_bps` to 200, `waiting_area_retail_weight_bps` to 6000, `tip_minimum_minutes` to 240, `clearance_buffer_hours` to 24, `food_storage_corridor_units` to 40 and `max_check_total_cents` to 50000. A new brand starts with `fiscal_period_status` of `OPEN` and with `provisional_retail_revenue_cents`, `recognized_retail_revenue_cents` and `brand_retail_sales_per_guest_cents` all at zero. A `PlatformAdmin` later updates the brand to change any of those configuration values including the `fiscal_period` label and the `fiscal_period_status`, which is how a reporting period is opened or closed.

A `Location` is created by a `PlatformAdmin` with a `brand_id`, a `store_number` unique within the brand, a `region_code`, an `assortment_tier`, a `backroom_capacity_units` greater than zero, an optional `clearance_max_active` defaulting to 3 and an optional `local_event_codes` defaulting to an empty list. A new location starts with `backroom_occupancy_units` of zero, `backroom_committed_units` of zero and `food_storage_units` of zero, with `active_clearance_count` of zero, and with `staging_free_units` already set to `backroom_capacity_units` minus the brand's `food_storage_corridor_units`. That last figure is the standing corridor the shared space must never lose: the default of 40 units is food storage that retail receiving may not displace, so a location's usable staging space is its capacity less that corridor from the moment it is created.

`staging_free_units` is the derived quantity every receiving decision turns on, and it is defined as `backroom_capacity_units` minus `backroom_occupancy_units` minus `backroom_committed_units` minus the brand's `food_storage_corridor_units`. Its two variable terms move in opposite phases of a transfer's life, which is what makes the accounting conserved: while a transfer is in flight its `declared_units` are committed, and when it lands those units stop being committed and become occupancy.

A `RetailLead` creates a transfer with a `location_id`, a `store_day_id`, an `item_id`, a `declared_units` greater than zero, a `received_units` that is never negative, and a `receiving_zone` of `BACKROOM` or `FOOD_STORAGE`. A new transfer starts in `PLANNED` with `variance_units` and `variance_bps` both zero. The shared space is retail-only, and the zone rule enforces that bluntly: a transfer whose `receiving_zone` is `FOOD_STORAGE` is refused outright when it carries item lines in any category at all, because food storage is never used for retail merchandise. That refusal is `ERR_FOOD_STORAGE_ZONE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 409) and it applies both when the transfer is created and when it is received. Every `item_id` a transfer names must also resolve to an existing item, with a failure reported as `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) at priority 9.

Dispatching moves a `PLANNED` transfer to `IN_TRANSIT` and is the step that stakes a claim on the space. Dispatch requires either that the location's `staging_free_units` is at least one, or that the transfer's own `declared_units` does not exceed the location's `backroom_capacity_units` outright. On success the transfer's `dispatched_at` is stamped, the location's `backroom_committed_units` is increased by the transfer's `declared_units`, and `staging_free_units` is recomputed. If that recomputation drives `staging_free_units` to zero or below, the transfer's store day has its `conversion_gate` moved to `GATED_RESTRICTED` — the shared space is now fully spoken for, so the floor may still sell but the backroom will not take more. The transfer's `version` is incremented. Dispatch is one of the three operations a `SUSPENDED` gate refuses at priority 8.

Receiving moves an `IN_TRANSIT` transfer to `RECEIVED` on a single supplied reading, `received_units`. The physical count is written to the transfer, `received_at` is stamped, and the variance is recorded in two forms: `variance_units` is the received count minus the declared count, and `variance_bps` is the absolute value of that difference multiplied by ten thousand and divided by the declared count, rounded half-up to a whole basis point. Before the write is accepted, the staging-free-units floor of priority 17 is evaluated against the projected post-receiving posture — the location's current `staging_free_units`, adjusted by releasing the claim staked at dispatch and by taking in the received count — and a projection that would drive `staging_free_units` below zero is refused with `ERR_BACKROOM_OVER_CAPACITY` (HTTP 409); that priority-17 floor is evaluated before the priority-24 restricted-gate rule, because the numeric ladder of §2.12 is a total order. The location's `backroom_committed_units` is then reduced by the transfer's `declared_units`, releasing the claim staked at dispatch, and `staging_free_units` is recomputed.

Receiving then re-measures the space rather than trusting the arithmetic. A `BackroomCapacityReading` is appended for that location and store day with `source` of `RECEIVING`, with `backroom_units` equal to the received count and with `trigger_transfer_id` pointing back at the transfer. That reading carries its own `measured_at`, its own `food_storage_units` and `dated_units`, and derives `occupancy_units` as the sum of `backroom_units`, `food_storage_units` and `dated_units`. It snapshots the location's `backroom_committed_units` into its own `committed_units`, derives `free_units` as the location's `backroom_capacity_units` minus its `occupancy_units` minus its `committed_units`, and sets `capacity_breach` true when that `free_units` is below the brand's `food_storage_corridor_units`. The location's `backroom_occupancy_units`, `food_storage_units` and `staging_free_units` are all overwritten from the reading, so the reading is authoritative and the location's posture is a projection of the most recent measurement rather than a running tally.

Two consequences follow the goods. When the transfer's `receiving_zone` is `BACKROOM`, the item's `on_hand_units` is increased by the received count; a transfer staged into `FOOD_STORAGE` never reaches this step, because the zone rule refuses it earlier. And when the appended reading's `capacity_breach` is true, every `ClearanceSchedule` of that location in status `ACTIVE` or `DRAFT` — note that both statuses are swept, not only the live ones — has its markdown stage advanced by one step along `S1` to `S2` to `S3` to `S4`, capped at `S4`, with `markdown_pct`, the item's `current_markdown_pct`, `effective_price_cents` and `compounded_price_cents` all recomputed. A backroom that has burst its corridor therefore marks the store's seasonal goods down a stage immediately. The transfer's `version` is incremented.

The same breach rule fires on a manual measurement. A `BoutiqueManager` records a manual capacity reading for a location with a `store_day_id`, a `measured_at`, a `backroom_units`, a `food_storage_units`, a `dated_units` and a `source` of `RECEIVING`, `SALES_FLOOR_MOVE` or `CYCLE_COUNT`; a manual reading names no `trigger_transfer_id`. The reading derives `occupancy_units`, `committed_units`, `free_units` and `capacity_breach` exactly as above, overwrites the location's occupancy, food-storage and staging-free figures, and advances the markdown stage of every `ACTIVE` or `DRAFT` clearance schedule of the location by one step when the breach flag is true. It increments the location's `version`.

Reconciliation closes a receiving's variance. A transfer moves from `RECEIVED` to `RECONCILED` — and thereby to its terminal status — only when its `variance_bps` is at or below the brand's `transfer_variance_tolerance_bps`, a tolerance that defaults to 200 basis points of the declared units. A larger shortfall cannot be reconciled at all and is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) at priority 27; such a transfer stays un-reconciled until it is voided or re-dispatched. This check sits later in the ladder than the suspended-gate check, so a receipted transfer with an excessive variance posted against a suspended day reports the gate failure rather than the tolerance failure. A transfer within tolerance that is reconciled by the system automatically also increments its `version`.

Voiding a receiving is the `BoutiqueManager` reversal, and it is deliberately hard to reach. A transfer may be voided only when it is `RECEIVED`, only when its `variance_bps` exceeds the brand's `transfer_variance_tolerance_bps` — the same threshold that would have permitted reconciliation now becomes the gate that permits reversal — and only when its store day still has `day_status` of `OPEN`. A `RECEIVED` transfer whose variance is within tolerance is refused on the tolerance ground itself, reported with `ERR_TRANSFER_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) — the identifier of the receiving-tolerance rule at priority 27 — because that rule, not a generic state failure, is the ground on which the reversal is denied. Voiding moves the status to `VOID_RECEIVING`, reverses the item's stock by subtracting the received count and adding back the declared count, and appends a compensating reading with `source` of `RECEIVING`, `backroom_units` of negative the received count, and `trigger_transfer_id` pointing at the same transfer. The location's `backroom_occupancy_units` and `staging_free_units` are recomputed from that compensating reading, so the conserved quantity `backroom_occupancy_units + backroom_committed_units` is restored to exactly the value it held before the receiving happened. The transfer's `version` is incremented.

Re-dispatch closes the loop. A transfer in `VOID_RECEIVING` returns to `IN_TRANSIT` under the same admission test dispatch uses — either `staging_free_units` at least one, or `declared_units` within the location's capacity — increasing `backroom_committed_units` by the declared count and recomputing `staging_free_units`, and resetting `received_units`, `variance_units` and `variance_bps` all to zero so the corrected manifest starts clean. The transfer's `version` is incremented.

A discrepancy read exposes the reconciliation arithmetic for a transfer without mutating it: it reports the transfer's `variance_units` and `variance_bps`, the brand's `tolerance_bps`, whether the variance is `within_tolerance`, and the `conserved_backroom_units` the location currently carries. It is a pure read and changes nothing.

### 3.8. The Clearance Cadence and Its Two Divergent Prices

Seasonal decor does not go on sale once; it walks down. A `ClearanceSchedule` is created for one item at one location by a `BoutiqueManager` or an `AssortmentPlanner`, who supplies a `location_id`, an `item_id`, a `start_date`, an `end_date` that must be at or after the `start_date`, and `expected_item_version` recording the version of the target item the caller last read. The schedule opens in `DRAFT` status with `stage` of `S1`, `markdown_pct` of zero — note that a draft schedule carries no markdown at all despite sitting at `S1` — `remaining_units` of zero, `liquidated_units` of zero, `extension_count` of zero and `reactivation_count` of zero.

Two rules govern where a window may be placed. The window is treated as the half-open interval from its `start_date` inclusive to its `end_date` exclusive, and no two non-cancelled windows on the same location and item pair may be closer than the brand's `clearance_buffer_hours`, which defaults to 24 hours. A gap of zero between windows is a rejection, not an acceptance, and the refusal is `ERR_CLEARANCE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) at priority 13 — it guards the initial creation of a schedule, the reactivation of an extended one, and any other placement of a window on the same pair. Separately, every `item_id` a schedule names must resolve to an existing item, with a failure reported as `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) at priority 9.

Activation is what puts an item on clearance, and it is a `BoutiqueManager` action. A schedule in `DRAFT` may be activated only when the logical clock's current instant falls within the window — that is, at or after the `start_date` and strictly before the `end_date` — and only when the location's `active_clearance_count` is still below the location's `clearance_max_active`, a limit that defaults to 3 and must be at least 1. A location already at its limit refuses the activation with `ERR_CLEARANCE_SLOT_LIMIT` (HTTP 409) — the active-clearance slot limit of priority 14, which is evaluated before the condition that the clock fall inside the window, so a location at its cap reports the slot limit rather than the invalid-state refusal the window condition would otherwise produce — and the same limit is exposed as a slot-count check whenever a new clearance window is proposed for the location. Meeting those conditions, activation stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock, freezes the schedule's `remaining_units` at the item's current `on_hand_units`, sets `stage` to `S1` and applies that stage's markdown percent of 0.25, mirrors that percent onto the item's `current_markdown_pct`, moves the item's `status` to `CLEARANCE`, recomputes the item's `effective_price_cents` and `compounded_price_cents` through the stacked-markdown arithmetic below, increases the location's `active_clearance_count` by one, and increments the schedule's `version` and the item's `version`.

The `remaining_units` snapshot deserves care because it is the one place in this system where a quantity is deliberately frozen and must not be moved twice. When the schedule first activates, `remaining_units` is a snapshot of the item's `on_hand_units`. From that moment the item's `on_hand_units` is the live quantity and is what the liquidation transition decrements; the schedule's `remaining_units` is its own record and is decremented only by the schedule's own liquidation event. Subtracting a liquidation from both the item and the schedule's snapshot — or from the snapshot alone while also treating it as live — double-counts the goods, and the two figures must be allowed to diverge between activation and liquidation rather than being kept permanently equal.

The cadence advances on a marketing-reset event raised against a store day, which sets the logical clock to a supplied `from_iso8601` instant and then walks every clearance schedule of that store day's location forward. A schedule in `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED` whose time since `activated_at` has crossed a further whole seven-day week advances its stage by one step for each completed week the crossing has accumulated, capped at `S4`; a schedule that has crossed two whole weeks advances two stages in that one event. When an `ACTIVE` schedule advances, its `markdown_pct` is set from the stage table, the item's `current_markdown_pct` is mirrored from it, the item's `effective_price_cents` and `compounded_price_cents` are recomputed, and the item's `clearance_week` is recomputed. When an `EXTENDED` schedule advances, its stage moves the same way and the item's prices are recomputed, but the item's `current_markdown_pct` is not mirrored. Both cases increment the schedule's `version`, and an `ACTIVE` advance also increments the item's `version`.

The stage table is fixed: stage `S1` carries a markdown percent of 0.25, `S2` carries 0.40, `S3` carries 0.55 and `S4` carries 0.70. A schedule's `stage` never moves backward, never skips a stage on a single crossed week, and never advances beyond `S4`.

The item's `clearance_week` is the number of completed whole weeks on clearance since the schedule's most recent activation, capped at 4, and it is the index into the week-factor table described below. Because it is counted from the most recent activation, a reactivation resets the count rather than continuing the old one.

Two prices are computed from an item's overlapping schedules at every recomputation, and they deliberately disagree. The **highest-of** price, `effective_price_cents`, begins at the item's `unit_retail_cents` and applies each overlapping active schedule's markdown in turn: for each schedule of this item whose status is `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED`, the running amount is multiplied by one minus that schedule's `markdown_pct` and the product is rounded half-up to a whole cent before the next schedule is applied. Because every schedule lowers the running amount, re-applying a markdown that is already in force can never raise the settled price, which is the defining property of the highest-of variant: two successive fifty-percent stages leave the effective price at half of retail, not at a quarter. A week factor is then applied on top, drawn from the table indexed by the item's `clearance_week` — the factor is 1.00 at week 0, 0.95 at week 1, 0.88 at week 2, 0.78 at week 3 and 0.70 at week 4, with 0.70 used as the standing factor for any index beyond the table. The settled price is the running amount multiplied by that factor, rounded half-up to a whole cent.

The **compounded** price, `compounded_price_cents`, begins at the item's `unit_retail_cents` and applies the same overlapping schedules, but it multiplies each stage's result by the next stage rather than resetting to the highest single markdown, and it processes the schedules in activation order: ascending `activated_at`, and ascending `created_at` as the tie-break when two schedules were activated at the same instant. Each multiplication is rounded half-up to a whole cent before the next stage. Two successive fifty-percent stages therefore leave the compounded price at a quarter of retail — genuinely different money from the highest-of result. The compounded price carries no week factor.

The difference between the two is reported as `delta_cents` on a clearance schedule read, defined as the highest-of price minus the compounded price, floored to a whole cent. The two variants coincide whenever an item carries a single active schedule, which is the ordinary seasonal-decor case, and they diverge whenever an item carries two overlapping schedules at once — the seasonal-decor window and the nostalgia-assortment window whose date ranges intersect.

A window may also be extended manually. A `BoutiqueManager` extends a window by supplying `extra_days`; the schedule moves to `EXTENDED`, its `extension_count` is increased by one, and its `end_date` is pushed out by that number of days.

Extension also happens automatically, and only when the window has actually run out. When a store day closes with a retail-sales-per-guest figure below the brand's `liquidation_trigger_rpg_cents` — the rule described in §3.5 — every `ClearanceSchedule` of that location in `ACTIVE` status is extended, but only those whose `end_date` lies before the logical clock's current instant. A schedule whose window is still running is untouched by the automatic extension even on a disappointing day. The extension moves the status to `EXTENDED`, increases `extension_count` by one and pushes `end_date` out by 14 days. The location's `active_clearance_count` is unchanged, because an `EXTENDED` window still occupies a slot exactly as an `ACTIVE` one does; that is why the count is only reduced by cancellation or liquidation.

Reactivation reopens an extended window as a fresh, non-contiguous one. A `BoutiqueManager` reactivates a schedule in `EXTENDED` status, and only when its `reactivation_count` is still below 2 — a window may be reopened from `EXTENDED` at most twice, and a third attempt is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_WINDOW_REACTIVATION_LIMIT` (HTTP 409) at priority 25. Reactivation moves the status back to `ACTIVE`, increases `reactivation_count` by one, and replaces the window: the new `start_date` is the end date the schedule previously carried plus the brand's `clearance_buffer_hours`, and the new `end_date` is that new `start_date` plus seven days. The buffer rule of priority 13 applies to the reactivated window as well, so a reactivation that would leave less than the buffer between windows is refused. Reactivation increments the schedule's `version`.

Liquidation is the terminal exit, and it is a `BoutiqueManager` action that reaches two states. From `ACTIVE` or from `EXTENDED`, liquidation is permitted only when the schedule's `stage` has reached `S4` and — for the `ACTIVE` branch — the item's `status` is `CLEARANCE`. A liquidation attempted before the final stage, or against an item that is not on clearance, is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_NOT_FINAL` (HTTP 409) at priority 15. On liquidation the schedule's `liquidated_units` takes the value of its `remaining_units` and `remaining_units` is reset to zero; the item's `on_hand_units` is reduced by the liquidated units; the item's `status` moves to `DISCONTINUED`; and the location's `active_clearance_count` is reduced by one. The two branches then part company. A liquidation from `ACTIVE` additionally clears the item's `current_markdown_pct` to zero and restores the item's `effective_price_cents` to the item's plain `unit_retail_cents`, unwinding the clearance pricing entirely. A liquidation from `EXTENDED` does not perform that unwinding: it leaves the item's markdown percent and its effective price exactly where the clearance run had taken them. Both branches increment the schedule's `version`. `LIQUIDATED` is one of the schedule's two terminal states, and an extended schedule is just as liquidatable as an active one provided its stage has reached `S4`.

Cancellation is the other exit and is available only from `DRAFT`. A `BoutiqueManager` cancels a draft window, moving its status to `CANCELLED` and leaving the item's `current_markdown_pct` untouched — a cancellation of a window that never activated has no markdown to reverse. It increments the schedule's `version`. `CANCELLED` is the schedule's second terminal state. A cancelled window is not counted by the overlap rule, so cancelling is the way to clear a collision.

Liquidation prices are set only at the liquidation moment, and they are computed by a waterfall rather than a single rate. The waterfall splits the schedule's `remaining_units` across three price stages in ascending stage order. The base value each stage discounts is the smaller of the item's `unit_cost_cents` and its `unit_retail_cents` — the goods are never liquidated against a value above their cost or above their ticket, whichever is lower. The three stages carry a share of the total units and a discount rate against that base value: the first stage takes 40 percent of the units at a rate of 0.90, the second takes 30 percent at 0.70, and the third takes 30 percent at 0.55. Each stage's take is the number of units that stage is entitled to — the total units multiplied by the stage's share, floored to a whole unit — limited to whatever units remain unspilled at that point; the stage's bucket is recorded only when its take is greater than zero, and the take is then removed from the remaining pool. Because the entitlement is floored against the total units rather than against the remaining pool, a stage whose share of the total falls below one whole unit takes nothing at all and is skipped. After the three stages, any units still unspilled are gathered into one final bucket priced at the third stage's rate of 0.55. Each bucket's per-unit price is the base value multiplied by that bucket's rate and rounded half-up to a whole cent, and the buckets are computed independently rather than by chaining.

Three outputs fall out of the waterfall. `liquidation_bucket_units` is the list of unit counts, in stage order, that the waterfall produced. `liquidation_price_cents` is the per-unit price of the first bucket — that is, the highest liquidation price in the waterfall, the rate applied to the largest share of the goods. `liquidation_blended_cents` is the unit-weighted average of the buckets, computed as the sum of each bucket's unit count multiplied by that bucket's price, divided by the total units, rounded half-up to a whole cent. A schedule with five or more remaining units will ordinarily fill all three stages and carry a fourth spill bucket; a schedule with three remaining units floors the first stage to a single unit and floors the second and third stages to nothing, producing a two-element bucket list of one unit and then the remaining two units.

A clearance schedule read also exposes the current list price for the item by region, `item_active_price_by_region_cents`, which a client may supply as a query filter when listing schedules, alongside `location_id`, `item_id` and `status`.

### 3.9. Assortment, Planogram Execution and Import Planning

An `AssortmentPlanner` creates an `Item` with a `sku` unique across the brand, a `name`, a `theme_code` drawn from `HARVEST`, `WINTER_HOLIDAY`, `SPRING`, `SUMMER` and `NOSTALGIA_CLASSIC`, an optional `nostalgia_flag` defaulting to false, a `unit_retail_cents` greater than zero, a `unit_cost_cents` never below zero, and an `import_lead_time_days` never below zero. A new item starts in `ACTIVE` status with `current_markdown_pct` of zero, `effective_price_cents` and `compounded_price_cents` both equal to its `unit_retail_cents`, and `on_hand_units` of zero. It starts with `order_deadline_reached` false and `clearance_week` of zero. Paragraph 2 of §2.9 describes the theme, tier and `nostalgia_flag` semantics; here we deal with what happens once an item exists.

`on_hand_units` is the item's live stock and has exactly two sources and one sink. It rises by the received count of every transfer of that item whose `receiving_zone` is `BACKROOM` and whose status is `RECEIVED` or `RECONCILED`, and it rises again by the declared count when a receiving is voided. It falls when a schedule liquidates, by the liquidated units. It is the sole source for a clearance schedule's activation snapshot and for the clearance dilutive decay described in §3.5, which is why the snapshot rule in §3.8 matters: because `on_hand_units` is live, it moves at liquidation while the schedule's own `remaining_units` snapshot does not.

An item's import planning answers one question: can a fresh order still reach the shelf before the theme's season opens, and if so, how much should be ordered. The answer is derived fresh on every call rather than stored from creation time, so an item's `order_deadline_reached` is a function of the logical clock's week position, not a flag set once. The item is compared against its theme's season-open week: `HARVEST` opens at week 38, `WINTER_HOLIDAY` at week 40, `SPRING` at week 10, `SUMMER` at week 26 and `NOSTALGIA_CLASSIC` at week 1. The deadline is reached when the number of days between the theme's opening week and the current week — the difference in weeks multiplied by seven days — is less than the item's `import_lead_time_days`. In other words, an item whose lead time no longer fits inside the days remaining before the season opens has missed its order window.

The projected need is a four-week order-up-to figure, and it is expressed in units. The target quantity is the number of units that four weeks of demand at a fixed weekly demand value would require, divided by the item's retail price and rounded up to a whole unit, plus the units already on hand. The four-week horizon and the weekly demand value are brand-level planning constants, and the weekly demand carries a value of 300000 cents — three thousand dollars of demand a week — so the horizon is four weeks at that rate. The margin test is separate: the item's unit margin is its retail price minus its cost, and the margin floor is the item's cost increased by a thirty-percent markup, rounded half-up to a whole cent. An item fails the margin test when its unit margin is strictly below that floor.

The need is zeroed only by a conjunction. When the item has reached its order deadline **and** its unit margin fails the floor, the projected need is zero — the season can no longer be reached by a fresh import and the goods would not clear their margin anyway, so nothing is ordered. A dead margin on its own does not zero the need, and an elapsed deadline on its own does not zero it either; both must hold. Items in `DISCONTINUED` status are excluded from the calculation entirely and the need is zero for them, as it is for any item whose status is neither `ACTIVE` nor `CLEARANCE`. The margin floor uses flooring rather than half-up rounding, so the floor is the largest whole cent not exceeding the cost-plus-markup figure.

Two reads expose this arithmetic without mutating anything. One re-derives and stores `order_deadline_reached` and `clearance_week` on the item and returns the item; the other returns the computed plan as a set of values — `projected_import_need_units`, `order_deadline_reached`, `unit_margin_cents` and `margin_floor_cents` — without touching the stored item. Both are available to the `AssortmentPlanner` and the `RetailLead`.

Planogram execution records which items occupy which positions on the floor. An `AssortmentPlanner` creates a `PlanogramVersion` for a location with a `location_id`, an `assortment_tier`, an `effective_date` and an optional `clone_slots_from_version_id`. A new version starts in `DRAFT` with `slot_count` of zero and a null `published_at`; a version's `published_at` stays null for as long as its status is `DRAFT`. When `clone_slots_from_version_id` is supplied, every non-`VACATED` slot of that source version is copied into the new version in `PLANNED` status, and `slot_count` reflects the copies rather than remaining zero. A draft version may be updated — its `assortment_tier` and its `effective_date` — and may be deleted outright; a published version may not be deleted and must instead be superseded and retired.

A slot is added to a draft version by supplying a `planogram_version_id`, an `item_id`, a `slot_position`, an optional `facings` defaulting to 1 and never below 1, and an optional `min_presentation_units` defaulting to 1 and never below 1. A new slot starts in `PLANNED`, and `status` runs through `PLANNED`, `APPLIED`, `VERIFIED`, `FROZEN` and `VACATED` over its life. Adding a slot recomputes the parent version's `slot_count`. `slot_count` counts the slots of a version whose status is anything other than `VACATED`, so a vacated slot stops counting.

Slot placement enforces both uniqueness and the regional carry rule. A `slot_position` must be unique within its `planogram_version_id` — the same index cannot be used twice in one version — and the item being merchandised must have a `theme_code` the location's `assortment_tier` actually carries, with the tier semantics given in §2.13. A placement that violates either is refused with `ERR_PLANOGRAM_SLOT_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409) at priority 21. A placement that fails only the theme-carry test is reported as `ERR_NO_REGIONAL_ASSORTMENT` (HTTP 403) at priority 23 when it is raised as a first-class assortment failure rather than through the slot-conflict ladder; the two identifiers describe the same underlying refusal at different points in the precedence order, and which one a caller sees depends on which rule caught the request. Every `planogram_version_id` and `item_id` a slot names must resolve, with an unallocated version reported as `ERR_PLANOGRAM_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) and an unknown item as `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), both from the resolution pass of §2.7.

Reshaping a slot changes its `facings` or its `slot_position` and is available only while the slot is still `PLANNED`. It requires `expected_planogram_version_version`, the version of the slot's parent planogram version the caller last read; a stale value is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), exactly as §2.6 describes, because a reshape is legitimate whenever the slot is `PLANNED`, including immediately after a sibling slot of the same version was reshaped, so no state field would have rejected the write. A reshape recomputes the version's `slot_count` and increments both the version's `version` and the slot's own `version`.

Applying and verifying are the execution steps. A `RetailLead` or a `BoutiqueManager` applies a `PLANNED` slot, moving it to `APPLIED` and stamping `applied_at`. A `BoutiqueManager` verifies an applied slot, moving it to `VERIFIED` and stamping `verified_at`. Each increments the slot's `version`. Publish performs the whole version's apply in one movement, described below.

Publishing is an `AssortmentPlanner` action and it is where the fleet-wide discipline lands. A version in `DRAFT` may be published only when its `slot_count` is at least 6 and when no existing `PlanogramVersion` of the same location already carries `PUBLISHED` status with an `effective_date` earlier than this version's own `effective_date` — the older effective version must be superseded first, and a competing draft version of the same location carrying an earlier `effective_date` is likewise a conflict. A publish that fails either test is refused with `ERR_PLANOGRAM_VERSION_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409) at priority 22. On success the version's status moves to `PUBLISHED`, `published_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the location's `active_planogram_version_id` is pointed at this version, and every slot of this version in `PLANNED` status moves to `APPLIED` with `applied_at` stamped. The location's previously published version is simultaneously moved to `SUPERSEDED` with its `superseded_by_version_id` set to the newly published version, and every one of that older version's slots in `APPLIED` status moves to `FROZEN`. The newly published version's `version` is incremented, and so is the superseded version's.

Retirement is the terminal step and it is the only transition out of `SUPERSEDED`; a retired version is terminal and its `RETIRED` status is never left. It is gated on time. An `AssortmentPlanner` retires a version that is in `SUPERSEDED` status and whose `published_at` the logical clock has reached or passed by at least thirty days — a superseded version must have been off the floor for a month before it can be retired. Retirement moves the status to `RETIRED` and moves every one of the version's slots still in `FROZEN` status to `VACATED`, and it increments the version's `version`. A version in `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED` or `RETIRED` cannot be retired; `PUBLISHED` must first be superseded by the publish of a competing version.

The region-fit read projects how many slots a version's location ought to carry, and it is a function of the location's region and its tier rather than of the version's current slot list. The projection starts from the number of slots the item is targeted to carry in that region, eighteen. A tier multiplier is then drawn from the location's `assortment_tier`: `FULL` carries 1.00, `REGIONAL_TWEAK` carries 1.25 and `MINIMAL` carries 0.55. The drift from the baseline is the multiplier minus 1.00, expressed in hundred-thousandths of a unit — so `REGIONAL_TWEAK` drifts plus 25000 and `MINIMAL` drifts minus 45000. When the drift exceeds 15000 hundred-thousandths the target gains a regional tweak bonus of four slots, which is reported as a `regional_tweak_bonus` of plus four; when the drift falls below minus 15000 the target loses four slots, and the `regional_tweak_bonus` is reported as minus four; a drift inside that band, as `FULL` at 0 necessarily is, applies no bonus at all and reports a bonus of zero. The location's `local_event_codes` then adds one slot for each code the location lists. The read reports the resulting `regionally_adjusted_slot_count` together with the `regional_tweak_bonus`, the `local_event_bonus` and the `drift_bps`. Because a `MINIMAL` store's drift is negative and large, it loses the four-slot bonus and, per §2.13, carries only the themes its `local_event_codes` name — a minimal store with no local event codes carries nothing.

Grouping and reading planogram resources follows the flat topology of the rest of this API. Versions are listed filtered by `location_id`, `status`, `assortment_tier` or `superseded_by_version_id`, and slots are listed filtered by `planogram_version_id`, `item_id` or `status`. Items may be listed filtered by `theme_code`, `status` or `nostalgia_flag`; locations by `brand_id`, `region_code` or `assortment_tier`; brands by `brand_code`; store days by `location_id`, `day_status`, `conversion_gate`, `business_date_from` or `business_date_to`; staff shifts by `location_id`, `employee_id`, `workcode`, `status`, `tip_eligible` or `crossover_authorized_by_shift_id`; transfers by `location_id`, `store_day_id`, `item_id`, `status` or `receiving_zone`; checks by `location_id`, `store_day_id`, `presented_shift_id`, `check_status` or `tip_eligible`; receipts by `location_id`, `store_day_id`, `guest_check_id`, `status` or `payment_method`; and backroom readings by `store_day_id` or `trigger_transfer_id`. Every such list obeys §2.3.

The day's revenue attribution is exposed as a single computed read that takes a required `store_day_id` and an optional `variant`, and reports the day's `provisional_retail_cents`, its `recognized_retail_cents`, its `recognition_delta_cents`, the `restaurant_share_cents` and `retail_share_cents` the combined tickets carried, and the `receipt_count` that contributed — the same variant pair that §3.4 defines, presented as one reconciled view of the day rather than as a per-ticket figure. The day's `recognition_delta_cents` is the sum of the day's tickets' own stored per-ticket `recognition_delta_cents` values rather than the difference between the day's two summed revenue totals, so under `FINAL_ONLY` it reads zero even where those two summed totals differ.

Finally, the logical clock is itself addressable. A `PlatformAdmin` sets it by supplying `to_iso8601`, and the endpoint returns `current_utc` reflecting the value in force; before any call to it the clock reads exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` and never falls back to the host's wall time. The marketing-reset advance of §3.8 sets the same clock from `from_iso8601` as a side effect of walking the clearance cadence.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

A deletion that removes a draft resource returns `204 No Content` with no body.
