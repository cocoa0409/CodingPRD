# Vascular Access Center API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A freestanding vascular access center sits at an awkward junction of the dialysis care pathway. It does not own the patients — partner dialysis facilities do — yet it is accountable for keeping their vascular accesses patent, for absorbing same-day urgencies without collapsing its own room schedule, and for defending every claim it submits with documentation that a payer will accept. The operational reality is that four largely independent clocks run at once: a surveillance clock, in which flow and pressure measurements arrive from the facilities and slowly describe a failing access; a scheduling clock, in which angioplasty, thrombectomy, catheter exchange and maturation intervention cases compete for angiography suites, credentialed sedation nurses and recovery bays; a revenue clock, in which coding, attestation, billing, denial and rebilling advance on their own timetable long after the patient has gone home; and a partnership clock, in which each referring facility's missed-treatment burden is scored over a rolling ninety-day window and its contract posture rises or falls accordingly.

This document specifies the API that governs all four. Its central design commitment is that no clinical, fiscal or inventory quantity may drift. Device units are conserved from the moment a lot is received until the moment a case consumes or releases them; money on the payer's desk is conserved across a denial-and-rebill loop; a facility's partnership score is a pure function of a window whose membership rule is stated once and read identically everywhere. Where two plausible evaluation orders exist — which rounding happens first, which capacity gate fires first, which tier feeds the grace credit — this document pins exactly one.

The API is deliberately unforgiving about identity and sequence. A facility coordinator may only speak for their own facility. The interventionalist who performed a case may never code it, and only that same interventionalist may attest its medical necessity. A booking built on a stale reading of the referral it answers is refused outright rather than silently reconciled. Readers implementing this specification should assume that every threshold, boundary inclusivity and ordering statement below is load-bearing.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold across every collection in the API. Collections are mounted as top-level peers — there is no shared API prefix — and the hierarchy between resources is expressed by foreign keys and by nested action paths rather than by a single containment tree.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which it acts. Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `CenterAdmin`, `AccessCoordinator`, `FacilityCoordinator`, `Interventionalist`, `SedationRN` and `Coder`. A request that omits either header, or that supplies an `X-User-Role` value outside those six, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). A request whose role is recognized but does not appear in the authorized-role list of the operation being called is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). These two checks run before anything else, in that order.

The meaning of `X-User-ID` depends on the role, and the difference matters because ownership checks compare it against persisted foreign keys.

- When `X-User-Role` is `CenterAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record or log it, but never compares it to any row's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `AccessCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and is never compared to any row's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `FacilityCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `DialysisFacility`; ownership checks on any resource that references a facility compare the resource's facility foreign key to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Interventionalist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Clinician`, whose `clinician_role` is `INTERVENTIONALIST`; case-level checks compare it to the procedure's `primary_operator_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `SedationRN`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Clinician`, whose `clinician_role` is `SEDATION_RN`; case-level checks compare it to the procedure's `sedation_rn_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Coder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Clinician`, whose `clinician_role` is `CODER`; segregation-of-duties checks compare it to both the procedure's `primary_operator_id` and its `sedation_rn_id`.

`CenterAdmin` is the reference-data, inventory, revenue-cycle and clock authority: it alone may set the logical clock, run the expiry sweep, register facilities, clinicians, procedure rooms and device lots, quarantine and release lots, and bill, deny or rebill a procedure. `AccessCoordinator` is the centre-side coordinator: it triages and declines referrals, requests more information, books and cancels cases, manages accesses and conversion plans, and may read everything except the reimbursement preview. `FacilityCoordinator` speaks only for its own facility, and is the only role permitted to resubmit a held referral. `Interventionalist` runs the case in the room — start, recovery, abort — attests medical necessity and files maturation checks, and may never code. `SedationRN` charts recovery observations and discharges the patient. `Coder` files procedural code lines and may never attest, bill, deny or rebill.

The facility-scope rule deserves its own statement. When `X-User-Role` is `FacilityCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` must equal the facility identified by the request: the `referring_facility_id` supplied in a referral body, the `reported_by_facility_id` supplied in a surveillance-reading body, the `referring_facility_id` of the referral named in the path for resubmission and missed-treatment updates, or the facility `id` in the path of a facility read. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). Precisely seven operations enforce it: referral submission, referral resubmission, the missed-treatment update, surveillance-reading submission, the single-referral read, the single-facility read, and the facility partnership-metrics read.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn at random from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits; like every other timestamp in the system it is read from the logical clock of §2.4 and never from real time, so it is identical on every response until an administrator moves the clock.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

List-returning operations accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted or supplied empty, and a supplied value is **clamped rather than rejected** — a value below 1 is raised to 1 and a value above 100 is lowered to 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip, defaults to 0 when omitted or supplied empty, and a negative value is likewise clamped up to 0. Neither parameter is subject to shape validation: a `limit` or `offset` that is not a base-ten integer is discarded and that parameter falls back to its default rather than producing `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Both are echoed in the response `meta` alongside `total_count` (the number of items matching the filter before the window is applied) and `has_more` (true when items remain beyond the current window).

Unless a specific list states otherwise, resources are returned sorted by their creation timestamp `created_at` in ascending order. Several lists deliberately deviate because their natural reading order carries meaning, and the deviation is stated with the operation that owns it: surveillance-reading lists are ordered by `reading_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, because that is exactly the order the triage calculation walks to identify the latest and previous readings; device-lot lists are ordered by `expires_on` ascending then `created_at` ascending, the same first-expiry-first-out order the allocation waterfall walks; procedure lists are ordered by `scheduled_start` ascending then `created_at` ascending; code-line lists are ordered by `rank` ascending; recovery-observation lists are ordered by `observed_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending; and infection-event lists are ordered by `onset_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending. Ordering is never by `id`. Because the logical clock advances only when an administrator moves it, rows written inside one clock instant share a `created_at` to the millisecond, so every ordering above — including the ones whose declared tie-break is `created_at` — can still tie. When it does, the surviving tie-break is the order in which the rows were inserted into the store, which is the order they were created; `id` is never consulted.

Every list also accepts optional equality filters drawn from the resource's own fields, which are combined conjunctively with pagination. The facility patient list filters on `dialysis_modality` and `payer_program`; surveillance-reading lists filter on `kind`; the device-lot list filters on `device_type` and `status`; the access list filters on `patient_id`, `access_type`, `status` and `predecessor_access_id`; the referral list filters on `access_id`, `referring_facility_id`, `status` and `urgency`; the device-usage list filters on `procedure_id`, `device_lot_id` and `state`; the procedure list filters on `referral_id`, `access_id`, `room_id`, `primary_operator_id`, `sedation_rn_id`, `attested_by_id`, `supporting_reading_id`, `clinical_status` and `coding_status`; the conversion-plan list filters on `patient_id`, `from_access_id`, `target_access_id` and `status`; and the infection-event list filters on `procedure_id`, `access_id` and `attributable`. Every one of those filters is an exact match of the supplied query-string value against the stored field value; a filter supplied empty is treated as absent rather than as a match on the empty string, and a query parameter the list does not declare is ignored. The sole boolean filter, `attributable`, accepts `true`, `false`, `1` and `0` in any letter case, and any other value is ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. Every temporal comparison — window membership, credential expiry, schedule-in-the-past rejection, day counting — resolves against a **logical clock** that the server holds and that only a `CenterAdmin` may move. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. It never falls back to real time and it never advances on its own; it changes only when an administrator supplies a new instant as `to_iso8601`, and the response echoes the new value as `current_utc` — reproduced as the caller supplied it, surrounding whitespace trimmed but never re-formatted to the three-fractional-digit form, even though every comparison and every server-written timestamp thereafter uses the normalised UTC instant that string denotes. Throughout this document, the phrase "the clock" means that value at the instant the request is processed, and "the current calendar date" means the UTC calendar date of that value.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties, Immutable Records and Enumerations

Most resources carry a standard system-managed set of properties:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and clients never supply it.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recording when the row was first written.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row and written equal to `created_at` when the row is first created.
- **`version`** (integer): a monotonically increasing counter that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly one on every mutation of the row, including mutations the row undergoes as a cascade victim rather than as the direct target of a request. Mutation is counted **per request, not per write**: a single request advances a given row's `version` by at most one, however many times that request writes the row. A quarantine that both flips a lot's `status` and restores its `quantity_reserved`, a consumption that both decrements a lot's quantities and takes it to `DEPLETED`, and a cascade that rewrites a row the same request had already written each leave that row's `version` exactly one higher than it stood before the request.

Two resource kinds are deliberately immutable and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — for these, both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the response body entirely. The first is `SurveillanceReading`: once a facility submits a flow-and-pressure measurement, no operation in this API alters it. The second is `RecoveryObservation`: a charted Aldrete observation is a legal record and is never edited. `DialysisFacility`, `Patient`, `VascularAccess`, `Referral`, `ProcedureRoom`, `Clinician`, `Procedure`, `ProcedureCode`, `DeviceLot`, `DeviceUsage`, `AccessConversionPlan` and `InfectionEvent` all carry the full four-field set.

Because so much of the specification below turns on exact enumeration members, they are collected here once. A `DialysisFacility` has a `contract_status` of `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`, and a `partnership_tier` of `GOLD`, `SILVER` or `WATCH`. A `Patient` has a `dialysis_modality` of `IN_CENTER_HD`, `HOME_HD` or `PD`, and a `payer_program` of `BUNDLED_ESRD` or `FEE_FOR_SERVICE`. A `VascularAccess` has an `access_type` of `TUNNELED_CATHETER`, `AVF` or `AVG`, a `laterality` of `LEFT` or `RIGHT`, a `site` of `FOREARM`, `UPPER_ARM`, `THIGH`, `INTERNAL_JUGULAR` or `SUBCLAVIAN`, and a `status` of `PLANNED`, `PLACED`, `MATURING`, `IN_USE`, `DYSFUNCTIONAL` or `ABANDONED`. A `SurveillanceReading` has a `kind` of `ROUTINE` or `URGENT`. A `Referral` has a `suspected_problem` of `STENOSIS`, `THROMBOSIS`, `CATHETER_DYSFUNCTION` or `MATURATION_FAILURE`, a `status` of `SUBMITTED`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `TRIAGED`, `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED` or `DECLINED`, a `triage_variant` of `TREND_BASED` or `ABSOLUTE_BASED`, an `urgency` of `SAME_DAY`, `NEXT_DAY` or `ROUTINE`, and a `hold_reason` of `COORDINATOR_REQUEST` or `FACILITY_POSTURE_HOLD`. A `ProcedureRoom` has a `room_type` of `ANGIO_SUITE` or `PROCEDURE_ROOM`. A `Clinician` has a `clinician_role` of `INTERVENTIONALIST`, `SEDATION_RN`, `RECOVERY_RN` or `CODER`. A `Procedure` has a `procedure_type` of `ANGIOPLASTY`, `THROMBECTOMY`, `CATHETER_EXCHANGE` or `FISTULA_MATURATION_INTERVENTION`, a `sedation_level` of `NONE` or `MODERATE`, a `clinical_status` of `SCHEDULED`, `IN_ROOM`, `RECOVERY`, `DISCHARGED`, `ABORTED` or `CANCELLED`, a `coding_status` of `UNCODED`, `CODED`, `ATTESTED`, `BILLED`, `DENIED` or `REBILLED`, a `reimbursement_variant` of `BUNDLED_ESRD` or `FEE_FOR_SERVICE`, a `cancel_reason` of `COORDINATOR_CANCEL` or `FACILITY_POSTURE_HOLD`, and a `denial_reason` of `MEDICAL_NECESSITY`, `BUNDLING`, `DOCUMENTATION` or `TIMELY_FILING`. A `ProcedureCode` has a `code_family` of `ANGIOPLASTY`, `THROMBECTOMY`, `CATH_EXCHANGE`, `IMAGING` or `ACCESS_MAINTENANCE_BUNDLE`, and a `modifier` of `NONE`, `M59` or `M52`. A `DeviceLot` has a `device_type` of `SHEATH`, `BALLOON` or `STENT` and a `status` of `AVAILABLE`, `QUARANTINED`, `EXPIRED` or `DEPLETED`. A `DeviceUsage` has a `state` of `RESERVED`, `CONSUMED` or `RELEASED`. An `AccessConversionPlan` has a `target_access_type` of `AVF` or `AVG` and a `status` of `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `MATURATION_HOLD`, `CONVERTED` or `ABANDONED`. An `InfectionEvent` has an `event_type` of `ACCESS_SITE_INFECTION` or `BACTEREMIA`.

Calendar-date fields — `placed_on`, `credential_expires_on`, `expires_on` and `target_conversion_date` — are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings with no time component. Instant fields — `reading_at`, `scheduled_start`, `scheduled_end`, `sedation_end_at`, `observed_at`, `onset_at`, and every server-written timestamp such as `triaged_at`, `completed_at`, `discharged_at`, `abandoned_at`, `converted_at`, `consumed_at`, `released_at`, `last_check_at` and `last_scored_at` — are ISO 8601 UTC datetimes. A client-supplied date or instant is validated and then persisted and echoed back **exactly as it was supplied**, apart from surrounding whitespace being trimmed: the server never re-formats it to a canonical fractional width. Server-written timestamps, by contrast, always carry exactly three fractional digits, any finer precision in the clock value being truncated rather than rounded. On input an instant is accepted with a trailing `Z`, with an explicit numeric UTC offset, or with no offset at all — an explicit offset is normalised to UTC before any comparison and an absent one is read as UTC — while a calendar date must be exactly `YYYY-MM-DD` with no time component.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Left-to-Right Existence Checks

Identifiers embedded in a request path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve to a live row decides the response with a 404. Consider a request against a code-line collection nested under a procedure, of the shape `/procedures/{id}/codes`: if the procedure identifier is unknown, the response is `ERR_PROCEDURE_NOT_FOUND` and no further work is attempted. Each entity has its own not-found identifier, and the correct one is always the one belonging to the *segment that failed*: `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCESS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_READING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLINICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROCEDURE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVICE_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVICE_USAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_INFECTION_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`.

Foreign keys supplied in a request *body* are resolved by the same catalogue of errors, but they are checked after body-shape validation, and among themselves they are checked in the order the operation declares its input fields. The relevant identifiers for body-supplied keys are `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND` (for `dialysis_facility_id`, `referring_facility_id` and `reported_by_facility_id`), `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND` (for `patient_id`), `ERR_ACCESS_NOT_FOUND` (for `access_id`, `predecessor_access_id`, `from_access_id` and `target_access_id`), `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND` (for `referral_id`), `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND` (for `room_id`), `ERR_CLINICIAN_NOT_FOUND` (for `primary_operator_id` and `sedation_rn_id`), `ERR_DEVICE_LOT_NOT_FOUND` (for `device_lot_id`) and `ERR_READING_NOT_FOUND` (for `supporting_reading_id`).

### 2.7. Validation Precedence

When a single request violates several rules at once, the response is decided by the rule that sits earliest in the following ladder — never by whichever check the implementation happens to reach first. The ladder is global; it applies to every operation, and an operation simply skips the rungs that do not concern it.

1. **Authentication.** Both context headers present and `X-User-Role` among the six roles, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorization.** The acting role appears in the called operation's authorized-role list, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Path existence.** Every identifier in the path resolves, left to right, otherwise the segment's own not-found identifier.
4. **Body shape.** Every required field present, every enum value inside its declared member set, every integer inside its declared domain, dates well-formed `YYYY-MM-DD`, datetimes well-formed ISO 8601 UTC, and `scheduled_end` strictly after `scheduled_start` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Applies to every creating or acting operation that takes a body. Every required string must additionally be non-empty, and a JSON boolean is never accepted where an integer or a numeric measurement is required. A request that carries no body at all is read as an empty JSON object, so an operation whose every field is optional succeeds without one; a body that is present but is not a JSON object — a list, a bare scalar or malformed JSON — is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. That reading of the body happens only in the operations that declare at least one body field. The eight acting operations that declare none — the expiry sweep, device-lot release, referral triage, case start, discharge, billing, rebilling and conversion-plan activation — together with every read never inspect the request body at all, so whatever such a request carries, including a JSON array, a bare scalar or malformed JSON, is silently ignored rather than reported. An optional field supplied explicitly as `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted and takes its documented default.
5. **Body foreign-key existence.** Every foreign key in the body resolves, in the order the operation declares its inputs.
6. **Facility scope.** The `FacilityCoordinator` ownership rule of §2.1, otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
7. **Clinician role fit and case assignment.** `primary_operator_id` must name a `Clinician` whose `clinician_role` is `INTERVENTIONALIST` and `sedation_rn_id` must name one whose `clinician_role` is `SEDATION_RN`, otherwise `ERR_CLINICIAN_ROLE_MISMATCH` (422); and where an operation is reserved to the assigned clinician, `X-User-ID` must match — the primary operator for starting a case, moving it to recovery and aborting it, and the sedation nurse for charting a recovery observation and for discharging — otherwise `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_CLINICIAN` (403). Six operations sit on this rung: case booking, start, recovery, abort, recovery-observation charting and discharge. Attestation is deliberately *not* one of them; its operator-identity check lives at rung 14 and reports a different identifier, so the two never compete for the same request.
8. **Declared state.** The target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared. Depending on the entity this reports `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_TRIAGED`, `ERR_REFERRAL_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PROCEDURE_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_PROCEDURE_NOT_IN_ROOM`, `ERR_PROCEDURE_NOT_IN_RECOVERY`, `ERR_PROCEDURE_TERMINAL`, `ERR_CODING_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_DEVICE_LOT_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_ACCESS_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`, all with HTTP 409.
9. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied expected-version field equals the parent row's current `version`, otherwise the rule's stale-version identifier with HTTP 412. See §2.8.
10. **Moderate-sedation credentialing**, otherwise `ERR_SEDATION_CREDENTIAL_INVALID` (422).
11. **Device-lot posture**, otherwise `ERR_DEVICE_LOT_QUARANTINED` or `ERR_DEVICE_LOT_EXPIRED` (both 422).
12. **Recovery discharge criteria**, otherwise `ERR_RECOVERY_CRITERIA_NOT_MET` (422).
13. **Access not abandoned at start**, otherwise `ERR_ACCESS_ABANDONED` (409). This rung governs the case-start check; the identically named gate on referral triage instead runs after that operation's surveillance gate of rung 23, as §3.3 records.
14. **Segregation of duties**, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` or `ERR_ATTESTATION_NOT_OPERATOR` (both 403).
15. **Medical-necessity support**, otherwise `ERR_MEDICAL_NECESSITY_UNSUPPORTED` (422).
16. **Booking not in the past**, otherwise `ERR_SCHEDULE_IN_PAST` (422).
17. **Urgency window**, otherwise `ERR_SAME_DAY_WINDOW_MISSED` (422).
18. **Room occupancy including turnover buffer**, otherwise `ERR_ROOM_OCCUPIED` (409).
19. **Room daily case cap**, otherwise `ERR_ROOM_DAILY_CAP_REACHED` (409).
20. **Sedation-nurse concurrency ceiling**, otherwise `ERR_SEDATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
21. **Recovery-bay capacity**, otherwise `ERR_RECOVERY_BAY_UNAVAILABLE` (409).
22. **Referring facility not suspended at triage**, otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED` (409).
23. **Surveillance sufficiency at triage**, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SURVEILLANCE` (422).
24. **Code-line rank uniqueness**, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE_RANK` (409).
25. **Scalar uniqueness** of `facility_code`, `mrn`, `npi`, `room_code` and the `device_type`-plus-`lot_number` pair, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_FACILITY_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_MRN`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_NPI`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROOM_CODE` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOT_NUMBER` (all 409). These five apply respectively to facility registration, patient registration, clinician registration, room registration and device-lot receipt.
26. **Conversion indication**, otherwise `ERR_CONVERSION_NOT_INDICATED` (422).
27. **Target-access convertibility**, otherwise `ERR_TARGET_ACCESS_NOT_CONVERTIBLE` (409). Two operations sit here: plan activation and maturation-check filing.
28. **No open case on an access being abandoned**, otherwise `ERR_ACCESS_HAS_OPEN_CASE` (409).
29. **Rebill limit**, otherwise `ERR_REBILL_LIMIT_REACHED` (409).
30. **Device stock**, otherwise `ERR_LOT_DEPLETED` (409), `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DEVICE_STOCK` (409) or `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_LOT` (422). Two operations sit here: the explicit single-lot reservation and the server-side allocation.
31. **Code-to-device consistency at billing**, otherwise `ERR_CODE_DEVICE_MISMATCH` (422).
32. **Per-session allowed-amount cap**, otherwise `ERR_SESSION_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409).

Two precedence consequences are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. Billing checks code-to-device consistency (rung 31) *strictly before* the session cap (rung 32), so a claim that is both undocumented and over the cap reports `ERR_CODE_DEVICE_MISMATCH`. And within case booking, room occupancy (rung 18) is evaluated before any of the three capacity headrooms, which are themselves evaluated in the fixed order room cap, then sedation-nurse ceiling, then recovery bays.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Rows

Three write operations reach across aggregate boundaries in a way that makes a lost update genuinely dangerous, and each therefore requires the client to declare the parent version it last observed. The declared field is **not optional** in any of the three; omitting it is a body-shape failure. Operations not named here accept no version field at all.

**Case booking** requires `expected_referral_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Referral`. If it does not equal the referral's current `version`, the booking is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REFERRAL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters because a `TRIAGED` referral legitimately keeps moving: a missed-treatment update overwrites `missed_treatment_count` and rescores the referring facility, and a facility-posture drop can push the very same referral into `INFO_REQUESTED`. A booking built on a stale view would schedule against a superseded missed-treatment burden, or against a referral that is no longer bookable at all. Note also that a successful booking advances the referral itself from `TRIAGED` to `SCHEDULED` and increments its `version`, so a client booking a second case against the same referral must re-read it first.

**Explicit device reservation** requires `expected_device_lot_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the named `DeviceLot`. A mismatch with the lot's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEVICE_LOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Another reservation, a quarantine, a release or an expiry sweep all legitimately change the lot's headroom, so a reservation computed from a stale `quantity_on_hand` and `quantity_reserved` pair would over-commit the lot.

**Conversion-plan creation** requires `expected_access_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `VascularAccess` named as `from_access_id`. A mismatch with that access's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACCESS_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Creating a plan supersedes any existing `DRAFT` plan on the same source access, and the source access's own `status` may meanwhile have moved to `DYSFUNCTIONAL` or `ABANDONED`, so planning from a stale view would retire the wrong access.

The server-side device allocation deliberately accepts **no** version field, because the server rather than the caller chooses which lots to draw from.

### 2.9. Money, Counts and Rounding Order

Every monetary quantity in this system is an integer number of cents and crosses the wire as an integer, never as a decimal or a string. `base_allowed_cents`, `allowed_cents`, `unit_cost_cents`, `allowed_amount_cents`, `billed_amount_cents`, `denied_amount_cents` and `net_receivable_cents` are all whole cents. Where a percentage or a modifier factor produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded half up to the nearest whole cent.

The **order** of rounding relative to summation is pinned and is not a matter of implementation taste: each code line is rounded to whole cents *first*, and the rounded per-line amounts are then summed into the procedure's total. Summing the unrounded per-line products and rounding once at the end is explicitly the rejected alternative, because the two disagree. For three lines whose fee-schedule amounts are 180050, 45075 and 90050 cents, all carrying no modifier, under fee-for-service reimbursement the pinned order yields a total of 225101 cents while sum-then-round yields 225100; this specification requires 225101.

Scores and counts are integers with no rounding except where explicitly stated. The referral triage score, the facility partnership score and the access intervention burden score are all integers. The grace credit applied to a facility's missed-treatment burden uses **floor**, never rounding. The two derived rates in the partnership-metrics read are scaled to integers precisely so that no floating-point value ever crosses the API boundary. Recirculation percentages are the characteristic non-integer measurement on the wire: `recirculation_pct` carries one decimal place, and so do the maturation-check measurements `diameter_mm` and `depth_mm` (recorded as `last_diameter_mm` and `last_depth_mm`). Those three, together with the maturation check's `flow_ml_min`, are validated as numbers rather than as integers, and the one-decimal convention is a convention and not a gate: a value carrying more decimal places is not rejected but stored and echoed back exactly as supplied. Every other measurement crossing the wire — a surveillance reading's `access_flow_ml_min` and `venous_pressure_mmhg`, an observation's `aldrete_score`, every quantity and every cents field — must be a JSON integer.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Hierarchical Consistency

Several operations in this system fire long chains of consequences across unrelated aggregates. Three rules govern all of them.

First, **cascades are synchronous and complete within the triggering request**. There is no deferred reconciliation, no background worker and no eventual convergence: by the time the response is written, every downstream row the chain touches has reached its new state. A caller who reads any affected row immediately afterwards sees the settled value.

Second, **every row a cascade touches is mutated in its own right**. Its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by one, exactly as if it had been the direct target of a request. A procedure cancelled by a facility-posture drop has its `version` bumped; so does the device lot whose `quantity_reserved` the cascade restores; so does the referral that was held. The one exception is the allowed-amount recomputation of §3.8, which rewrites the derived fields of a procedure's other code lines in place without those lines counting as mutated, as that section records.

Third, **cascade depth is bounded and its hop order is fixed**. Two chains reach five hops and both are specified hop-by-hop in §3. The discharge chain runs: consume the case's reserved devices; complete the parent referral; re-evaluate the vascular access; convert any ready conversion plan targeting that access; abandon the converted plan's source access. The facility-posture chain runs: rescore the facility; drop its contract posture; hold its open referrals; cancel the scheduled procedures beneath them; release those procedures' reserved device units. A single pass is made in each case — in particular, the facility rescoring that begins the posture chain is **not** re-run at the end of it, even though the chain changes referral statuses that the scoring window filter reads.

One asymmetry is intentional and must not be smoothed away: a facility posture that *improves* fires no cascade at all. Referrals held under a posture drop are never auto-released; the facility must resubmit them.

### 2.11. Device-Unit Conservation

Device inventory obeys a conservation invariant anchored on `received_quantity`, which is written once when a lot is received and never mutated again. At every instant, a lot's `quantity_on_hand` counts units physically present and `quantity_reserved` counts units promised to cases that have not yet consumed them; the difference between them is the lot's **headroom**, the quantity a new reservation may draw on.

The two ways a reservation ends are deliberately asymmetric. **Consumption** — which happens when a case is discharged or aborted — decrements both `quantity_on_hand` and `quantity_reserved` by the reservation's `quantity`, because the units left the shelf and are no longer promised. **Release** — which happens when a booking is cancelled, when a lot is quarantined, when the expiry sweep catches a lapsed lot, or when the facility-posture chain cancels a case — decrements `quantity_reserved` only and leaves `quantity_on_hand` untouched, because the units never left the shelf. This is why a full reserve-then-release round trip returns the lot to its exact prior arithmetic with zero drift.

### 2.12. Segregation of Duties across the Case Triangle

The operator, coder and attester of a case form a fixed triangle that no request may collapse. The clinician who ran the case may never code it: when the acting `X-User-ID` on code-line filing equals either the procedure's `primary_operator_id` or its `sedation_rn_id`, the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Conversely, medical-necessity attestation is reserved to exactly the primary operator: an interventionalist other than the one named in `primary_operator_id` is refused with `ERR_ATTESTATION_NOT_OPERATOR`. And the money is reserved to the centre administrator, who can neither code nor attest — so a claim can only reach a payer after at least three distinct principals have touched it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Reference Data: Facilities, Patients, Clinicians and Rooms

Before any clinical work can be recorded, a `CenterAdmin` populates the centre's reference data. A partner dialysis facility is registered with its legal `name` and an operator-assigned `facility_code` of between 1 and 16 characters that must be unique across all facilities; a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FACILITY_CODE`. A newly registered facility starts from a position of full trust: its `contract_status` is `ACTIVE`, its `partnership_tier` is `SILVER`, its `partnership_score` is 100, every window aggregate — `referral_volume_window`, `raw_missed_count_window`, `grace_credit_window`, `adjusted_missed_count_window` and `same_day_honored_window` — is 0, its `last_scored_at` is null because no scoring evaluation has yet run, and its `version` is 1. None of those computed fields is ever set directly by a request; each is a product of the partnership scoring described in §3.4.

Patients are registered by a `CenterAdmin` or an `AccessCoordinator` with a medical record number `mrn` that must be unique across all patients (`ERR_DUPLICATE_MRN` on a repeat), a `full_name`, the `dialysis_facility_id` of their home facility, a `dialysis_modality` and a `payer_program`. That last field is quietly one of the most consequential values in the system: it selects which reimbursement variant every claim on this patient's cases will be paid under. A new patient's `access_count` is 0 and their `version` is 1.

Clinicians are registered by a `CenterAdmin` with a `full_name`, a `clinician_role`, and a ten-digit `npi` that must be unique across all clinicians (`ERR_DUPLICATE_NPI`). Ten digits is the real-world convention rather than a validation gate: `npi` is checked only as a non-empty string, so a shorter or non-numeric value is accepted, and uniqueness is an exact character-for-character comparison against the stored values. Sedation capability is described by three further fields. `moderate_sedation_credentialed` states whether the clinician may staff a case under moderate sedation and defaults to `false` when omitted. `credential_expires_on` is the calendar date on which that credential lapses; it is optional and is recorded as null when omitted. The credential counts as valid for as long as `credential_expires_on` falls on or after the clock's current calendar date, so a credential expiring today is still good today and stops being good tomorrow, while a clinician whose `credential_expires_on` is null is never validly credentialed for moderate sedation. `max_concurrent_sedation_cases` is the ceiling on how many overlapping moderate-sedation cases may be assigned to this clinician at once; it defaults to 2 when omitted and must be at least 0, so a ceiling of 0 is a legal registration that simply admits no moderate-sedation case at all. Note that `clinician_role` admits `RECOVERY_RN` as a member even though no operation in this API requires that role — it exists so a centre can hold its full staffing roster.

Procedure rooms are registered by a `CenterAdmin` with a `room_code` unique across all rooms (`ERR_DUPLICATE_ROOM_CODE`) and a `room_type`. Only an `ANGIO_SUITE` earns the same-day surge allowance described in §3.5; a plain `PROCEDURE_ROOM` never does. Three capacity parameters have defaults: `turnover_buffer_min`, the number of minutes of room turnover appended to every booking for overlap arithmetic, defaults to 30; `daily_case_cap`, the maximum number of non-cancelled cases per calendar day in this room before surge, defaults to 6; and `recovery_bay_count`, the number of moderate-sedation recovery bays attached to the room, defaults to 3. Each of the three must be at least 0 when supplied, and 0 is accepted for each: a room registered with a `recovery_bay_count` of 0 exists but can never admit a moderate-sedation case, and one registered with a `daily_case_cap` of 0 leaves only the same-day surge allowance of §3.5 to book against.

### 3.2. Vascular Accesses and the Surveillance Feed

A **vascular access** is the physical conduit through which a patient dialyses, and the object this whole system exists to maintain. A `CenterAdmin` or `AccessCoordinator` records one for a patient by supplying the `patient_id`, an `access_type`, a `laterality`, a `site`, and `placed_on` — the calendar date the access was surgically placed, which is the anchor from which maturation day counts are measured. An optional `predecessor_access_id` names the access this one is intended to replace, forming a self-referential conversion lineage; it is null for a patient's first access, and beyond having to resolve it is unconstrained — it may name an access of another patient, or one that is already `ABANDONED`. `placed_on` is likewise never compared to the clock, so a placement date in the future is accepted and simply yields a negative `days_since_placement` on any maturation check of §3.9. A newly recorded access begins in `PLANNED`, with `angioplasty_count`, `thrombectomy_count`, `catheter_exchange_count`, `maturation_intervention_count`, `attributable_infection_count` and `intervention_burden_score` all 0, and `abandoned_at` null. Recording it increases the owning patient's `access_count` by one; `access_count` counts the patient's accesses whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `PLACED`, `MATURING`, `IN_USE` or `DYSFUNCTIONAL`, so an access that later reaches `ABANDONED` decrements it again. `access_count` is always brought up to date by **recounting** the patient's accesses rather than by applying a delta, and the patient row is written — refreshing its `updated_at` and advancing its `version` — only when the recounted value actually differs from the value already stored.

The access lifecycle is driven by a single status operation on which a `CenterAdmin` or `AccessCoordinator` supplies the desired `to_status`. Any pair of current status and requested `to_status` that is not one of the declared transitions below — including any attempt to leave the terminal `ABANDONED` state — is refused with `ERR_ACCESS_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`.

`PLANNED → PLACED` is unconditional once the access is `PLANNED`. From `PLACED` the path forks by modality, and the fork is exclusive in both directions. `PLACED → MATURING` requires the `access_type` to be `AVF` or `AVG`; a `TUNNELED_CATHETER` never matures, and the attempt is refused. `PLACED → IN_USE` requires the `access_type` to be exactly `TUNNELED_CATHETER`, because a fistula or graft must pass through `MATURING` first; that transition also refreshes the owning patient's `access_count`, which — because both `PLACED` and `IN_USE` are counted statuses — recounts to the same number and therefore leaves the patient row untouched. `MATURING → IN_USE` is available to an `AVF` or `AVG` and is the moment a permanent access becomes usable — it is also the moment any conversion plan waiting on that access may complete, as §3.9 describes.

The maintenance loop between `IN_USE` and `DYSFUNCTIONAL` is where most of the centre's work lives. An access moves `IN_USE → DYSFUNCTIONAL` only as a system consequence — either when a referral naming it is triaged with a mechanical suspicion, or when its attributable infection count reaches two — never by direct request. It returns `DYSFUNCTIONAL → IN_USE` when the intervention that fixed it is discharged and its parent referral thereby completes; that return also recomputes the access's four intervention counters and its intervention burden score. The loop is countable and can be traversed any number of times.

Abandonment is terminal. An access may be abandoned from `PLANNED`, `PLACED`, `MATURING`, `IN_USE` or `DYSFUNCTIONAL`. When it is, `abandoned_at` is set to the clock, the owning patient's `access_count` is decremented, and every conversion plan whose `target_access_id` is this access and whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `MATURATION_HOLD` is itself moved to `ABANDONED`. A directly requested abandonment is blocked while any procedure on the access has a `clinical_status` of `SCHEDULED`, `IN_ROOM` or `RECOVERY`, and is refused with `ERR_ACCESS_HAS_OPEN_CASE`. The conversion cascade described in §3.9 is deliberately **exempt** from that block: the entire point of a conversion is to retire the old access, and any booking still standing on it is intentionally left in place so that it fails later, at the moment someone tries to bring the case into the room.

Surveillance data flows the other way, from the facilities into the centre. A `FacilityCoordinator`, `AccessCoordinator` or `CenterAdmin` submits a reading naming the `access_id` it was taken on, the `reported_by_facility_id` that measured it, the instant `reading_at`, the measured access blood flow `access_flow_ml_min` in millilitres per minute, the dynamic venous pressure `venous_pressure_mmhg` in millimetres of mercury, the measured `recirculation_pct` to one decimal place, and a `kind` distinguishing routine monthly surveillance from an urgent bedside check. Flow and pressure must both be at least 0, and recirculation must fall between 0.0 and 100.0 inclusive. `reading_at` is never compared to the clock, so a reading dated in the future is accepted and simply sits outside every lookback window until the clock reaches it; nor is the reporting facility required to be the home facility of the access's patient. A `FacilityCoordinator` whose `X-User-ID` differs from `reported_by_facility_id` is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Readings are immutable: the response carries `id` and `created_at` but no `version`, and nothing in this API ever edits one. The `kind` value has no effect on triage scoring — a `ROUTINE` reading and an `URGENT` one carry identical weight, and the only thing that matters for scoring is whether the reading falls inside the lookback window.

### 3.3. Referral Intake, the Revision Loop and Triage Scoring

A referral is a partner facility's assertion that one of its patients' accesses is failing. A `FacilityCoordinator`, `AccessCoordinator` or `CenterAdmin` submits one naming the `access_id` it believes is failing, the `referring_facility_id`, a `suspected_problem`, a free-text `clinical_narrative` that is later carried into medical-necessity documentation, and optionally a `missed_treatment_count` — the number of dialysis treatments this patient has already missed because of this access problem, which must be between 0 and 60 inclusive and defaults to 0 when omitted. The named access need only exist: submission does not require it to belong to a patient of the referring facility, nor to be in any particular `status`, so a referral may be filed against an `ABANDONED` access and is stopped only later, by the triage gates below. A new referral enters `SUBMITTED` with `info_request_count` at 0 and `triage_score`, `triage_variant`, `urgency`, `triaged_at`, `completed_at`, `hold_reason` and `decline_reason` all null. Submitting a referral immediately rescores the referring facility, which can itself drop the facility's contract posture and fire the whole posture chain within the same request.

Before committing to a case, a coordinator may push a thin referral back for more detail. **Requesting more information** moves the referral `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED`, records `hold_reason` as `COORDINATOR_REQUEST`, increases `info_request_count` by one, and accepts an optional `info_request_note` — validated as a non-empty string whenever it is supplied, but recorded nowhere: no field of the referral carries it and no response echoes it back. Only the owning facility can close the loop: **resubmission** is exclusive to a `FacilityCoordinator` whose `X-User-ID` equals the referral's `referring_facility_id`, and moves it `INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`. Resubmission overwrites `clinical_narrative` with the new narrative and overwrites `missed_treatment_count` when the payload supplies one, clears `hold_reason` back to null, and — critically — clears `triage_score`, `triage_variant`, `urgency` and `triaged_at` all back to null, because a resubmitted referral must be re-triaged from scratch. `info_request_count` is deliberately **not** decremented, which is what makes the revision loop countable: a referral that has been round-tripped three times still carries a count of 3. Requesting information on a referral that is not `SUBMITTED`, or resubmitting one that is not `INFO_REQUESTED`, is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_STATE_INVALID`.

A referral may also be **declined** by an `AccessCoordinator` or `CenterAdmin` from either `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED`, with a free-text `decline_reason` recorded on the row; declining a referral that was held leaves its `hold_reason` in place rather than clearing it. `DECLINED` is terminal. Declining is not a neutral act for the referring facility: because a declined referral drops out of the missed-treatment window entirely, the rescoring that follows a decline can *raise* the facility's partnership score and lift its posture. Declining a referral that has already reached `TRIAGED`, `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED` or `DECLINED` is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_STATE_INVALID`.

**Triage** is the pivot of the whole workflow. An `AccessCoordinator` or `CenterAdmin` triages a `SUBMITTED` referral, moving it `SUBMITTED → TRIAGED`; triaging a referral in any other status — including one that is already `TRIAGED` — is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_STATE_INVALID`. Three gates guard it, and at triage they are evaluated in exactly the order written here, which is therefore the order that decides the identifier when more than one of them is failing. The referring facility's `contract_status` must be `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`; a `SUSPENDED` facility's referrals cannot be triaged and the attempt is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED`. At least one surveillance reading must exist on the referral's access with a `reading_at` at or before the clock, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SURVEILLANCE`; the same identifier is also returned when readings exist but none of them falls inside the scoring window described below, because in that case there is nothing to score. And the access must not be `ABANDONED`, otherwise `ERR_ACCESS_ABANDONED`; because that third gate sits last rather than at its ladder position, a triage against an abandoned access that also has no qualifying readings reports `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SURVEILLANCE`.

The score itself is computed from a tightly specified input set: those surveillance readings whose `access_id` equals the referral's `access_id` and whose `reading_at` falls inside the closed window beginning twenty-one days before the clock and ending at the clock. Readings on other accesses and readings older than three weeks are excluded regardless of `kind`. The qualifying readings are ordered by `reading_at` ascending, with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break and never by `id`; the **latest** reading is the last element of that ordering and the **previous** reading is the one immediately before it.

Which of two variants applies is decided by the data, not by the caller. When two or more readings qualify, `triage_variant` is `TREND_BASED`. The system computes a flow-decline percentage as the previous reading's `access_flow_ml_min` minus the latest reading's `access_flow_ml_min`, divided by the previous reading's `access_flow_ml_min`, then multiplied by 100 — a figure that is negative when flow actually improved. When the previous reading's `access_flow_ml_min` is exactly 0 the quotient is undefined and no division is attempted: the trend component is 0 for that referral, and only the absolute-level component and the three shared components score. Trend points are that percentage multiplied by a weight of 1.2, rounded half up to a whole number, floored at 0 so an improving access earns nothing, and then capped at 40. An absolute-level component is added on top: 25 points if the latest reading's `access_flow_ml_min` is below 600 millilitres per minute, otherwise 12 points if it is below 800, otherwise none.

When exactly one reading qualifies, `triage_variant` is `ABSOLUTE_BASED`. There is no trend component at all — trend points are 0 — and the absolute-level component is doubled: 40 points if the latest reading's `access_flow_ml_min` is below 600 millilitres per minute, otherwise 20 points if it is below 800, otherwise none. The consequence is deliberate and must not be smoothed away: two referrals with identical latest-reading values can earn different scores, and land in different urgency bands, purely because one facility had submitted a second reading inside the window and the other had not.

Both variants then add the same three components. Fifteen points are added when the latest reading's `venous_pressure_mmhg` is 150 or above. Ten points are added when its `recirculation_pct` is 10.0 or above. And the missed-treatment burden contributes six points per missed treatment, capped at 18 points — which is to say the cap binds from the fourth missed treatment onward. The five components are summed and the total is capped at 100; that integer is written to `triage_score`. The only rounding anywhere in this calculation is the half-up rounding of the trend component before its cap is applied.

`urgency` is then banded off the score: `SAME_DAY` when `triage_score` is 70 or above, otherwise `NEXT_DAY` when it is 45 or above, otherwise `ROUTINE`. `triaged_at` is set to the clock, and the calendar date of `triaged_at` becomes the anchor of the scheduling window enforced in §3.5.

Triage carries two further consequences in the same request. When the `suspected_problem` is `STENOSIS`, `THROMBOSIS` or `CATHETER_DYSFUNCTION` — that is, a mechanical failure rather than a `MATURATION_FAILURE` — and the referral's access is currently `IN_USE`, that access is moved `IN_USE → DYSFUNCTIONAL`. And the referring facility is rescored, in a single pass, as described next.

Finally, a coordinator or the facility itself may update a referral's missed-treatment burden after submission. That operation overwrites `missed_treatment_count` with the supplied **absolute** value — it is not a delta — and then triggers the five-hop partnership chain of §3.4. No state gate guards it: the referral's `status` is never examined, so the burden may be overwritten on a `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED` or even a `DECLINED` referral. Nor does the update re-derive any triage outcome — `triage_score`, `triage_variant`, `urgency` and `triaged_at` are left exactly as they stood, and only the facility rescoring that follows may disturb them. It is the ordinary way a facility's posture degrades mid-flight, and it is the reason a booking must declare the referral version it observed.

### 3.4. Facility Partnership Scoring and the Posture Cascade

Partnership scoring is the system's judgement on how well a referring facility is holding up its end of the relationship, and it runs on exactly five occasions: when a referral is submitted, when a referral is triaged, when a referral is declined, when a referral's missed-treatment count is updated, and when a case is discharged. It writes nine fields on the facility — `partnership_score`, `partnership_tier`, `contract_status`, `referral_volume_window`, `raw_missed_count_window`, `grace_credit_window`, `adjusted_missed_count_window`, `same_day_honored_window` and `last_scored_at` — and its evaluation order is pinned, because the quantities are mutually dependent: the grace credit depends on the tier, the tier depends on the score, and the score depends on the grace credit. That circle is broken by reading the tier that the **previous** evaluation persisted.

The evaluation proceeds in four steps, in this order.

**Step A — window membership.** The tier to be used for the grace credit is read out of the facility's currently stored `partnership_tier` *before any field is written*. Then the window is assembled: all referrals whose `referring_facility_id` is this facility, whose `status` is one of `SUBMITTED`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `TRIAGED`, `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`, and whose `created_at` falls inside the closed ninety-day window beginning ninety days before the clock and ending at the clock. Both window endpoints are inclusive. `DECLINED` referrals are excluded from membership, which is the mechanism by which declining a referral raises the score. `referral_volume_window` is the count of those referrals and `raw_missed_count_window` is the sum of their `missed_treatment_count` values.

**Step B — grace credit.** Higher-tier partners are forgiven a proportion of their misses. The grace credit is the in-window referral volume multiplied by the rate for the tier read in Step A — one fifth for a `GOLD` partner, one tenth for a `SILVER` partner, and nothing at all for a `WATCH` partner — with the product taken to its **floor**, never rounded. That value is `grace_credit_window`. The `adjusted_missed_count_window` is the raw missed count minus the grace credit, floored at 0 so a well-behaved partner can never bank negative misses.

**Step C — the same-day honouring bonus.** `same_day_honored_window` counts the procedures that answered an in-window referral, whose own `clinical_status` is `DISCHARGED`, whose referral's **currently stored** `urgency` is `SAME_DAY` and whose `triaged_at` is still non-null, and whose `scheduled_start` fell on the same calendar date as that referral's `triaged_at`. Those two triage fields are read live rather than historically, so a referral whose triage outcome has since been cleared — by a resubmission or by a posture hold — stops contributing its honoured case. In plain terms: the centre earns credit for every same-day urgency it actually honoured on the day it was raised and then carried through to discharge.

**Step D — score, then tier, then posture, in that order.** The score starts at 100, loses four points for each adjusted missed treatment, gains three points for each honoured same-day case, and is then clamped into the range 0 to 100 inclusive. `partnership_tier` is set from that score: `GOLD` at 85 or above, otherwise `SILVER` at 70 or above, otherwise `WATCH`. `contract_status` is set from the same score: `ACTIVE` at 70 or above, otherwise `PROBATION` at 50 or above, otherwise `SUSPENDED`. `contract_status` is never written directly by any operation — it is purely a function of the score. Finally `last_scored_at` is set to the clock.

Only **one** pass is made. The referral, procedure and device cascades that a posture drop fires do change referral statuses that the Step A filter reads, but scoring is not re-run inside the same request to account for them. Scoring also always runs **after** the write that occasioned it, so the Step A filter already sees the new state: a newly submitted referral counts toward its own facility's volume and raw missed burden, a freshly declined one has already dropped out of the window, a freshly triaged one is read with its new `TRIAGED` status, and an overwritten `missed_treatment_count` is the value summed. The triggering referral is therefore itself exposed, in that same request, to the posture cascade described below whenever its status is still holdable: a submission or a missed-treatment update whose rescoring drops the posture returns a body already showing `INFO_REQUESTED`, a `hold_reason` of `FACILITY_POSTURE_HOLD` and an `info_request_count` one higher, and a triage whose rescoring drops the posture returns a referral whose `triage_score`, `triage_variant`, `urgency` and `triaged_at` have already been cleared back to null.

The posture transitions themselves are what make scoring dangerous. `ACTIVE → PROBATION` fires when a freshly computed score lands between 50 and 69 inclusive. `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and `PROBATION → SUSPENDED` both fire when the fresh score is 49 or below. Recovery works the same way in reverse — `PROBATION → ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` fire at a score of 70 or above, and `SUSPENDED → PROBATION` fires at a score between 50 and 69 inclusive — and each posture change increments the facility's `version`. The facility row is in fact written on **every** scoring occasion, not only on a posture change, so its `updated_at` and `version` advance even when the recomputed score, tier and posture all match the values already stored; `last_scored_at` is the field that always moves. A rescoring that leaves the posture where it already stood — including an already-`SUSPENDED` facility that rescores to `SUSPENDED` — is not a transition and fires no cascade, so a referral submitted to a facility that is already suspended is simply left in `SUBMITTED`.

Only the *downward* transitions cascade, and the two downward directions cascade to different depths. **On a drop to `PROBATION`**, every referral of that facility whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `TRIAGED` is moved to `INFO_REQUESTED` with `hold_reason` set to `FACILITY_POSTURE_HOLD`, its `info_request_count` increased by one, and its `triage_score`, `triage_variant`, `urgency` and `triaged_at` cleared back to null — a held referral has lost its triage and must earn it again. Referrals already `SCHEDULED` survive a drop to probation untouched.

**On a drop to `SUSPENDED`**, the same hold applies but the eligible set widens to include `SCHEDULED` referrals, and two further hops follow. Every procedure beneath a just-held referral whose `clinical_status` is `SCHEDULED` is moved to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to `FACILITY_POSTURE_HOLD`; and every `RESERVED` device usage on each of those cancelled procedures is moved to `RELEASED`, restoring the owning lot's `quantity_reserved` while leaving its `quantity_on_hand` untouched. Counting from the rescoring itself, that is a five-hop chain: rescore, posture drop, referral hold, procedure cancellation, device release. Every row it touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented.

Upward posture transitions fire **no** cascade whatsoever. Referrals held under `FACILITY_POSTURE_HOLD` are not auto-released when the facility recovers; the facility must resubmit each one, which is exactly the revision loop of §3.3.

A read-only partnership-metrics view exposes the whole picture to a `CenterAdmin`, an `AccessCoordinator` or the facility's own coordinator. It persists nothing and re-runs no scoring, so two consecutive calls against an unchanged clock return identical `data` payloads. Alongside `facility_id` and the nine persisted values, it derives two rates, both deliberately integer-scaled so that no floating-point number crosses the API boundary. `missed_treatments_per_referral_x100` is the persisted `raw_missed_count_window` multiplied by 100 and divided by the persisted `referral_volume_window`, rounded half up to a whole number; it is reported as 0 when that volume is 0 rather than dividing by zero. `same_day_honor_rate_pct` is the persisted `same_day_honored_window` multiplied by 100 and divided by the number of in-window same-day referrals, rounded half up to a whole percent. That denominator is the one quantity the view counts **live** at read time: referrals of this facility whose `status` is one of the five non-declined values, whose `created_at` lies inside the closed ninety-day window ending at the current clock, and whose `urgency` is `SAME_DAY`. Both numerators, by contrast, are echoed exactly as the last scoring evaluation left them, so a clock that has moved since that evaluation leaves the numerators describing one window and this denominator another; the rate is likewise reported as 0 when the denominator is 0. The view also echoes `window_start` — the instant ninety days before the clock — and `window_end` — the clock itself — as ISO 8601 instants, so a caller can see exactly which referrals were in scope.

### 3.5. Booking a Case: Rooms, Sedation Staffing and Recovery Bays

Booking is where the centre's finite physical capacity meets the urgency it just promised. An `AccessCoordinator` or `CenterAdmin` books a case against a `TRIAGED` referral by supplying `referral_id`, `room_id`, `primary_operator_id`, `procedure_type`, `sedation_level`, `scheduled_start`, `scheduled_end` and `expected_referral_version`, with `sedation_rn_id` supplied conditionally. A booking against a referral in `SUBMITTED`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED` or `DECLINED` is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_TRIAGED`.

`sedation_rn_id` is **required** when `sedation_level` is `MODERATE` and **ignored** when it is `NONE`. The named primary operator must be a clinician whose `clinician_role` is `INTERVENTIONALIST`, and the named sedation nurse must be one whose `clinician_role` is `SEDATION_RN`; either mismatch is refused with `ERR_CLINICIAN_ROLE_MISMATCH`. For a `MODERATE` case the sedation nurse must additionally hold `moderate_sedation_credentialed` as `true` and a `credential_expires_on` falling on or after the clock's current calendar date, otherwise `ERR_SEDATION_CREDENTIAL_INVALID`.

The new procedure's `access_id` is copied verbatim from the referral's `access_id` at the moment of creation and is **never** re-derived afterwards — not even if the referral is later resubmitted against a different access. Everything else starts from zero: `clinical_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `coding_status` is `UNCODED`, `allowed_amount_cents`, `billed_amount_cents`, `denied_amount_cents`, `net_receivable_cents` and `rebill_count` are all 0, and `attested_by_id`, `supporting_reading_id`, `sedation_end_at`, `discharged_at`, `reimbursement_variant`, `medical_necessity_statement`, `abort_reason`, `cancel_reason`, `denial_reason` and `denial_code` are all null. As a system side effect in the same request the parent referral advances `TRIAGED → SCHEDULED`, which increments its `version`.

Two temporal gates come first. A booking whose `scheduled_start` is earlier than the clock is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_IN_PAST`. Then the urgency window earned at triage is enforced: when the referral's `urgency` is `SAME_DAY`, the calendar date of `scheduled_start` must equal the calendar date of the referral's `triaged_at`; when it is `NEXT_DAY`, the calendar date of `scheduled_start` must be either the date of `triaged_at` itself or the single calendar day after it — a difference of exactly 0 or 1 days — so a `NEXT_DAY` case booked on the triage date is legal while one two or more days later, or one dated earlier than `triaged_at`, is not. A violation of either is refused with `ERR_SAME_DAY_WINDOW_MISSED`. A `ROUTINE` referral carries no date constraint.

Room occupancy is checked next, and it is a **pairwise** rule that includes the room's turnover buffer on both sides. The candidate's occupied span runs from its `scheduled_start` up to but not including its `scheduled_end` plus the room's `turnover_buffer_min`. That span must not intersect the identically constructed span of any existing booking in the same room that is neither cancelled nor aborted. Because the upper endpoint is excluded, adjacency is legal exactly when the candidate's `scheduled_start` is at or after the existing booking's `scheduled_end` plus the turnover buffer — a case may begin at the very minute the previous case's turnover completes, but not one minute earlier. An intersection is refused with `ERR_ROOM_OCCUPIED`.

Three capacity headrooms are then evaluated **in a fixed order**: the room's daily case cap first, then the sedation nurse's concurrency ceiling, then recovery-bay availability. The effective capacity for a booking is the smallest of the three headrooms, floored at 0, and a booking proceeds only when that value is at least 1 — but the *error identifier* returned is always the one belonging to the first headroom found wanting, which is why the order is pinned.

The **room daily case cap** counts the procedures already in that room on the same calendar date as the candidate's `scheduled_start` whose `clinical_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_ROOM`, `RECOVERY` or `DISCHARGED`; aborted and cancelled cases are excluded from the count and therefore free capacity back up. Room headroom is the room's `daily_case_cap`, plus a surge allowance, minus that count. The surge allowance is exactly one extra case, and it is granted only when all three of the following hold: the referral's `urgency` is `SAME_DAY`, the room's `room_type` is `ANGIO_SUITE`, and the candidate's `scheduled_start` falls at a UTC hour strictly before 15 — that is, at or before 14:59 UTC. Otherwise the allowance is zero. Exhausted room headroom is refused with `ERR_ROOM_DAILY_CAP_REACHED`.

The **sedation nurse's concurrency ceiling** applies only to a `MODERATE` candidate. It counts the moderate-sedation procedures assigned to the same `sedation_rn_id` whose `clinical_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_ROOM` or `RECOVERY` and whose booked span — from `scheduled_start` up to but not including `scheduled_end`, with no turnover buffer — intersects the candidate's identically constructed span. Nurse headroom is that clinician's `max_concurrent_sedation_cases` minus the count. This is an aggregate-cardinality rule rather than a pairwise exclusion: overlapping cases on the same nurse are perfectly legal right up until the ceiling is reached, and the count must remain strictly below the ceiling for the booking to proceed. Exhausting it is refused with `ERR_SEDATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

**Recovery-bay availability** likewise applies only to a `MODERATE` candidate, and its interval arithmetic differs in a way that catches people out. Each moderate-sedation case projects a recovery window running from its `scheduled_end` to ninety minutes after its `scheduled_end`, and that window is **fully closed** — both endpoints inclusive. The system counts the moderate-sedation procedures in the same room whose `clinical_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_ROOM` or `RECOVERY` and whose closed recovery window intersects the candidate's closed recovery window; because both endpoints are inclusive, two cases whose windows merely *touch* at a single instant do collide. Bay headroom is the room's `recovery_bay_count` divided by one bay per case, taken to its floor, minus that count, and the count must remain strictly below the bay count. Exhausting it is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_BAY_UNAVAILABLE`.

A candidate whose `sedation_level` is `NONE` skips the nurse ceiling and the bay check entirely — both are treated as unlimited — but it still consumes room headroom and still participates in room-occupancy arithmetic.

A booking may be withdrawn before the patient enters the room. Cancellation by an `AccessCoordinator` or `CenterAdmin` accepts an optional `cancel_note` — validated as a non-empty string whenever it is supplied, but recorded nowhere: no field of the procedure carries it and no response echoes it back — requires the procedure's `clinical_status` to be `SCHEDULED` (otherwise `ERR_PROCEDURE_NOT_SCHEDULED`), and moves it `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` recorded as `COORDINATOR_CANCEL`. As a compensating action every `RESERVED` device usage on the procedure moves to `RELEASED`, decrementing the owning lot's `quantity_reserved` and leaving its `quantity_on_hand` untouched, so the device-unit invariant holds with zero drift. On this explicitly requested cancellation the parent referral reverts `SCHEDULED → TRIAGED` so that the case can be rebooked; when the same `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` transition happens instead as the fourth hop of the facility-posture chain, `cancel_reason` is `FACILITY_POSTURE_HOLD` and the referral is not reverted to `TRIAGED` — it has already been held in `INFO_REQUESTED` by the hop above.

### 3.6. Consumable Inventory: Lots, Reservations and Expiry Control

Sheaths, balloons and stents arrive in lots, and a lot is the unit of both expiry control and recall. A `CenterAdmin` receives one by recording its `device_type`, the manufacturer's `catalog_code` and `lot_number`, the expiry date `expires_on`, the `received_quantity` of units, and the acquisition `unit_cost_cents` per unit. The received quantity must be at least 1 and the unit cost at least 0. The `lot_number` need only be unique *within* a device type — the same lot number under a different `device_type` is accepted, while a repeat of the same pair is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOT_NUMBER`. On receipt, `quantity_on_hand` is initialised to `received_quantity`, `quantity_reserved` is 0, `status` is `AVAILABLE` and `quarantine_reason` is null. `received_quantity` is never mutated again; it is the anchor of the conservation invariant of §2.11.

A lot can be pulled from circulation and put back. **Quarantine** requires the lot's `status` to be `AVAILABLE`, records the supplied `quarantine_reason` — a mandatory non-empty string, whose absence is a body-shape failure decided before the lot's state is examined — and moves it `AVAILABLE → QUARANTINED`; every `RESERVED` device usage drawn from that lot is simultaneously moved to `RELEASED`, decrementing the lot's `quantity_reserved` by each released `quantity` while leaving `quantity_on_hand` untouched. **Release** requires the lot's `status` to be `QUARANTINED` and moves it `QUARANTINED → AVAILABLE`, but only while the lot is still in date: a lot whose `expires_on` falls before the clock's current calendar date cannot be brought back and the attempt is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_LOT_EXPIRED`. The `quarantine_reason` is deliberately **retained** rather than cleared, so the audit trail survives the release. Quarantining a lot that is not `AVAILABLE`, or releasing one that is not `QUARANTINED`, is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_LOT_STATE_INVALID`.

Expiry is enforced by an administrator-only sweep rather than by a background timer, which keeps the whole system deterministic against the logical clock. The sweep moves every lot whose `status` is `AVAILABLE` or `QUARANTINED` and whose `expires_on` falls strictly before the clock's current calendar date into `EXPIRED`, and moves every `RESERVED` device usage on each swept lot to `RELEASED`, decrementing that lot's `quantity_reserved` and leaving its `quantity_on_hand` untouched. It reports `expired_lot_ids` and `released_usage_ids`, together with the tallies `expired_count` and `released_count`. `expired_lot_ids` is ordered by `expires_on` ascending then `created_at` ascending; `released_usage_ids` follows that same lot ordering, grouping each swept lot's released usages together and ordering them within their own lot by the order in which those usages were created. The sweep is idempotent: an `EXPIRED` lot is no longer in the input set, so a second sweep against an unchanged clock reports nothing.

There are two ways to commit units to a case, and they differ in who chooses the lot. An **explicit reservation** — available to an `Interventionalist`, `AccessCoordinator` or `CenterAdmin` — names the `device_lot_id`, the `quantity` of units wanted — an integer of at least 1, exactly as on the server-side allocation below, so a `quantity` of 0 or a negative one is a body-shape failure rather than a no-op reservation — and `expected_device_lot_version`. The named lot must have `status` of `AVAILABLE`; a `QUARANTINED` lot is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_LOT_QUARANTINED`. Its `expires_on` must be **strictly after** the calendar date of the procedure's `scheduled_start`, so a lot expiring on the case date is not usable for that case; a violation is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_LOT_EXPIRED`. A lot the expiry sweep has already moved to `EXPIRED` is refused with that same identifier on its `status` alone, even in the case where its `expires_on` still falls after the case date — which a case booked before the clock advanced can produce. A lot whose `status` is `DEPLETED`, or whose headroom is exactly 0, is refused with `ERR_LOT_DEPLETED`. Otherwise the headroom — `quantity_on_hand` minus `quantity_reserved` — must be at least the requested `quantity`, boundary inclusive, and a request larger than that single lot's headroom is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DEVICE_STOCK`. On success a device usage is created with `state` of `RESERVED`, `wasted_quantity` 0, `consumed_at` and `released_at` null; the lot's `quantity_reserved` increases by the reserved `quantity`, its `quantity_on_hand` is untouched, and its `version` increments.

A **server-side allocation** instead names only the `device_type` and the total `quantity`, and lets the server walk its stock first-expiry-first-out. The candidate set is every lot of the requested type whose `status` is `AVAILABLE`, whose `expires_on` is strictly after the calendar date of the procedure's `scheduled_start`, and whose headroom is strictly positive; quarantined, expired and depleted lots are excluded even when they still hold units on hand. Candidates are ordered by `expires_on` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and `id` is never read. If the candidate set is empty the call reserves nothing and is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_LOT`. If the candidates' aggregate headroom is less than the requested quantity the call again reserves nothing and is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DEVICE_STOCK` — the allocation is all-or-nothing, never partial. Otherwise the server walks the ordered candidates taking from each the lesser of the units still outstanding and that lot's headroom, creating one device usage per lot it draws on with `state` of `RESERVED`, incrementing that lot's `quantity_reserved` by the amount taken and its `version` by one, and stopping as soon as the request is fully covered. The created usages are returned in the same first-expiry-first-out order, under HTTP 201 with a `response_type` of `array`; because this is a creating operation and not a list, its `meta` carries only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp` — none of `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` or `has_more` — and the array is never windowed or filtered. This operation accepts no expected-version field, because the caller did not choose the lots.

A reservation ends exactly once, in one of two ways described in §2.11, and the arithmetic of each is worth restating in inventory terms. On **consumption** — which happens when the case is discharged or aborted — `consumed_at` is set to the clock and both `quantity_on_hand` and `quantity_reserved` fall by the usage's `quantity`; on the abort branch the usage's `wasted_quantity` is additionally set equal to its `quantity`, because devices opened for a case that was abandoned in the room cannot go back on the shelf. If a consumption drives a lot's `quantity_on_hand` to 0, that lot moves `AVAILABLE → DEPLETED` in the same request. On **release** — cancellation, quarantine, the expiry sweep, or the facility-posture chain — `released_at` is set to the clock and only `quantity_reserved` falls. Both paths increment the usage's `version` and the lot's `version` — the lot's by exactly one for the whole request under the per-request counting rule of §2.5, however many of its usages that request ends and whether or not the same request also flips the lot's `status`.

### 3.7. In the Room: Start, Recovery, Discharge and Abort

The clinical dimension of a procedure, tracked in `clinical_status`, advances entirely independently of the revenue-cycle dimension tracked in `coding_status`. The two live on the same row and never read each other, with a single exception noted in §3.8: a case must have reached `DISCHARGED` or `ABORTED` before its first code line may be filed. A discharged case may sit `UNCODED` indefinitely, and an aborted case may still be coded, attested and billed.

**Starting** a case is exclusive to an `Interventionalist`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the procedure's `primary_operator_id` — any other interventionalist is refused with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_CLINICIAN`. The procedure's `clinical_status` must be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_PROCEDURE_NOT_SCHEDULED`. The access the case is being performed on must be in `MATURING`, `IN_USE` or `DYSFUNCTIONAL`; if it reached `ABANDONED` after the booking was made — which is precisely what the conversion cascade of §3.9 arranges — the start is refused with `ERR_ACCESS_ABANDONED`. An access still sitting in `PLANNED` or `PLACED` is refused with that same identifier: booking never examines the access status, so either is reachable at start time, and both report `ERR_ACCESS_ABANDONED` even though the access was never abandoned. For a `MODERATE` case the sedation credential is re-checked against **this** request's clock, so a booking that was perfectly legal yesterday can fail today with `ERR_SEDATION_CREDENTIAL_INVALID`. On success the case moves `SCHEDULED → IN_ROOM`.

**Moving to recovery** is likewise the assigned primary operator's act, requires `clinical_status` of `IN_ROOM` (otherwise `ERR_PROCEDURE_NOT_IN_ROOM`), and carries the single field `sedation_end_at` — the instant sedation actually stopped. That instant must fall at or after the procedure's `scheduled_start`; an earlier one is a body-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at rung 4, so it is decided before the assigned-clinician and state checks. It is recorded on the procedure and read by nothing afterwards: the closed ninety-minute recovery-bay window of §3.5 remains anchored on `scheduled_end` rather than on `sedation_end_at`, so charting a late sedation end never retroactively alters another booking's bay arithmetic. The case moves `IN_ROOM → RECOVERY`.

Recovery is charted by the **assigned sedation nurse**, whose `X-User-ID` must equal the procedure's `sedation_rn_id`. Each observation records the instant `observed_at` it was charted, a modified `aldrete_score` between 0 and 10 inclusive, and `on_room_air` stating whether the patient is breathing without supplemental oxygen. Observations are immutable legal records: the response carries `id` and `created_at` and no `version`, and no operation edits or deletes one.

**Discharge** is the sedation nurse's act too, and it is the single most consequential operation in the system. It requires `clinical_status` of `RECOVERY` (otherwise `ERR_PROCEDURE_NOT_IN_RECOVERY`) and three recovery criteria, all of which must hold or the request is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_CRITERIA_NOT_MET`: at least two observations must exist for the procedure; the observation with the greatest `observed_at`, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending, must have an `aldrete_score` of 9 or above **and** `on_room_air` true; and the span between the earliest and latest `observed_at` must be at least fifteen minutes, boundary inclusive, so a pair of observations charted exactly a quarter of an hour apart qualifies.

Discharge then fires a five-hop cascade, in this fixed order.

**Hop 1.** The procedure's `discharged_at` is set to the clock, and every `RESERVED` device usage on it moves `RESERVED → CONSUMED` with the inventory arithmetic of §3.6, taking any emptied lot to `DEPLETED`.

**Hop 2.** The parent referral moves `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` with `completed_at` set to the clock. This hop only applies to the referral the discharging procedure actually names in its `referral_id`, and hops 2 through 5 together are conditional on that referral still standing in `SCHEDULED`. If it has since been held to `INFO_REQUESTED` by a facility-posture drop — which widens to `SCHEDULED` referrals while cancelling only the cases beneath them that are themselves still `SCHEDULED`, and so leaves a case already in `RECOVERY` dischargeable — the discharge still succeeds and still performs hop 1, but the referral, the vascular access, every conversion plan and every source access are all left exactly as they stood. The facility rescoring below runs either way.

**Hop 3, caused by hop 2.** The vascular access is re-evaluated. An access in `DYSFUNCTIONAL` returns to `IN_USE`; an access in `MATURING` also moves to `IN_USE`, but only when the discharging procedure's `procedure_type` is `FISTULA_MATURATION_INTERVENTION`. Whichever branch applies, the access's intervention counters are recomputed — `angioplasty_count`, `thrombectomy_count`, `catheter_exchange_count` and `maturation_intervention_count` each count the `DISCHARGED` procedures on that access of the matching `procedure_type` — and its `intervention_burden_score` is recomputed as described below. The recount and the rescore happen on this hop whether or not either status branch applied, and the access row is written in its own right — `updated_at` refreshed, `version` incremented by one for the request — because the counter matching the discharging procedure's own `procedure_type` necessarily changes when that procedure has just become `DISCHARGED`.

**Hop 4, caused by hop 3.** Every conversion plan whose `target_access_id` is that access, whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `readiness_met` is true moves `ACTIVE → CONVERTED` with `converted_at` set to the clock.

**Hop 5, caused by hop 4.** Each newly converted plan's source access — the one named by its `from_access_id` — moves to `ABANDONED` with `abandoned_at` set to the clock, decrementing the owning patient's `access_count`. This is the hop that is exempt from the open-case block of §3.2.

Discharge finally rescores the referring facility, which may raise `same_day_honored_window` and lift the facility's posture.

**Aborting** a case is the divergent branch, and its divergence is the whole point. An abort is the assigned primary operator's act, requires `clinical_status` of `IN_ROOM`, and records the supplied `abort_reason` on the procedure as it moves `IN_ROOM → ABORTED`. It shares hop 1 exactly — every `RESERVED` device usage on the case is consumed — except that each consumed usage additionally records `wasted_quantity` equal to its `quantity`. It fires **none** of hops 2 through 5: the parent referral deliberately stays `SCHEDULED` so the case can be rebooked, the access keeps its `status` and every one of its counters, no conversion plan converts, and no source access is abandoned.

`DISCHARGED`, `ABORTED` and `CANCELLED` are all terminal for the clinical dimension. Any clinical transition attempted from one of them is refused with `ERR_PROCEDURE_TERMINAL`, and that identifier **overrides** the state identifier the operation itself names: cancelling, starting, moving to recovery, discharging or aborting a case whose `clinical_status` is already `DISCHARGED`, `ABORTED` or `CANCELLED` reports `ERR_PROCEDURE_TERMINAL` rather than `ERR_PROCEDURE_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_PROCEDURE_NOT_IN_ROOM` or `ERR_PROCEDURE_NOT_IN_RECOVERY`, so the second cancel of a cancelled case is refused rather than absorbed and a cancelled case can never be brought into the room. Charting a recovery observation is not a clinical transition — it moves no `clinical_status` — and reports `ERR_PROCEDURE_NOT_IN_RECOVERY` whenever the case is not in `RECOVERY`, terminal or not.

The **intervention burden score** on an access is a weighted sum of its persisted counters: twice its `angioplasty_count`, plus three times its `thrombectomy_count`, plus its `catheter_exchange_count`, plus its `maturation_intervention_count`, plus five times its `attributable_infection_count`. It is an integer with no rounding. Only counters derived from `DISCHARGED` procedures and attributable infections feed it — procedures in `SCHEDULED`, `IN_ROOM`, `RECOVERY`, `ABORTED` or `CANCELLED` never contribute. The score is recomputed whenever any of those counters changes, which is to say on hop 3 of the discharge cascade and on the recording of an attributable infection. It matters because it is the clinical gate on conversion planning: a burden of exactly 4 is sufficient, while 3 is not.

### 3.8. The Revenue Cycle: Coding, Attestation, Billing, Denial and Rebill

The `coding_status` dimension is a cycle, not a ladder: `UNCODED` to `CODED`, `CODED` to itself once per additional line, then `ATTESTED`, then `BILLED`, and from `BILLED` or `REBILLED` into `DENIED` and back out to `REBILLED`. It has no terminal state. No coding transition reads or writes `clinical_status` beyond the single precondition on the first code line.

**Filing a code line** is exclusive to a `Coder`. Each line records the `hcpcs_code` string as submitted, its bundling `code_family`, the payer's fee-schedule amount `base_allowed_cents` before any adjustment, the submission `rank`, and optionally a billing `modifier` that defaults to `NONE`. The base amount must be at least 0 and the rank at least 1; the rank must be unique among that procedure's existing lines, and a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE_RANK`. The procedure's `clinical_status` must already be `DISCHARGED` or `ABORTED` and its `coding_status` must be `UNCODED` or `CODED`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_CODING_STATE_INVALID`. Segregation of duties applies: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from both the procedure's `primary_operator_id` and its `sedation_rn_id`, and the operator or the sedation nurse attempting to code their own case is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The first accepted line moves `UNCODED → CODED`; each subsequent line re-enters `CODED` from `CODED`. Lines are never deleted.

Every accepted line triggers a **full recomputation of the allowed amount over the whole line set** — not an incremental addition — and the recomputation rewrites `is_bundled`, `reduction_factor_pct` and `allowed_cents` on every line as well as the procedure's `allowed_amount_cents` and `reimbursement_variant`. Those three per-line fields are derived values rewritten in place: a code line's own `updated_at` and `version` are written when the line is filed and never move again, so the arrival of a later line that collapses or reduces an earlier one leaves that earlier line's `version` at 1. Only the procedure row advances, and by exactly one for the request. The calculation runs as follows.

The variant is chosen from the payer model of the patient reached through the procedure's referral and its access: a `payer_program` of `BUNDLED_ESRD` selects the bundled variant, and `FEE_FOR_SERVICE` selects the fee-for-service variant. The lines are then ordered by `rank` ascending; because `rank` is unique per procedure no tie is possible, but the declared tie-break is `created_at` ascending, and `id` is never used for ordering. The **primary** line is the one with the smallest `rank`. Two multipliers are derived per line. The modifier factor is 0.50 when the line's `modifier` is `M52` and 1.00 for both `NONE` and `M59`. The position factor depends on the line's **position in the ordered list, not on its rank value**: 100 percent for the first line, 50 percent for the second, and 25 percent for the third and every line after it — so ranks 3, 7 and 40 filed as the only three lines are treated as positions 1, 2 and 3 and take 100, 50 and 25 percent.

Each line is then resolved. Under the bundled variant, a line that is **not** in first position, whose `modifier` is not `M59`, and whose `code_family` either equals the primary line's `code_family` or is `IMAGING`, collapses: its `is_bundled` becomes true, its `reduction_factor_pct` becomes 0, and its `allowed_cents` becomes 0. An `M59` modifier marks a distinct anatomical site and blocks that collapse; a family that differs from the primary's and is not `IMAGING` also survives. Every other line — every line under fee-for-service, the primary line under either variant, and the surviving lines under the bundled variant — takes `is_bundled` false, `reduction_factor_pct` equal to its position factor, and an `allowed_cents` of `base_allowed_cents` multiplied by the modifier factor multiplied by the reduction percentage divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents. Surviving bundled-variant lines still take the position-based reduction; surviving is not the same as being paid in full.

The procedure's `allowed_amount_cents` is the **sum of the already-rounded per-line amounts**. The rounding order is pinned as described in §2.9 and the alternative of summing unrounded products and rounding once at the end is explicitly not used. For three lines with base amounts of 180050, 45075 and 90050 cents, all carrying `modifier` of `NONE`, the fee-for-service variant yields 180050 at 100 percent, 22538 at 50 percent (45075 halved is 22537.5, rounded half up to 22538) and 22513 at 25 percent (90050 quartered is 22512.5, rounded half up to 22513), for a total of exactly 225101 cents — sum-then-round would yield 225100, which is wrong. Under the bundled variant, if the second and third of those lines share the primary's family or are `IMAGING` and carry no `M59`, both collapse to 0 and the total is 180050 cents. The same three lines therefore produce a materially different total for a `BUNDLED_ESRD` patient than for a `FEE_FOR_SERVICE` one.

**Attestation** is the primary operator's act and no one else's. It records the `medical_necessity_statement` narrative and a `supporting_reading_id`, sets `attested_by_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, and moves `CODED → ATTESTED`. The procedure's `coding_status` must be `CODED`. The acting user must be exactly the procedure's `primary_operator_id`; any other interventionalist — including one who was not on the case at all — is refused with `ERR_ATTESTATION_NOT_OPERATOR`, which is a distinct identifier from the assignment check applied to the room operations, so the two never compete for the same request. The cited reading must sit on the procedure's own `access_id` and its `reading_at` must fall inside the closed thirty-day lookback window ending at the procedure's `scheduled_start` — that is, at or after thirty days before `scheduled_start` and at or before `scheduled_start`. A reading on a different access, a reading after the case was scheduled to begin, or a reading older than the window is refused with `ERR_MEDICAL_NECESSITY_UNSUPPORTED`. A reading taken exactly thirty days before, or exactly at, `scheduled_start` qualifies.

**Billing** is the `CenterAdmin`'s act and requires `coding_status` of `ATTESTED`. Two gates apply, in this pinned order. First, **code-to-device consistency**: every line whose `is_bundled` is false and whose `code_family` is `ANGIOPLASTY`, `THROMBECTOMY` or `CATH_EXCHANGE` must be matched by at least one `CONSUMED` device usage on the same procedure drawn from a lot whose `device_type` is that family's required consumable — `ANGIOPLASTY` requires `BALLOON`, `THROMBECTOMY` requires `SHEATH`, and `CATH_EXCHANGE` requires `SHEATH`. A line without its device is refused with `ERR_CODE_DEVICE_MISMATCH`. Lines whose `is_bundled` is true are exempt, as are the `IMAGING` and `ACCESS_MAINTENANCE_BUNDLE` families, and a `RESERVED` or `RELEASED` usage does not satisfy the requirement — only `CONSUMED` does. Second, and **only after** the consistency gate has passed, the per-session cap: `allowed_amount_cents` must be at most 480000, boundary inclusive, and an amount above it is refused with `ERR_SESSION_CAP_EXCEEDED`. A request that violates both must report `ERR_CODE_DEVICE_MISMATCH`. On success the procedure moves `ATTESTED → BILLED`, `billed_amount_cents` is set to `allowed_amount_cents`, `denied_amount_cents` is set to 0, and `net_receivable_cents` is set equal to `billed_amount_cents`.

**Denial** is available from `BILLED` and from `REBILLED`. It records the payer's `denial_reason` category and remittance `denial_code`, sets `denied_amount_cents` equal to `billed_amount_cents` and `net_receivable_cents` to 0, and moves the procedure to `DENIED`. `billed_amount_cents` itself is not reduced; the net receivable is the difference between what was billed and what was denied, and it is that difference which must be conserved.

**Rebilling** closes the loop. It requires `coding_status` of `DENIED` and a `rebill_count` strictly below 2, so at most two rebills are ever accepted and a third is refused with `ERR_REBILL_LIMIT_REACHED`. It is a compensating action: the allowed-amount calculation is re-run over the **unchanged** line set — no line was deleted, so the recomputation reads exactly the same inputs — `billed_amount_cents` is set to `allowed_amount_cents`, `denied_amount_cents` returns to 0 and `net_receivable_cents` returns to `billed_amount_cents`. The money must land back on its exact pre-denial value with zero drift; a cycle of bill, deny, rebill, deny, rebill must leave `net_receivable_cents` identical to its value after the original billing. `rebill_count` increments by one and the procedure moves `DENIED → REBILLED`.

A **reimbursement preview** is available to a `CenterAdmin` or a `Coder` and is a pure read that persists nothing at all. It evaluates **both** variants of the allowed-amount calculation over the stored line set and reports the `procedure_id`, the `applicable_variant` echoing the patient's `payer_program`, the `bundled_esrd_total_cents` and `fee_for_service_total_cents` the two variants would produce, the `variant_delta_cents` obtained by subtracting the bundled total from the fee-for-service total, and the per-line `line_items`. `line_items` is ordered by `rank` ascending, the same ordering the calculation walks, and each entry carries exactly `id`, `rank`, `hcpcs_code`, `code_family`, `modifier`, `base_allowed_cents`, `position_factor_pct`, `bundled_esrd_is_bundled`, `bundled_esrd_reduction_factor_pct`, `bundled_esrd_allowed_cents`, `fee_for_service_is_bundled`, `fee_for_service_reduction_factor_pct` and `fee_for_service_allowed_cents` — the two variants reported side by side against the same line, with `position_factor_pct` the position-based percentage shared by both. No `coding_status` gates the read, so a procedure with no code lines at all previews successfully: both totals and the delta are 0 and `line_items` is empty. Calling it never changes a line's `is_bundled`, `reduction_factor_pct` or `allowed_cents`, never changes the procedure's `allowed_amount_cents` or `reimbursement_variant`, and never bumps a `version`.

### 3.9. Conversion Planning, Maturation Follow-up and Infection Surveillance

A catheter-dependent patient is meant to end up on a fistula or graft, and a conversion plan is the record of that intent. Creating one is an `AccessCoordinator` or `CenterAdmin` act and names the `patient_id`, the `from_access_id` being converted away from, the `target_access_type` intended, the `target_conversion_date` aimed at, the mandatory `expected_access_version` of the source access, and optionally the `target_access_id` if the successor access already exists. The plan is created with `status` of `DRAFT`, `maturation_check_count` 0, `readiness_met` false, `readiness_failure_reasons` an empty list, `last_check_at`, `last_diameter_mm`, `last_flow_ml_min`, `last_depth_mm`, `days_since_placement` and `converted_at` all null. Creation **supersedes**: any existing plan on the same `from_access_id` whose `status` is `DRAFT` moves `DRAFT → ABANDONED` in the same request. That supersession is exactly why the source access carries an expected version here — two coordinators drafting concurrently are a genuine lost update, not a state precondition.

**Activating** a plan moves it `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and requires three things. The plan's `status` must be `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. Its `target_access_id` must be non-null and the target access's `status` must be `PLANNED`, `PLACED` or `MATURING`; a target that is already `IN_USE`, `DYSFUNCTIONAL` or `ABANDONED` is refused with `ERR_TARGET_ACCESS_NOT_CONVERTIBLE`. And conversion must be clinically indicated: either the source access's `access_type` is `TUNNELED_CATHETER`, or its `intervention_burden_score` is at least 4, boundary inclusive — a burden of exactly 4 qualifies and 3 does not — otherwise `ERR_CONVERSION_NOT_INDICATED`. The three are listed above in their natural reading order, but precedence follows §2.7 rather than that order: the plan-state gate first, then conversion indication at rung 26, then target convertibility at rung 27 — so a `DRAFT` plan whose conversion is not indicated *and* whose target is not convertible reports `ERR_CONVERSION_NOT_INDICATED`. Activation also writes the target access's `predecessor_access_id` to the value of `from_access_id` and increments that target access's `version`, so the successor chain is recorded on the access itself and not only on the plan.

**Maturation checks** may be filed by an `Interventionalist` or an `AccessCoordinator` and record the three ultrasound measurements `diameter_mm`, `flow_ml_min` and `depth_mm` of the target access. Each of the three is validated as a JSON number of at least 0 with **no** upper bound, so a negative measurement is a body-shape failure while an implausibly large one is accepted and simply scored against the criteria below. Checks are refused with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` when the plan's `status` is `DRAFT`, `CONVERTED` or `ABANDONED`. Every accepted check evaluates five criteria, all boundary inclusive:

- `diameter_mm` must be at least 6.0; a smaller measurement adds the failure reason `DIAMETER`.
- `flow_ml_min` must be at least 600; a smaller measurement adds `FLOW`.
- `depth_mm` must be at most 6.0 — deeper is worse, so this criterion is the only one with an upper bound; a greater measurement adds `DEPTH`.
- `days_since_placement`, the count of whole calendar days from the target access's `placed_on` to the clock's current calendar date, must be at least 42; fewer days adds `MATURATION_DAYS`.
- The number of surveillance readings on the target access whose `reading_at` is at or before the clock must be at least 2; fewer adds `SURVEILLANCE_COUNT`.

Measurements of exactly 6.0 millimetres diameter, exactly 600 millilitres per minute, exactly 6.0 millimetres depth, exactly day 42 and exactly two readings all **pass**; 5.9, 599, 6.1, day 41 and one reading all fail. `readiness_met` is true only when all five criteria hold, and `readiness_failure_reasons` holds the failure tokens of exactly the criteria that failed, sorted in ascending ASCII order, and is an empty list on a passing check. Every accepted check writes `last_check_at` to the clock, copies the three measurements into `last_diameter_mm`, `last_flow_ml_min` and `last_depth_mm`, writes the computed `days_since_placement`, and increments `maturation_check_count` by one — the counter counts checks, not passes, and is never reset. The plan's `status` changes only where a transition is declared for the current state and the outcome: a failing check on an `ACTIVE` plan moves it `ACTIVE → MATURATION_HOLD`, and a passing check on a plan in `MATURATION_HOLD` moves it back `MATURATION_HOLD → ACTIVE` with no further condition attached — a target access that has already reached `IN_USE` cannot get this far, the convertibility gate of rung 27 having refused the check before any measurement was recorded. There is no self-transition, so a passing check on an already-`ACTIVE` plan and a failing check on an already-held plan record their measurements and leave the `status` as it was.

**Conversion** is not an operation a caller invokes: there is no convert endpoint, and no request names a plan in order to convert it. It fires wherever a target access *reaches* `IN_USE` inside a request, and exactly two paths do that — hop 4 of the discharge cascade of §3.7, and the `MATURING → IN_USE` status transition of §3.2 requested directly on the target access, which fires hops 4 and 5 identically and in the same order. The caller-driven `PLACED → IN_USE` transition of a tunneled catheter is not one of them, and neither is a transition that leaves the access where it already stood. A plan moves `ACTIVE → CONVERTED` with `converted_at` set to the clock when the target access it names has just reached `IN_USE` and the plan's `readiness_met` is true. A plan sitting in `MATURATION_HOLD`, or one that is `ACTIVE` with `readiness_met` false, does not convert, even when its target access reaches `IN_USE`. Immediately afterwards, hop 5 abandons the plan's source access. A plan in `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `MATURATION_HOLD` instead moves to `ABANDONED` when its target access is abandoned, and both `CONVERTED` and `ABANDONED` are terminal.

**Infection events** close the loop back onto access health. Recording one is available to an `Interventionalist`, `AccessCoordinator` or `CenterAdmin` and names the `procedure_id` the infection is being attributed to, the `event_type`, the `organism` cultured and the `onset_at` instant symptoms began. The `access_id` is copied verbatim from the named procedure and is never re-derived through the referral. The procedure's `clinical_status` must be `DISCHARGED`, so that its `discharged_at` is non-null and there is an anchor to measure from; a procedure that is still in the room, or that was aborted or cancelled, cannot carry an attribution. Every non-`DISCHARGED` status is refused with the rung-8 identifier `ERR_PROCEDURE_TERMINAL` (409) — `SCHEDULED`, `IN_ROOM` and `RECOVERY` included, so this one operation reports that identifier for cases that are not terminal at all. The event's `days_post_procedure` is the whole number of days between `discharged_at` and `onset_at`, computed as the elapsed seconds between the two instants divided by 86400 and floored — so 29 days and 23 hours is 29, not 30. `attributable` is true when `days_post_procedure` is between 0 and 30 inclusive and false otherwise: exactly 30 days is attributable, 31 is not, and an `onset_at` before `discharged_at` yields a negative count and is not attributable.

When and only when an event is attributable, the access's `attributable_infection_count` increments by one and its `intervention_burden_score` is recomputed with the weight of five per attributable infection, which can push an access over the burden threshold that makes conversion indicated. A second attributable infection carries a further consequence: once the access's `attributable_infection_count` reaches 2 or more, an access whose `status` is `IN_USE` moves `IN_USE → DYSFUNCTIONAL` in the same request. A non-attributable event is still persisted in full — it simply touches neither counter nor status.

## 5. Error Code Catalogue
