# Release Of Information API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A release-of-information (ROI) vendor sits between covered-entity hospitals and everyone who asks them for a medical record: the patient exercising a statutory right of access, the personal representative of a deceased patient, the plaintiff's attorney, the disability insurer, the employer running a fitness-for-duty file, the academic researcher, the subpoenaing agency, and the receiving provider who simply needs continuity of care. Each of those parties arrives under a different legal theory, and the legal theory — not the size of the record — decides how fast the record must go out, how much the requester may be charged, which protected categories may travel at all, and what happens to the vendor's money when a deadline slips. Today that reasoning lives in a spreadsheet per state and a tribal-knowledge checklist per facility, and it fails predictably: patient right-of-access requests get billed at third-party retail rates, behavioral-health pages leave the building without a specific authorization, and statutory penalties accrue silently because nobody recomputes a deadline after an authorization defect is cured.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces that spreadsheet. The service is mounted under `/roi` and owns the whole fulfillment chain: intake by requester type with authorization-validity checking, per-state fee and timeline regimes, segregation of sensitive record categories for a named reviewer's decision, compilation across current EHR, legacy archive, imaging and scanned-paper sources, apply-and-approve redaction workflow, invoicing under per-page tiers and statutory total caps, secure transmission with patient-directed exceptions, turnaround monitoring with a dual penalty measure, and the denial, review and amendment machinery that HIPAA requires around all of it.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **segregation of duties is structural, not advisory**: the specialist who takes a request in may not be the reviewer who releases it, the analyst who blacks out a page may not be the reviewer who approves the blackout, and the officer who denies a request may not be the officer who reviews the denial. Second, **every temporal computation reads a logical clock rather than the wall clock**, so a lateness story — deadline, tolling, extension, breach, penalty accrual, fee forfeiture — is reproducible from a fixed sequence of calls.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. A call that arrives with `X-User-ID` absent, empty, or composed entirely of whitespace is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A call whose `X-User-Role` is not among the roles the addressed endpoint permits — including a call that omits the `X-User-Role` header altogether, and a call that spells a real role with different casing — is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403). The authentication check always precedes the authorization check, so a call missing both a user id and a permitted role reports `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.

Eight roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `FacilityAdmin`, `IntakeSpecialist`, `RecordsAnalyst`, `ReleaseReviewer`, `BillingClerk`, `PrivacyOfficer`, and `Requester`.

The `PlatformAdmin` is the vendor's platform operator: it registers state fee schedules, onboards facilities, suspends and reactivates them, drives the compliance sweep, and sets the logical clock. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `actor_user_id` on a compliance event, or `intake_user_id` on a request) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same opaque-identifier rule holds for `IntakeSpecialist`, `RecordsAnalyst`, `ReleaseReviewer`, `BillingClerk`, and `PrivacyOfficer`: each of those five is a staff role whose `X-User-ID` is an opaque workforce identifier, recorded on the entities their actions touch — `intake_user_id`, `release_reviewer_user_id`, `redaction_applied_by_user_id`, `redaction_approved_by_user_id`, `issued_by_user_id`, `reviewer_user_id`, `actor_user_id` — and compared only against *other recorded user identifiers* for segregation-of-duties purposes, never against an entity `id`.

Two roles are nominally entity-backed, but only one of them is actually policed. When `X-User-Role` is `FacilityAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is understood to be the `id` of the acting `Facility`, yet no endpoint ever compares it against a resource's `facility_id`: a `FacilityAdmin` exercises every endpoint its role permits across every facility on the platform, and a `FacilityAdmin` list read is never narrowed. Requester ownership is the only ownership gate the server enforces. When `X-User-Role` is `Requester`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Requester`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Requester` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, when a caller acts as `Requester` on any endpoint whose permitted roles include `Requester`, the addressed request — reached directly, or indirectly through an addressed invoice, delivery, denial decision, amendment request or authorization — must have a `requester_id` equal to `X-User-ID`; if it does not, the call is rejected with `ERR_NOT_REQUEST_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Collection list endpoints behave differently: rather than erroring, a list read performed by a `Requester` is silently narrowed to rows whose `requester_id` equals `X-User-ID`. That narrowing applies only to reads addressing a whole collection; a paginated sub-resource read that carries a path identifier — an invoice's priced line set — resolves ownership through the addressed entity instead and reports `ERR_NOT_REQUEST_OWNER` rather than returning an empty page. An addressed requesting party is itself outside the comparison: any `Requester` may read any single requesting party.

Role capability is otherwise partitioned by function. `PlatformAdmin` alone registers fee schedules, onboards facilities, changes facility status and sets the clock; it shares patient registration with `FacilityAdmin`, requester registration with `IntakeSpecialist`, and the compliance sweep with `PrivacyOfficer`; it is forbidden every request-lifecycle write (intake decision, extension, compilation completion, sensitive review, release, withdrawal) and every record-segment, invoice, delivery, denial and amendment write. `FacilityAdmin` acts for one covered entity, shares patient registration with `PlatformAdmin`, may read staff-visible resources, and is forbidden facility onboarding, facility status changes, and every request, authorization, record-segment, invoice, delivery, denial and amendment write. `IntakeSpecialist` alone creates requests, records intake decisions, notices statutory extensions and cures defective authorizations; it shares requester registration with `PlatformAdmin` and authorization recording with `PrivacyOfficer`; it may never release a request. `RecordsAnalyst` alone opens record segments, records their dispositions, applies redactions and declares compilation finished; it may never approve a redaction, run a sensitive review, or release. `ReleaseReviewer` alone runs the sensitive review, approves or reworks redactions, releases requests, opens deliveries and records transmission attempts; it shares delivery confirmation with `Requester`; it may never create or disposition a record segment and may never touch an invoice, denial or amendment. `BillingClerk` alone creates, issues and settles invoices. `PrivacyOfficer` alone issues denials, reviews them, and decides amendment requests; it shares authorization recording with `IntakeSpecialist`, authorization revocation with `Requester`, and the compliance sweep with `PlatformAdmin`; it may not submit or resubmit an amendment request. `Requester` alone withdraws its own request, submits amendment requests and resubmits them with additional information; it shares authorization revocation with `PrivacyOfficer` and delivery confirmation with `ReleaseReviewer`; it may not read facilities, patients, record segments, fee schedules, or a request's compliance trail.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one datetime in the whole system read from the host wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, so it keeps advancing on its own no matter what instant a caller has set the logical clock to.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses — the 4xx status codes, and equally the 500 an unhandled server fault produces — follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. When `limit` is omitted it defaults to 10; a supplied `limit` must be at least 1 and at most 100. When `offset` is omitted it defaults to 0, and its minimum is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). So is a value that is not the plain canonical decimal spelling of an integer: a fraction such as `2.0`, a sign-prefixed or zero-padded numeral such as `+5` or `05`, and any non-numeric text are all rejected the same way. An `offset` at or beyond `total_count` is not an error — it yields an empty `data` array with `has_more` false.

Unless a specific endpoint pins a different ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending, with ties broken by the order in which the rows were appended to the store; no identifier ever participates in the ordering, so rows minted inside the same millisecond still come back in creation order. Two endpoints override this: a request's compliance trail is ordered by `occurred_at` ascending and then by append order, with no identifier participating in the ordering; and an invoice's priced line set is ordered by `sequence` ascending, with `sequence` as the only ordering key.

For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filter before pagination), and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond this page). Single-resource responses omit all four. Every filter a list endpoint declares is an exact string comparison against the stored field, applied before pagination: a query parameter the endpoint does not declare is ignored rather than rejected, and a filter value outside the field's declared vocabulary is not a validation error either — it simply matches no row and yields an empty page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix, generated by the server and never accepted from a client. Most resources also carry three further common properties: `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the resource was first persisted; `updated_at`, the same format, refreshed on every mutation of that resource; and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the resource.

Two resource kinds are deliberately leaner. Invoice lines and compliance events are append-only artefacts, never edited after they are written, and they therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — an invoice line has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and a compliance event has no `updated_at` and no `version`. Every other resource kind — fee rules, facilities, patients, requesting parties, authorizations, requests, record segments, invoices, deliveries, denial decisions and amendment requests — carries the full set of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

All `source: system` datetime fields, all deadline arithmetic and both lateness measures read a **logical clock** rather than the host wall clock. The logical clock starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` when the process starts. A `PlatformAdmin`-only test-driving capability sets it: the caller supplies `to_iso8601`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant, and the response body returns the new `current_utc` — `data` carries that one field and nothing else, re-rendered in the canonical three-fractional-digit spelling rather than echoed as written. The supplied instant may move the clock backwards as freely as forwards; no ordering constraint relates it to the clock's previous value. Setting the clock mutates no business entity — no `version` moves, no deadline is recomputed eagerly — but the next business call observes the new instant. Every field described below as "the current logical clock" means exactly this value at the moment the call is served.

Datetime rendering is uniform. Every datetime a response body carries that the server itself derives or stamps is rendered exactly as `created_at` is — an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits, any sub-millisecond remainder truncated. That covers, among others, `received_at`, `statutory_due_at`, `defect_opened_at`, `extension_noticed_at`, `released_at`, `closed_at`, `retrieved_at`, `legacy_job_end_at`, `revoked_at`, an invoice's or a denial's `issued_at`, an invoice's `settled_at`, `sent_at`, `confirmed_at`, `review_due_at`, `decided_at`, `decision_due_at`, a compliance event's `occurred_at`, the `effective_completion_at` of the computed turnaround read, and the `current_utc` the clock-setting response returns. A datetime the *client* supplies is by contrast stored and echoed back exactly as the client wrote it rather than re-rendered, so `signed_at`, `expires_at` and `legacy_job_start_at` come back in whatever ISO 8601 UTC spelling they arrived in. The grammar the server *accepts* for a client-supplied instant is wider than the one it emits: a four-digit year, month and day separated by hyphens, then `T`, `t` or a single space, then `HH:MM` with an optional `:SS` and an optional fractional part of one to nine digits, optionally closed by `Z`, `z` or a numeric UTC offset, with surrounding whitespace tolerated. A missing designator is read as UTC and a zero offset such as `+00:00` is accepted, but any non-zero offset means the value is not a UTC instant and is therefore the schema violation of §2.8. Date-only fields — `date_of_birth`, `date_range_start` and `date_range_end` — accept only the exact `YYYY-MM-DD` form and are likewise echoed as written.

### 2.5. Optimistic Concurrency: Repricing and Superseding

Two write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because in both cases a later writer that has not observed an earlier writer's effect would silently discard it.

An invoice creation request must carry `expected_request_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Request`; the field is declared as zero or greater, so a negative value — or a non-integer one — is the schema violation of §2.8 reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the `request_id` is even resolved, not a version mismatch. If `expected_request_version` does not equal the request's current `version`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REQUEST_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists because pricing reads `billable_page_count` and `penalty_exposure_cents`, and every segment disposition, redaction approval, denial overturn and extension notice moves the request's `version`. A `DRAFT` invoice does not block a new invoice — it is voided and superseded — so without the version guard a clerk who priced against a stale page ledger would win, and the earlier clerk's priced line set would vanish unobserved.

An authorization creation request must carry `expected_patient_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the client's last observed `version` of the `Patient` named by `patient_id`. If `expected_patient_version` does not equal that patient's current `version`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful authorization creation increments the patient's `version` by 1, which is what makes the guard bite: a new authorization carrying `supersedes_authorization_id` retires the named predecessor and is exempt from the coverage-window overlap check, so a specialist who has not observed the patient's changed authorization set would otherwise retire an authorization it never saw.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Issuing an invoice, settling it, dispositioning a segment, approving a redaction, releasing a request, opening a delivery, denying, reviewing or amending all carry no expected-version input.

### 2.6. Segregation of Duties Across the Release Chain

Three pairs of steps in the fulfillment chain must be performed by different workforce members, and each pair is enforced by comparing `X-User-ID` against a user identifier the earlier step recorded. Releasing a request requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the request's `intake_user_id`. Deciding an applied redaction requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the segment's `redaction_applied_by_user_id`. Reviewing a denial requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the denial decision's `issued_by_user_id`. All three violations report the same code, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

The segregation check is evaluated early — before the target entity's own state is examined. If the analyst who applied a redaction decides again on a segment whose `redaction_status` has already reached `APPROVED`, both the segregation rule and the segment-state rule are violated; the response is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, not `ERR_SEGMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

### 2.7. Money, Millicents and Per-Line Rounding

All money in this system is integral. Totals, fees, caps, penalties and settlements are expressed in whole **cents**; per-page rates are expressed in **millicents**, where 1000 millicents equal 1 cent, so that a rate of two and three-quarter cents per page is representable exactly as 2750.

Each priced line computes its own amount as the line's `quantity` multiplied by its `unit_millicents`, divided by 1000, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Rounding is applied **per line and never to a subtotal**: the invoice's `subtotal_cents` is the plain sum of the already-rounded line amounts. This is load-bearing, not cosmetic. A tier-1 line of 25 pages at 27500 millicents rounds 687.5 up to 688, and a tier-2 line of 37 pages at 12500 millicents rounds 462.5 up to 463; summing the two unrounded figures and rounding once would yield a total one cent lower. The per-line rule is the contract.

Because every monetary field is an integer number of cents, there are no fractional currency amounts anywhere in a response body, and no field is ever emitted as a decimal string.

### 2.8. Route Resolution and Validation Precedence

Every business route is either a collection path or a collection path plus a single resource identifier, optionally followed by an action or sub-resource segment; no entity is nested inside another entity's path, and a parent identifier never appears in a child's URL. Ownership is expressed by foreign-key fields in request bodies, not by URL nesting. When a route carries a resource identifier and no entity of the addressed collection has that identifier, the call is rejected 404 with the collection's own code: `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUESTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DENIAL_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Foreign keys inside a write body are resolved with the same vocabulary, plus `ERR_STATE_FEE_RULE_NOT_FOUND` for an unknown fee schedule reference; a segment named in a sensitive-review decision list that belongs to a different request also reports `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`, whereas an amendment request's `record_segment_id` is resolved against the whole segment collection and is deliberately not required to belong to the amendment's parent request. Two dispatch-level codes sit outside that per-collection vocabulary and outside the §5 catalogue: a path matching no declared route at all is rejected 404 with `ERR_NOT_FOUND`, and a declared path addressed with an undeclared HTTP method is rejected 405 with `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED`. A trailing slash is insignificant, so `/roi/requests` and `/roi/requests/` reach the same handler. An unhandled server fault is reported 500 with `ERR_INTERNAL` in the same error envelope.

Body schema validation applies to every write endpoint and covers six failure shapes, all of which report `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400): a required field is missing; an enum-typed value falls outside its declared set; a numeric field arrives negative where the field is declared as zero or greater; a string exceeds its declared length bound; a datetime is not a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant; or a record segment declares `source_system` as `LEGACY_ARCHIVE` without a `legacy_job_start_at`, or declares any other source system while supplying one. Three further conventions complete that stage. A field present with a JSON `null` counts as absent throughout: a required field supplied as `null` is the missing-field violation, and an optional field supplied as `null` falls back to its declared default. Type discipline is exact rather than coercive — a field declared as an integer rejects a float, a numeric string and a boolean alike; a field declared as a boolean rejects `0` and `1`; a field declared as an array of enum members rejects any value that is not an array; and a string field rejects the empty string, since every declared string carries a minimum length of 1. And fields the schema does not declare are ignored rather than rejected, while a body that is present but is not a JSON object is rejected; an endpoint that declares no body field at all accepts an absent body, an empty body and `{}` interchangeably.

Validation is a single ordered pipeline, and the **first** violated rule determines the response; later violations are not reported. The order is: (1) missing or empty `X-User-ID`, reported as `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`; (2) role not permitted, reported as `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE`; (3) requester ownership, reported as `ERR_NOT_REQUEST_OWNER`; (4) body schema, reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; (5) interval ordering on request and authorization creation, reported as `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`; (6) path-identifier existence, reported as the addressed collection's not-found code; (7) body foreign-key existence, reported as the referenced collection's not-found code; (8) optimistic-concurrency version match, reported as `ERR_STALE_REQUEST_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION`; (9) segregation of duties, reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; (10) the addressed request's own `fulfillment_status`, reported as `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`; (11) the addressed segment's own three state dimensions, reported as `ERR_SEGMENT_STATE_INVALID`; (12) the addressed authorization's own `status`, reported as `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_STATE_INVALID`; (13) the addressed invoice's own `status`, reported as `ERR_INVOICE_STATE_INVALID`; (14) the addressed delivery's own `status`, reported as `ERR_DELIVERY_STATE_INVALID`; (15) the addressed denial decision's or amendment request's own `status`, reported as `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`; then, in ascending order, the cross-entity-state rules (16 through 19), the disclosure-safety rules (20 through 24), the referential business rules (25 through 29), the remaining business constraints (30 through 43), and finally the fiscal rules (44 through 47). The individual rules and their codes are described in §3 alongside the workflows they guard; the numbering here fixes their relative precedence.

Three precedence pairs are worth calling out explicitly because both members are plausibly violated at once. A release attempt on a request still sitting in `SENSITIVE_REVIEW` whose `billing_status` is `PENDING_INVOICE` violates both the state rule at position 10 and the prepayment rule at position 47; the answer is `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`. A delivery opened for a `THIRD_PARTY_AUTHORIZATION` request with format `UNENCRYPTED_EMAIL` and `encryption` `NONE`, aimed at an endpoint that is not the authorization's designated recipient, violates both position 23 and position 25; the answer is `ERR_UNENCRYPTED_DELIVERY_NOT_DIRECTED`. And a redaction decision by the analyst who applied it, on a segment already `APPROVED`, resolves to `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` as described in §2.6.

### 2.9. The Append-Only Compliance Trail

Compliance events are the system's evidentiary record and are never updated or deleted. Each event names the `request_id` it is recorded against, an `event_type` drawn from `STATUTORY_DEADLINE_BREACHED`, `PENALTY_ACCRUED`, `DENIAL_ISSUED`, `DENIAL_OVERTURNED`, `AUTHORIZATION_REVOKED`, `AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED`, `SENSITIVE_WITHHOLD`, `AMENDMENT_NOTICE_REQUIRED`, `FEE_CAP_APPLIED` and `DELIVERY_FAILED`, a `days_late` value equal to the strict lateness measure in whole days at the moment of appending, a `penalty_delta_cents` value equal to the change in the request's `penalty_exposure_cents` that the triggering action caused, an `actor_user_id` holding the `X-User-ID` of the caller whose action produced the event (or the literal string `system` for events the compliance sweep generates), an `occurred_at` set to the current logical clock, and a short machine-readable `detail` string such as `segment_category=HIV`. Both `days_late` and `penalty_delta_cents` default to 0 for events that carry no lateness meaning, and the split is fixed. `days_late` carries the strict measure on `STATUTORY_DEADLINE_BREACHED`, `PENALTY_ACCRUED`, `DENIAL_ISSUED`, `DENIAL_OVERTURNED`, `AUTHORIZATION_REVOKED` and `AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED`, and is 0 on `SENSITIVE_WITHHOLD`, `FEE_CAP_APPLIED`, `AMENDMENT_NOTICE_REQUIRED` and `DELIVERY_FAILED`; `penalty_delta_cents` is non-zero only on `STATUTORY_DEADLINE_BREACHED` and `PENALTY_ACCRUED`, where both carry that same increase, and is 0 on every other type. The `detail` string is a single `key=value` pair naming the salient particular of the triggering action: `statutory_due_at=…` on a breach, `penalty_exposure_cents=…` on an accrual, `grounds=…` on a denial, `denial_id=…` on an overturn, `authorization_id=…` on both a revocation and a lapse, `segment_category=…` on a withhold, `amendment_request_id=…` on an amendment notice, `cap_adjustment_cents=…` on a fee cap, and for `DELIVERY_FAILED` either `attempt_count=…` after a failed attempt or `delivery_returned=true` after a return.

### 2.10. Two Independent Status Dimensions on a Request

A request carries two state fields that advance independently. `fulfillment_status` tracks the record work and takes one of `INTAKE_REVIEW`, `AUTH_DEFECT_PENDING`, `COMPILATION`, `SENSITIVE_REVIEW`, `REDACTION`, `RELEASE_READY`, `RELEASED`, `DENIED`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`, starting at `INTAKE_REVIEW`, with `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` terminal. `billing_status` tracks the money and takes one of `PENDING_INVOICE`, `INVOICED`, `PAID`, `WAIVED` or `NOT_BILLABLE`, starting at `PENDING_INVOICE`, with `PAID` and `NOT_BILLABLE` terminal; `WAIVED` is deliberately not terminal, because a waived invoice may still be voided and thereby return the request to `PENDING_INVOICE`.

Neither dimension gates its own transitions on the other, with exactly one exception: the move from `RELEASE_READY` to `RELEASED` reads `billing_status` and refuses to proceed unless the money is settled. Both dimensions are cyclic. `AUTH_DEFECT_PENDING` returns to `INTAKE_REVIEW` when a defective authorization is cured, `DENIED` returns to `COMPILATION` when a denial is overturned, and `INVOICED` returns to `PENDING_INVOICE` when the invoice is voided.

Whenever an operation mutates more than one resource, every touched resource's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1 — including cascade targets the caller never named. In particular, any write against an *existing* record segment — a disposition, a redaction application, a redaction decision — re-derives the parent request's four page-ledger fields and therefore increments the parent request's `version` as well as the segment's own. Opening a brand-new segment is the exception: a `PENDING` segment carrying a `page_count` of 0 changes no aggregate, so its creation leaves the parent request's `updated_at` and `version` untouched.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Configuring State Fee Regimes and Onboarding Facilities

Everything the platform charges and every deadline it enforces derives from a **state fee rule**, the vendor's transcription of one US state's release-of-information statute. A `PlatformAdmin` registers one by supplying a two-letter `state_code` matching exactly two uppercase letters — any other spelling, whether the wrong length or a character outside `A`–`Z`, is the schema violation of §2.8 reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the rule's `state_code` must be unique across the platform — registering a second rule for a state already on file is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATE_FEE_RULE`. The pricing half of a rule consists of `base_retrieval_fee_cents`, the flat clerical search-and-retrieval charge; `tier1_page_limit`, at least 1, defining that pages one through that limit inclusive price at `tier1_per_page_millicents` while every page above it prices at `tier2_per_page_millicents`; `patient_access_per_page_millicents`, a single flat cost-based rate with no tiers at all; `certification_fee_cents` for a certification of records; `media_fee_cents` for physical media; `postage_per_delivery_cents` for mailing; `electronic_delivery_total_cap_cents`, the statutory ceiling that applies when records travel electronically; and `total_fee_cap_cents`, the statutory ceiling that applies to any format. Every one of those money fields must be zero or greater.

The timeline half of a rule consists of `patient_access_deadline_days` and `third_party_deadline_days`, each a whole number of days of at least 1, one for each access basis; `extension_days`, the whole days the single permitted statutory extension adds, which may be zero to mean "this state grants no extension"; `penalty_per_day_cents`, accrued for each late day; `penalty_cap_cents`, the ceiling on accrued penalty for any one request; and `fee_forfeiture_on_late`, a boolean which, when true, makes a late patient right-of-access request surrender copying fees up to the accrued penalty. Fee schedules are readable by every staff role and may be listed with an optional `state_code` filter.

A **facility** is one covered entity the vendor serves. Onboarding one requires a `name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `state_fee_rule_id` naming the fee regime every request of that facility will be priced and timed under; an unknown `state_fee_rule_id` is refused with `ERR_STATE_FEE_RULE_NOT_FOUND`. The remaining facility inputs are optional and default as follows: `legacy_archive_enabled` defaults to false, meaning no legacy-archive compilation task may be opened against the facility; `legacy_retrieval_surcharge_cents` defaults to 0; `legacy_gateway_job_minutes` defaults to 30 and must be at least 1; `legacy_gateway_buffer_minutes` defaults to 10 and may be 0; and `sensitive_categories_requiring_specific_authorization` defaults to the full protected set `BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH`, `SUD_PART2`, `HIV`, `GENETIC`, `REPRODUCTIVE_HEALTH`, `MINOR_CONFIDENTIAL` and `PSYCHOTHERAPY_NOTES`. A facility's `status` is system-owned, starts at `ACTIVE`, and takes only `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`.

A `PlatformAdmin` may flip that `status` at any time, supplying the new `status` and an optional `reason` of at most 500 characters that is validated but never persisted on the facility. The flip reads no current state, so re-asserting the `status` a facility already holds is accepted and counts as a flip like any other. Suspension is a hard gate on intake: while a facility's `status` is `SUSPENDED`, no new request may be created for it and no existing intake decision may be accepted, both refused with `ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED`. Suspension does not disturb work already past intake. Each status flip increments the facility's `version` by 1.

Four ledger fields on a facility are computed rather than supplied, and all four initialise to 0 when the facility is onboarded. Consider the population of invoices whose request belongs to this facility. `receivable_cents` is the sum, over those invoices whose `status` is `ISSUED`, of the invoice's `amount_due_cents` minus its `paid_cents`. `collected_cents` is the sum, over those whose `status` is `PAID`, of `paid_cents`. `written_off_cents` is the sum, over those whose `status` is `WAIVED`, of `amount_due_cents`. Invoices in `DRAFT` or `VOIDED` contribute to no bucket. Because a payment must settle the full amount due, `paid_cents` is always 0 on an `ISSUED` invoice, so the three buckets partition the issued money exactly: their sum always equals the sum of `amount_due_cents` over every invoice whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `PAID` or `WAIVED`. All three are re-derived whenever an invoice is issued, whenever it is settled under any action, and whenever a cascade voids one. The fourth field, `penalty_exposure_cents` on the facility, is the sum of `penalty_exposure_cents` over every request whose `facility_id` names this facility, and it is refreshed whenever any request's penalty exposure moves.

### 3.2. Patients, Requesting Parties and Signed Authorizations

A **patient** record is the anchor for a facility's designated record set. Registration, available to `FacilityAdmin` and `PlatformAdmin`, takes a `facility_id`, an `mrn` of 1 to 32 characters, a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, and a `date_of_birth` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. The pair of `facility_id` and `mrn` must be unique: repeating it is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MRN`. Two optional booleans both default to false: `is_minor`, which drives the expectation that `MINOR_CONFIDENTIAL` material will be segregated, and `deceased`, which signals that only a personal representative may obtain access. Patients may be listed with optional `facility_id` and `mrn` filters.

A **requesting party** is registered by an `IntakeSpecialist` or a `PlatformAdmin` and is characterised first by its `requester_type`, one of exactly eight values: `PATIENT`, `PERSONAL_REPRESENTATIVE`, `ATTORNEY`, `INSURER`, `EMPLOYER`, `RESEARCHER`, `GOVERNMENT_AGENCY` or `OTHER_PROVIDER`. A `contact_email` of at most 200 characters is mandatory and must contain an at-sign; one that does not is the schema violation of §2.8, reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Three inputs are optional and default to null: `organization_name`, of at most 200 characters, conventionally left null for a party whose `requester_type` is `PATIENT` although the server never rejects one that supplies it; `credential_number`, of at most 100 characters, holding a bar number, NPI or agency identifier; and `patient_id`, which is mandatory when `requester_type` is `PATIENT` — its absence there being the missing-field violation of §2.8 — and merely optional for every other type, where a supplied value is resolved like any other foreign key and stored rather than refused. A fourth optional input, `signature_on_file`, defaults to false and records whether a wet or verified electronic signature specimen has been lodged. Requesting parties may be listed with optional `requester_type` and `patient_id` filters, and a single party is readable by every staff role and by the `Requester` itself.

An **authorization** is the paper that lets a third party receive records. Recording one — a capability shared by `IntakeSpecialist` and `PrivacyOfficer` — takes the `patient_id` whose records it covers, the `requester_id` it names as recipient, the `expected_patient_version` described in §2.5, a `signed_at` instant beginning the coverage interval, an `expires_at` instant ending it exclusively, a `scope_categories` array listing the record categories the authorization permits at all, a `designated_recipient_endpoint` of 1 to 200 characters naming the only transmission endpoint a third-party delivery may target, and a `signature_verified` boolean recording whether intake compared the signature against the specimen on file. Three inputs are optional: `sensitive_categories_authorized`, defaulting to an empty array, listing the categories the requester may receive despite segregation — nominally a subset of `scope_categories`, though nothing checks that it is one, so a category named here and absent from `scope_categories` is accepted as written; `part2_redisclosure_prohibition_ack`, defaulting to false, recording the requester's acknowledgement of the 42 CFR Part 2 redisclosure prohibition; and `supersedes_authorization_id`, defaulting to null, naming an incumbent authorization this one retires. If `expires_at` is at or before `signed_at`, the creation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`.

The authorization's `status` is computed at creation rather than supplied. It is `VALID` when `signature_verified` is true, `scope_categories` is non-empty, and `expires_at` is strictly after the current logical clock; otherwise it is `DEFECTIVE`, and `defect_reasons` — an array that otherwise stays empty — is populated with the applicable reason codes drawn from `SIGNATURE_UNVERIFIED` and `SCOPE_EMPTY`, in that order when both apply. Those two codes are the only members the array ever carries, and they name only the two input defects: an authorization whose sole defect is that its `expires_at` already sits at or before the current logical clock is recorded as `DEFECTIVE` with an empty `defect_reasons`. The five states an authorization may occupy are `VALID`, `DEFECTIVE`, `REVOKED`, `EXPIRED` and `SUPERSEDED`; the last three are terminal. A `revoked_at` field stays null unless the `status` is `REVOKED`.

Coverage windows must not collide. Before accepting a new authorization the system looks for an existing authorization on the same `patient_id` and the same `requester_id` whose `status` is `VALID` or `DEFECTIVE`, whose `id` is not the one named by `supersedes_authorization_id`, and whose `scope_categories` share at least one member with the new one's; if any such incumbent's coverage interval — half-open, beginning at its `signed_at` and running up to but not including its `expires_at` — overlaps the new one's interval on the same half-open reading, the creation is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Because the interval is half-open, a new authorization whose `signed_at` is exactly the incumbent's `expires_at` is adjacent rather than overlapping and is accepted.

When `supersedes_authorization_id` is present and names an authorization whose `patient_id` equals the new authorization's `patient_id`, the named predecessor is driven from `VALID` or `DEFECTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED` and its `version` increments by 1; the successor is exempt from the overlap check above. A named predecessor whose `patient_id` differs from the new authorization's, or whose `status` is already `REVOKED`, `EXPIRED` or `SUPERSEDED`, is left in whatever status it held — no error is raised, and the successor still records the reference and still enjoys the overlap exemption. An unknown `supersedes_authorization_id` reports `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`. Authorizations may be listed with optional `patient_id`, `requester_id`, `supersedes_authorization_id` and `status` filters.

**Curing a defect** is an `IntakeSpecialist` capability and moves the authorization from `DEFECTIVE` to `VALID`. It re-supplies `signed_at`, `expires_at`, `scope_categories`, `designated_recipient_endpoint` and `signature_verified`, and may re-supply `sensitive_categories_authorized` and `part2_redisclosure_prohibition_ack`; the payload must satisfy every condition that produced the defect, meaning the resupplied values must themselves yield a `VALID` computation — a payload that would still compute to `DEFECTIVE`, whether because `signature_verified` is false, because `scope_categories` is empty, or because `expires_at` is at or before the current logical clock, is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_STATE_INVALID`, the same code the wrong-status case reports. The cure applies no interval-ordering check of its own, so an `expires_at` at or before the resupplied `signed_at` is accepted whenever the computation still yields `VALID`. On success `defect_reasons` is cleared, the authorization's `version` increments by 1, and every request whose `authorization_id` names this authorization and which is parked in `AUTH_DEFECT_PENDING` cascades back to `INTAKE_REVIEW`, closing its tolling interval and recomputing its `statutory_due_at` as described in §3.3. Attempting a cure on an authorization whose `status` is anything other than `DEFECTIVE` is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_STATE_INVALID`.

**Revocation** is shared by `PrivacyOfficer` and the `Requester` named on the authorization, takes a mandatory `revocation_reason` of at most 500 characters that is validated but never persisted, and is permitted only from `VALID` or `DEFECTIVE` — from `REVOKED`, `EXPIRED` or `SUPERSEDED` it is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_STATE_INVALID`. Revocation is the widest cascade in the system. It sets `revoked_at` to the current logical clock, increments the authorization's `version` by 1, and then drives every request on that authorization whose `fulfillment_status` is one of exactly `INTAKE_REVIEW`, `COMPILATION`, `SENSITIVE_REVIEW`, `REDACTION` or `RELEASE_READY` to `AUTH_DEFECT_PENDING` with the full effect list: `defect_opened_at` is set to the current logical clock, opening a tolling interval; `release_reviewer_user_id` is cleared; every record segment of that request whose `sensitivity_status` is `AUTHORIZED_INCLUDE` or `WITHHELD` reverts to `SEGREGATED_PENDING_REVIEW`, and every segment whose `redaction_status` is `OPEN`, `APPLIED`, `APPROVED` or `REWORK` is reset to `OPEN` with `redaction_approved_by_user_id` cleared — a segment the cascade changes in neither dimension is left entirely alone and its `version` does not move, while `redaction_applied_by_user_id` survives the reset even on the segments that do move; the request's four page-ledger fields are re-derived; every invoice of that request whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `ISSUED` is driven to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `AUTHORIZATION_REVOKED`, and the request's `billing_status` is returned to `PENDING_INVOICE` only when at least one invoice was actually voided — a request carrying no `DRAFT` or `ISSUED` invoice keeps whatever `billing_status` it held, `NOT_BILLABLE` and `PAID` included; the facility's `receivable_cents` is re-derived; a compliance event of type `AUTHORIZATION_REVOKED` is appended against the request; and the request's `version` increments by 1. A request on that authorization already parked in `AUTH_DEFECT_PENDING` is passed over rather than re-parked: its `defect_opened_at` is not restamped, none of its segments or invoices move, no event is appended against it and its `version` stays put; requests in `RELEASED`, `DENIED`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` are likewise untouched.

Separately, a `VALID` authorization whose `expires_at` is at or before the current logical clock lapses to `EXPIRED` during the compliance sweep described in §3.9, incrementing its `version` by 1 and appending an `AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED` compliance event against every request on that authorization whose `fulfillment_status` is non-terminal.

### 3.3. Request Intake, the Access Basis, and the Statutory Clock

An `IntakeSpecialist` opens a **request** by supplying the `facility_id` whose designated record set is being searched, the `patient_id` who is the subject, the `requester_id` who is asking, an `access_basis` of either `PATIENT_RIGHT_OF_ACCESS` or `THIRD_PARTY_AUTHORIZATION`, a `requested_categories` array naming the record categories asked for, which must be non-empty — an empty array is the schema violation of §2.8 reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — an inclusive `date_range_start` and `date_range_end` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and a `delivery_format` drawn from exactly six values: `PAPER_MAIL`, `CD_ROM`, `SECURE_PORTAL`, `DIRECT_MESSAGE`, `UNENCRYPTED_EMAIL` or `FAX`. Three inputs are optional: `authorization_id`, defaulting to null; `predecessor_request_id`, defaulting to null and naming the earlier request this one re-files after a denial or a withdrawal; and `certification_requested`, defaulting to false. A `date_range_end` earlier than `date_range_start` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`.

The `access_basis` is the polymorphic pivot of the whole system: it selects which deadline field governs, which invoice variant prices the pages, whether an authorization is needed at all, and whether an unsecured transmission is ever permissible. Four referential checks police it at intake, evaluated in this relative order. First, when the requesting party's `requester_type` is `PATIENT`, that party's own `patient_id` must equal the body's `patient_id`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_PATIENT_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. Second, a `PATIENT_RIGHT_OF_ACCESS` request is only open to a requesting party whose `requester_type` is `PATIENT` or `PERSONAL_REPRESENTATIVE`; any other type is refused with `ERR_ACCESS_BASIS_REQUESTER_MISMATCH`. Third, the access basis and the authorization must agree: a `THIRD_PARTY_AUTHORIZATION` request with a null `authorization_id`, a `PATIENT_RIGHT_OF_ACCESS` request with a non-null `authorization_id`, or a referenced authorization whose own `patient_id` or `requester_id` differs from the body's, are all refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_REQUIRED`. Fourth, duplicate work is refused: if another request already shares this one's `facility_id`, `patient_id`, `requester_id`, `date_range_start` and `date_range_end`, and that other request's `fulfillment_status` is neither `CLOSED` nor `CANCELLED`, and it is not the request named by this one's `predecessor_request_id`, the creation is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_INFLIGHT_REQUEST`. A suspended facility refuses intake outright, as §3.1 describes.

On success the system stamps `received_at` with the current logical clock — this is the statutory clock's arrival anchor — records `intake_user_id` as the creating `X-User-ID`, sets `fulfillment_status` to `INTAKE_REVIEW` and `billing_status` to `PENDING_INVOICE`, leaves `release_reviewer_user_id` null, and computes `statutory_due_at`. Requests are readable individually and listable with optional `facility_id`, `patient_id`, `requester_id`, `authorization_id`, `predecessor_request_id`, `fulfillment_status` and `billing_status` filters.

**Computing the deadline.** The `statutory_due_at` instant composes four terms, and the order in which they compose is part of the contract because reordering any two of them yields a different instant. Step one selects the statutory window: for a `PATIENT_RIGHT_OF_ACCESS` request it is the governing fee rule's `patient_access_deadline_days`, and for a `THIRD_PARTY_AUTHORIZATION` request it is that rule's `third_party_deadline_days`. Step two selects the anchor, and it does so *before* any days are added: for a `PATIENT_RIGHT_OF_ACCESS` request the anchor is simply the request's `received_at`, because such a request has no authorization; for a `THIRD_PARTY_AUTHORIZATION` request the anchor is the later of the request's `received_at` and the governing authorization's `signed_at`, so an authorization signed after the request arrived pushes the anchor forward. Step three adds the selected number of whole days to that anchor, each day being 86400 seconds. Step four adds the tolling allowance, and the allowance is capped *before* it is added: the number of seconds added is the smaller of the request's `tolled_seconds` and a ceiling of ten whole days, which is 864000 seconds. Step five, last of all, adds the extension: when `extension_applied` is true, the governing rule's `extension_days` multiplied by 86400 seconds is added; when it is false, nothing is added. Capping the toll after the addition, adding the raw uncapped toll, applying the ten-day ceiling to the toll and the extension together, or taking the anchor after the days have been added, each produce a different and incorrect deadline.

`statutory_due_at` is re-derived at request creation, on every `fulfillment_status` transition, when a cure changes `tolled_seconds`, and when the statutory extension is noticed.

**Tolling.** An `IntakeSpecialist` records an intake decision by supplying a `decision` and, optionally, `defect_reasons`, which must be an array of strings when it is supplied at all and is validated and then discarded — the intake decision persists nothing of it on the request. A `decision` of `ACCEPT` moves the request from `INTAKE_REVIEW` to `COMPILATION`; it requires that the governing facility's `status` be `ACTIVE`, and, when the `access_basis` is `THIRD_PARTY_AUTHORIZATION`, that the governing authorization's `status` be `VALID` and its `expires_at` be strictly after the current logical clock; when the `access_basis` is `PATIENT_RIGHT_OF_ACCESS` the `authorization_id` must be null. An authorization whose `status` is not `VALID` reports `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_VALID`; an authorization whose `expires_at` is at or before the current logical clock reports `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED` — the boundary is exclusive at the top, so an `expires_at` exactly equal to the clock is already lapsed. Acceptance re-derives `statutory_due_at`, re-derives both `penalty_exposure_cents` and `safe_harbor_penalty_cents`, and increments the request's `version` by 1.

A `decision` of `DEFECT` instead moves the request from `INTAKE_REVIEW` to `AUTH_DEFECT_PENDING`, and is available only when the `access_basis` is `THIRD_PARTY_AUTHORIZATION` and the governing authorization's `status` is `DEFECTIVE`: a `DEFECT` on a `PATIENT_RIGHT_OF_ACCESS` request is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_REQUIRED`, and a `DEFECT` whose governing authorization is absent or holds any `status` other than `DEFECTIVE` is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_STATE_INVALID`. It sets `defect_opened_at` to the current logical clock, opening a tolling interval, and increments the request's `version` by 1. While `defect_opened_at` is non-null the request is tolling; when the cure described in §3.2 returns the request to `INTAKE_REVIEW`, the interval closes: the whole seconds between `defect_opened_at` and the current logical clock are added to `tolled_seconds`, but only up to whatever remains of the ten-day, 864000-second total allowance — and nothing at all is added when the clock has meanwhile been moved backwards, since a negative interval contributes zero rather than subtracting — after which `defect_opened_at` returns to null, `statutory_due_at` is re-derived using the new `tolled_seconds`, both penalty measures are re-derived, and the request's `version` increments by 1. `tolled_seconds` counts only closed intervals and starts at 0.

**The single extension.** An `IntakeSpecialist` may notice the statutory extension exactly once per request, supplying a mandatory `notice_note` of at most 500 characters that is validated but never persisted. It sets `extension_applied` to true and `extension_noticed_at` to the current logical clock, re-derives `statutory_due_at` and both penalty measures, and increments the request's `version` by 1 — but it never changes `fulfillment_status`, and it is permitted from any non-terminal `fulfillment_status`, which makes it the cheapest way to advance a request's `version` without advancing its workflow. The notice is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_AVAILABLE` when `extension_applied` is already true, when the governing rule's `extension_days` is 0, or when the current logical clock is already past `statutory_due_at`; at an instant exactly equal to `statutory_due_at` the extension is still available.

**Withdrawal.** The `Requester` that owns a request may withdraw it, supplying a mandatory `withdrawal_reason` of at most 500 characters that is validated but never persisted. Withdrawal is permitted from `INTAKE_REVIEW`, `AUTH_DEFECT_PENDING`, `COMPILATION`, `SENSITIVE_REVIEW`, `REDACTION` or `RELEASE_READY` and moves the request to `CANCELLED`; it requires that `X-User-ID` equal the request's `requester_id`. It sets `closed_at` to the current logical clock and freezes both penalty measures at that instant, drives every invoice of the request whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `ISSUED` to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `BILLING_CLERK_VOID`, sets `billing_status` to `NOT_BILLABLE`, re-derives the facility's `receivable_cents` and `penalty_exposure_cents`, and increments the request's `version` by 1. Any transition attempted from outside its declared origin set is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.4. Compiling the Record Set Across Source Systems

A `RecordsAnalyst` opens one **record segment** per chunk of work: a segment is "everything of one category from one source system for this request". Creating one takes the `request_id` it belongs to, a `source_system` of `EHR_CURRENT`, `LEGACY_ARCHIVE`, `IMAGING_PACS` or `SCANNED_PAPER`, a `category` of `GENERAL_MEDICAL`, `BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH`, `SUD_PART2`, `HIV`, `GENETIC`, `REPRODUCTIVE_HEALTH`, `MINOR_CONFIDENTIAL` or `PSYCHOTHERAPY_NOTES`, and — only for a legacy source — a `legacy_job_start_at` instant. That last field is strictly conditional: a `source_system` of `LEGACY_ARCHIVE` arriving without `legacy_job_start_at`, or any other `source_system` arriving with one, is a schema violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Creation reads nothing of the parent request beyond its existence and the facility it belongs to: a segment may be opened while the request sits in any `fulfillment_status`, terminal ones included, its `category` need not appear among the request's `requested_categories`, and nothing forbids two segments of the same request sharing a `source_system` and a `category`. A newly created segment has `retrieval_status` `PENDING`, `sensitivity_status` `UNCLASSIFIED`, `redaction_status` `NOT_REQUIRED`, `page_count` 0, `redacted_page_count` 0, `retrieved_at` null, `unavailable_reason` null, `redaction_applied_by_user_id` null, `redaction_approved_by_user_id` null, and `amended` false.

Legacy archives are the operational bottleneck: the facility's gateway to its retired systems runs one retrieval job at a time. Two constraints follow. A legacy segment may only be opened when the governing facility's `legacy_archive_enabled` is true; otherwise the creation is refused with `ERR_LEGACY_ARCHIVE_DISABLED`. And a legacy job must not contend for the gateway. When `legacy_job_start_at` is supplied the system derives `legacy_job_end_at` by adding the facility's `legacy_gateway_job_minutes` to it; `legacy_job_end_at` stays null whenever `legacy_job_start_at` is null. It then tests the new job's **buffered window** — which begins the facility's `legacy_gateway_buffer_minutes` before `legacy_job_start_at` and ends the facility's `legacy_gateway_buffer_minutes` after the job's end, and which is closed at both ends — against the buffered window of every existing legacy segment on the same facility whose `retrieval_status` is `PENDING` or `RETRIEVED`. Because both windows are closed at both ends, two windows that merely touch at a single endpoint already collide, and the creation is refused with `ERR_LEGACY_WINDOW_CONTENTION`. With the default 30-minute job and 10-minute buffer, a job starting on the hour occupies the gateway from ten minutes before the hour through forty minutes past it inclusive; because the next job's own buffered window opens ten minutes before its start, the next job may not begin at or before fifty minutes past the hour, and the earliest whole minute at which it may begin is fifty-one minutes past.

Recording the outcome of a retrieval attempt is also a `RecordsAnalyst` capability and takes a `disposition` plus, conditionally, `page_count` and `unavailable_reason`. A `disposition` of `RETRIEVED` moves the segment's `retrieval_status` from `PENDING` to `RETRIEVED`; it requires that the parent request's `fulfillment_status` be `COMPILATION` and that `page_count` be supplied as zero or greater — a `RETRIEVED` disposition arriving without `page_count` is the schema violation of §2.8 and is therefore answered before the addressed segment is even resolved. It sets `retrieved_at` to the current logical clock and classifies the segment's `sensitivity_status`: the segment's `category` is tested for membership in the governing facility's `sensitive_categories_requiring_specific_authorization` list, and the `sensitivity_status` becomes `SEGREGATED_PENDING_REVIEW` when the category is in that list and `NOT_SENSITIVE` when it is not. That single set-membership test runs exactly once, at this transition, and never again. Because the list is per-facility configuration, a facility that omits a category from its list classifies that category as `NOT_SENSITIVE` and never routes it to a reviewer — with the standing exception that psychotherapy notes are never releasable no matter how the list is configured, a block that lives in the release decision rather than in this classification. A `NOT_SENSITIVE` segment needs no further review; a `SEGREGATED_PENDING_REVIEW` segment blocks the reviewer's pass until a decision names it.

A `disposition` of `UNAVAILABLE` moves the segment from `PENDING` to `UNAVAILABLE`, also requires the parent request to be in `COMPILATION`, records the supplied free-text `unavailable_reason` — itself optional, staying null when omitted, and validated but ignored when it rides any other disposition — forces `page_count` to 0 whatever the body supplied, and leaves `sensitivity_status` at `UNCLASSIFIED`. A `disposition` of `WITHDRAWN` moves the segment from `PENDING` or `RETRIEVED` to `WITHDRAWN` and is permitted while the parent request's `fulfillment_status` is `COMPILATION`, `SENSITIVE_REVIEW` or `REDACTION`; it resets `sensitivity_status` to `UNCLASSIFIED` from whichever of `NOT_SENSITIVE`, `SEGREGATED_PENDING_REVIEW`, `AUTHORIZED_INCLUDE` or `WITHHELD` it held, resets `redaction_status` to `NOT_REQUIRED` from whichever of `OPEN`, `APPLIED`, `APPROVED` or `REWORK` it held, and resets `redacted_page_count` to 0, so the segment leaves every page aggregate of the parent request. The withdrawal leaves the segment's own `page_count`, `retrieved_at`, `redaction_applied_by_user_id` and `redaction_approved_by_user_id` exactly as they stood; a segment withdrawn out of `RETRIEVED` therefore still reports the pages it once held, and it drops out of the aggregates only because those aggregates read `RETRIEVED` segments alone. A withdrawal also re-evaluates whether the parent request can now leave `REDACTION` for `RELEASE_READY`, since removing the last unapproved segment unblocks that move. `UNAVAILABLE` and `WITHDRAWN` are both terminal for the retrieval dimension. Every one of these three dispositions increments the segment's `version` by 1 and, because it re-derives the parent request's page ledger, increments the parent request's `version` by 1 as well. A disposition attempted on a segment that is no longer `PENDING` where `PENDING` is required is refused with `ERR_SEGMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

**The page ledger.** Four fields on the request summarise the compiled set, all defaulting to 0, all re-derived on every segment write and on every `fulfillment_status` transition, and all computed over the same input population: the segments of this request whose `retrieval_status` is `RETRIEVED`. Segments that are still `PENDING`, or that ended `UNAVAILABLE` or `WITHDRAWN`, appear in no aggregate. The gross measure, `page_count_total`, is the plain sum of `page_count` over that population and answers "how many pages did we pull". The net measure is threefold. Call a segment *releasable* when its `sensitivity_status` is `NOT_SENSITIVE` or `AUTHORIZED_INCLUDE`. Then `billable_page_count` is the sum, over the releasable segments, of that segment's `page_count` less its `redacted_page_count` when its `redaction_status` is `APPROVED` and less nothing otherwise; `withheld_page_count` is the sum of `page_count` over the segments whose `sensitivity_status` is `WITHHELD`; and `redacted_page_count_total` is the sum of `redacted_page_count` over the releasable segments whose `redaction_status` is `APPROVED`. Redacted pages therefore count only once a redaction has been approved: an `APPLIED`-but-unapproved redaction leaves `billable_page_count` completely untouched. Segments are aggregated in ascending `created_at` order and no identifier participates in the aggregation. `billable_page_count` is the only page count an invoice may ever price.

**Declaring compilation finished** is a `RecordsAnalyst` capability with no request body. It moves the request from `COMPILATION` to `SENSITIVE_REVIEW`, and it requires that the request have at least one record segment and that no segment of the request still hold `retrieval_status` `PENDING`; a request with no segments at all, or with any segment still `PENDING`, is refused with `ERR_SEGMENTS_PENDING`. On success all four page-ledger fields and both penalty measures are re-derived and the request's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.5. Segregating Protected Categories and Working the Redactions

Segments are readable individually — showing all three of their state dimensions at once — and listable with optional `request_id`, `source_system`, `category` and `sensitivity_status` filters. The `sensitivity_status` dimension takes `UNCLASSIFIED`, `NOT_SENSITIVE`, `SEGREGATED_PENDING_REVIEW`, `AUTHORIZED_INCLUDE` or `WITHHELD` and has no terminal state, because a decided segment can be pushed back for re-decision. The `redaction_status` dimension takes `NOT_REQUIRED`, `OPEN`, `APPLIED`, `APPROVED` or `REWORK` and likewise has no terminal state, because a rejected redaction can be reworked without bound.

A `ReleaseReviewer` decides every segregated segment of a request in a single pass, supplying `segment_decisions`: an array whose entries each carry a `record_segment_id`, a `decision` of either `INCLUDE` or `WITHHOLD`, an optional `redaction_required` boolean defaulting to false, and an optional `note` of at most 500 characters that is validated but never persisted. The pass is all-or-nothing on completeness. Every segment of the request whose `sensitivity_status` is `SEGREGATED_PENDING_REVIEW` must appear in `segment_decisions` exactly once — and the array may legitimately arrive empty when the request holds no segregated segment at all, in which case the pass still applies its routing and its recomputations exactly as a non-empty pass would; a segregated segment omitted from the array, a segment named twice, or a decision naming a segment whose `sensitivity_status` is not `SEGREGATED_PENDING_REVIEW`, is each refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_REVIEW_INCOMPLETE`. A `record_segment_id` naming a segment that belongs to a different request reports `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND` instead. When the request's `access_basis` is `THIRD_PARTY_AUTHORIZATION` and the governing authorization's `expires_at` is at or before the current logical clock, the whole pass is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED` — the same exclusive-at-the-top boundary that governs intake acceptance.

Three disclosure-safety rules police each `INCLUDE` decision, and they cascade in a fixed order. First, a segment whose `category` is `PSYCHOTHERAPY_NOTES` may never be included: psychotherapy notes are excluded from the right of access outright and no authorization can unlock them, so the decision is refused with `ERR_PSYCHOTHERAPY_NOTES_NOT_RELEASABLE`. Second, on a `THIRD_PARTY_AUTHORIZATION` request, an `INCLUDE` for a segment whose `category` is not among the governing authorization's `sensitive_categories_authorized` is refused with `ERR_SENSITIVE_CATEGORY_NOT_AUTHORIZED`. Third, on a `THIRD_PARTY_AUTHORIZATION` request, an `INCLUDE` of a `SUD_PART2` segment whose governing authorization has `part2_redisclosure_prohibition_ack` false is refused with `ERR_PART2_ACK_MISSING`. The cascade matters: because the category-coverage rule is evaluated before the Part 2 acknowledgement rule, a `SUD_PART2` segment that is *also* outside `sensitive_categories_authorized` reports the coverage failure, not the missing acknowledgement. On a `PATIENT_RIGHT_OF_ACCESS` request neither the coverage rule nor the acknowledgement rule applies, since a patient reaching for their own record needs no authorization to see it; the psychotherapy-notes block still applies.

An accepted `INCLUDE` moves the segment's `sensitivity_status` from `SEGREGATED_PENDING_REVIEW` to `AUTHORIZED_INCLUDE`, and when that entry carried `redaction_required` true the segment's `redaction_status` moves from `NOT_REQUIRED` to `OPEN` in the same call. A `WITHHOLD` moves the segment to `WITHHELD` with no preconditions of its own beyond the completeness rule, ignores any `redaction_required` its entry carried — the flag opens a redaction only on an `INCLUDE` — appends a `SENSITIVE_WITHHOLD` compliance event for that segment, and moves that segment's `page_count` out of `billable_page_count` and into `withheld_page_count`.

The pass then routes the request. When at least one accepted `INCLUDE` decision carried `redaction_required` true, the request moves from `SENSITIVE_REVIEW` to `REDACTION`; when no accepted `INCLUDE` decision carried `redaction_required` true — including the case where every decision was a `WITHHOLD`, and the case of an empty decision array — the request moves from `SENSITIVE_REVIEW` straight to `RELEASE_READY`. Either way all decisions are applied to their segments, the four page-ledger fields are re-derived, a `SENSITIVE_WITHHOLD` event is appended for each decision that produced `WITHHELD`, and the request's `version` increments by 1. The two branches differ in exactly one further respect: the route to `RELEASE_READY` also evaluates the billing entry rule described below, whereas the route to `REDACTION` defers it.

**The billing entry rule at `RELEASE_READY`.** On any entry into `RELEASE_READY` — whether it arrives directly from `SENSITIVE_REVIEW` or by bubbling up out of `REDACTION` — the system computes the prospective subtotal that an invoice for this request's variant would produce from the request's `billable_page_count` as it stands at that moment — the plain per-line sum of §2.7, taken before any cap or forfeiture is applied, and 0 outright when the governing facility or fee rule cannot be resolved. If that prospective subtotal is exactly 0 cents, the request's `billing_status` moves from `PENDING_INVOICE` to `NOT_BILLABLE`, and no invoice may be created for the request afterwards: the attempt reports `ERR_NOTHING_TO_INVOICE`. If the prospective subtotal is above 0 cents, `billing_status` stays at `PENDING_INVOICE` and awaits a clerk.

**Applying a redaction** is a `RecordsAnalyst` capability taking a `redacted_page_count` and an optional `redaction_note` of at most 500 characters that is validated but never persisted. It moves the segment's `redaction_status` from `OPEN` or from `REWORK` to `APPLIED`, and requires that the parent request's `fulfillment_status` be `REDACTION` and that `redacted_page_count` be at least 0 and no greater than the segment's `page_count`. A `redacted_page_count` strictly greater than `page_count` is refused with `ERR_REDACTION_PAGE_COUNT_INVALID`; a `redacted_page_count` exactly equal to `page_count` is accepted and leaves that segment contributing zero billable pages. Applying records `redaction_applied_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, does **not** yet move the page ledger — the redacted pages are not counted until approval — and increments both the segment's `version` and the parent request's `version` by 1. An attempt on a segment whose `redaction_status` is neither `OPEN` nor `REWORK` is refused with `ERR_SEGMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

**Deciding a redaction** is a `ReleaseReviewer` capability taking a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REWORK` plus an optional `note` of at most 500 characters that is validated but never persisted, permitted only from `redaction_status` `APPLIED`, and in both branches requiring that `X-User-ID` differ from the segment's `redaction_applied_by_user_id`. The decision reads no state from the parent request at all: whatever `fulfillment_status` that request now holds, the decision proceeds, so a segment left `APPLIED` when its request was withdrawn out of `REDACTION` may still be approved or reworked afterwards — the page ledger is simply re-derived and the bubble-up below finds no `REDACTION` request to advance. The two branches carry equal weight and equal consequence. `APPROVE` moves `redaction_status` from `APPLIED` to `APPROVED`, records `redaction_approved_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, and only now folds the segment's `redacted_page_count` into the parent request's `redacted_page_count_total` and subtracts it from `billable_page_count`; it then re-derives `redacted_page_count_total` and `billable_page_count`, and bubbles the parent request from `REDACTION` to `RELEASE_READY` provided that, after this decision is applied, no segment of the request holds `redaction_status` `OPEN`, `APPLIED` or `REWORK`. That bubble-up evaluates the billing entry rule and increments the parent request's `version` by 1. `REWORK` instead moves `redaction_status` from `APPLIED` to `REWORK`, clears `redaction_applied_by_user_id` so the same analyst is free to apply again, leaves the parent request in `REDACTION`, and touches no page-ledger field. Both branches increment the segment's `version` by 1 and, because the parent's page ledger is re-derived on every segment write, the parent request's `version` by 1.

### 3.6. Pricing the Released Page Set: Tiers, Caps and Forfeiture

A `BillingClerk` prices a request by creating an **invoice**, supplying only the `request_id` and the `expected_request_version` described in §2.5. Pricing is refused unless the request's `fulfillment_status` is `RELEASE_READY` or `RELEASED`, with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_RELEASE_READY`; unless every redaction is settled, since a segment still holding `redaction_status` `OPEN`, `APPLIED` or `REWORK` yields `ERR_REDACTION_NOT_APPROVED`; and unless there is something to bill, since a request whose `billing_status` is already `NOT_BILLABLE`, or whose computed `subtotal_cents` works out to 0, yields `ERR_NOTHING_TO_INVOICE`. An invoice may not be created while another invoice for the same `request_id` holds `status` `ISSUED`, `PAID` or `WAIVED`; that is `ERR_INVOICE_ALREADY_EXISTS`, and that test runs *before* the nothing-to-bill test, so a request that both carries an `ISSUED` invoice and now prices to a subtotal of 0 reports `ERR_INVOICE_ALREADY_EXISTS` rather than `ERR_NOTHING_TO_INVOICE`. A `DRAFT` invoice, by contrast, does **not** block: the new invoice is a legitimate re-price, and every existing `DRAFT` of that request is driven to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED_BY_REPRICE`, `settled_at` stamped with the current logical clock and its own `version` incremented by 1 — the stamp and the increment attend every void the system performs, whatever drives it. That supersession is applied only after every precondition above has passed, so a refused re-price leaves the incumbent `DRAFT` exactly as it stood. That deliberate permissiveness is exactly why the version guard on `expected_request_version` exists. Because step one of the waterfall below re-derives the request's penalty exposure, a successful creation also increments the *request's* `version` by 1 while minting the invoice at `version` 1 — so a clerk who re-prices twice in a row must quote the freshly incremented number the second time.

The invoice's `variant` is computed, never supplied: it is `PATIENT_ACCESS_COST_BASED` when the request's `access_basis` is `PATIENT_RIGHT_OF_ACCESS`, and `THIRD_PARTY_RETAIL` otherwise. At creation the request's `billable_page_count` is frozen onto the invoice as `billable_page_count_snapshot`, and that frozen page count — call it the priced page count — is what every per-page line uses.

**Building the line set.** Lines are emitted in a pinned order, each with a computed `line_type`, `quantity` and `unit_millicents`, and each rounded independently as §2.7 requires. Every line also carries the `invoice_id` it belongs to. The `sequence` field is 1-based over the **emitted** lines only, assigned contiguously from 1 upward in the pinned order, so an omitted line type leaves no gap; `sequence` is the sole ordering key for reading lines back.

Under the `THIRD_PARTY_RETAIL` variant the pinned order is seven positions. First, a `BASE_RETRIEVAL` line with `quantity` 1 and a `unit_millicents` equal to the fee rule's `base_retrieval_fee_cents` multiplied by 1000. Second, a `PER_PAGE_TIER1` line whose `quantity` is the smaller of the priced page count and the rule's `tier1_page_limit`, at a `unit_millicents` of `tier1_per_page_millicents`, omitted entirely when that quantity is 0. Third, a `PER_PAGE_TIER2` line whose `quantity` is the priced page count less `tier1_page_limit` but never below 0, at a `unit_millicents` of `tier2_per_page_millicents`, omitted entirely when that quantity is 0. Fourth, a `LEGACY_RETRIEVAL` line with `quantity` 1 and a `unit_millicents` equal to the facility's `legacy_retrieval_surcharge_cents` multiplied by 1000, emitted only when at least one `RETRIEVED` segment of the request has `source_system` `LEGACY_ARCHIVE` **and** that surcharge is above 0, and charged exactly once per invoice no matter how many legacy segments the request contains. Fifth, a `CERTIFICATION` line with `quantity` 1 and a `unit_millicents` equal to `certification_fee_cents` multiplied by 1000, emitted only when the request's `certification_requested` is true. Sixth, a `MEDIA` line with `quantity` 1 and a `unit_millicents` equal to `media_fee_cents` multiplied by 1000, emitted only when the request's `delivery_format` is `CD_ROM`. Seventh, a `POSTAGE` line with `quantity` 1 and a `unit_millicents` equal to `postage_per_delivery_cents` multiplied by 1000, emitted only when the request's `delivery_format` is `PAPER_MAIL` or `CD_ROM`.

Under the `PATIENT_ACCESS_COST_BASED` variant the pinned order is only three positions, because a patient exercising the right of access pays copying cost and nothing else. First, a `PER_PAGE_FLAT` line whose `quantity` is the priced page count at a `unit_millicents` of `patient_access_per_page_millicents`, omitted entirely when the priced page count is 0. Second, a `MEDIA` line with `quantity` 1 at `media_fee_cents` multiplied by 1000, only when `delivery_format` is `CD_ROM`. Third, a `POSTAGE` line with `quantity` 1 at `postage_per_delivery_cents` multiplied by 1000, only when `delivery_format` is `PAPER_MAIL` or `CD_ROM`. This variant emits no `BASE_RETRIEVAL` line, no `CERTIFICATION` line, no `LEGACY_RETRIEVAL` line, and no tiered per-page lines at all: there is no clerical fee and no certification fee under the right of access.

Each emitted line's `amount_cents` is its own `quantity` multiplied by its own `unit_millicents`, divided by 1000, rounded half up to whole cents. The invoice's `subtotal_cents` is the plain sum of `amount_cents` over the emitted lines, and the two adjustment line types described next — `CAP_ADJUSTMENT` and `LATE_FEE_FORFEITURE` — are deliberately **not** part of that subtotal.

**Selecting and applying the cap.** A request delivers electronically when its `delivery_format` is `SECURE_PORTAL`, `DIRECT_MESSAGE` or `UNENCRYPTED_EMAIL`. For an electronic delivery the invoice's `applicable_cap_cents` is the smaller of the fee rule's `electronic_delivery_total_cap_cents` and its `total_fee_cap_cents`; for any other format it is simply `total_fee_cap_cents`. The `cap_adjustment_cents` is then the lesser of 0 and the difference between `applicable_cap_cents` and `subtotal_cents` — which is to say, it is 0 whenever the subtotal is at or below the cap, and otherwise it is the negative amount that pulls the subtotal down to exactly the cap. The `gross_total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` plus `cap_adjustment_cents`. The boundary is strict: a `subtotal_cents` exactly equal to `applicable_cap_cents` leaves `cap_adjustment_cents` at 0 and emits no adjustment line, because the cap bites only on strict excess. When `cap_adjustment_cents` is not 0, a `CAP_ADJUSTMENT` line is emitted as the next `sequence` after the priced lines with `quantity` 1, a `unit_millicents` of `cap_adjustment_cents` multiplied by 1000, and an `amount_cents` equal to `cap_adjustment_cents` — so the line set always reconciles to the gross total — and a `FEE_CAP_APPLIED` compliance event is appended against the request.

**The seven-step waterfall to the amount due.** These steps run in exactly this order, and the order changes the answer. Step one recomputes the request's `penalty_exposure_cents` at the current logical clock and stores that figure on the invoice as `penalty_snapshot_cents`, which defaults to 0. Step two builds the line set and sums it into `subtotal_cents` with per-line rounding already applied. Step three selects `applicable_cap_cents`. Step four computes `cap_adjustment_cents` as the lesser of 0 and the difference between `applicable_cap_cents` and `subtotal_cents`. Step five sets `gross_total_cents` to `subtotal_cents` plus `cap_adjustment_cents`. Step six computes `forfeiture_cents`, which defaults to 0: the forfeiture applies only when all three of the following hold — the governing fee rule's `fee_forfeiture_on_late` is true, the request's `access_basis` is `PATIENT_RIGHT_OF_ACCESS`, and `penalty_snapshot_cents` is strictly above 0 — and when it does apply, `forfeiture_cents` is the smaller of `gross_total_cents` and `penalty_snapshot_cents`. Note carefully that the forfeiture is measured against the **capped** figure, never against the raw subtotal. Step seven sets `amount_due_cents` to `gross_total_cents` less `forfeiture_cents`, and that is the only amount a payment may settle. When `forfeiture_cents` is above 0 a `LATE_FEE_FORFEITURE` line is emitted as the final `sequence` with `quantity` 1, a `unit_millicents` of the negated `forfeiture_cents` multiplied by 1000, and an `amount_cents` equal to the negated `forfeiture_cents`.

Reversing steps five and six is the classic error and it costs real money. Take a flat-rate patient-access invoice whose priced lines come to 8000 cents against an electronic cap of 6500 cents, with a penalty snapshot of 2500 cents. Under the pinned order the cap bites first, giving an adjustment of negative 1500 and a gross of 6500; the forfeiture is then the smaller of 6500 and 2500, which is 2500; and the amount due is 4000. Under the reversed order the forfeiture is taken from the raw 8000, giving 5500, which already sits under the cap and is left alone — an amount due of 5500 on identical inputs. Only the pinned order is correct.

An invoice's `status` starts at `DRAFT` and takes `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `PAID`, `WAIVED` or `VOIDED`, with `PAID` and `VOIDED` terminal. `paid_cents` starts at 0 and stays 0 unless the `status` is `PAID`. `issued_at`, `settled_at`, `void_reason` and `waiver_reason` all start null. Invoices are readable individually and listable with optional `request_id` and `status` filters — and, like the delivery, denial-decision and amendment-request collections, an invoice list read performed by a `Requester` is narrowed through the parent request's `requester_id`, since none of those four row shapes carries a requester field of its own — and the priced line set is readable in its pinned `sequence` order with pagination.

**Issuing.** A `BillingClerk` issues a `DRAFT` invoice with no request body. Issuance requires that the invoice's `status` be `DRAFT`, that its `amount_due_cents` be strictly above 0, and that the parent request's `fulfillment_status` be `RELEASE_READY` or `RELEASED` — a request outside those two states reports `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_RELEASE_READY`, and an invoice that is not `DRAFT` reports `ERR_INVOICE_STATE_INVALID`. Those three tests are ordered: the invoice's own `status` first, the parent request's `fulfillment_status` second, and the amount-due test last, an `amount_due_cents` of 0 or below reporting `ERR_NOTHING_TO_INVOICE`. Issuance sets `issued_at` to the current logical clock, moves the invoice to `ISSUED`, moves the request's `billing_status` from `PENDING_INVOICE` to `INVOICED`, adds the invoice's `amount_due_cents` to the facility's `receivable_cents`, and increments both the invoice's and the parent request's `version` by 1.

**Settling.** A `BillingClerk` settles an invoice with an `action` of `PAY`, `WAIVE` or `VOID`, plus `amount_cents` for a payment and a free-text `reason` for a waiver or a void. `PAY` is permitted only from `ISSUED` and demands exact settlement: the settlement body's `amount_cents` is an optional integer field declared as zero or greater, so a negative or non-integer value there is the schema violation of §2.8, but its mere absence is not — `amount_cents` absent, or present but unequal to `amount_due_cents`, is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, because partial payment is what would otherwise break the facility ledger's clean three-way partition. A successful payment records `paid_cents` as the settled `amount_cents` and `settled_at` as the current logical clock, moves the invoice to `PAID` and the request's `billing_status` to `PAID`, subtracts `amount_due_cents` from the facility's `receivable_cents` and adds it to `collected_cents`. `WAIVE` is also permitted only from `ISSUED`, records the supplied `reason` as `waiver_reason` along with `settled_at` — the `reason` being an optional field, so a waiver that omits it leaves `waiver_reason` null — moves the invoice to `WAIVED` and the request's `billing_status` to `WAIVED`, and subtracts `amount_due_cents` from `receivable_cents` while adding it to `written_off_cents`. `VOID` is permitted from `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `WAIVED`, records the supplied `reason` as `void_reason` and falls back to the literal `BILLING_CLERK_VOID` when no `reason` is supplied — the three values `SUPERSEDED_BY_REPRICE`, `AUTHORIZATION_REVOKED` and `BILLING_CLERK_VOID` name what the system itself writes rather than an enum the field is validated against, so a clerk's own free-text `reason` of 1 to 500 characters is stored verbatim — sets `settled_at`, moves the invoice to `VOIDED`, returns the request's `billing_status` to `PENDING_INVOICE`, an assignment made unconditionally so that voiding a `DRAFT` merely re-asserts the `PENDING_INVOICE` the request already held while voiding from `INVOICED` or from `WAIVED` genuinely reverses a move, and reverses whichever ledger postings that invoice had made, so that the facility's `receivable_cents`, `collected_cents` and `written_off_cents` return together to their pre-issue values. Every settlement action increments the invoice's `version` by 1 and, because it also rewrites the parent request's `billing_status`, that request's `version` by 1 as well. Any settlement action attempted from a state its branch does not permit — a payment or waiver on an invoice that is not `ISSUED`, or any action at all on a `PAID` or `VOIDED` invoice — is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.7. Release, Secure Transmission and Closure

Before any page leaves the building, three conditions must hold together. A `ReleaseReviewer` releases a request from `RELEASE_READY` to `RELEASED`, supplying an optional `release_note` of at most 500 characters that is validated but never persisted. The request's `billing_status` must be `PAID`, `WAIVED` or `NOT_BILLABLE`; a `billing_status` of `PENDING_INVOICE` or `INVOICED` means the money is still outstanding and the release is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_UNSETTLED` (HTTP 402), because prepayment is required before records leave the building. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the request's `intake_user_id`, or the release is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And no segment of the request may hold `redaction_status` `OPEN`, `APPLIED` or `REWORK`, or the release is refused with `ERR_REDACTION_NOT_APPROVED` — which, being a business constraint rather than a fiscal one, is the answer whenever an unsettled invoice and an unapproved redaction are both outstanding. A release attempted from any `fulfillment_status` other than `RELEASE_READY` reports `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`, and as §2.8 records, that state failure outranks the unsettled-invoice failure when both are present.

A successful release sets `released_at` to the current logical clock and `release_reviewer_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, then freezes both `penalty_exposure_cents` and `safe_harbor_penalty_cents` by evaluating them with the completion instant taken as `released_at` rather than as the moving clock. When `released_at` is strictly later than `statutory_due_at`, a `STATUTORY_DEADLINE_BREACHED` compliance event is appended. The facility's `penalty_exposure_cents` is re-derived, and the request's `version` increments by 1. Should the request later be pushed backwards out of `RELEASE_READY` by an authorization revocation, `release_reviewer_user_id` is cleared again.

A **delivery** is the transmission record. A `ReleaseReviewer` opens one by supplying the `request_id`, a `delivery_format` that must equal the request's own `delivery_format` — a field constraint measured against the request it serves, so a divergent format is the schema violation of §2.8 reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, answered once the `request_id` has resolved but still ahead of the `RELEASED` gate below — a `recipient_endpoint` of 1 to 200 characters holding a postal address, portal handle, direct address, email address or fax number, an `encryption` value of `TLS`, `AES256_ZIP` or `NONE`, and an optional `patient_direction_ack` defaulting to false which records that the patient directed transmission to an unsecured endpoint and accepted the risk. A delivery may only be opened for a request whose `fulfillment_status` is `RELEASED`; anything else reports `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_RELEASE_READY`. Only one live transmission per request is permitted: if another delivery for the same `request_id` holds `status` `PENDING`, `SENT` or `FAILED`, the creation is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_ALREADY_EXISTS`. That uniqueness rule is evaluated last of all, after both channel-safety rules and the designated-recipient rule described next, so a second delivery that is *also* unsecured or misdirected reports the safety or the referential failure rather than the duplicate.

Two safety rules guard the channel, and they are evaluated before the designated-recipient rule. When the `delivery_format` is `UNENCRYPTED_EMAIL`, or the `encryption` is `NONE`, the transmission is permitted only if the request's `access_basis` is `PATIENT_RIGHT_OF_ACCESS` and `patient_direction_ack` is true; in every other combination it is refused with `ERR_UNENCRYPTED_DELIVERY_NOT_DIRECTED`, since only a patient exercising the right of access may direct their own records to an unsecured endpoint. When the `delivery_format` is `FAX`, the transmission is refused with `ERR_FAX_NOT_PERMITTED` if any `RETRIEVED` segment of the request has `sensitivity_status` `AUTHORIZED_INCLUDE` and a `category` of `SUD_PART2`, `HIV` or `GENETIC` — those three categories may not ride a fax. Then the referential rule: on a `THIRD_PARTY_AUTHORIZATION` request the `recipient_endpoint` must exactly equal the governing authorization's `designated_recipient_endpoint`, and any other endpoint is refused with `ERR_RECIPIENT_NOT_DESIGNATED`.

A delivery's `status` starts at `PENDING` and takes `PENDING`, `SENT`, `FAILED`, `CONFIRMED` or `RETURNED`, with `CONFIRMED` and `RETURNED` terminal. `attempt_count` starts at 0, `sent_at` and `confirmed_at` start null, and `failure_reason` starts null. Deliveries are readable individually — showing the attempt count — and listable with optional `request_id` and `status` filters.

A `ReleaseReviewer` records each transmission attempt by supplying an `outcome` and, for a failure, a `failure_reason` of 1 to 500 characters: a `FAILED` outcome arriving without one is the missing-field violation of §2.8 reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while a `failure_reason` riding a `SENT` outcome is validated and then discarded. An `outcome` of `SENT` moves the delivery from `PENDING` or from `FAILED` to `SENT`, requires that the parent request's `fulfillment_status` be `RELEASED` — any other state reports `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`, tested before the attempt ceiling below — adds 1 to `attempt_count`, sets `sent_at` to the current logical clock, leaves standing whatever `failure_reason` an earlier failed attempt recorded rather than clearing it, and increments the delivery's `version` by 1. An `outcome` of `FAILED` moves the delivery from `PENDING` or from `FAILED` back to `FAILED` — the loop is deliberate, since a failed transmission may be retried — reads no state from the parent request beyond its existence, adds 1 to `attempt_count`, records the supplied `failure_reason`, appends a `DELIVERY_FAILED` compliance event, and increments the delivery's `version` by 1. Both outcomes require `attempt_count` to be strictly below the ceiling of three attempts; once `attempt_count` has reached 3 no further attempt is accepted and the call is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_ATTEMPT_LIMIT`. An attempt recorded against a delivery whose `status` is already `SENT`, `CONFIRMED` or `RETURNED` is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_STATE_INVALID`.

Receipt is confirmed by a `ReleaseReviewer` or by the owning `Requester`, supplying an `outcome` of `CONFIRMED` or `RETURNED`, and is permitted only from `SENT`; from any other `status` it reports `ERR_DELIVERY_STATE_INVALID`. An `outcome` of `CONFIRMED` sets `confirmed_at` to the current logical clock, moves the delivery to `CONFIRMED`, increments the delivery's `version` by 1, and cascades the parent request from `RELEASED` to `CLOSED`: `closed_at` is set to the current logical clock, the facility's `penalty_exposure_cents` is re-derived, and the request's `version` increments by 1. An `outcome` of `RETURNED` also sets `confirmed_at` and increments the delivery's `version` by 1, but moves the delivery to `RETURNED`, appends a `DELIVERY_FAILED` compliance event, and deliberately leaves the parent request in `RELEASED` — a returned envelope is not a completed disclosure.

### 3.8. Denials and Their Second-Officer Review

A `PrivacyOfficer` denies a request by creating a **denial decision** with the `request_id`, a `denial_type` of `FULL` or `PARTIAL`, a `grounds` value drawn from exactly six statutory bases — `NO_VALID_AUTHORIZATION`, `PSYCHOTHERAPY_NOTES_EXCLUDED`, `RECORD_NOT_MAINTAINED`, `HARM_REVIEW_LICENSED_PROFESSIONAL`, `INMATE_SAFETY` or `RESEARCH_IN_PROGRESS` — and a `rationale` of 1 to 500 characters supplied to the requester. A denial may be issued while the request's `fulfillment_status` is `INTAKE_REVIEW`, `AUTH_DEFECT_PENDING`, `COMPILATION`, `SENSITIVE_REVIEW` or `REDACTION`, and requires that no denial decision of that request already hold `status` `ISSUED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` — a guard kept for completeness but unreachable in practice, since an open denial always leaves its request in `DENIED`, which the origin set above already refuses with `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`; were the duplicate guard ever reached it would report that same code.

The `reviewable` flag is computed, never supplied: it is true exactly when `grounds` is `HARM_REVIEW_LICENSED_PROFESSIONAL` or `INMATE_SAFETY`, and false for the other four grounds. `issued_at` is stamped with the current logical clock, `issued_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, `reviewer_user_id` and `decided_at` start null, `status` starts at `ISSUED`, and `review_due_at` is derived by adding a review window of thirty whole days to `issued_at`. Creating the denial moves the parent request to `DENIED`, appends a `DENIAL_ISSUED` compliance event, then re-derives `statutory_due_at` and both penalty measures — in that order, so any `STATUTORY_DEADLINE_BREACHED` or `PENALTY_ACCRUED` event the accrual produces is appended *after* the `DENIAL_ISSUED` event it shares an `occurred_at` with, and the `days_late` on `DENIAL_ISSUED` reads the deadline the request still carried on entry — and increments the request's `version` by 1. Crucially, a denial does **not** stop the statutory clock: `penalty_exposure_cents` keeps accruing against the moving logical clock while the request sits in `DENIED`. Denial decisions are readable individually and listable with optional `request_id` and `status` filters.

Review is a `PrivacyOfficer` capability taking an `action` of `START_REVIEW`, `UPHOLD` or `OVERTURN` plus an optional `rationale` of at most 500 characters which, whenever it is supplied, overwrites the denial's stored `rationale` — on `START_REVIEW` just as on the two deciding actions — and which leaves the text already recorded there standing whenever it is omitted, and in all three branches requiring that `X-User-ID` differ from `issued_by_user_id`, failing which the call is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The four denial states are `ISSUED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `UPHELD` and `OVERTURNED`, the last two terminal, and any action requested from outside its own origin set is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`.

`START_REVIEW` moves the decision from `ISSUED` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, requires `reviewable` to be true, records `reviewer_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, and increments the decision's `version` by 1. A `START_REVIEW` on a denial whose `reviewable` is false is refused with `ERR_DENIAL_NOT_REVIEWABLE`, and so is an `OVERTURN` on such a denial.

`UPHOLD` moves the decision to `UPHELD` from either `ISSUED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`; when the origin is `ISSUED` the denial's `reviewable` must be false, since a reviewable denial must pass through review before it can be upheld. An `UPHOLD` taken straight from `ISSUED` records no `reviewer_user_id` — only `START_REVIEW` ever writes that field — so a denial upheld without review keeps `reviewer_user_id` null. Upholding sets `decided_at` to the current logical clock and cascades the parent request from `DENIED` to `CLOSED`: `closed_at` is set to the current logical clock, both penalty measures are frozen with the completion instant taken as `closed_at`, the request's `billing_status` moves to `NOT_BILLABLE` if and only if it was still `PENDING_INVOICE`, the facility's `penalty_exposure_cents` is re-derived, and the request's `version` increments by 1. The decision's own `version` increments by 1 as well.

`OVERTURN` moves the decision from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `OVERTURNED`, sets `decided_at` to the current logical clock, increments the decision's `version` by 1, and cascades the parent request from `DENIED` all the way back to `COMPILATION` with its full effect list: every record segment of the request whose `sensitivity_status` is `WITHHELD` reverts to `SEGREGATED_PENDING_REVIEW` — and any of those segments holding `redaction_status` `APPLIED`, `APPROVED` or `REWORK` is reset to `OPEN` with `redaction_approved_by_user_id` cleared — so the reviewer must decide the same segments again; the four page-ledger fields are re-derived; both penalty measures are re-derived at the current logical clock; the facility's `penalty_exposure_cents` is re-derived; a `DENIAL_OVERTURNED` compliance event is appended, and it is appended before the penalty re-derivation runs, so that the `PENALTY_ACCRUED` event which follows it when the penalty figure actually changed always lands second in the trail; and the request's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.9. Amendments, Turnaround Monitoring and the Compliance Sweep

A patient who believes a disclosed page is wrong may ask for it to be amended. The owning `Requester` submits an **amendment request** naming the `request_id` whose fulfilment disclosed the disputed pages, the `patient_id` — which must equal the parent request's own `patient_id`, a mismatch being refused with `ERR_PATIENT_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` — the `record_segment_id` of the disputed segment (resolved against the whole segment collection, and deliberately *not* required to belong to the parent request, so a `RETRIEVED` segment of some other request is accepted here where a sensitive-review decision list naming it would have reported `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`), and a `requested_change` of 1 to 500 characters. The target must actually have been disclosed: if the parent request's `released_at` is still null, or the named segment's `retrieval_status` is anything other than `RETRIEVED`, the submission is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_TARGET_NOT_RELEASED`. On creation `status` starts at `SUBMITTED`, `submission_count` starts at 1, `additional_information`, `rationale` and `decided_at` start null, and `decision_due_at` is derived by adding an amendment window of sixty whole days to the record's `created_at`. Amendment requests are readable individually and listable with optional `patient_id`, `record_segment_id`, `request_id` and `status` filters.

A `PrivacyOfficer` drives the review with a `decision` of `START_REVIEW`, `REQUEST_INFO`, `ACCEPT` or `DENY`, plus an optional `rationale` of at most 500 characters that is recorded on whichever decision supplies it — `START_REVIEW` included — and that leaves the text already recorded there standing whenever it is omitted. The five amendment states are `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `ACCEPTED` and `DENIED`, with `ACCEPTED` and `DENIED` terminal; a decision requested from outside its own origin state is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`. `START_REVIEW` moves the amendment from `SUBMITTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW` with no further precondition. `REQUEST_INFO` moves it from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `INFO_REQUESTED`, again with no further precondition. `DENY` moves it from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `DENIED` and sets `decided_at` to the current logical clock, with no further precondition. `ACCEPT` moves it from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `ACCEPTED`, requires that the target segment's `retrieval_status` still be `RETRIEVED`, sets `decided_at` to the current logical clock, sets the target segment's `amended` flag to true, sets the parent request's `amendment_notice_required` to true when and only when that request's `released_at` is not null, and appends an `AMENDMENT_NOTICE_REQUIRED` compliance event whether or not that flag moved. Like the opening of a brand-new segment, an acceptance re-derives no page-ledger field — the `amended` flag is not a page count — yet it still increments both the target segment's `version` and the parent request's `version` by 1. All four decisions increment the amendment request's `version` by 1.

From `INFO_REQUESTED` the owning `Requester` may resubmit, supplying a mandatory `additional_information` of 1 to 500 characters; the acting `X-User-ID` must be the requesting party that owns the parent request. Resubmission returns the amendment to `SUBMITTED`, stores the supplied `additional_information`, leaves the `rationale` the reviewer had recorded exactly as it stands, adds 1 to `submission_count`, and increments the amendment request's `version` by 1 — the review starts over, and the cycle may repeat without bound. A resubmission attempted on an amendment whose `status` is not `INFO_REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`.

**Two lateness measures.** Turnaround is measured twice over the same interval, and both measures are maintained continuously on every request. First fix the completion instant: the **effective completion instant** is the request's `released_at` when that is not null; failing that it is the request's `closed_at` when that is not null; failing both it is the current logical clock, so an open request's lateness grows as the clock moves. If the effective completion instant is at or before `statutory_due_at`, both measures are 0 and no compliance event is appended — a request delivered exactly at its deadline is on time.

When the effective completion instant is strictly later than `statutory_due_at`, the statutory measure, reported as `penalty_exposure_cents`, counts raw elapsed time with no grace: take the seconds between `statutory_due_at` and the effective completion instant, divide by 86400 seconds per day, and round the quotient **up** to the next whole day to obtain the strict days-late figure; multiply that figure by the fee rule's `penalty_per_day_cents`; and take the smaller of that product and the rule's `penalty_cap_cents`. Five and a quarter days overrun therefore counts as six late days.

The contractual measure, reported as `safe_harbor_penalty_cents`, counts UTC business days and forgives the first two of them. Count the UTC calendar dates that fall strictly after the calendar date of `statutory_due_at` and at or before the calendar date of the effective completion instant, keeping only those that are a Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday or Friday; that count is the business days-late figure. Subtract a grace of two business days from it, floored at 0, to obtain the chargeable days; multiply the chargeable days by the same `penalty_per_day_cents`; and take the smaller of that product and the same `penalty_cap_cents`. Both measures therefore share one per-day rate and one ceiling, and differ only in how they count days.

Whenever the statutory measure increases, a `PENALTY_ACCRUED` compliance event is appended carrying the increase as `penalty_delta_cents` and the strict figure as `days_late`. The first time the effective completion instant passes `statutory_due_at`, a `STATUTORY_DEADLINE_BREACHED` event is appended. Any movement in a request's statutory measure re-derives the facility's `penalty_exposure_cents`. The two *stored* measures are re-derived by a fixed list of operations: intake acceptance, the declaration that compilation is finished, the statutory extension notice, release, withdrawal, the cure of a defective authorization, the issue of a denial, its upholding and its overturning, the first step of invoice creation, and the compliance sweep. The confirmed delivery that closes a request is pointedly *not* on that list: it re-derives the facility's `penalty_exposure_cents` but leaves the request's own two stored figures exactly as `released_at` froze them, which changes nothing, since the effective completion instant was already pinned to `released_at` before `closed_at` was ever stamped. The remaining state changes deliberately leave the two stored figures where they stand and re-derive only `statutory_due_at`: parking a request on an authorization defect, the sensitive-review pass, every record-segment write including the bubble-up out of `REDACTION` into `RELEASE_READY`, and the authorization-revocation cascade. The non-persisting computed read described next always reflects the moving clock, so it may legitimately disagree with the stored figures until the next refreshing operation runs.

A non-persisting computed read exposes the whole calculation for one request: it returns `statutory_due_at`, `effective_completion_at`, `tolled_seconds`, `extension_applied`, a `breached` boolean that is true exactly when the effective completion instant is strictly later than `statutory_due_at`, `days_late_strict`, `days_late_business`, `chargeable_days`, `penalty_exposure_cents` and `safe_harbor_penalty_cents`. It writes nothing and moves no `version`.

**The compliance sweep** is the platform's batch conscience, available to `PlatformAdmin` and `PrivacyOfficer` with no request body. It lapses every `VALID` authorization whose `expires_at` is at or before the current logical clock to `EXPIRED`, incrementing that authorization's `version` by 1 and appending an `AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED` compliance event against every request on it whose `fulfillment_status` is non-terminal. Every lapse and every one of those events lands before any deadline or penalty work begins, so their `days_late` reads the `statutory_due_at` each request still carried on entry. It then re-derives `statutory_due_at` and, from it, both penalty measures on every request whose `fulfillment_status` is non-terminal, appending `STATUTORY_DEADLINE_BREACHED` and `PENALTY_ACCRUED` events where the rules above call for them, and re-derives every affected facility's `penalty_exposure_cents`. The response reports `swept_request_count`, the number of non-terminal requests examined (a `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` request is skipped and counted nowhere); `breached_request_ids`, the identifiers of requests whose deadline is breached; `penalized_request_ids`, the identifiers of requests whose statutory penalty moved; `total_penalty_delta_cents`, the summed increase across the sweep; and `expired_authorization_ids`, the identifiers of the authorizations that lapsed. Both identifier lists are returned in ascending `created_at` order of the underlying requests and authorizations respectively. Beyond the authorizations it lapses, the sweep moves no `version` at all: a request whose `statutory_due_at` or penalty figures the sweep rewrites keeps the `updated_at` and `version` it already had, and re-deriving a facility's `penalty_exposure_cents` never advances that facility's `version` either.

A request's compliance trail is readable by every staff role with an optional `event_type` filter, ordered as §2.3 and §2.9 describe.
