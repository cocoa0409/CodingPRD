# Disaster Loan Servicing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a federally declared disaster strikes a community, the window between the declaration and the arrival of recovery capital determines whether households rebuild and whether small businesses survive. This system operates the full life of a disaster loan program on behalf of the administering agency: it opens loan availability for a defined geography when a declaration is published, enforces the statutory filing deadlines that differ between physical-damage and economic-injury assistance, and carries each application through intake, credit review, repayment-ability analysis, and damage verification before an approval decision is rendered.

The financial integrity of the program rests on two disciplines that pervade every workflow in this document. First, duplication of benefits is prevented at every money-moving step: insurance proceeds are deducted from verified losses before approval, external grant awards are checked against a benefits ceiling when they are reported, and post-disbursement insurance settlements claw back loan commitment and, where funds have already gone out the door, generate a receivable the borrower must repay. Second, servicing is honest about time: disbursements move in stages against verified repair costs, interest accrues daily on the outstanding principal, deferments and hardship accommodations shift the payment calendar without erasing delinquency history, and a portfolio-level aging sweep moves loans from current to delinquent to default to charge-off on fixed day-count boundaries.

The audience for this document is the engineering team building the API. Every resource is served under the `/disaster-relief` URL prefix; a single platform-level clock endpoint sits outside that prefix and is described in Section 2.4. Roles, response envelopes, pagination, validation ordering, and concurrency rules in Section 2 are contract-level specifications; Section 3 presents the business workflows in narrative form; Sections 4 and 5 summarize the endpoint and error catalogues.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role under which that user acts. A request missing either header, or carrying an empty value for either, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose role is not in the endpoint's authorized role list is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). These two checks, in that order, precede every other rule in the system.

Eight roles exist, spelled exactly as follows:

- `Anonymous` — an unauthenticated registrant who may only self-register as an applicant.
- `Applicant` — a disaster borrower (homeowner, renter, or business) who acts only on their own resources.
- `LoanOfficer` — the intake and credit-review officer.
- `LossVerifier` — the damage inspector and loss verifier.
- `LoanApprover` — the approval authority for applications and modification requests.
- `ServicingAgent` — the portfolio servicing operator.
- `GrantCoordinator` — the external grant program coordinator.
- `SystemAdmin` — the platform operator who declares disasters and drives the logical clock.

Role identity resolves differently for borrowers than for staff. When `X-User-Role` is `Applicant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Applicant` record; ownership checks on any resource that references an applicant — an application, a loan, a verification reached through its application, a milestone or servicing action or settlement reached through its loan, a receivable reached through its loan — compare the resource's `applicant_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is any of `Anonymous`, `LoanOfficer`, `LossVerifier`, `LoanApprover`, `ServicingAgent`, `GrantCoordinator`, or `SystemAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow calls for attribution — for example as `reviewer_user_id` on a credit review, `verifier_user_id` on a damage verification or milestone decision, or `decided_by` on a modification request or servicing action — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Segregation of duties is enforced between two staff roles: the `LossVerifier` who decides a repair milestone must not be the same user who completed the application's latest completed damage verification, and a violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, taken from the wall clock at response time; only entity timestamps (`created_at`, `updated_at`, and the workflow dates of Section 3) are driven by the logical clock of Section 2.4.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- A request to an unknown path, or to a known path with an unsupported method, is rejected with the `ERR_NOT_FOUND` envelope at HTTP 404. An unexpected server failure is reported in the same envelope with `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 500.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` sets the page size: it defaults to 10, with a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` sets the number of records to skip and defaults to 0. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` in its `meta` object. Unless a workflow states a different ordering, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with ties broken by insertion order. A `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); an out-of-range integer is clamped into the allowed range rather than rejected (`limit` into [1, 100], `offset` up to 0). `total_count` counts all records matching the applied filters before pagination, and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of records returned is less than `total_count`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and the Time-Driven Sweep

The system runs on a logical clock, not wall-clock time. At startup, before any clock call has been made, the current logical instant is exactly 2026-09-01T00:00:00Z, and the server never falls back to wall-clock time. A `SystemAdmin` advances time through an admin-only test-driving capability that sets the logical clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant; the response echoes the `current_utc` now in effect.

Setting the clock runs a full time-driven sweep over the portfolio, in this order of concern:

1. **Declaration rollup.** Each declaration's `status` is advanced to match the new instant: `OPEN` while the current instant is on or before `physical_filing_deadline`; `PHYSICAL_CLOSED` once the current instant is strictly after `physical_filing_deadline` but still on or before `economic_injury_filing_deadline`; `FULLY_CLOSED` once the current instant is strictly after `economic_injury_filing_deadline`. A single clock set that jumps past both deadlines moves a declaration directly from `OPEN` to `FULLY_CLOSED`. Each rollup transition increments the declaration's `version` by 1, and such a direct jump is a single transition: one clock set that moves a declaration from `OPEN` straight to `FULLY_CLOSED` increments `version` once.
2. **Servicing-action activation and expiry.** An `APPROVED` servicing action whose `start_date` has been reached (current instant on or after `start_date`) becomes `ACTIVE` and flips the loan's `servicing_status`; an `ACTIVE` action whose `end_date` has been reached (current instant on or after `end_date`) becomes `EXPIRED` and the loan's `servicing_status` returns to `CURRENT`. Within one clock set the sweep expires due `ACTIVE` actions first, then activates due `APPROVED` actions, then expires any action whose `end_date` has also passed — so an approved window lying entirely in the past passes through `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED` in a single set. Activation, here and at approval time in Section 3.9, additionally requires the loan's `servicing_status` to be `CURRENT` at that moment: an `APPROVED` action whose `start_date` passes while the loan is `DELINQUENT` or `DEFAULT` remains `APPROVED`, and may still activate on a later clock set once the loan has returned to `CURRENT`. Every activation also resets the loan's `days_past_due` to zero.
3. **Delinquency aging.** Evaluated per loan with `lifecycle_status` `ACTIVE`, in ascending `created_at` order. Fees are assessed first, then the furthest applicable status transition is applied: a late fee at 15 or more days past due, `DELINQUENT` at 30 or more days (exactly 30 qualifies), `DEFAULT` at 120 or more days (exactly 120 qualifies), and lifecycle `CHARGED_OFF` at 180 or more days (exactly 180 qualifies). Section 3.9 gives the full aging rules.
4. **Default cascades.** A loan entering `DEFAULT` escalates its open excess-funds receivable and denies its still-requested servicing actions, as detailed in Sections 3.8 and 3.9.

The clock may be set to any instant, including one earlier than the current logical instant. Declaration rollup re-derives each declaration's status from the new instant on every set, so a backward set can move a declaration back toward `OPEN`; servicing-action and delinquency transitions are never reversed by a backward set — an action already `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` stays so, and a loan keeps the servicing-status rungs and lifecycle it has reached — though `days_past_due` is recomputed against the new instant.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Timestamps, and Monetary Precision

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. Most resources also carry the common audit triple: `created_at` (ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits, set at creation), `updated_at` (same format, refreshed on every mutation), and `version` (an integer that starts at 1 at creation and increments by 1 on every mutation of that resource). Three record types are immutable ledgers and carry only `id` and `created_at`: the credit review, the payment, and the grant award. For those three, `updated_at` and `version` are absent entirely — they are never updated after creation. Where a request field calls for an ISO 8601 datetime, a date-only string is accepted and read as midnight UTC, and a datetime without an offset is read as UTC.

All monetary amounts are integer cents. Where a computation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded half-up to integer cents, and each calculation in Section 3 states exactly where that rounding happens. Percentages such as the annual interest rate are exact decimal values; intermediate arithmetic is performed in exact decimal and rounded only where the owning calculation says so.

### 2.6. Validation Order and Nested-Path Resolution

Every endpoint evaluates its guards in a fixed global order, and a request is rejected at the first guard it fails. The order is:

1. **Authentication** — `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorization** — the role is permitted on this endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Ownership** — for `Applicant`-role requests, the caller owns the target resource, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_OWNER`.
4. **Existence** — path identifiers resolve to existing rows, validated left to right along the URL. The first identifier that does not resolve yields `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404); a request to act on a child of a missing parent therefore reports the parent as not found.
5. **Request well-formedness** — required fields present, enum values in range, amounts positive where required, and the shape rules of Section 3 (for example, `depreciation_pct` present and within range exactly when the `valuation_basis` calls for it, `end_date` strictly after `start_date`, a grant's `purpose_category` matching the application's `loan_purpose`), else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
6. **Optimistic concurrency** — on the two version-guarded writes of Section 2.7, `expected_loan_version` must equal the loan's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
7. **Business rules** — the purpose, state, temporal, eligibility, safety, and fiscal gates of Section 3, each returning its own error identifier.

Within the business-rule tier, two orderings are pinned because one failure must not mask another. On application submission, the purpose-specific filing-deadline checks are evaluated before county eligibility, so a late submission to an ineligible county reports the deadline error. On application approval, the damage-verification state gate is evaluated before any fiscal check, so an open verification surfaces as `ERR_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` rather than being masked by a collateral computation. On servicing-action approval, the overlap check against sibling accommodations is evaluated before the default-status admissibility check.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Loan-Mutating Writes

Two writes rewrite loan financial state that other clients may have based decisions on, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control:

- `POST /disaster-relief/loans/{id}/modification-requests` — creating a modification request against a loan requires the request body field `expected_loan_version`, carrying the `version` of the loan the client last observed.
- `POST /disaster-relief/insurance-settlements/{id}/apply` — applying an insurance settlement requires the request body field `expected_loan_version`, again carrying the client's last observed loan `version`.

In both cases the field is non-optional: a missing or non-integer `expected_loan_version` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). If the presented value does not equal the loan's current `version` — because another writer has advanced the loan since the client last read it — the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), preventing the client from overwriting loan state it has not observed. Endpoints not listed here take no version field.

### 2.8. Rate, Term, and Deferral Policy

Disaster loan pricing is fixed by applicant type and loan purpose, and no request may override it. The monthly rate used in every payment computation is the annual rate divided by 12, computed in exact decimal.

- A `HOMEOWNER` borrowing for `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE` pays an annual `interest_rate` of 0.03125 over a `term_months` of 360.
- A `RENTER` borrowing for `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE` pays an annual rate of 0.03125 over 84 months.
- A `BUSINESS` borrowing for `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE` pays an annual rate of 0.04 over 360 months.
- Every `ECONOMIC_INJURY` loan, regardless of applicant type, pays an annual rate of 0.04 over 360 months.

Every disaster loan carries a deferral: `deferral_months` is 12 on every originated loan. The first payment comes due 12 plus 1 calendar months after the first tranche release — that is, the first tranche's release date advanced by 13 calendar months sets `next_due_date` (calendar-month addition clamps the day-of-month to the target month's last day, so a January 31 anchor lands on February 28 or 29). Interest accrues during the deferral window and is capitalized into principal before the scheduled monthly payment is computed, as detailed in Section 3.6. Daily interest on the outstanding principal accrues at the annual rate divided by 365.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Declaring a Disaster and the Filing Windows

A `SystemAdmin` opens a lending geography by declaring a disaster. The declaration carries a public `declaration_number` (for example DR-4821) that must be unique across all declarations — a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) — a `disaster_type` of exactly one of `HURRICANE`, `FLOOD`, `EARTHQUAKE`, `TORNADO`, or `WILDFIRE`, an inclusive incident window given by `incident_start_date` and `incident_end_date` where the end date must be on or after the start date, a `declared_date` recording when the disaster was declared, and a `counties` array naming the covered geography. Each counties entry pairs a `county_fips` code with an `eligible_purposes` list drawn from `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE` and `ECONOMIC_INJURY`; at least one county entry is required, and `county_fips` values must be unique within a single declaration. A county entry's `eligible_purposes` may be an empty list; such a county is then eligible for no purpose. Malformed payloads — a missing field, an out-of-range enum, an inverted incident window, an empty county list — are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Two filing deadlines are computed at declaration time and anchor the entire intake pipeline. The `physical_filing_deadline` is `declared_date` plus 90 days; the `economic_injury_filing_deadline` is `declared_date` plus 270 days. A `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE` submission is accepted while the current logical instant is on or before the physical deadline — the deadline instant itself is inclusive — and an `ECONOMIC_INJURY` submission is accepted while the current instant is on or before the economic-injury deadline, likewise inclusive. The declaration's own `status` tracks these windows automatically through the clock sweep of Section 2.4: `OPEN`, then `PHYSICAL_CLOSED`, then `FULLY_CLOSED`, which is terminal.

Geography may not be double-declared for overlapping time. When a declaration is created, every county it names is checked against every other declaration: if another declaration covers the same `county_fips` with an incident window that intersects the new one — both window ends inclusive, so sharing a single boundary day counts as overlap — the create is rejected with `ERR_DECLARATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409).

### 3.2. Applicant Onboarding and Application Intake

Anyone may self-register: an `Anonymous` caller provides `full_name`, `email`, `applicant_type` (`HOMEOWNER`, `RENTER`, or `BUSINESS`), `annual_income_cents` (verified annual gross income in cents, strictly greater than zero), and `monthly_debt_cents` (self-reported recurring monthly debt in cents, zero or more). The `email` must be unique across all applicants; a duplicate `email` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The applicant type chosen here later drives the loan caps, terms, and interest rates of Sections 2.8 and 3.6.

A registered `Applicant` begins the borrowing workflow by creating a loan application in `DRAFT` status, born with `reconsideration_count` at zero, `submitted_at` null until submission, `decline_reason` null, and `loan_id` null until an approval sets it. The create payload names the `declaration_id` of the declaration being filed under, the `county_fips` where the damaged property or injured business sits, the `loan_purpose` (`PHYSICAL_DAMAGE` or `ECONOMIC_INJURY`), and the `requested_amount_cents` (strictly greater than zero). The referenced declaration is not required to exist at create time; its existence is first enforced at submission. The `applicant_id` is always taken from the caller's `X-User-ID` and never from the request body. Applications can be listed with optional `applicant_id`, `declaration_id`, `loan_id`, and `status` filters, ordered by `created_at` ascending; for an `Applicant` caller the list is scoped to that caller's own applications and the `applicant_id` filter is ignored.

Submitting a draft is the borrower's attestation, and it is gated in a fixed order. The caller must own the application, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_OWNER`. The referenced declaration must exist, else `ERR_NOT_FOUND`. The application must still be in `DRAFT`, else `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_DRAFT` (HTTP 409). Then the purpose-specific deadline applies: a `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE` submission with the current instant strictly after the declaration's `physical_filing_deadline` is rejected with `ERR_PHYSICAL_FILING_CLOSED` (HTTP 422), and an `ECONOMIC_INJURY` submission strictly after the `economic_injury_filing_deadline` is rejected with `ERR_EIDL_FILING_CLOSED` (HTTP 422) — both deadlines inclusive of their exact instant. Only after the deadline checks does county eligibility run: the application's `county_fips` must appear in the declaration's `counties` with the application's `loan_purpose` among that county's `eligible_purposes`, else the submission is rejected with `ERR_COUNTY_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). Because eligibility is evaluated after the deadlines, a past-deadline submission to an ineligible county reports the deadline error. A successful submission stamps `submitted_at` with the current logical instant and moves the application to `SUBMITTED`.

A declined applicant may ask for reconsideration, which moves the application from `DECLINED` back to `DECISION_PENDING`. The caller must own the application (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_OWNER`), the application must be in `DECLINED` (else `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_DECLINED`), and `reconsideration_count` must be below the program maximum of 2 — a request when the count has already reached 2 is rejected with `ERR_RECONSIDERATION_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). Each granted reconsideration increments `reconsideration_count` by 1 and clears `decline_reason` to null. Once the count reaches 2, `DECLINED` is absorbing: no transition out remains reachable, since reconsideration fails with `ERR_RECONSIDERATION_LIMIT` and a fresh decision fails with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_DECISION_PENDING`. The machine-set `decline_reason` is one of `CREDIT_FAIL`, `DTI_EXCEEDED`, or `APPROVER_DECLINE`, and the workflows below state when each is written.

### 3.3. Credit Review and Repayment-Ability Analysis

Once an application is `SUBMITTED`, a `LoanOfficer` files a credit review against it; filing against an application in any other status is rejected with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_SUBMITTED`. The officer supplies `credit_score` — a bureau score that must lie between 300 and 850 inclusive — and `verified_monthly_debt_cents`, the officer-verified recurring monthly debt in cents, zero or more. The officer's `X-User-ID` is recorded as `reviewer_user_id`. A review body that violates these shape rules is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the computed verdict itself is never an error response.

Three quantities are computed at filing time. First, `proposed_payment_cents` is the standard amortized monthly payment on the `requested_amount_cents`, using the rate and term implied by the applicant's type crossed with the loan purpose per Section 2.8: the monthly rate is the annual rate divided by 12 in exact decimal, and the payment is the principal times the monthly rate times the quantity (1 plus the monthly rate) raised to the number of months in the term, divided by the quantity (1 plus the monthly rate) raised to the term minus one, computed in exact decimal and rounded half-up to integer cents at the final step only. Second, `score_pass` is true exactly when the `credit_score` is at least 640. Third, `dti_pass` is true exactly when the sum of `verified_monthly_debt_cents` and `proposed_payment_cents`, multiplied by 100, is less than or equal to 43 times the applicant's `annual_income_cents` divided by 12 and floored to a whole number — that is, a back-end debt-to-income ratio capped at 43 percent of gross monthly income, with the monthly income computed by integer-dividing the annual figure by 12 and discarding the remainder. The review's `verdict` is `PASS` when both `score_pass` and `dti_pass` hold, and `FAIL` otherwise.

The verdict drives the application. A `PASS` on a `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE` application moves it to `VERIFICATION`, where it awaits damage verification. A `PASS` on an `ECONOMIC_INJURY` application moves it directly to `DECISION_PENDING` — economic-injury files skip damage verification entirely. A `FAIL` moves the application to `DECLINED`, writing `decline_reason` `CREDIT_FAIL` when `score_pass` was false and `DTI_EXCEEDED` otherwise. Each transition increments the application's `version` by 1.

### 3.4. Damage Verification and the Dispute Cycle

For applications in `VERIFICATION`, a `LossVerifier` opens a damage verification. Opening one against an application in any other status is rejected with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_IN_VERIFICATION`, and opening one against an `ECONOMIC_INJURY` application is rejected with `ERR_VERIFICATION_NOT_APPLICABLE` (HTTP 409) — economic-injury files have no physical verification step. On a create that violates both, the purpose gate reports first: `ERR_VERIFICATION_NOT_APPLICABLE` is evaluated before `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_IN_VERIFICATION`, and the estimation-amount gate below is evaluated after both. The verifier chooses a `method` of `INSPECTION` or `ESTIMATION`; the estimation shortcut is reserved for smaller asks, so `ESTIMATION` is allowed only when the application's `requested_amount_cents` is at most 2500000 cents ($25,000), and a larger request is rejected with `ERR_ESTIMATION_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 422). The verifier also declares a `valuation_basis`: `REPLACEMENT_COST` counts the full assessed loss, while `ACTUAL_CASH_VALUE` reduces it by depreciation. The `depreciation_pct` field is required exactly when the basis is `ACTUAL_CASH_VALUE` — it must then be greater than 0 and at most 0.90 — and must be omitted for `REPLACEMENT_COST` (an explicit JSON null is treated as omitted); any violation of this pairing is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A verification is born in `REQUESTED`. Completing it — allowed from `REQUESTED` and also from `DISPUTED`, with any other status rejected as `ERR_VERIFICATION_NOT_OPEN` — is where the loss figures are recorded. The verifier supplies `assessed_loss_cents` (the gross assessed loss, zero or more) and `insurance_proceeds_cents` (insurance proceeds received or receivable by the applicant for this loss, zero or more). The system then computes `verified_loss_cents`: under `REPLACEMENT_COST` it equals `assessed_loss_cents`; under `ACTUAL_CASH_VALUE` it is the assessed loss multiplied by the quantity (1 minus `depreciation_pct`), multiplied in exact decimal and rounded half-up to integer cents — so the same assessed loss yields a strictly smaller verified loss whenever depreciation is positive. The `net_verified_loss_cents` is then the verified loss minus `insurance_proceeds_cents`, floored at zero; this is the duplication-of-benefits deduction. Completion stamps `verifier_user_id` with the acting verifier's `X-User-ID` and `completed_at` with the current logical instant, and moves the verification to `COMPLETED`. The first completion cascades: the linked application advances from `VERIFICATION` to `DECISION_PENDING` and its `version` increments. A completion that resolves a `DISPUTED` verification re-records and recomputes the figures and refreshes `completed_at`, but the linked application is already in `DECISION_PENDING` and stays there with its version unchanged. Wherever the workflows refer to an application's latest verification — the latest completed one or the latest overall — latest is by creation order.

The borrower may contest the figures: the owning `Applicant` disputes a verification, moving it from `COMPLETED` to `DISPUTED`. Dispute requires ownership (else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_OWNER`), a verification currently `COMPLETED` (else `ERR_VERIFICATION_NOT_DISPUTABLE`), a filing no later than 30 days after `completed_at` — the thirtieth day itself is admissible, and anything later is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422) — and a linked application that has not yet been `APPROVED`; once the application is approved the verification is frozen, dispute is rejected with `ERR_VERIFICATION_NOT_DISPUTABLE`, and post-approval loss changes flow through modification requests and insurance settlements instead. The post-approval freeze is evaluated before the dispute-window check, so a dispute against an approved application's verification reports `ERR_VERIFICATION_NOT_DISPUTABLE` even when the 30-day window has also closed. The dispute cycle between `DISPUTED` and `COMPLETED` has no terminal state of its own: each completion from `DISPUTED` simply supersedes the prior figures.

### 3.5. Grants and the Duplication-of-Benefits Ceiling

Outside money enters the file through the `GrantCoordinator`, who reports an external grant award on an application at any point in its lifecycle. The report names the `program_name`, an `award_amount_cents` strictly greater than zero, and a `purpose_category` of `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE` or `ECONOMIC_INJURY` that must equal the application's `loan_purpose` — a mismatched category is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Physical-damage assistance has a hard ceiling. When a `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE` grant is reported, the system totals all assistance for that purpose: the `insurance_proceeds_cents` recorded on the latest completed damage verification (zero if none exists), plus the sum of `settlement_amount_cents` over the loan's applied insurance settlements (if a loan exists), plus the sum of `award_amount_cents` over all grants on the application whose `purpose_category` is `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE` including the new one, plus the loan's `commitment_amount_cents` if the application is `APPROVED` (zero otherwise). If that assistance total is strictly greater than the `verified_loss_cents` of the latest completed verification (zero if none exists), the grant is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATION_OF_BENEFITS` (HTTP 422). An assistance total exactly equal to the verified loss is allowed — the rejection boundary is strictly greater. Grants reported for `ECONOMIC_INJURY` are always accepted, because no verified-loss ceiling exists for that purpose; economic-injury grant offsets instead apply only to this ceiling check's physical counterpart, never to the approved-amount computation.

### 3.6. Approval, Collateral, and Origination

A `LoanApprover` renders the decision on an application in `DECISION_PENDING` — any other status is rejected with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_DECISION_PENDING`. The payload carries `decision`, either `APPROVED` or `DECLINED`. A decline writes `decline_reason` `APPROVER_DECLINE` and moves the application to `DECLINED`, from which reconsideration (Section 3.2) is the only exit. An approval is considerably more involved.

Before any money is computed, a physical file must carry a settled loss figure: for a `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE` application, the latest damage verification must be in `COMPLETED` — a `DISPUTED` or otherwise open verification blocks approval with `ERR_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409). This state-family gate is evaluated before any fiscal check, so an open verification is reported as such rather than being masked by a collateral or affordability failure.

The approved amount is then computed in a pinned four-step order, each step's output feeding the next. Step one takes the statutory cap for the applicant type crossed with purpose: 50000000 cents ($500,000) for a `HOMEOWNER` physical loan, 10000000 cents ($100,000) for a `RENTER` physical loan, 200000000 cents ($2,000,000) for a `BUSINESS` physical loan, and 200000000 cents for any `ECONOMIC_INJURY` loan regardless of applicant type. Step two bounds the amount by the loss: for `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE` this bound is the `net_verified_loss_cents` of the latest completed verification minus the sum of `award_amount_cents` over all grants on the application with `purpose_category` `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE`, floored at zero; for `ECONOMIC_INJURY` the loss bound is omitted entirely. Step three is the affordability cap: the largest principal whose standard-variant monthly payment (the Section 3.3 formula at the loan's rate and term, with no deferral capitalization) keeps the borrower's verified monthly debt plus that payment, multiplied by 100, at or below 43 times the floored monthly income — computed by inverting the amortization formula and flooring the result to whole cents. The verified monthly debt in this step is taken from the application's latest `PASS`-verdict credit review; when no such review exists — possible only through reconsideration of a declined file — the applicant's self-reported `monthly_debt_cents` is used. Step four sets `approved_amount_cents` to the smallest of the requested amount, the statutory cap, the loss bound, and the affordability cap — for `ECONOMIC_INJURY`, the minimum is taken over the requested amount, the cap, and the affordability cap only.

Collateral rides on the approved figure. When the computed `approved_amount_cents` is strictly greater than the collateral threshold for the loan's purpose — 2500000 cents for `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE`, 5000000 cents for `ECONOMIC_INJURY` — the loan requires collateral, recorded as `collateral_required` true, and the approval payload must include a free-text `collateral_description` and a `collateral_value_cents` at least equal to `approved_amount_cents`; a missing field or a shortfall is rejected with `ERR_COLLATERAL_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). At or below the threshold, `collateral_required` is false: the approval payload need not include the collateral fields, and the loan record then carries `collateral_description` and `collateral_value_cents` as null.

Approval then cascades in two hops. Hop one creates the `Loan` in `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE` lifecycle status with `servicing_status` `CURRENT`: `application_id` and a denormalized `applicant_id` tie it to the file, `loan_purpose` is copied from the application, `interest_rate` and `term_months` follow Section 2.8, `deferral_months` is 12, and `commitment_amount_cents` starts equal to `approved_amount_cents`. The loan's scheduled `monthly_payment_cents` uses the deferred-capitalized variant of the payment formula: interest accrues simply over the 12 deferral months at the monthly rate and is capitalized into principal — the principal is multiplied by the quantity (1 plus the monthly rate times 12) — and that enlarged principal is then amortized over the term with the standard formula, rounding half-up to integer cents at the final step. The same principal, rate, and term therefore yield a strictly larger scheduled payment than the standard variant. All ledger counters — `disbursed_total_cents`, `outstanding_balance_cents`, `accrued_interest_balance_cents`, `late_fee_balance_cents`, `total_principal_paid_cents`, `total_interest_paid_cents`, `days_past_due` — begin at zero, with `last_accrual_date` and `next_due_date` null until the first release. Hop two spawns the disbursement schedule: tranche stage 1 is the smaller of 2500000 cents and the approved amount, and any remainder is split into stage 2 taking the floor of half the remainder and stage 3 taking the rest; a tranche whose computed amount would be zero is not created. Each tranche is born `SCHEDULED` with its `stage_number` and `amount_cents` fixed. Finally, the application's `loan_id` is set — exactly once, linking the file to the new loan — and the application's `version` increments.

### 3.7. Staged Disbursement and Repair-Milestone Verification

An approved loan is not yet live. The owning `Applicant` accepts it, moving `lifecycle_status` from `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE` to `ACTIVE` — an accept against a loan in any other lifecycle status is rejected with `ERR_LOAN_NOT_PENDING_ACCEPTANCE`, and ownership is enforced as `ERR_FORBIDDEN_OWNER`. Acceptance cascades: tranche stage 1 flips from `SCHEDULED` to `ELIGIBLE`, though its money still moves only through an explicit release.

Money moves in stages against completed repairs. A `ServicingAgent` releases a tranche, which requires the tranche to be `ELIGIBLE` — anything else, including an out-of-order release attempt or one whose verified-cost coverage is insufficient, is rejected with `ERR_TRANCHE_NOT_RELEASABLE` — and the loan's `lifecycle_status` to be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_LOAN_NOT_ACTIVE`. Eligibility is computed by walking the schedule in ascending `stage_number`: stage 1 is eligible exactly when the loan is `ACTIVE`, and each later stage becomes eligible when every earlier stage is `RELEASED` and the cumulative `verified_cost_cents` over the loan's `VERIFIED` milestones is at least the sum of the `amount_cents` of all earlier stages — equality at that boundary suffices. Eligibility is re-evaluated at every milestone verification and at every tranche release. A release moves the full `amount_cents`; partial releases do not exist. On release the tranche's `released_at` is stamped with the current instant, the loan's `disbursed_total_cents` and `outstanding_balance_cents` each increase by the released amount, and the loan's `version` increments. The first release on a loan also sets `last_accrual_date` to the release date and `next_due_date` to the release date advanced by `deferral_months` plus 1 calendar months; each later release first folds the interest accrued since `last_accrual_date` into `accrued_interest_balance_cents` and advances `last_accrual_date`; the folded interest is computed on the pre-release `outstanding_balance_cents` at the daily rate (annual rate divided by 365) over the calendar days elapsed, rounded half-up to integer cents at each fold.

Borrowers document their progress by reporting repair milestones on an `ACTIVE` loan (a report against a loan in any other lifecycle status is rejected with `ERR_LOAN_NOT_ACTIVE`). A report carries a free-text `description` and a `claimed_cost_cents` strictly greater than zero, and the milestone is born in `REPORTED`. A `LossVerifier` then decides it: the milestone must still be `REPORTED`, else `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_REPORTED`, and the deciding verifier must not be the user who completed the loan's application's latest completed damage verification, when one exists — a violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and the `REPORTED` state check is evaluated before this segregation-of-duties check. A decision of `VERIFIED` records a `verified_cost_cents` between zero and `claimed_cost_cents` inclusive — anything outside that band is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — stamps the milestone's `verifier_user_id`, and re-runs the tranche eligibility walk, flipping any tranche that has crossed its threshold from `SCHEDULED` to `ELIGIBLE`. A decision of `REJECTED` stamps the verifier and closes the milestone with no cascade. `VERIFIED` and `REJECTED` are both terminal for the milestone. The tranche list of a loan is always presented in ascending `stage_number` order.

### 3.8. Mid-Stream Changes: Modifications, Insurance Settlements, and Excess Funds

Repair costs change, and the borrower may ask to change the total approved amount on an `ACTIVE` loan (else `ERR_LOAN_NOT_ACTIVE`). The owning `Applicant` files a modification request carrying the new total `requested_amount_cents` — strictly greater than zero and different from the current `approved_amount_cents`, with violations rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a free-text `reason`, and the OCC field `expected_loan_version` per Section 2.7. The differs-from-current check is a request-well-formedness rule evaluated before the optimistic-concurrency check, which in turn is evaluated before the loan-state gate, per the global order of Section 2.6. The request is born `SUBMITTED`. Creating it bumps the loan's `version` by 1, and if a predecessor request on the same loan is still `SUBMITTED` the newer create supersedes it: the predecessor is moved to `DENIED` automatically, with no deciding officer recorded.

A `LoanApprover` decides a request that is still `SUBMITTED` (else `ERR_MODIFICATION_NOT_SUBMITTED`), recording `decided_by`. A decision of `DENIED` simply closes it. A decision of `APPROVED` first checks the new total against the same statutory cap used at origination — 50000000, 10000000, or 200000000 cents by applicant type for physical loans, 200000000 cents for economic injury — and rejects an over-cap request with `ERR_MODIFICATION_EXCEEDS_CAP` (HTTP 422). On approval the loan's `approved_amount_cents` becomes the requested amount and the delta drives the schedule. When the new amount is larger, `commitment_amount_cents` grows by the delta, a new tranche carrying exactly the delta is appended at the next `stage_number` in `SCHEDULED` status, and `monthly_payment_cents` is recomputed with the deferred-capitalized variant on the new approved amount. When the new amount is smaller, `commitment_amount_cents` becomes the smaller of its current value and the new approved amount, the clawback waterfall described below runs over the un-released tranches with total insurance held at its current value, and if `disbursed_total_cents` exceeds the new commitment an excess-funds receivable is created or topped up for the difference, and the scheduled `monthly_payment_cents` is recomputed with the deferred-capitalized variant on the new approved amount exactly as in the grow case. Either way the loan's `version` increments.

Insurance money arriving after disbursement unwinds the loan instead of enriching it. The borrower reports a settlement on an `ACTIVE` loan (else `ERR_LOAN_NOT_ACTIVE`) with a `settlement_amount_cents` strictly greater than zero and the `received_date`; the report sits in `REPORTED`. A `ServicingAgent` then applies it — the settlement must be `REPORTED`, else `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_REPORTED`, and the apply carries `expected_loan_version` per Section 2.7, with a mismatch rejected as `ERR_STALE_VERSION`; per the global order of Section 2.6 the optimistic-concurrency check is evaluated before the `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_REPORTED` state check. The apply runs the clawback computation in this exact, pinned order. First, total insurance is summed: the `insurance_proceeds_cents` of the latest completed verification (zero if none), plus every applied settlement's `settlement_amount_cents`, plus this settlement's amount. Second, the new net loss is the `verified_loss_cents` minus that total, floored at zero. Third, the new commitment is the smaller of `approved_amount_cents` and the new net loss, and the loan's `commitment_amount_cents` is set to it. Fourth, the waterfall walks the un-released tranches in descending `stage_number`: whole tranches are cancelled, largest stage first, while the sum of un-released amounts minus the tranche's amount stays at or above the target — the target being the new commitment minus `disbursed_total_cents`, floored at zero — and the tranche where that test fails is instead reduced so the sum of un-released amounts lands exactly on the target; reduced amounts never go below zero, and cancelled tranches move to `CANCELLED` from either `SCHEDULED` or `ELIGIBLE`. `CANCELLED` is terminal short of a settlement void: because stage eligibility requires every earlier stage to be `RELEASED`, a cancelled stage permanently blocks the eligibility of every later stage. Fifth, once the tranche cascade has settled, any excess is computed as `disbursed_total_cents` minus the new commitment, floored at zero. Sixth, a positive excess creates the loan's excess-funds receivable — or tops up the existing one — by that amount. The settlement records the commitment reduction as `clawback_commitment_delta_cents` and the generated excess as `excess_due_cents`, moves to `APPLIED`, and the loan's `version` increments.

An apply can be undone: a `ServicingAgent` voids a settlement that is `APPLIED` (else `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_APPLIED`). The void exactly compensates the apply cascade: `commitment_amount_cents` grows back by `clawback_commitment_delta_cents`, tranche amounts and statuses are restored from the snapshot taken at apply time — except that a tranche released since the apply is left untouched — and when `excess_due_cents` was positive the receivable's `amount_due_cents` is reduced by that amount — a receivable whose `amount_due_cents` thereby falls to its `amount_paid_cents` becomes `PAID`, and one reduced to zero due with zero paid is removed outright. The settlement moves to `VOIDED`, which is terminal, and the loan's `version` increments.

The excess-funds receivable is the borrower's obligation to return over-disbursed principal. At most one receivable per loan is open at a time — a later clawback against a loan whose receivable is `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` increases that row's `amount_due_cents` rather than creating a new row. The receivable's `due_date` is its `created_at` plus 30 days. The owning `Applicant` pays it down in payments of `amount_cents` that must be positive and no more than the remaining `amount_due_cents` minus `amount_paid_cents` — an overpayment is rejected with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, and a payment against a receivable in `PAID` or `ESCALATED` is rejected with `ERR_RECEIVABLE_NOT_OPEN` (both HTTP 422 and 409 respectively). Each payment adds to `amount_paid_cents`, reduces the loan's `outstanding_balance_cents` by the same amount (the excess was disbursed principal), adds to the loan's `total_principal_paid_cents`, and increments both the receivable's and the loan's versions. A receivable payment is not persisted as a row of its own; the endpoint responds 201 with the updated receivable in the data field. A partial payment moves the receivable from `OPEN` to `PARTIALLY_PAID`; a payment equal to the full remaining balance moves it to `PAID`. Because these payments reduce outstanding principal, a receivable payment that drives `outstanding_balance_cents` to exactly zero transitions the loan to `PAID_IN_FULL`. When the loan enters `DEFAULT`, any receivable still `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` escalates to `ESCALATED`, which is terminal.

### 3.9. Long-Run Servicing: Payments, Accommodations, Delinquency, and Charge-Off

The borrower services the loan with payments against an `ACTIVE` loan (a payment against any other lifecycle status — including `CHARGED_OFF` — is rejected with `ERR_LOAN_NOT_ACTIVE`). The `amount_cents` must be positive and no greater than the sum of `late_fee_balance_cents`, the interest due at posting, and `outstanding_balance_cents`; an overpayment is rejected with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE` (HTTP 422), while an amount exactly equal to that sum is an allowed exact payoff. Each payment is allocated by a pinned waterfall. First, the interest due is computed as `accrued_interest_balance_cents` plus the interest accrued since the last accrual: `outstanding_balance_cents` times the annual `interest_rate` times the number of calendar days since `last_accrual_date`, divided by 365, rounded half-up to integer cents — and this accrual rounding happens exactly once, here, before any allocation. Second, the payment covers late fees: `fee_portion_cents` is the smaller of the payment amount and `late_fee_balance_cents`. Third, `interest_portion_cents` is the smaller of the remainder and the interest due. Fourth, `principal_portion_cents` takes whatever remains — the remainder always lands on principal. The ledger then settles: `late_fee_balance_cents` falls by the fee portion, `accrued_interest_balance_cents` becomes the interest due minus the interest portion, `outstanding_balance_cents` falls by the principal portion, `last_accrual_date` is set to the current date, and `total_principal_paid_cents` and `total_interest_paid_cents` accumulate the respective portions. The payment is born `POSTED`. If the principal portion drives `outstanding_balance_cents` to exactly zero, the loan's `lifecycle_status` becomes `PAID_IN_FULL`, which is terminal. If the loan was `DELINQUENT` and the payment covers the late fees and accrued interest in full — repaying the missed scheduled principal is not required — the loan cures: `servicing_status` returns to `CURRENT` and `days_past_due` resets to zero.

A payment can be undone. A `ServicingAgent` reverses a payment that is `POSTED` — reversing anything else is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — moving it to `REVERSED`. The reversal compensates the allocation exactly: `outstanding_balance_cents` grows back by `principal_portion_cents`, `total_principal_paid_cents` falls by the same, `total_interest_paid_cents` falls by `interest_portion_cents`, `accrued_interest_balance_cents` grows back by the interest portion, `late_fee_balance_cents` grows back by `fee_portion_cents`, and `last_accrual_date` is restored to its pre-payment value. If the reversed payment had transitioned the loan to `PAID_IN_FULL`, the loan returns to `ACTIVE`. A reversal does not touch `servicing_status` or `days_past_due`: a loan whose `DELINQUENT` was cured by the reversed payment stays `CURRENT`. The loan's `version` increments.

Hardship accommodation runs on half-open calendar windows. The owning `Applicant` requests a servicing action on an `ACTIVE` loan with an `action_type` of `DEFERMENT` or `HARDSHIP`, a `start_date` that is the first day of the accommodation, and an `end_date` that is the first day after it — the interval includes `start_date` and excludes `end_date`, `end_date` must be strictly after `start_date`, and the span between them may not exceed 360 days; shape violations are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The request is born `REQUESTED`. A `ServicingAgent` decides it: the action must be `REQUESTED` (else `ERR_ACTION_NOT_REQUESTED`), the loan must be `ACTIVE` (else `ERR_LOAN_NOT_ACTIVE`), and two further business rules apply in order. The new window must not overlap any sibling action on the same loan that is `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE` — two half-open windows overlap when one's `start_date` falls before the other's `end_date` while its own `end_date` falls after the other's `start_date`, and mere adjacency, where one's `end_date` equals the other's `start_date`, is allowed; an overlap is rejected with `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Separately, an accommodation cannot be approved while the loan's `servicing_status` is `DEFAULT`, which is rejected with `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 409). A decision of `DENIED` closes the request; either decision records `decided_by`. An approval whose `start_date` has already been reached activates immediately within the same request: the action continues to `ACTIVE` and the loan's `servicing_status` flips to `DEFERRED` or `HARDSHIP` according to the action type. Immediate activation, like sweep activation (Section 2.4), requires the loan's `servicing_status` to be `CURRENT`; otherwise the decision leaves the action `APPROVED`. Otherwise the clock sweep activates an `APPROVED` action once the current instant reaches its `start_date`, and expires an `ACTIVE` action once the current instant reaches its `end_date`, returning the loan's `servicing_status` to `CURRENT`; both sweep transitions increment the action's and the loan's versions. Activating a `DEFERMENT` also shifts `next_due_date`, when one is set (a loan whose first release has not yet occurred has no due date to shift), forward by the length of the accommodation in days — `end_date` minus `start_date` — while a `HARDSHIP` leaves the due date untouched. When a loan enters `DEFAULT`, every servicing action still `REQUESTED` on it is denied by the system.

Delinquency is the sweep's domain, and its arithmetic is fixed. While a loan's `servicing_status` is `DEFERRED` or `HARDSHIP`, `days_past_due` stays at zero — no aging and no fees accrue. Otherwise, while `next_due_date` is null or the current date is on or before it, `days_past_due` is zero; past that, it is the count of calendar days between `next_due_date` and the current date. At 15 or more days past due, and provided no late fee has yet been assessed for that due-date cycle, a late fee of 5 percent (0.05) of `monthly_payment_cents`, rounded half-up to integer cents, is added to `late_fee_balance_cents` — at most one fee per due-date cycle, the cycle being keyed to the value of `next_due_date`: a deferment that shifts `next_due_date` begins a new cycle, so a fee may be assessed against the shifted date even if one was assessed before the shift. Status boundaries are inclusive at every rung: at 30 or more days past due a `CURRENT` loan becomes `DELINQUENT`; at 120 or more a `DELINQUENT` loan becomes `DEFAULT`; at 180 or more a loan in `DEFAULT` is charged off, moving `lifecycle_status` to `CHARGED_OFF`, which is terminal and accepts no further payments (`ERR_LOAN_NOT_ACTIVE`). A single sweep applies the full cascade — fees first, then the furthest applicable status — and the `DEFAULT` transition carries two cascades of its own: any `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` excess-funds receivable on the loan becomes `ESCALATED`, and every `REQUESTED` servicing action on the loan becomes `DENIED`. A sweep that crosses status rungs increments the loan's `version` by one per rung crossed, with the fee assessment and the `days_past_due` update riding along; a sweep that changes only `days_past_due` and/or the late-fee balance without crossing a rung increments `version` once.
