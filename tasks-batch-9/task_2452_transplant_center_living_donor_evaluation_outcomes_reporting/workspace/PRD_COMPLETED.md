# Transplant Living Donor Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A living-donor transplant program carries two obligations that pull in opposite directions. It must move quickly enough that a matched kidney or liver reaches a recipient while the organ, the donor and the operating theatre are all simultaneously available; and it must move slowly enough that no donor is ever hurried past an independent advocate, a mandatory cooling-off period, a medical workup and a psychosocial workup. Today those obligations are reconciled by hand — a coordinator holds the chain topology in a spreadsheet, an advocate keeps consent dates in a notebook, a quality officer reconciles one-year survival against expectation after the fact, and a finance office reimburses donor costs from three different pockets without a shared ledger. The result is that the center only discovers it has a problem when a regulator asks, and by then the paired exchange has already been executed.

This document specifies the API for a system that makes those obligations mechanical. Every recipient candidacy, every donor referral, every consent disclosure, every exclusion finding, every organ offer, every operating-room reservation, every follow-up submission and every dollar of donor cost reimbursement is a persisted resource with a derived state, and the system — not a human — decides whether the next step is permitted. The center's own outcome record feeds back into that decision: a program whose one-year graft survival falls short of its own expectation, or whose donor follow-up compliance drops below its own threshold, or whose cold-ischemia discipline has slipped twice, is progressively restricted from referring new donors and from committing large exchange chains until it has filed and had accepted an improvement plan.

Three design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **segregation of duties is structural, not advisory**: the coordinator who refers a donor cannot advocate for that donor, the reviewer who assesses a donor's medicine cannot also assess that donor's psychosocial fitness, the quality officer who files an improvement plan cannot be the administrator who accepts it, and the coordinator who files a donor cost claim cannot be the finance administrator who adjudicates it. Second, **compensation is exact**: when a chain breaks, every donor it locked is released, every operating-room minute it reserved is returned, and every aggregate it moved is moved back — no residue. Third, **time is a server-controlled resource**: all clock-relative rules (cooling-off elapse, offer response windows, follow-up due dates, waitlist interval arithmetic) are evaluated against a logical UTC clock that tests advance explicitly, never against wall-clock time.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies to every endpoint unless a later section narrows it. Where §3 describes a workflow, the contract rules here still hold.

### 2.1. Authentication, Center Personnel, and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. If the header names a role that is not one of the six declared roles, or names a role that the target endpoint does not authorize, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `TransplantCoordinator`, `DonorAdvocate`, `EvaluationReviewer`, `QualityOfficer` and `FinanceAdmin`.

Four of the six are **entity-backed**: the identifier in `X-User-ID` must resolve to a persisted `StaffMember` row.

- When `X-User-Role` is `TransplantCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StaffMember`, and that staff member must have `staff_role` equal to `TRANSPLANT_COORDINATOR` with `is_active` true. Ownership and provenance checks on any resource that references a staff member compare that resource's staff foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — for example, a donor referral records `referred_by_staff_id` from the header, and a first candidacy approval records `candidacy_approved_by_staff_id` from the header.
- When `X-User-Role` is `DonorAdvocate`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StaffMember`, whose `staff_role` must be `LIVING_DONOR_ADVOCATE` with `is_active` true. In addition, for any operation on a donor candidate the header value must equal that donor's `advocate_staff_id`; if it names a different staff member the request fails with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_ADVOCATE` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `EvaluationReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StaffMember`, whose `staff_role` must be `EVALUATION_REVIEWER` with `is_active` true. For any operation on an existing evaluation the header value must equal that evaluation's `reviewer_staff_id`; if it names a different staff member the request fails with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_REVIEWER` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `QualityOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StaffMember`, whose `staff_role` must be `QUALITY_OFFICER` with `is_active` true.

The remaining two roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as the `actor_user_id` of a waitlist event that a `ProgramAdmin` drove) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `FinanceAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier; it is recorded as `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a claim the finance administrator decides, and is not a foreign key.

A `StaffMember` row therefore has two purposes: it is the credential store that gates which principal may act in an entity-backed role, and it is the person referenced by clinical assignments. Its `staff_role` is one of `TRANSPLANT_COORDINATOR`, `LIVING_DONOR_ADVOCATE`, `EVALUATION_REVIEWER`, `QUALITY_OFFICER` or `SURGEON`, and `is_active` marks whether the person may be referenced by new assignments at all. Note that `SURGEON` is a credentialed duty without a matching API role: a surgeon is booked into theatre by a coordinator and never authenticates.

Read endpoints are open to all six roles. Write endpoints are narrowly scoped: `ProgramAdmin` alone configures programs, staff, rooms, advocate assignments, follow-up waivers, improvement-plan decisions and the logical clock; `TransplantCoordinator` alone drives candidacy, donor referral, chain construction, offers, theatre bookings, follow-up submissions and cost-claim filing; `DonorAdvocate` alone runs consent disclosure and the advocacy determination; `EvaluationReviewer` alone submits, determines and resubmits donor evaluations; `QualityOfficer` alone creates and finalizes outcome reviews and files improvement plans; `FinanceAdmin` alone adjudicates and reverses cost claims. Compliance sweeps are shared between `QualityOfficer` and `ProgramAdmin`. The prohibitions are as load-bearing as the permissions: `ProgramAdmin` cannot file the improvement plan it decides on, `QualityOfficer` cannot decide the plan it filed, `TransplantCoordinator` cannot adjudicate the claim it filed, and `FinanceAdmin` cannot touch any clinical state.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by sixteen lowercase hexadecimal characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.4.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range from 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a subsection states otherwise, with rows sharing a `created_at` — which is the common case, because the logical clock only moves when a caller advances it — broken by ascending creation order. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource exposes an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits. Almost every resource additionally exposes an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and an integer `version` that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation that changes the row. `updated_at` is refreshed on the same mutations that advance `version`. A single request advances a given row's `version` by at most 1 however many separate writes or cascade hops inside that request reach it, and a re-derivation that leaves the row's stored values exactly as they were does not advance it at all.

The one exception is the waitlist status event. A waitlist event is an immutable audit record: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because it is appended and never altered.

Time is a server resource. The logical UTC clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` before any test-driving call adjusts it, and it never falls back to wall-clock time. An administrative capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant; the clock may only move forward, and an attempt to set it to an earlier instant than its current reading is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422), while setting it to exactly its current reading is accepted. That capability answers `200 OK` with an empty `data` object rather than a resource. Setting the clock mutates no entity and sweeps no delinquency — a separate compliance sweep does that work explicitly. Every clock-relative rule in this specification — cooling-off elapse, offer response deadlines, follow-up due dates, waitlist interval arithmetic, delinquency grace — is evaluated against the logical clock at the moment the request is handled.

### 2.5. Nested Path Resolution and Single-Program Containment

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, in this order: program, then staff member, then recipient, then donor, then evaluation, then chain, then segment, then room, then booking, then follow-up, then review, then plan, then claim. The first identifier that resolves to nothing determines the error, so a request naming a real program and a fictional chain reports the chain failure and not the other way round. The thirteen not-found codes are `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECIPIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DONOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHAIN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, each returned with HTTP 404.

The same codes cover foreign keys supplied in a request body rather than in the path. Every reference — `program_id`, `intended_recipient_id`, `referred_by_staff_id`, `advocate_staff_id`, `donor_candidate_id`, `recipient_candidate_id`, `reviewer_staff_id`, `exchange_chain_id`, `predecessor_segment_id`, `operating_room_id`, `chain_segment_id`, `surgeon_staff_id` and `program_outcome_review_id` — must resolve to an existing row, and the failure reports the not-found code of the entity type that reference points at.

Once every reference resolves, they must all belong to the same program. A single request may not mix a staff member, recipient, donor, chain, segment, room, review or claim drawn from two different transplant programs; such a request is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_PROGRAM_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422). The check runs on nine write endpoints — donor referral, advocate assignment, evaluation creation, chain creation, segment insertion, booking creation, outcome-review creation, improvement-plan creation and cost-claim creation — and on all of them except advocate assignment the acting staff member's own `program_id` joins the compared set, so the comparison is meaningful even when the body carries a single reference. Recipient-candidate creation, staff-member creation and operating-room registration compare nothing, because a program is the only reference they carry.

### 2.6. Request Shape, Numeric Precision, and Rounding

Request-shape validation, reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400), covers a fixed list of conditions: every input field that has no default must be present; every enum-typed field must hold a declared member; integers and numbers must be of the right type and inside their declared range; cents-denominated fields must be non-negative integers; datetimes must be ISO 8601 UTC; `end_at` must be later than `start_at`, `period_end_on` must not precede `period_start_on`, and `service_to_on` must not precede `service_from_on`; `lost_wage_days` must be present and at least 1 exactly when `claim_type` is `LOST_WAGES` and must be absent otherwise; a reason code that a transition requires must be present — this covers `objection_reason_code`, `decline_reason_code`, `break_reason_code`, `waiver_reason_code`, `decision_reason_code` and `reversal_reason_code`; `limit` must lie in the inclusive range 1 to 100 and `offset` must be at least 0; and `observed_survivor_count` must not exceed `cohort_size`.

Rounding is **half up** everywhere a decimal result is produced. Unless a specific rule says otherwise, monetary-adjacent and score-style derived numbers are rounded half up to **2 decimal places**. Two rules deviate deliberately and are called out where they live: the observed-to-expected survival ratio is rounded half up to **4 decimal places**, and the waitlist priority score rounds each of its components half up to 2 decimal places *before* summing them and does not round the sum again.

Money is integer cents throughout. No reimbursement figure is ever rounded; the only non-integer operation in the money path is a ceiling applied to a week count. Cold-ischemia durations are whole minutes **truncated toward zero**, so a fifty-nine-second remainder is discarded rather than rounded up. Whole-day accumulations over waitlist intervals apply their floor once, to the summed total, never per interval. Comparisons on submitted clinical measurements use the exact decimal values as submitted.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations reach into a parent resource whose shape the client must already have observed, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency on that parent's `version`.

Inserting a segment into a chain — `POST /exchange-chains/{id}/segments` — requires a non-optional `expected_chain_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ExchangeChain`. A segment insertion is only meaningful against a known topology: which positions are taken, which donors and recipients already participate. If the value does not match the chain's current `version`, the insertion is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CHAIN_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because the writer validated its position and predecessor against a topology that no longer exists. A successful insertion increments the chain's `version`, which makes any other holder of the previous value stale.

Creating an improvement plan — `POST /improvement-plans` — requires a non-optional `expected_review_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ProgramOutcomeReview`. A new plan legitimately supersedes any `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` plan of the same review, so nothing in the review's own state blocks a second creation; the stale-version check is precisely what prevents a writer from superseding a plan it never saw. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REVIEW_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful creation increments the review's `version`.

Both checks run **before** any state or business rule on the request, so a client holding an out-of-date view learns that fact rather than being told about a state it may never have observed. No other endpoint accepts a version field of any kind.

### 2.8. Compliance Posture: the Program-Wide Gate

Each program carries a derived `compliance_posture` of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. It is never supplied by a caller. It is re-derived, inside the same request, whenever anything that feeds it changes: an outcome review is finalized, an improvement plan is decided or closed, a compliance sweep runs, a follow-up is submitted or waived, or a transplant is completed.

The derivation asks two questions first. A review counts as an **unanswered flagged review** when it belongs to this program, its `status` is `FINALIZED`, its `flag_level` is `FLAGGED`, and no improvement plan of that review has reached `ACCEPTED` or `CLOSED`. A review counts as an **open watch** when it belongs to this program, its `status` is `FINALIZED`, its `flag_level` is `WATCH`, and no improvement plan of that review has reached `CLOSED`. Then: if an unanswered flagged review exists, the posture is `FROZEN`. Otherwise, if `follow_up_compliance_pct` is strictly below the program's `follow_up_compliance_threshold_pct`, or an open watch exists, or `cit_breach_count` is at least 2, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. The compliance comparison is strict: a compliance figure of exactly 75.00 against a threshold of 75 keeps the program `OPEN`. A `cit_breach_count` of 1 keeps the program `OPEN`; 2 degrades it to `RESTRICTED`. `FROZEN` always outranks `RESTRICTED`.

Posture is a gate over child operations. While a program is `FROZEN`, referring a new donor candidate and committing an exchange chain are both refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). While a program is `RESTRICTED`, committing a chain whose `segment_count` is greater than 2 is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409), while two-segment chains remain committable.

Posture changes cascade. Entering `FROZEN` — whether from `OPEN` or from `RESTRICTED` — moves every exchange chain of that program whose status is `DRAFT` or `VALIDATED` to `SUSPENDED`, which in turn reverts each of those chains' `OFFER_SENT` segments to `PLANNED` with their offer and planning fields cleared, and cancels every `ACTIVE` procedure booking belonging to *any* segment of those chains with a `cancel_reason_code` of `POSTURE_FROZEN`, decrementing each affected room's `reserved_minutes_total` accordingly. Leaving `FROZEN` — to `RESTRICTED` when a degrading condition still holds, or to `OPEN` when none does — restores every `SUSPENDED` chain of that program to `VALIDATED` without re-running validation. Moving between `OPEN` and `RESTRICTED` in either direction changes no chain. Every posture change increments the program's `version` by 1; a re-derivation that lands on the posture already stored writes nothing and leaves the program's `version` and `updated_at` alone. Re-derivation is triggered only by the events listed above, so advancing the logical clock alone re-derives nothing: a program may read `follow_up_compliance_pct` below its threshold while its `compliance_posture` still reads `OPEN` until a sweep or another feeding event runs.

The transition out of `FROZEN` requires that every finalized `FLAGGED` review of the program now has a plan in `ACCEPTED` or `CLOSED`. It lands on `OPEN` only when, in addition, no finalized `WATCH` review is still open, `follow_up_compliance_pct` is at least the threshold, and `cit_breach_count` is below 2; otherwise it lands on `RESTRICTED`. Symmetrically, returning from `RESTRICTED` to `OPEN` requires `follow_up_compliance_pct` at or above the threshold, `cit_breach_count` below 2, and no finalized review at either `WATCH` or `FLAGGED` still lacking its answering plan — with the asymmetry that a `WATCH` review is only cleared by a plan that has reached `CLOSED`, whereas a `FLAGGED` review is cleared by a plan at `ACCEPTED` or `CLOSED`.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Conservation Invariants

Cascades in this system are multi-hop and they complete inside the request that triggered them. When a donor is excluded, the donor's own status changes, its live segments are withdrawn, the chains those segments belonged to break, and the bookings those chains held are cancelled — all before the response is written. Every entity a cascade touches has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed, exactly as if it had been mutated directly — once, however many hops of the same cascade reach the same row.

Three invariants must hold after every request, and they are the practical test of whether a cascade was implemented completely.

**The donor-fund ledger.** A program's `donor_fund_committed_cents` is the sum of `fund_part_cents` over every donor cost claim whose donor belongs to that program and whose `status` is `ADJUDICATED`. Its `donor_fund_remaining_cents` is `donor_fund_pool_cents` minus `donor_fund_committed_cents`. Committed plus remaining always equals the pool, and remaining is always between 0 and the pool inclusive, after every adjudication, denial and reversal. A reversed claim leaves the sum entirely, restoring the pool exactly with no residue; a denied claim never entered the sum in the first place. Both figures are derived from the claim rows at read time and are not stored on the program, so an adjudication, denial or reversal mutates only the claim: the program row is not written and its `version` does not move.

**Chain commitment conservation.** A chain's `committed_donor_count` is the number of distinct `donor_candidate_id` values across that chain's segments whose status is not `WITHDRAWN` and whose own `created_at` is at or before the chain's `committed_at`, evaluated only while the chain's status is `COMMITTED` or `EXECUTING`; for every other chain status it is 0, including for a chain that has reached `COMPLETED`. Summed over all chains of a program, `committed_donor_count` must equal the number of that program's donor candidates whose `overall_status` is `CHAIN_COMMITTED`. Breaking a chain must therefore release every donor it locked and zero its `committed_donor_count` in the same request, so the two sides never disagree mid-flight.

**Room minute conservation.** A room's `reserved_minutes_total` is the sum of `duration_minutes` over that room's procedure bookings whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED`. A booking that moves to `CANCELLED` must have been subtracted exactly once — never twice, never zero times — whether the cancellation came from a caller or from a cascade. A booking that moves to `COMPLETED` leaves the total unchanged, because completed bookings stay in the ledger.

### 2.10. Validation Ordering and the First-Violation Rule

Validation rules are evaluated strictly in ascending priority order, and the **first** rule violated determines the response. A request that breaks several rules always reports the lowest-numbered one. The order is:

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty — `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER`, 401.
2. `X-User-Role` names a declared role that the endpoint authorizes, and for an entity-backed role `X-User-ID` is an active staff member with the matching `staff_role` — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403.
3. Every identifier in the path exists, resolved left to right — the matching not-found code, 404.
4. Request shape, as specified in §2.6 — `ERR_VALIDATION`, 400.
5. Every foreign key supplied in the request body exists — the matching not-found code, 404.
6. `expected_chain_version` matches the chain's `version` on segment insertion, and `expected_review_version` matches the review's `version` on improvement-plan creation — `ERR_STALE_CHAIN_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_REVIEW_VERSION`, 412.
7. Resource-scoped actor binding: `X-User-ID` equals the donor's `advocate_staff_id` for consent disclosure and the advocacy determination, and equals the evaluation's `reviewer_staff_id` for an evaluation determination and resubmission — `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_ADVOCATE` or `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_REVIEWER`, 403.
8. The requested transition exists for the target's current state, and no transition at all is accepted out of a terminal state — `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, 409. This priority also carries the bars that read a companion field of the request's own target rather than a status name: the donor `overall_status` bars on advocate assignment, consent disclosure and evaluation resubmission, the bar on deferring an already-cleared dimension of a `CHAIN_COMMITTED` donor, and the bar on cancelling a booking whose chain segment has reached `TRANSPLANTED` — all reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.
9. Companion-entity state gates about the request's own target — `ERR_CHAIN_NOT_COMMITTED`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FINALIZED`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_ADJUDICATED`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_ACTIVE` or `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_TERMINAL`, 409.
10. Role-type fitness of a referenced staff member — `ERR_STAFF_ROLE_INVALID`, 422.
11. Segregation of duties — `ERR_ADVOCATE_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST` or `ERR_REVIEWER_SOD_CONFLICT`, 422.
12. Informed-consent safety — `ERR_COOLING_OFF_NOT_ELAPSED`, `ERR_CLEARANCE_INCOMPLETE` or `ERR_HARD_EXCLUSION_PRESENT`, 422.
13. Program compliance posture gate — `ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN` or `ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED`, 409.
14. Participant eligibility — `ERR_DONOR_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_RECIPIENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_DONOR_NOT_COMMITTED` or `ERR_DONOR_NOT_DONATED`, 422.
15. Single-program containment — `ERR_CROSS_PROGRAM_REFERENCE`, 422.
16. Chain topology — `ERR_CHAIN_TOO_SHORT`, `ERR_CHAIN_POSITIONS_NOT_CONTIGUOUS`, `ERR_CHAIN_LINKAGE_BROKEN`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PARTICIPANT`, `ERR_ABO_INCOMPATIBLE`, `ERR_LOOP_NOT_CLOSED` or `ERR_NDD_START_INVALID`, 422.
17. Uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_CENTER_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_NPI`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROOM_CODE`, `ERR_POSITION_TAKEN`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_EVALUATION` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_REVIEW_PERIOD`, 409.
18. Operating-room occupancy with turnover buffer — `ERR_OR_SLOT_CONFLICT`, 409.
19. Surgeon availability — `ERR_SURGEON_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, 409.
20. Cold-ischemia planning prerequisites — `ERR_DONOR_BOOKING_MISSING`, `ERR_IMPLANT_BOOKING_MISSING` or `ERR_PREDECESSOR_TIMING_INVALID`, 422. Within this priority the predecessor-timing comparison is made before the two booking-existence checks, so a segment whose predecessor timing is impossible reports `ERR_PREDECESSOR_TIMING_INVALID` even when its own bookings are missing.
21. Clock-relative windows — `ERR_OFFER_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_NOT_DUE`, `ERR_EFFECTIVE_AT_OUT_OF_ORDER` or `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`, 422.
22. Aggregate ischemia budgets — `ERR_CIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` then `ERR_CHAIN_CIT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, 422.
23. Improvement plan admissibility — `ERR_PLAN_NOT_REQUIRED`, 422.
24. Donor lifetime reimbursement headroom — `ERR_DONOR_LIFETIME_CAP_REACHED`, 422.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis. First, the terminal-state rule at priority 8 is **exempted** for four operations, because priority 9 declares a domain-specific code for exactly the same question about exactly the same target, and the specific code must win: reversing a donor cost claim reports `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_ADJUDICATED`, cancelling a procedure booking reports `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_ACTIVE`, and both submitting and waiving a donor follow-up report `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_TERMINAL`. Second, within priority 22 the per-segment ceiling is checked before the chain-wide budget, so a single over-long segment reports its own error even when the chain budget is also blown.

Applied scopes are exact. The rule at priority 7 guards four operations: consent disclosure, the advocacy determination, an evaluation determination and an evaluation resubmission. Priority 9 guards seven: segment offer, transplant completion, improvement-plan transitions, claim reversal, booking cancellation, follow-up submission and follow-up waiver. Priority 10 guards three: advocate assignment, evaluation creation and booking creation. Priority 11 guards two: advocate assignment and evaluation creation. Priority 12 guards two: the advocacy determination and the evaluation determination. Priority 13 guards two: donor referral and chain commitment. Priority 14 guards five: chain validation, chain commitment, segment offer, cost-claim creation and cost-claim adjudication. Priority 16 guards one: chain validation, where the whole topology is judged once as a set so that segment insertion remains free to build an intermediate shape that is not yet valid. Priority 17 guards six creation or finalization operations. Priorities 18 and 19 guard booking creation. Priority 20 guards segment offer and transplant completion. Priority 21 guards offer response, follow-up submission, waitlist status update and the clock-setting capability. Priority 22 guards segment offer. Priority 23 guards improvement-plan creation. Priority 24 guards cost-claim adjudication.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Program: Policy, People, and Theatres

A transplant program is the root of everything else and it is where the center's own policy thresholds are written down. Registering one requires a display `name` and a `center_code`; the code must be unique across all programs, and a collision is reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CENTER_CODE`. Eight further inputs are optional and each has a default, so a center can accept the house policy and adjust later: `organ_type` selects which organ program the center runs and is either `KIDNEY` or `LIVER`, defaulting to `KIDNEY`; `cooling_off_days` is the mandatory number of days a donor must be left between a consent disclosure and the advocate's attestation and defaults to 7, and it must be at least 1; `follow_up_completeness_threshold_pct` is the per-submission completeness percentage at or above which a follow-up interval counts as met and defaults to 80; `follow_up_compliance_threshold_pct` is the program-wide follow-up percentage below which the posture degrades and defaults to 75; both percentages must be greater than 0 and at most 100. `expected_one_year_survival_rate` is the expected one-year graft survival rate that outcome reviews measure against, defaults to 0.95, and must be greater than 0 and at most 1. `donor_fund_pool_cents` is the total program money available to reimburse donor costs, defaults to 5,000,000 cents (fifty thousand dollars) and may not be negative. `lost_wage_daily_cap_cents` defaults to 30,000 cents (three hundred dollars a day) and `lost_wage_weekly_cap_cents` defaults to 150,000 cents (fifteen hundred dollars a week); both bound lost-wage claims.

Registration also fixes the program's starting derived state: `compliance_posture` begins at `OPEN`, `follow_up_compliance_pct` begins at 100 because no follow-up interval is due yet, `cit_breach_count` begins at 0, `donor_fund_committed_cents` begins at 0 and `donor_fund_remaining_cents` begins equal to `donor_fund_pool_cents`. The `last_sweep_at` field is null until the first compliance sweep runs.

Adding a person to the center requires the owning `program_id`, a `full_name`, a `staff_role` drawn from the five credentialed duties, and an `npi` national provider identifier; `is_active` is optional and defaults to true. An `npi` must be unique within its program, and a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_NPI`. Registering a theatre requires `program_id` and a `room_code` that is unique within the program — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROOM_CODE` — plus an optional `turnover_buffer_minutes` that defaults to 45 and may not be negative; the room's `reserved_minutes_total` starts at 0.

One administrative operation exists purely to let the passage of time register on the program's books. A compliance sweep, available to both `QualityOfficer` and `ProgramAdmin` principals, does four things in order: it moves every donor follow-up of the program that is still `DUE` or `INCOMPLETE` and whose grace period has run out to `DELINQUENT`; it recomputes `follow_up_compliance_pct` and `cit_breach_count`; it re-derives `compliance_posture` with the chain suspend-and-restore cascade described in §2.8; and it records the current logical clock in `last_sweep_at`, incrementing the program's `version`. The grace period is thirty days past an interval's `due_on`: the sweep only declares an interval delinquent once the logical clock has reached or passed that thirtieth day. Note that `cit_breach_count` is a count of this program's chain segments whose `cit_breach` flag is true.

Reading back is unrestricted by role. A program read exposes its derived fund, compliance and posture figures. Two program-scoped list capabilities are the only way to discover child rows by their owning program: the staff-member listing, which additionally filters on `staff_role` and `is_active`, and the operating-room listing, which exposes each room's `reserved_minutes_total`. Both accept `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.2. Recipient Candidacy and the Waitlist Ledger

A coordinator opens a recipient workup by supplying the owning `program_id`, the patient's `mrn`, an `abo_blood_type` of `O`, `A`, `B` or `AB`, an `age_years` value of at least 0, and a `cpra` calculated panel reactive antibody fraction between 0 and 1 inclusive. Two further inputs are optional: `dialysis_years`, the years the patient has spent on dialysis, defaults to 0, and `medical_urgency_points`, the committee-assigned urgency points, defaults to 0 and is a plain integer with no declared bound of its own. The new candidate's `listing_status` starts at `WORKUP`, `active_days_total` at 0, `offer_decline_count` at 0, and `waitlist_priority_score` is computed immediately — with zero accumulated waiting time, since the candidate has never been active.

Everything that happens to a candidacy afterwards flows through a single status-update operation that takes three inputs: the desired `to_status`, a free-form `reason_code`, and an `effective_at` instant at which the change takes effect. The `to_status` enum admits the six listing statuses other than `WORKUP`, which is a starting state no update may return to: a `to_status` of `WORKUP` is a request-shape failure at priority 4 and reports `ERR_VALIDATION`, whereas `TRANSPLANTED` is a declared member that passes shape and is then refused at priority 8 because only the system may drive that move. The operation both drives the candidate's `listing_status` and appends an immutable waitlist event recording the `recipient_candidate_id`, the `from_status` the candidate held, the `to_status` it moved to, the supplied `reason_code` and `effective_at`, and an `actor_user_id` taken from `X-User-ID`. That last field is polymorphic and is not a strict foreign key: for a `TransplantCoordinator` it is a staff member's identifier, and for a `ProgramAdmin` it is an opaque operator identifier. Waitlist events are listed for a candidate in `effective_at` order.

The `effective_at` value is fenced on both sides. It must be at or after the `effective_at` of the candidate's most recent waitlist event — at the `WORKUP` starting point no event exists, so only the upper bound applies — and it must be at or before the current logical clock. A value that precedes the latest event or lies in the clock's future is rejected with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_AT_OUT_OF_ORDER`.

The permitted moves are these. From `WORKUP` a candidate may go to `APPROVED_NOT_LISTED`, which additionally stamps `candidacy_approved_by_staff_id` with the approving coordinator's `X-User-ID`, or to `DECLINED`, which is terminal. From `APPROVED_NOT_LISTED` the candidate may be registered on the waitlist by moving to `ACTIVE`; the first time this happens the candidate's `registered_at` is set to the supplied `effective_at`, and later re-entries into `ACTIVE` leave `registered_at` alone. From `ACTIVE` the candidate may move to `INACTIVE_HOLD`, to `REMOVED`, or — driven by the system rather than a caller — to `TRANSPLANTED`. From `INACTIVE_HOLD` the candidate may return to `ACTIVE` or move to `REMOVED`; the `ACTIVE`-to-`INACTIVE_HOLD`-to-`ACTIVE` cycle may repeat any number of times. `DECLINED`, `TRANSPLANTED` and `REMOVED` are terminal and accept no further update.

Every one of these moves recomputes `active_days_total` and `waitlist_priority_score` and increments the candidate's `version` by 1. Two of them carry cascades. Placing an active candidate on hold reverts each of that candidate's chain segments that currently sits at `OFFER_SENT` back to `PLANNED`, clearing that segment's `planned_cit_minutes`, `planned_cross_clamp_at`, `planned_reperfusion_at`, `planned_transport_minutes`, `offer_sent_at` and `offer_response_deadline_at` to null — an offer cannot stand for a recipient who is not currently listed. Removing a candidate, from either `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE_HOLD`, cascades in two hops: first, every chain segment of that recipient whose status is `PLANNED`, `OFFER_SENT` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`; second, because those segments have been withdrawn, each parent chain whose status is `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED`, `SUSPENDED`, `COMMITTED` or `EXECUTING` moves to `BROKEN` with a `break_reason_code` of `RECIPIENT_REMOVED`, dragging its own compensating cascade behind it — the break of §3.6, which is not selective: it also moves that chain's other remaining segments whose status is `PLANNED`, `OFFER_SENT` or `ACCEPTED` to `WITHDRAWN`, including segments naming a different recipient, releases the chain's committed donors, zeroes `committed_donor_count` and cancels the chain's `ACTIVE` bookings. The system-driven move to `TRANSPLANTED` fires when a chain segment naming this candidate is completed; it appends a waitlist event whose `reason_code` is `TRANSPLANT_COMPLETED`, whose `effective_at` is the current logical clock, and whose `actor_user_id` is the `X-User-ID` of the coordinator who recorded the completion.

**Accumulated active time.** `active_days_total` counts whole days the candidate has spent listed. It is derived by replaying the candidate's waitlist events ordered by `effective_at` ascending and, for ties, `created_at` ascending. An active interval opens at every event whose `to_status` is `ACTIVE` and closes at the next event whose `from_status` is `ACTIVE`; an interval still open when the figure is evaluated closes at the current logical clock. Those half-open windows are then merged — overlapping or exactly touching windows become one, and a window that opens and closes at the same instant contributes nothing. The seconds of every merged window are summed, and the whole-day floor is applied once to that total. Applying the floor once rather than per interval matters: two separate twelve-hour intervals sum to a full day and count as 1, where per-interval flooring would have counted 0. A candidate for whom no `ACTIVE` event was ever recorded has `active_days_total` of 0.

**Allocation priority.** `waitlist_priority_score` is the sum of five components, each of which is rounded half up to two decimal places *before* the addition, with the sum itself left unrounded. The urgency component is `medical_urgency_points` multiplied by 2. The waiting component is `active_days_total` multiplied by 0.007, so a full year of listed time contributes 2.56 points, and the small weight is deliberate so that waiting never overwhelms urgency. The dialysis component is `dialysis_years` multiplied by 1.005. The sensitization component is a tier lookup on `cpra`, inclusive at each lower bound: 10 when `cpra` is at least 0.98, otherwise 4 when it is at least 0.80, otherwise 1 when it is at least 0.20, otherwise 0. The pediatric component is 3 when `age_years` is below 18 and 0 otherwise, so a candidate who is exactly 18 earns no bonus. Because each component is rounded first, component-then-sum and sum-then-round can disagree in the second decimal place, and the component-first reading is the correct one.

`offer_decline_count` on a candidate is the number of that candidate's chain segments currently sitting at `DECLINED`. Candidates are listed with optional `program_id`, `listing_status` and `candidacy_approved_by_staff_id` filters plus `limit` and `offset`, and read individually to expose `active_days_total`, `waitlist_priority_score` and `offer_decline_count`.

### 3.3. Donor Referral, Independent Advocacy, and the Cooling-Off Window

Referring a living donor requires the owning `program_id`, the donor's `mrn`, an `abo_blood_type` of `O`, `A`, `B` or `AB`, and an `age_years` value of at least 0. The `intended_recipient_id` field is optional and it is the single most consequential choice in the referral: supplying a recipient makes this a **directed** donation, while leaving it null makes the donor **non-directed**, which lengthens the cooling-off window, surcharges the final risk index, and is the only way to start a non-directed donor chain. The referral records `referred_by_staff_id` from the referring coordinator's `X-User-ID` for provenance. A new donor starts with `overall_status` `REFERRED`, `advocacy_status` `UNASSIGNED`, both `medical_status` and `psychosocial_status` at `PENDING`, `disclosure_sequence` 0, `deferral_count` 0, `medical_risk_points` and `psychosocial_risk_points` 0, and `screening_risk_index` 0; `advocate_staff_id`, `disclosure_at`, `cooling_off_ends_at`, `attestation_at`, `objection_reason_code`, `final_risk_index` and `donated_at` are all null. Referral is blocked with `ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN` while the program's posture is `FROZEN`.

A donor candidate carries **three independent state dimensions** plus a fourth compound one. The advocacy dimension tracks the independent living donor advocate's progress; the medical and psychosocial dimensions mirror the two clinical workups; and `overall_status` is derived from all three plus the donor's chain participation. The three input dimensions advance independently — a donor may be `ATTESTED` while medically `PENDING`, or medically `CLEARED` while advocacy is still `UNASSIGNED` — and none of them blocks another.

**Assigning the advocate.** A `ProgramAdmin` assigns the advocate by supplying an `advocate_staff_id`, moving `advocacy_status` from `UNASSIGNED` to `ASSIGNED` and recording the staff member on the donor. Assignment is one-shot: because `UNASSIGNED` is the only accepted starting point, a second assignment on a donor whose `advocacy_status` has already left it — including an attempt to swap advocates — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Three further conditions must hold. The named staff member must exist, have `is_active` true, have `staff_role` equal to `LIVING_DONOR_ADVOCATE`, and belong to the same program as the donor; a wrong duty or an inactive person is `ERR_STAFF_ROLE_INVALID`. When the donor has an `intended_recipient_id`, the proposed advocate must not already be the `advocate_staff_id` of a *different* donor candidate pointing at that same intended recipient, because one advocate cannot independently represent two donors competing for the same recipient; a violation is `ERR_ADVOCATE_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`. And the donor's `overall_status` must not be `EXCLUDED`, `ADVOCATE_BLOCKED` or `DONATED`. A successful assignment increments the donor's `version` by 1.

**The consent disclosure.** Only the assigned advocate may record an informed-consent disclosure session, and the operation takes no body fields at all. It advances `disclosure_sequence` by 1, sets `disclosure_at` to the current logical clock, and re-derives `cooling_off_ends_at`. The donor's `overall_status` must not be `EXCLUDED`, `ADVOCATE_BLOCKED`, `CHAIN_COMMITTED` or `DONATED`. Disclosure is reachable from `ASSIGNED` — the first disclosure — and also from `ATTESTED`, which is the re-consent path taken after a material change in the donor's circumstances. Re-consent is the more interesting case: besides advancing the sequence and the window, it clears `attestation_at` to null, clears `final_risk_index` to null, and re-derives `overall_status` down to `EVAL_IN_PROGRESS`. A donor who is already `CHAIN_COMMITTED` may not be re-disclosed at all; re-disclosure is only available while the donor's `overall_status` is `APPROVED`, `CONDITIONAL_APPROVED` or `EVAL_IN_PROGRESS`. Each pass around the attest-and-re-disclose cycle recomputes the window from scratch.

**How long the donor must wait.** `cooling_off_ends_at` is computed from three things: the program's `cooling_off_days`, whether the donation is directed, and how many disclosures have been recorded. Start with the program's `cooling_off_days` as the base. If the donor has no `intended_recipient_id`, add a further seven days to the base, because a non-directed donor has no relationship pressing them toward a decision and is given a longer window to reconsider. Then, if `disclosure_sequence` has reached 2 or more — that is, on every re-consent — the effective window is the larger of two days and half the base rounded up to a whole day; otherwise the effective window is the base itself. Finally, `cooling_off_ends_at` is `disclosure_at` plus that many whole days, with the time-of-day component carried over untouched. Two worked readings: a directed donor at a program with a seven-day base disclosed at midnight on the first of the month may be attested at exactly midnight on the eighth; a non-directed donor at the same program gets a fourteen-day base and cannot be attested until the fifteenth; and a directed donor at that program who is re-disclosed at six in the morning on the tenth faces a window of four days — the larger of two and seven halved rounded up — and may be attested from six in the morning on the fourteenth.

**Attestation or objection.** The advocacy determination takes an `outcome` of either `ATTESTED` or `OBJECTED` and, when the outcome is `OBJECTED`, an `objection_reason_code`. Attesting requires that the current logical clock has reached `cooling_off_ends_at` — the boundary is inclusive, so exactly at that instant the attestation succeeds and one second earlier is refused with `ERR_COOLING_OFF_NOT_ELAPSED` — and that both `medical_status` and `psychosocial_status` are `CLEARED`, failing which the attestation is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_INCOMPLETE`. Attestation is only reachable from `DISCLOSED`. It sets `attestation_at` to the current logical clock, computes `final_risk_index`, and derives the donor's `overall_status` into either `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL_APPROVED`.

Objecting is reachable from either `DISCLOSED` or `ATTESTED` — an advocate who has already attested may still object if new information emerges — requires an `objection_reason_code`, and is terminal for the advocacy dimension. It records the reason code on the donor and then runs a four-hop cascade. First, the donor's `overall_status` becomes `ADVOCATE_BLOCKED`. Second, because the donor is blocked, every chain segment naming this donor whose status is `PLANNED`, `OFFER_SENT` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`. Third, because those segments were withdrawn, each parent chain whose status is `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED`, `SUSPENDED`, `COMMITTED` or `EXECUTING` moves to `BROKEN` with a `break_reason_code` of `ADVOCATE_OBJECTION`, and every remaining non-withdrawn segment of that chain whose donor is `CHAIN_COMMITTED` releases that donor back to `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL_APPROVED` according to the donor's stored `final_risk_index`. Fourth, because each chain broke, every procedure booking belonging to any segment of that chain whose status is `ACTIVE` moves to `CANCELLED` with a `cancel_reason_code` of `CHAIN_BROKEN`, and each affected room's `reserved_minutes_total` is decremented by that booking's `duration_minutes`. When the objection came from `ATTESTED`, the donor's `final_risk_index` is retained for audit rather than cleared. Either way the donor's `version` increments by 1.

**The compound status.** `overall_status` is never set by a caller. It is re-derived after any change to the three dimensions or to the donor's chain participation, by evaluating these clauses in exactly this order and taking the first that matches. If `medical_status` or `psychosocial_status` is `EXCLUDED`, the result is `EXCLUDED`. Otherwise, if `advocacy_status` is `OBJECTED`, the result is `ADVOCATE_BLOCKED`. Otherwise, if `donated_at` is set, the result is `DONATED`. Otherwise, if the donor holds a segment that is not `WITHDRAWN` whose chain is `COMMITTED` or `EXECUTING` and whose own `created_at` is at or before that chain's `committed_at` — a segment inserted *after* commitment does not retroactively commit its donor — the result is `CHAIN_COMMITTED`. Otherwise, if `advocacy_status` is `ATTESTED` and both clinical dimensions are `CLEARED`, the result is `CONDITIONAL_APPROVED` when `final_risk_index` is at or above the ceiling of 8.0 and `APPROVED` when it is below. Otherwise, if at least one evaluation row exists for this donor, the result is `EVAL_IN_PROGRESS`. Otherwise the result is `REFERRED`. The ordering makes `EXCLUDED` and `ADVOCATE_BLOCKED` absorbing: once either clause has fired, no later clearance can promote the donor — a donor who is attested and medically cleared but psychosocially excluded reads `EXCLUDED`, not `APPROVED`.

Donor candidates are listed with optional `program_id`, `intended_recipient_id`, `referred_by_staff_id`, `advocate_staff_id` and `overall_status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`; reading one exposes all four state dimensions, both risk indices and `cooling_off_ends_at`.

### 3.4. Clinical Evaluation: Exclusion Screening, Determinations, and Risk Indices

A donor's fitness is established by up to two evaluation rows, one per `domain`: `MEDICAL` and `PSYCHOSOCIAL`. Submitting an evaluation requires the `donor_candidate_id` and the `domain`, and records the authoring reviewer's `X-User-ID` as `reviewer_staff_id`. The findings themselves are domain-shaped. A medical submission carries `bmi`, `egfr_ml_min` (estimated GFR in millilitres per minute per 1.73 square metres), `systolic_bp` in millimetres of mercury, and `hba1c` as a percentage; all four are required on a medical submission and none of them carries a declared range, so any numeric value of the right type is accepted and the exclusion thresholds below are the only judgement applied to them; these are null on a psychosocial submission. A psychosocial submission carries `financial_hardship_score` and `social_support_score`, both integers from 0 to 10 inclusive; these are null on a medical submission. Three psychosocial findings are boolean with defaults: `coercion_flag`, marking suspected coercion or valuable consideration, defaults to false; `unmanaged_substance_use`, marking active unmanaged substance use, defaults to false; and `decisional_capacity_ok`, marking that the donor retains decisional capacity, defaults to true. Unlike the domain-shaped numeric findings, these three are read on both domains and are never nulled: a medical submission that omits them stores the same three defaults a psychosocial submission would.

Three guards apply at submission. The named reviewer must have `staff_role` equal to `EVALUATION_REVIEWER` and be active, or the request fails `ERR_STAFF_ROLE_INVALID`. The reviewer, however, is not a body field: it is the authenticated caller, whose duty and active flag priority 2 has already confirmed, so an inactive or wrongly-credentialed principal is turned away earlier with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and `ERR_STAFF_ROLE_INVALID` is unreachable on this endpoint. The reviewer of a donor's psychosocial evaluation may not be the reviewer of that same donor's medical evaluation — a single person may not certify both halves of one donor's fitness — and a violation is `ERR_REVIEWER_SOD_CONFLICT`. And a donor may hold at most one evaluation per domain in `SUBMITTED` status, so a second open evaluation in the same domain is `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_EVALUATION`. A new evaluation row starts at `SUBMITTED` with `submission_count` 1 and `determined_at` null, and the first evaluation row ever created for a donor moves that donor's `overall_status` from `REFERRED` to `EVAL_IN_PROGRESS`.

**Exclusion screening.** Every submission and every resubmission is screened immediately, producing three derived fields: `exclusion_codes`, the sorted list of hard exclusion codes that fired; `hard_exclusion_count`, the length of that list; and `risk_points`, the accumulated soft findings. Only codes belonging to the evaluation's own domain are considered.

For a medical evaluation, five hard codes may fire. `EX_BMI_TOO_HIGH` fires when `bmi` is strictly greater than 35.0. `EX_RENAL_FUNCTION_LOW` fires when `egfr_ml_min` is strictly below 80.0. `EX_HYPERTENSION` fires when `systolic_bp` is at least 160. `EX_DIABETES_RANGE` fires when `hba1c` is at least 6.5. `EX_UNDERAGE` fires when the donor's `age_years` is below 18. For a psychosocial evaluation, three hard codes may fire: `EX_COERCION_SUSPECTED` when `coercion_flag` is true, `EX_SUBSTANCE_USE` when `unmanaged_substance_use` is true, and `EX_CAPACITY_ABSENT` when `decisional_capacity_ok` is false.

Soft findings do not exclude anyone; they accumulate `risk_points`. On a medical evaluation, 2 points are added when `bmi` is strictly greater than 30.0 and at most 35.0; 3 points when `egfr_ml_min` is at least 80.0 and strictly below 90.0; 2 points when `systolic_bp` is at least 140 and strictly below 160; 2 points when `hba1c` is at least 5.7 and strictly below 6.5; and 1 point when the donor's `age_years` is at least 60. On a psychosocial evaluation, 3 points are added when `financial_hardship_score` is at least 7, and 2 points when `social_support_score` is at most 3. The bands are constructed so that the hard region lies strictly outside the soft region: a `bmi` of exactly 35.0 is a soft two-point finding rather than a hard exclusion, an `egfr_ml_min` of exactly 80.0 is a soft three-point finding, a `systolic_bp` of exactly 160 is hard, and an `hba1c` of exactly 6.5 is hard. A measurement that fires a hard code contributes no soft points for that same measurement. `risk_points` is always a non-negative integer.

**Determinations.** The assigned reviewer records an `outcome` on an evaluation, which must be one of `CLEARED`, `EXCLUDED` or `DEFERRED`, and which is only accepted while the evaluation is `SUBMITTED`. A `CLEARED` outcome is refused with `ERR_HARD_EXCLUSION_PRESENT` while the evaluation's `hard_exclusion_count` is greater than 0 — a hard finding must be resolved by resubmission, not overridden. Every determination stamps `determined_at` with the current logical clock and increments the evaluation's `version` by 1.

Each outcome mirrors into the donor's matching clinical dimension, which is the mechanism by which one evaluation row moves one donor dimension. A `CLEARED` determination sets that donor dimension to `CLEARED`, copies the evaluation's `risk_points` into the donor's `medical_risk_points` or `psychosocial_risk_points` as the domain dictates, recomputes the donor's `screening_risk_index` and re-derives `overall_status`. A `DEFERRED` determination sets that donor dimension to `DEFERRED`, increments the donor's `deferral_count` by 1, and recomputes `screening_risk_index`; `deferral_count` is cumulative and is never decremented, which is what makes a history of deferrals permanently visible in the final index. An `EXCLUDED` determination is terminal for that evaluation row and sets the donor dimension to `EXCLUDED`, then cascades in three hops: the donor's `overall_status` becomes `EXCLUDED`; every chain segment of this donor whose status is `PLANNED`, `OFFER_SENT` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`; and each affected parent chain whose status is `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED`, `SUSPENDED`, `COMMITTED` or `EXECUTING` moves to `BROKEN` with a `break_reason_code` of `DONOR_EXCLUDED`. The exclusion cascade is identical whether the donor dimension was previously `PENDING` or `DEFERRED`. Every one of these mirrored changes increments the donor's `version` by 1.

The clinical dimensions are cyclic in one direction. A dimension may move `PENDING` to `CLEARED`, `PENDING` to `DEFERRED`, `DEFERRED` to `CLEARED` after a resubmission, `PENDING` or `DEFERRED` to `EXCLUDED`, and — when a later determination defers a domain that had already been cleared — `CLEARED` back to `DEFERRED`, which also increments `deferral_count` and re-derives `overall_status` down to `EVAL_IN_PROGRESS`. That last move is only permitted while the donor's `overall_status` is not `CHAIN_COMMITTED`. `EXCLUDED` is terminal for the dimension.

**Resubmission.** A deferred evaluation is reopened by resubmitting its findings. The resubmission carries the same nine finding fields as the original submission — `bmi`, `egfr_ml_min`, `systolic_bp`, `hba1c`, `coercion_flag`, `unmanaged_substance_use`, `decisional_capacity_ok`, `financial_hardship_score` and `social_support_score` — and only the assigned reviewer may file it. The donor's `overall_status` must not be `EXCLUDED`, `ADVOCATE_BLOCKED`, `CHAIN_COMMITTED` or `DONATED`. The evaluation returns to `SUBMITTED`, `submission_count` increases by 1, `determined_at` is cleared to null, and `exclusion_codes`, `hard_exclusion_count` and `risk_points` are re-screened from the new findings. The donor's matching risk-points field and `screening_risk_index` are recomputed. Critically, `deferral_count` is **not** reset — the resubmission repairs the findings but not the history.

**The two risk indices.** A donor carries two weighted risk figures that use the same inputs with different weights, and confusing them is the most common modelling error in this area. The **screening index**, stored in `screening_risk_index`, is recomputed after every determination and every resubmission. It is six tenths of `medical_risk_points` plus four tenths of `psychosocial_risk_points`, rounded half up to two decimal places once, applied to the whole weighted sum. Medicine dominates while the workup is still open, and neither the deferral history nor non-directed status is surcharged. The **final index**, stored in `final_risk_index`, is computed exactly once, at the moment of attestation. It is one half of `medical_risk_points` plus one half of `psychosocial_risk_points`, plus 1.5 for each entry in `deferral_count`, plus a further 2.0 when the donor has no `intended_recipient_id`, all rounded half up to two decimal places once at the end. In the final reckoning the two clinical domains weigh equally, and the evaluation history plus non-directed status become first-class risk. The final index alone is compared against the ceiling of 8.0: a value at or above 8.0 yields `CONDITIONAL_APPROVED`, and a value below — 7.99 included — yields `APPROVED`. Two consequences follow. A donor with `medical_risk_points` of 10 and `psychosocial_risk_points` of 5 and no deferrals has a screening index of 8.0 but a final index of 7.5, and is approved; add a single deferral and the screening index is unchanged while the final index reaches 9.0 and the donor is only conditionally approved. And `final_risk_index` stays null until the donor has both clinical dimensions `CLEARED` and `advocacy_status` `ATTESTED`.

A `CONDITIONAL_APPROVED` donor is rejected by chain validation. The way out is a resubmission that lowers `risk_points`, followed by a fresh determination and a fresh attestation — which, because attestation is only reachable from `DISCLOSED`, requires a re-consent disclosure and therefore a fresh cooling-off window.

Evaluations are listed with optional `donor_candidate_id`, `reviewer_staff_id`, `domain` and `status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`, and read individually to expose `exclusion_codes`, `hard_exclusion_count`, `risk_points` and `submission_count`.

### 3.5. Building an Exchange Chain and Validating its Topology

An exchange chain is the graph object that turns individual donor-recipient pairs into a workable transplant plan. Opening one requires only the owning `program_id` and a `chain_type` of either `PAIRED_LOOP` or `NDD_CHAIN`. A `PAIRED_LOOP` must close back on itself; an `NDD_CHAIN` must begin at a non-directed donor and must not close. A new chain starts in `DRAFT` with `segment_count` 0, `is_closed_loop` false, `total_planned_cit_minutes` 0 and `committed_donor_count` 0, and with `validated_at`, `committed_at` and `break_reason_code` all null.

Segments are inserted one at a time. Each insertion supplies the `expected_chain_version` described in §2.7, a 1-based `position` of at least 1, the `donor_candidate_id` giving in this segment, the `recipient_candidate_id` receiving in it, and an optional `predecessor_segment_id` naming the same chain's segment at the previous position — which must be null for the segment at position 1. The new segment starts in `PLANNED` with every offer and planning field null, and with its realized fields — `actual_cross_clamp_at`, `actual_reperfusion_at` and `actual_cit_minutes` — null as well. `cit_breach` is not one of those nullable fields: it is a completion flag that starts **false** on a freshly inserted segment and stays false until a transplant completion evaluates it, so a segment that has never been completed reports `cit_breach` false rather than null. Insertion recomputes the chain's `segment_count` and `is_closed_loop` and increments the chain's `version`, so any other client holding the previous value is immediately stale. A chain may hold at most one non-withdrawn segment per position, and a second attempt at an occupied position is `ERR_POSITION_TAKEN`. Insertion is accepted while the chain is `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED`, `SUSPENDED`, `COMMITTED` or `EXECUTING` — a chain already committed may still be extended, though the new segment does not retroactively commit its donor — and only the two terminal statuses, `COMPLETED` and `BROKEN`, refuse it with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Insertion deliberately does **not** check the chain's shape. A coordinator building a five-segment loop must be free to pass through intermediate states that are not yet valid — positions momentarily out of order, a loop not yet closed — so the whole topology is judged once, as a set, at validation time. Accordingly a supplied `predecessor_segment_id` need only resolve to an existing segment of the same program: that it belongs to this chain, that it sits at the previous position, and that a segment at position 1 leaves it null are all deferred to validation and are not enforced at insertion.

**Validation.** Validating a chain takes no input fields and moves it from `DRAFT` to `VALIDATED`, recording `validated_at` at the current logical clock, recomputing `segment_count` and `is_closed_loop`, and incrementing the chain's `version`. It considers only segments whose status is not `WITHDRAWN`, ordered by `position` ascending, and it enforces seven topology conditions plus two participant conditions.

The chain must hold at least two such segments; a shorter chain fails `ERR_CHAIN_TOO_SHORT`, and this holds regardless of `chain_type`, so a single-segment chain can never validate. The positions must be exactly the consecutive whole numbers from 1 up to `segment_count`, with no gap and no duplicate; positions of 1 and 3 fail `ERR_CHAIN_POSITIONS_NOT_CONTIGUOUS`. The linkage must be exact: the first segment in the ordering must declare no `predecessor_segment_id`, and every subsequent segment's `predecessor_segment_id` must be the `id` of the segment immediately before it; a null predecessor on a second segment, or a predecessor pointing anywhere other than the previous position, fails `ERR_CHAIN_LINKAGE_BROKEN`. No `donor_candidate_id` and no `recipient_candidate_id` may appear on more than one of those segments; a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_PARTICIPANT`.

Every segment must be blood-group compatible in the donor-to-recipient direction. A donor of type `O` may give to a recipient of type `O`, `A`, `B` or `AB`. A donor of type `A` may give to `A` or `AB`. A donor of type `B` may give to `B` or `AB`. A donor of type `AB` may give only to `AB`. Any other pairing fails `ERR_ABO_INCOMPATIBLE`.

`is_closed_loop` is true exactly when the `recipient_candidate_id` of the last segment in the ordering equals the `intended_recipient_id` of the first segment's donor — the chain has given back to the person the first donor came in for. A `PAIRED_LOOP` requires `is_closed_loop` to be true and fails `ERR_LOOP_NOT_CLOSED` otherwise; a two-segment closed loop is the minimum valid loop. An `NDD_CHAIN` requires `is_closed_loop` to be false *and* requires the donor of the position-1 segment to have a null `intended_recipient_id`; either failure is `ERR_NDD_START_INVALID`.

Finally, participant eligibility — described last because it is the least structural of the conditions, but judged **first**, because §2.10 places it at priority 14 and the seven topology conditions above at priority 16. Within it every donor is judged before any recipient, so a chain whose donor is unapproved and whose recipient is inactive reports the donor failure. Every segment's donor must have `overall_status` exactly `APPROVED` — `CONDITIONAL_APPROVED` is explicitly not enough, and the rejection is `ERR_DONOR_NOT_APPROVED`. Every segment's recipient must have `listing_status` exactly `ACTIVE`, failing which the rejection is `ERR_RECIPIENT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Chains are listed with optional `program_id`, `chain_type` and `status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`, and read individually to expose `version`, `segment_count`, `is_closed_loop`, `total_planned_cit_minutes` and `committed_donor_count`. Segments are listed with optional `exchange_chain_id`, `donor_candidate_id`, `recipient_candidate_id`, `predecessor_segment_id` and `status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`, and read individually to expose their planned and actual cold-ischemia figures and `cit_breach`.

### 3.6. Commitment, Organ Offers, and Cold-Ischemia Logistics

**Committing the chain.** Commitment is the point at which a validated plan becomes binding, and it is the operation the compliance posture gates most tightly. It takes no input fields, and it is only reachable from `VALIDATED`. The program's posture must not be `FROZEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN`; and if the posture is `RESTRICTED`, the chain's `segment_count` must be at most 2, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED`. Every non-withdrawn segment's donor must still have `overall_status` `APPROVED` and every such segment's recipient must still be `ACTIVE`. If any segment of the chain has already been offered, the chain's `total_planned_cit_minutes` must not exceed the chain-wide budget of 2,400 minutes — forty hours of accumulated cold time across the whole chain. On success the chain moves to `COMMITTED`, `committed_at` is set to the current logical clock, `committed_donor_count` becomes the number of distinct donors on non-withdrawn segments, every one of those donors moves from `APPROVED` to `CHAIN_COMMITTED`, and the chain's `version` increments.

**Breaking the chain.** A coordinator may break a chain deliberately by supplying a `break_reason_code`, and the system breaks chains as a cascade whenever a participant is lost. Breaking is reachable from `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED`, `SUSPENDED`, `COMMITTED` and `EXECUTING`; only the caller-driven form requires the reason code, since a cascading break supplies its own. Breaking sets the chain to `BROKEN` with the reason recorded, moves every non-withdrawn segment whose status is `PLANNED`, `OFFER_SENT` or `ACCEPTED` to `WITHDRAWN`, releases every donor of this chain whose `overall_status` is `CHAIN_COMMITTED` back to `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL_APPROVED` according to that donor's stored `final_risk_index`, zeroes `committed_donor_count`, and moves every procedure booking of this chain's segments whose status is `ACTIVE` to `CANCELLED` with a `cancel_reason_code` of `CHAIN_BROKEN`, decrementing each room's `reserved_minutes_total` by that booking's `duration_minutes`. Segments that had already reached `TRANSPLANTED` are untouched and their donors remain `DONATED`. `COMPLETED` and `BROKEN` are terminal chain states and accept no further transition.

**Sending the offer.** An organ offer is sent per segment and carries the cold-ischemia plan: `planned_cross_clamp_at`, the instant the donor organ will be clamped; `planned_reperfusion_at`, the instant the recipient will be reperfused; and `planned_transport_minutes`, the transport time budgeted between the two, which is required and must be a non-negative whole number of minutes. Offers are reachable from `PLANNED` and, as a re-offer with a fresh plan, from `DECLINED`; the `OFFER_SENT` to `DECLINED` to `OFFER_SENT` cycle may repeat. The preconditions are identical in both cases. The parent chain's status must be `COMMITTED` or `EXECUTING`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CHAIN_NOT_COMMITTED` — a chain broken by an earlier decline can never be re-offered. The segment's recipient must be `ACTIVE`, failing which the rejection is `ERR_RECIPIENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; the segment's donor must be `CHAIN_COMMITTED`, failing which the rejection is `ERR_DONOR_NOT_COMMITTED`. `planned_reperfusion_at` must be strictly later than `planned_cross_clamp_at`; this is a request-shape condition judged at priority 4 and reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`. When the segment declares a `predecessor_segment_id`, its `planned_cross_clamp_at` must be at or after that predecessor's `planned_reperfusion_at` — one operation cannot start recovering an organ before the previous recipient in the chain has been reperfused — and a violation is `ERR_PREDECESSOR_TIMING_INVALID`; a predecessor that has not yet been offered, and therefore still carries a null `planned_reperfusion_at`, imposes no constraint at all. And there must already exist an `ACTIVE` procedure booking for this segment whose `booking_role` is `DONOR_RECOVERY` and whose half-open window from `start_at` inclusive to `end_at` exclusive contains `planned_cross_clamp_at`; without one the offer is refused with `ERR_DONOR_BOOKING_MISSING`.

**Planned versus actual cold ischemia.** `planned_cit_minutes` is computed when the offer is sent: it is the whole minutes between `planned_cross_clamp_at` and `planned_reperfusion_at`, truncated toward zero, plus a fixed forty-five-minute allowance for the final cross-match hold. The plan must reserve that hold, so the planned figure is deliberately more conservative than the realized one will be. `actual_cit_minutes`, by contrast, is computed at completion as simply the whole minutes between `actual_cross_clamp_at` and `actual_reperfusion_at`, truncated toward zero, with no allowance added, because the realized interval already contains whatever hold actually occurred. Both figures are compared against the same per-segment ceiling of 1,080 minutes — eighteen hours — which means an identical clamp-to-reperfusion span can pass as an actual figure and fail as a planned one. A planned figure of exactly 1,080 is accepted; 1,081 is refused with `ERR_CIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

The chain-wide budget is checked next. `total_planned_cit_minutes` on a chain is the sum of `planned_cit_minutes` over that chain's segments whose status is `OFFER_SENT`, `ACCEPTED` or `TRANSPLANTED`, accumulated in `position` ascending and, for ties, `created_at` ascending order. The check at offer time is **self-excluding**: it sums the *other* qualifying segments and adds this segment's own freshly computed `planned_cit_minutes`, so re-offering a segment never double-counts it. A total of exactly 2,400 minutes is accepted; 2,401 is refused with `ERR_CHAIN_CIT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. Three offered segments at 800 planned minutes each therefore exhaust the budget precisely, and a fourth offer of any positive length fails.

A successful offer sets the segment to `OFFER_SENT`, stamps `offer_sent_at` with the current logical clock, sets `offer_response_deadline_at` to six hours after `offer_sent_at`, stores the three planned fields, writes `planned_cit_minutes`, recomputes the chain's `total_planned_cit_minutes`, and increments the segment's `version`. A re-offer from `DECLINED` additionally clears `decline_reason_code` and `offer_responded_at` back to null.

**Answering the offer.** The response operation carries a `response` of either `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`, plus a `decline_reason_code` when the response is `DECLINE`. Either way the current logical clock must be at or before `offer_response_deadline_at`, and a late answer is refused with `ERR_OFFER_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. The two branches carry disjoint side effects. Accepting moves the segment to `ACCEPTED`, stamps `offer_responded_at` with the current logical clock, and increments the segment's `version` — nothing else changes. Declining moves the segment to `DECLINED`, stamps `offer_responded_at`, records the `decline_reason_code`, increments the recipient's `offer_decline_count` by 1, recomputes the chain's `total_planned_cit_minutes` because this segment has now left the sum, and increments the segment's `version`. The decline branch then forks a second time on the reason: when `decline_reason_code` is `DONOR_ORGAN_QUALITY`, the parent chain additionally moves to `BROKEN` with a `break_reason_code` of `OFFER_DECLINED_ORGAN_QUALITY` and its full compensating cascade runs; for any other reason code the chain keeps its status and only this segment is affected.

**Theatre and surgeon logistics.** A procedure booking reserves a room and a surgeon for one half of one segment. Creating one supplies the `operating_room_id`, the `chain_segment_id`, the `surgeon_staff_id`, a `booking_role` of either `DONOR_RECOVERY` or `RECIPIENT_IMPLANT`, and the interval bounds `start_at` and `end_at`. The named surgeon must have `staff_role` equal to `SURGEON` and be active, or the booking fails `ERR_STAFF_ROLE_INVALID`. The booking starts in `ACTIVE`, its `duration_minutes` is the whole minutes between `start_at` and `end_at`, its `cancel_reason_code` is null, and the room's `reserved_minutes_total` is incremented by that duration.

The two occupancy rules deliberately differ. **Room occupancy** is evaluated against that room's `ACTIVE` bookings only, and each side of the comparison is padded by the room's `turnover_buffer_minutes`: the new booking is rejected when its `start_at` is earlier than an existing booking's `end_at` plus the buffer *and* its `end_at` plus the buffer is later than that existing booking's `start_at`. The windows are half-open, so a booking that starts exactly the buffer's worth of minutes after another ends is accepted. A collision is `ERR_OR_SLOT_CONFLICT`. **Surgeon availability** is evaluated against that surgeon's `ACTIVE` bookings in any room, with no buffer at all and with closed intervals: the new booking is rejected when its `start_at` is at or before an existing booking's `end_at` *and* its `end_at` is at or after that existing booking's `start_at`. Touching endpoints therefore collide for a surgeon where they would not for a room. A collision is `ERR_SURGEON_DOUBLE_BOOKED`.

Cancelling a booking requires a `cancel_reason_code`. It is only accepted while the booking is `ACTIVE` — a booking already `CANCELLED` or `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_ACTIVE` — and only while the segment has not reached `TRANSPLANTED`. Cancellation sets `status` to `CANCELLED`, records the reason, decrements the room's `reserved_minutes_total` by exactly this booking's `duration_minutes` once, and increments the booking's `version`. `CANCELLED` and `COMPLETED` are terminal booking states. Bookings are listed with optional `operating_room_id`, `chain_segment_id`, `surgeon_staff_id`, `booking_role` and `status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`, and read individually to expose `duration_minutes` and `cancel_reason_code`.

### 3.7. Realizing the Transplant and the Post-Donation Follow-Up Obligation

Recording a transplant completion is the single most cascade-heavy operation in the system. It carries two inputs, `actual_cross_clamp_at` and `actual_reperfusion_at`, and is only reachable from `ACCEPTED`. The realized reperfusion instant must be strictly later than the realized clamp instant; this is a request-shape condition judged at priority 4 and reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`. The parent chain must be `COMMITTED` or `EXECUTING`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_CHAIN_NOT_COMMITTED`. And an `ACTIVE` procedure booking with `booking_role` `RECIPIENT_IMPLANT` must exist for this segment, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_IMPLANT_BOOKING_MISSING`.

On success the segment moves to `TRANSPLANTED` — a terminal segment state — records both actual timestamps, computes `actual_cit_minutes`, and sets `cit_breach` to true when `actual_cit_minutes` exceeds the eighteen-hour ceiling of 1,080 minutes and false otherwise. Then the cascade runs. Every procedure booking of this segment whose status is `ACTIVE` moves to `COMPLETED`, and the room's `reserved_minutes_total` is left unchanged, because completed bookings stay in the ledger. The segment's donor moves from `CHAIN_COMMITTED` to `DONATED` with `donated_at` set to the current logical clock; because the donor has now donated, three follow-up rows are created for that donor, each in `DUE` status, with `interval_code` values of `SIX_MONTH`, `ONE_YEAR` and `TWO_YEAR` and `due_on` values of `donated_at` plus 180, 365 and 730 days respectively — `due_on` is a full instant that carries `donated_at`'s time of day and is rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, not as a bare calendar date — each new row starting with `submitted_field_count` 0, `completeness_pct` 0 — a zero figure, not null — and `submitted_at` and `waiver_reason_code` null; and because those rows now exist, the program's `follow_up_compliance_pct` is recomputed and `compliance_posture` re-derived. The segment's recipient moves from `ACTIVE` to `TRANSPLANTED` with a waitlist event appended as described in §3.2. When `cit_breach` came out true, the program's `cit_breach_count` increases by 1 and `compliance_posture` is re-derived again — two breaches are enough to restrict the program. And the parent chain moves from `COMMITTED` to `EXECUTING` on the first completion, or from `EXECUTING` to `COMPLETED` when the count of non-withdrawn segments at `TRANSPLANTED` has reached the chain's `segment_count`; a chain reaching `COMPLETED` zeroes its `committed_donor_count`. Every entity the cascade touches has its `version` incremented.

**Follow-up submissions.** Each follow-up row is discoverable only through the donor-scoped listing, which filters on `interval_code` and `status` and accepts `limit` and `offset`. A row exposes its `donor_candidate_id`, `interval_code`, `due_on`, `required_fields_count`, `submitted_field_count`, `completeness_pct`, `status`, `submitted_at` and `waiver_reason_code`.

A coordinator submits interval data by supplying `submitted_fields`, a list of field names carrying the donor's reported data. Submission is refused before the interval's `due_on` with `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_NOT_DUE`, and is refused entirely against a row whose status is already `WAIVED` or `SUBMITTED_COMPLETE` with `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_TERMINAL`; submission is accepted while the row is `DUE`, `INCOMPLETE` or `DELINQUENT`. Each interval has a fixed required field count: a `SIX_MONTH` interval requires 12 fields, a `ONE_YEAR` interval requires 14, and a `TWO_YEAR` interval requires 10, and that number is stored as `required_fields_count`. The `submitted_field_count` is the number of *distinct*, non-empty string entries in `submitted_fields`: duplicates collapse to one, and an entry that is null, not a string, or the empty string does not count. There is no fixed vocabulary of required field names, so the names themselves are never checked against a per-interval set and any distinct non-empty name counts. `submitted_field_count` is capped at `required_fields_count`. The `submitted_fields` list itself is required, but it may be empty, in which case the count is 0, `completeness_pct` is 0.00 and the row lands on `INCOMPLETE`. `completeness_pct` is 100 multiplied by `submitted_field_count` divided by `required_fields_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places, so it never exceeds 100.00.

The threshold comparison is inclusive: when `completeness_pct` is at or above the program's `follow_up_completeness_threshold_pct` the row becomes `SUBMITTED_COMPLETE`, and otherwise it becomes `INCOMPLETE`. Eight distinct valid fields out of a two-year interval's ten give exactly 80.00 and, against the default threshold, count as complete; seven of ten give 70.00 and do not. Ten of a six-month interval's twelve give 83.33 and count; eleven of a one-year interval's fourteen give 78.57 and do not. Either outcome stamps `submitted_at` with the current logical clock, recomputes the program's `follow_up_compliance_pct`, re-derives `compliance_posture`, and increments the row's `version`. A repaired submission from `INCOMPLETE` and a late submission from `DELINQUENT` both work the same way: the previously submitted fields are replaced by the new ones and the row lands on `SUBMITTED_COMPLETE` or `INCOMPLETE` by the same threshold test.

**Delinquency and waiver.** A compliance sweep moves any row still at `DUE` or `INCOMPLETE` to `DELINQUENT` once the logical clock has reached thirty days past that row's `due_on`. A `ProgramAdmin` may instead waive an interval by supplying a `waiver_reason_code`; waiver is reachable from `DUE`, `INCOMPLETE` and `DELINQUENT`, sets the row to `WAIVED` — a terminal state — records the reason, leaves `submitted_field_count`, `completeness_pct` and `submitted_at` exactly as it found them, recomputes the program's compliance figure, re-derives posture, and increments the row's `version`. A row waived before any submission therefore keeps its creation values — `submitted_at` null and `completeness_pct` 0 — while a row waived after an incomplete submission keeps the figures that submission left behind.

**Program-wide compliance.** `follow_up_compliance_pct` is the percentage of the program's countable follow-up obligations that have actually been met. The denominator is the count of follow-up rows whose donor belongs to this program, whose `status` is one of `DUE`, `SUBMITTED_COMPLETE`, `INCOMPLETE` or `DELINQUENT`, and whose `due_on` is at or before the current logical clock. The numerator is the count of those same rows whose `status` is `SUBMITTED_COMPLETE`. When the denominator is zero the figure is 100.00 — a brand-new program with three future-dated rows reports the vacuous 100.00 rather than 0.00. Otherwise the figure is 100 multiplied by the numerator divided by the denominator, rounded half up to two decimal places. A `WAIVED` row leaves **both** the numerator and the denominator, which means waiving a delinquent interval raises compliance without ever counting as a submission. Four due rows of which three are complete and one delinquent give exactly 75.00, which against the default threshold of 75 is not below it and therefore does *not* degrade the posture; two complete and two delinquent give 50.00 and do. Three due rows of which one is complete, one incomplete and one waived give a denominator of 2 and a numerator of 1, or 50.00. Seven due rows with five complete give 71.43.

### 3.8. Outcome Monitoring, Flagging, and Improvement Plans

A quality officer measures the program against its own expectation by creating an outcome review for a period. The review carries the `program_id`, a `period_start_on` and `period_end_on` pair of `YYYY-MM-DD` dates where the end must not precede the start, a `cohort_size` of at least 0 counting the transplants in the review cohort, and an `observed_survivor_count` of at least 0 counting the one-year survivors in that cohort, which may not exceed `cohort_size`.

Creation snapshots the program's `expected_one_year_survival_rate` into the review's own `expected_survival_rate_used`. That snapshot is the whole point: editing the program's expectation afterwards never re-rates a review that already exists. `expected_survivor_count` is `cohort_size` multiplied by `expected_survival_rate_used`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Then the cohort-size floor applies. When `cohort_size` is below 10, the cohort is too small to rate at all: `oe_ratio` is null, `flag_level` is `NONE` and `requires_improvement_plan` is false, no matter how catastrophic the raw ratio would have looked. Otherwise `oe_ratio` is `observed_survivor_count` divided by `expected_survivor_count`, rounded half up to **four** decimal places, and `flag_level` is `FLAGGED` when `oe_ratio` is strictly below 0.85, `WATCH` when it is strictly below 0.95 but not below 0.85, and `NONE` otherwise. Both thresholds are strict, so an `oe_ratio` of exactly 0.8500 is `WATCH` and not `FLAGGED`, and exactly 0.9500 is `NONE` and not `WATCH`. `requires_improvement_plan` is true exactly when `flag_level` is `FLAGGED`. A new review's `status` is `DRAFT` and `finalized_at` is null; a draft review does not yet affect the program's posture at all.

**Finalization.** Finalizing a review takes no input fields and is the moment its flag level starts to bite. A program may hold at most one `FINALIZED` review per identical `period_start_on` and `period_end_on` pair, and a second attempt is `ERR_DUPLICATE_REVIEW_PERIOD`. On success `status` becomes `FINALIZED` — terminal — and `finalized_at` is stamped with the current logical clock. `requires_improvement_plan` keeps the value it was computed with at creation. The cascade then runs in three further hops: because the review is now finalized, the program's `compliance_posture` is re-derived, where a `FLAGGED` level drives `FROZEN` and a `WATCH` level drives `RESTRICTED`; because the posture entered `FROZEN`, every chain of the program whose status is `DRAFT` or `VALIDATED` moves to `SUSPENDED`; and because those chains are suspended, each of their `OFFER_SENT` segments reverts to `PLANNED` with the offer fields cleared and every `ACTIVE` procedure booking of *any* of their segments moves to `CANCELLED` with a `cancel_reason_code` of `POSTURE_FROZEN`, decrementing each room's `reserved_minutes_total`. The review's `version` increments.

**Filing a plan.** An improvement plan answers a review that asked for one. It carries the `program_outcome_review_id`, the `expected_review_version` described in §2.7, a `title`, a `milestone_count` of at least 1, and a `target_completion_on` date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. The parent review's `flag_level` must be `WATCH` or `FLAGGED`; filing against a review whose level is `NONE` is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_REQUIRED`. Creation puts the new plan in `SUBMITTED` with `decision_reason_code`, `decided_at` and `closed_at` all null, moves any existing plan of the same review whose status is `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` to `SUPERSEDED` — unconditionally, since a plan already at `ACCEPTED` or `CLOSED` is not in a supersedable status and so simply stays where it is rather than blocking the new filing — and increments the parent review's `version` so that a competing writer holding the old value is now stale.

**Deciding a plan.** A `ProgramAdmin` acts on a plan by supplying an `action` of `ACCEPT`, `REJECT` or `CLOSE` and, for a decision, a `decision_reason_code`. The two decisions — accepting and rejecting — each require the `decision_reason_code`, and its absence on either is the priority-4 `ERR_VALIDATION` of §2.6, which under §2.10 pre-empts every later gate including the review-not-finalized check; closing is not a decision, so it neither requires the code nor records or clears whatever code the plan already carries. Accepting a plan requires that the parent review's `status` is `FINALIZED`; acting on a plan whose review is still `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FINALIZED`. Accepting moves the plan from `SUBMITTED` to `ACCEPTED`, stamps `decided_at` with the current logical clock, records the reason code, and re-derives the program's posture — the parent review's `FLAGGED` level no longer forces `FROZEN`, so the posture drops to `RESTRICTED` or `OPEN` and every `SUSPENDED` chain returns to `VALIDATED`. Rejecting moves the plan from `SUBMITTED` to `REJECTED`, stamps `decided_at`, records the reason code, and re-derives the posture, which stays `FROZEN` while the flagged review remains unanswered. Closing moves a plan from `ACCEPTED` to `CLOSED`, stamps `closed_at`, and re-derives the posture, where the significant effect is that a `WATCH` review referenced by this plan stops holding the posture at `RESTRICTED` — only a `CLOSED` plan clears a watch. Every one of these increments the plan's `version`.

`REJECTED` is endpoint-terminal: no further action is accepted on a rejected plan through the transition operation, and an attempt reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The system may nonetheless move a rejected plan to `SUPERSEDED` when a newer plan is filed for the same review. `SUPERSEDED` and `CLOSED` are fully terminal.

Reviews are listed with optional `program_id`, `flag_level` and `status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`, and read individually to expose the snapshotted expected rate, `oe_ratio`, `flag_level` and `version`. A review's plans are listed through the review, filtered on `status`, with `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.9. Donor Cost Coverage and the Program Fund

No living donor should pay to donate, and this section is where that principle becomes arithmetic. A coordinator files a claim naming the `donor_candidate_id`, a `claim_type` of `MEDICAL`, `TRAVEL` or `LOST_WAGES`, a `coverage_source` of `RECIPIENT_INSURANCE`, `NLDAC_GRANT` or `PROGRAM_FUND` naming the primary pocket the submitter is asking for, a strictly positive `submitted_amount_cents`, and a `service_from_on` and `service_to_on` pair of `YYYY-MM-DD` dates where the end must not precede the start. `lost_wage_days`, the whole days of lost wages, is required and must be at least 1 exactly when `claim_type` is `LOST_WAGES`, and is null otherwise. The donor's `overall_status` must already be `DONATED`; filing for a donor who has not donated is refused with `ERR_DONOR_NOT_DONATED`. A new claim starts in `SUBMITTED` with `insurance_part_cents`, `grant_part_cents` and `fund_part_cents` all 0 and with `adjudicated_reimbursement_cents`, `unfunded_cents`, `adjudicated_at`, `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `reversal_reason_code` all null.

**The optimistic estimate.** `estimated_reimbursement_cents` is written once, at filing, and is never revised. It applies only the per-claim cap and deliberately ignores the donor's lifetime cap, the coverage buckets and the program fund balance. For a `MEDICAL` claim the cap is 1,000,000 cents (ten thousand dollars). For a `TRAVEL` claim the cap is 350,000 cents (three thousand five hundred dollars). For a `LOST_WAGES` claim the cap is the smaller of two figures: `lost_wage_days` multiplied by the program's `lost_wage_daily_cap_cents`, and the number of weeks — `lost_wage_days` divided by 7 and rounded **up** to a whole week — multiplied by the program's `lost_wage_weekly_cap_cents`. `estimated_reimbursement_cents` is then the smaller of `submitted_amount_cents` and that cap. With the default daily and weekly caps the nested comparison flips at the five-day mark: at four days the daily figure of 120,000 binds below the one-week figure of 150,000, at exactly five days the two are equal at 150,000, and at six days the weekly figure of 150,000 binds below the daily figure of 180,000. Ten days gives a daily figure of 300,000 and a two-week figure of 300,000, which tie.

**The pessimistic adjudication.** A `FinanceAdmin` decides a claim by supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DENY` and, for a denial, a `decision_reason_code`. Both branches are only reachable from `SUBMITTED`, and both stamp `adjudicated_at` with the current logical clock, record the deciding operator's `X-User-ID` as `adjudicated_by_user_id`, and increment the claim's `version`.

An approval first checks the donor's lifetime headroom. Sum `adjudicated_reimbursement_cents` over this donor's claims whose `status` is `ADJUDICATED`; the donor's lifetime cap is 1,500,000 cents (fifteen thousand dollars), and the headroom is the cap minus that prior total. The headroom must be strictly greater than zero, and a claim arriving at zero headroom cannot be approved at all and is refused with `ERR_DONOR_LIFETIME_CAP_REACHED`. Note that a *partially* fundable claim is still approved — the shortfall is recorded rather than treated as an error — so only zero headroom is a refusal. The funded need is then the smaller of `estimated_reimbursement_cents` and that headroom.

The need is then filled by an ordered waterfall in which each bucket fills as far as it can before the next is touched, and a bucket with no headroom is skipped without consuming the remainder. The insurance bucket has a per-claim ceiling of 600,000 cents (six thousand dollars), but only when the claim's `coverage_source` is `RECIPIENT_INSURANCE`; for any other coverage source the insurance headroom is zero, so a claim filed against the program fund never touches insurance even where a ceiling would have existed. `insurance_part_cents` is the smaller of the need and that headroom, and the remainder is the need less that part. The grant bucket is only open to claims whose `claim_type` is `TRAVEL` or `LOST_WAGES`; for a `MEDICAL` claim the grant headroom is zero. Where it is open, the headroom is 800,000 cents (eight thousand dollars) per donor less the sum of `grant_part_cents` over this donor's already-adjudicated claims, floored at zero. `grant_part_cents` is the smaller of the running remainder and that headroom, and the remainder shrinks again. `fund_part_cents` is then the smaller of what is left and the program's `donor_fund_remaining_cents`. `adjudicated_reimbursement_cents` is the sum of the three parts, and `unfunded_cents` is `estimated_reimbursement_cents` less `adjudicated_reimbursement_cents` — the shortfall between what the caps allowed and what the pockets could actually pay, recorded and never silently dropped, and the figure an improvement-plan narrative reports. The claim moves to `ADJUDICATED`, the program's `donor_fund_committed_cents` increases by `fund_part_cents` and `donor_fund_remaining_cents` is recomputed.

A denial requires a `decision_reason_code`; that code is a decision input only — a claim has no `decision_reason_code` attribute of its own, so it is neither stored on the claim nor present in the claim representation the denial returns. The denial moves the claim to `DENIED` — a terminal state — sets `adjudicated_reimbursement_cents` and all three part fields to 0, sets `unfunded_cents` to the full `estimated_reimbursement_cents`, and leaves the program fund untouched.

**Reversal.** A finance administrator reverses a mistaken approval by supplying a `reversal_reason_code`. Reversal is only accepted against a claim whose `status` is `ADJUDICATED`; a claim in any other state is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_ADJUDICATED`. It moves the claim to `REVERSED` — terminal — records the reason, decreases the program's `donor_fund_committed_cents` by this claim's `fund_part_cents` and recomputes `donor_fund_remaining_cents` so that the ledger identity of §2.9 still holds exactly. The claim's amounts are retained for audit but the claim leaves every aggregate: this donor's lifetime consumption and this donor's grant-pool consumption both drop by this claim's respective parts, so a later claim for the same donor sees the restored headroom. The claim's `version` increments.

Claims are listed through their donor, filtered on `claim_type` and `status`, with `limit` and `offset`, and the listing exposes each claim's estimated-versus-adjudicated split.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Endpoints that bring a new resource into existence respond with `201 Created`; endpoints that transition, modify or read an existing resource respond with `200 OK`.
