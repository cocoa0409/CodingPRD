# SALT Apportionment Filing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A multistate SALT (State and Local Tax) practice lives with a permanent tension: the tax law of every jurisdiction is different, but the underlying business data is one set of books. A single combined-reporting group may sell into thirty states, owe net income tax in eleven, be shielded by P.L. 86-272 solicitation protection in four, have a voluntary disclosure negotiation open in one, and be arguing with a revenue department about a proposed assessment in another — all at the same time, all against the same federal starting-point income. Today that reconciliation happens in spreadsheets, and the spreadsheets do not know that adding one more credit claim silently re-prices every credit that came before it, or that the analyst who prepared a return may not also be the person who approves it.

This document specifies a service that makes the multistate compliance chain executable. It carries a combined group and its member entities, opens a per-state profile that encodes that state's apportionment weighting, sourcing doctrine, throwback treatment, filing calendar and estimated-payment safe harbor, and then walks each tax year through nexus determination, factor computation, state modifications, the credit waterfall, estimated payments, extensions, review and approval, transmission to the state, and — where the state pushes back — notice response, appeal and final assessment. Past exposure that predates any of this is settled through a voluntary disclosure agreement whose lookback window and penalty relief the service computes and enforces.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **every derived number is the server's, not the client's**: apportionment percentages, taxable income, tax before credits, credit application, balance due and assessment totals are recomputed by the service in a pinned order with a pinned rounding discipline, and the client never supplies them. Second, **the compliance posture of the group is a live consequence of its state-level facts** — discovering nexus in one state can, in the same request, open a registration obligation, flip the group's posture, and knock an already-approved return in a completely different state back into review. Implementers should read the cascade requirements as literally as the arithmetic ones.

The service is mounted under the `/salt` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Practice Roles and Caller Identity

Every request to the API — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming that operator's role in the SALT practice. If either header is absent or carries an empty value, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the four role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorized for the target operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The four roles are `TaxManager`, `StateAnalyst`, `Controller` and `Auditor`.

A `TaxManager` is the practice lead. The role owns groups, member entities, state registrations, nexus determinations, filing elections, the filing calendar, the acknowledgement of extension requests, the state's side of correspondence (information requests, appellate outcomes, closure) and every stage of a voluntary disclosure agreement. A `TaxManager` also holds a hierarchical override across preparation and controller work: wherever a `StateAnalyst` or a `Controller` may act, a `TaxManager` may act too.

A `StateAnalyst` is the return preparer. The role writes activity records, opens returns, runs the factor computation, adds modification lines and credit claims, requests extensions, freezes the computed return and submits it for review. A `StateAnalyst` may not approve, may not transmit a return to the state, and may not move money.

A `Controller` is the approver and the cash owner: approving, rejecting, transmitting and amending returns, claiming refunds, recording and applying estimated remittances, and opening, answering and appealing state notices. A `Controller` may also submit a return for review — and when the same operator does both, the segregation-of-duties rule below forbids that operator from approving it.

An `Auditor` is a read-only reviewer. The role may read every resource in the system and may perform no write of any kind.

Identity in this system is deliberately shallow. For all four roles — `TaxManager`, `StateAnalyst`, `Controller` and `Auditor` alike — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail (as `preparer_user_id` on a `StateFiling` when a return is submitted for review, and as `approver_user_id` on that same return when it is approved) but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and no resource in the system is "owned" by a user. The one place the recorded identifier carries force is segregation of duties: approval compares the approving caller's `X-User-ID` against the `preparer_user_id` already stored on the return, and rejects a match with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a subsection below states an explicit alternative ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending — oldest first, newest last. For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filter before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true when `offset` plus the number of returned records is less than `total_count`.

Several list endpoints also accept filter parameters that act as reverse lookups on foreign keys. Activity records may be filtered by `registration_id`, `legal_entity_id` and `tax_year`. Returns may be filtered by `registration_id`, `election_id`, `primary_legal_entity_id`, `tax_year` and `preparation_status`. Estimated payments may be filtered by `registration_id`, `applied_filing_id`, `status` and `tax_year`. Voluntary disclosure agreements may be filtered by `registration_id`, `legal_entity_id` and `status`. Filters combine conjunctively, and an unsupplied filter imposes no restriction.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every resource the API returns carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits. The client never supplies an `id`; any client-supplied identifier in a creation payload is a foreign key to another resource, never the identity of the resource being created.

Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, and a `version`, which is an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation of that resource. `updated_at` is refreshed whenever `version` advances. Both `created_at` and `updated_at` are stamped from the server's real-time UTC clock, **not** from the logical compliance clock of Section 2.5 — that clock governs only the workflow instants it is named for (`registered_on_date`, `filed_on_date`, `requested_on_date`, `executed_on_date`, `shield_expires_at`, the interest accrual and every deadline comparison), and moving it therefore leaves `created_at` and `updated_at` untouched. `TaxpayerGroup`, `LegalEntity`, `StateRegistration`, `FilingElection`, `StateFiling`, `ApportionmentFactor`, `Modification`, `CreditClaim`, `EstimatedPayment`, `ExtensionRequest`, `Notice` and `VDAAgreement` all behave this way.

`ActivityRecord` is the single exception. An activity record is immutable once written: it is never updated, never re-versioned, and consequently carries **only** `id` and `created_at`. Neither `updated_at` nor `version` appears on an `ActivityRecord` at all.

Every enumeration value in this system — every state name, every category, every doctrine selector — is an exact uppercase string, and is compared literally. A payload carrying a value outside an enumeration's declared members is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

### 2.5. The Logical Compliance Clock

Deadlines, accrual windows and carryforward lifetimes are all measured against a **logical clock** that the server owns. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to wall-clock time. A `TaxManager` may move the clock to any instant by supplying a `to_iso8601` value to the clock-setting capability; the response reports the new instant as `current_utc`.

Every temporal comparison in this specification reads the logical clock at the instant the request is handled: filing deadlines, extension windows, notice response and appeal windows, interest accrual on an assessment, credit carryforward expiry, registration and execution stamps, and the shield period attached to an executed disclosure. Where a rule says an action must occur "at or before" a deadline, the boundary instant itself is allowed — a request handled exactly at the deadline succeeds, and only a strictly later instant fails.

### 2.6. Monetary Precision, Factor Precision and Rounding Order

All monetary amounts are carried to **2 decimal places**, and every monetary rounding in this system is **round half up**. Apportionment quantities are different: factor ratios, factor weights and the final apportionment percentage are carried to **6 decimal places**, likewise rounded half up.

Rounding *order* is as much a part of the specification as rounding *precision*, because the SALT arithmetic in Section 3 chains multiplications and summations in ways that give materially different cents depending on where the rounding lands. Three orderings are pinned system-wide and are restated at their point of use: each modification line is rounded to cents **before** the lines are summed, never after; state taxable income is rounded to cents **before** the state tax rate is applied to it, never after; and each factor ratio is rounded to six decimal places **before** it is multiplied by its weight, with the products summed and the sum rounded to six decimal places once more. Implementations that sum exact products and round once at the end are wrong, and will differ from the specified figures by whole cents or by units in the sixth decimal place.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Resource identifiers embedded in a URL path are resolved strictly **left to right**, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error. Each entity has its own not-found code: `ERR_GROUP_NOT_FOUND` for a taxpayer group, `ERR_LEGAL_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` for a member entity, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND` for a state registration, `ERR_ACTIVITY_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` for an activity record, `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_FOUND` for a filing election, `ERR_FILING_NOT_FOUND` for a state return, `ERR_CREDIT_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` for a credit claim, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND` for an estimated payment, `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_FOUND` for an extension request, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND` for a notice, and `ERR_VDA_NOT_FOUND` for a voluntary disclosure agreement. All eleven return HTTP 404.

Foreign keys supplied inside a request body are resolved after the path but before any business rule is evaluated, and they use the same codes. This applies to `legal_entity_id` when recording activity or drafting a disclosure, to `election_id` and `primary_legal_entity_id` when opening a return, and to `filing_id` when applying a remittance to a return: an unresolvable `legal_entity_id` or `primary_legal_entity_id` yields `ERR_LEGAL_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `election_id` yields `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_FOUND`, and an unresolvable `filing_id` yields `ERR_FILING_NOT_FOUND`. A foreign key that resolves to a real record but points somewhere it does not belong is a *business* failure, not a lookup failure, and is handled further down the ladder in Section 2.11.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Revisions

Two write operations in this system re-price data that already exists, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the parent resource's `version`.

Adding a credit claim to a return requires `expected_filing_version` in the request body. This field is **not optional**: the caller must supply the `version` of the target `StateFiling` as it was last observed. If that value does not equal the return's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FILING_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no claim is created. The guard cannot be replaced by a state precondition, because a second claim on the same return is entirely legitimate product behaviour — it re-runs the whole waterfall and can push an earlier claim from `FULLY_APPLIED` down to `PARTIALLY_APPLIED`. A stale `expected_filing_version` is the only evidence available that the caller priced this claim against an out-of-date liability.

Recording an estimated-tax installment against a state registration requires `expected_registration_version` in the request body, likewise non-optional, carrying the `version` of the target `StateRegistration` as the caller last observed it. A mismatch with the registration's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REGISTRATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no payment row is written. Here too the guard is necessary rather than decorative: multiple installments on one registration are ordinary, and every earlier remittance changes the cumulative safe-harbor arithmetic against which this installment is measured, so a stale registration view means the caller computed the safe-harbor requirement from superseded data.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field. Requests to any endpoint outside these two neither require nor interpret `expected_filing_version` or `expected_registration_version`.

### 2.9. The Obligation Counter and Group Compliance Posture

Each `TaxpayerGroup` carries two derived compliance fields that the client never writes. `open_registration_obligations` is an integer counter, initialized to 0 when the group is created, whose value must at all times equal the number of `StateRegistration` rows belonging to that group whose `registration_status` is `REGISTRATION_REQUIRED`. This equality is an invariant, not an approximation: every operation that moves a registration into or out of `REGISTRATION_REQUIRED` adjusts the counter in the same request.

`compliance_posture` is an enumeration over `COMPLIANT`, `EXPOSED` and `DELINQUENT`, initialized to `COMPLIANT`, and re-derived whenever the obligation counter changes or whenever any return's `balance_due` or `assessment_added_total` changes. It is resolved by a three-rung ladder whose first match wins, evaluated strictly from the top:

1. The posture is `DELINQUENT` if there exists any `StateFiling` on any registration of this group whose `preparation_status` is either `FILED` or `AMENDED`, whose `balance_due` is greater than zero, and whose `assessment_added_total` is also greater than zero.
2. Otherwise the posture is `EXPOSED` if `open_registration_obligations` is at least 1.
3. Otherwise the posture is `COMPLIANT`.

The comparison at the second rung is exact on an integer count: one open obligation is enough.

The posture is not merely a label. When a re-derivation moves the posture from `COMPLIANT` to `EXPOSED`, every `StateFiling` belonging to that group whose `preparation_status` is `APPROVED` at that moment is demoted back to `IN_REVIEW`, its `approver_user_id` is cleared to null, and its `version` advances. This is deliberate: a group that has just discovered an unmet registration obligation somewhere in its footprint should not be transmitting already-approved returns anywhere else until a reviewer has looked again.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this system mutate more than one resource. Where the requirements below describe a chain of consequences, each consequence is a first-class part of the operation and occurs within the same request — there is no deferred settlement, no eventual consistency, and no background sweep. A chain is described in the order its links must be evaluated, and a later link always reads the values a previous link has already written.

Whenever a resource is mutated as part of such a chain, that resource's own `version` advances by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed, exactly as though it had been mutated directly. A link that recomputes a resource to the values it already held is not a mutation: a credit claim whose waterfall outcome is unchanged, a factor row rewritten to identical figures, or a group whose posture and pool the chain leaves alone keeps its `version` and `updated_at` exactly as they were. And a resource touched by several links of one chain advances its `version` by 1 in total for the request, never once per link. An operation that touches a return and its registration therefore advances two counters, and the counters are what a caller's next optimistic-concurrency check will be measured against.

### 2.11. Order of Rejection

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined: checks are evaluated in the fixed ascending order below, and the **first** failing check produces the response. Text order in Section 3 carries no authority; this ordering does. The bands are, from first evaluated to last:

1. Authentication — headers present and role recognized (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. Authorization — the acting role is listed for the target operation (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`).
3. Path identifier resolution, left to right (the eleven 404 codes of Section 2.7).
4. Body foreign-key resolution — `legal_entity_id`, `election_id`, `primary_legal_entity_id` and `filing_id`, on activity-record creation, return creation, disclosure drafting and remittance application respectively (`ERR_LEGAL_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FILING_NOT_FOUND`).
5. Payload shape on every write — required fields present, enum members recognized, numeric ranges honored (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`).
6. Optimistic concurrency on the two guarded creates (`ERR_STALE_FILING_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_REGISTRATION_VERSION`).
7. The return's `preparation_status` is a legal source state for the requested operation (`ERR_INVALID_FILING_STATE`). This band position governs operations addressed to the return itself. On the four operations where the return whose status is judged is *not* the operation's own path resource, the check runs later than its band number alone would suggest: on remittance application it is evaluated after bands 13, 24 and 25; on remittance detachment after band 13; on a notice appeal after bands 9 and 37; and on registration withdrawal — where the check is that no return on the registration is still in flight — after band 14.
8. The return's `payment_status` is `OVERPAID` before a refund can be claimed (`ERR_INVALID_FILING_STATE`).
9. The notice's `status` is a legal source state for the requested notice operation (`ERR_INVALID_NOTICE_STATE`).
10. The election's `status` is a legal source state (`ERR_INVALID_ELECTION_STATE`).
11. The disclosure's `status` is a legal source state (`ERR_INVALID_VDA_STATE`).
12. The extension request's `status` is `REQUESTED` (`ERR_INVALID_EXTENSION_STATE`).
13. The remittance's `status` is a legal source state (`ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_STATE`).
14. The owning registration has not been withdrawn (`ERR_REGISTRATION_WITHDRAWN`). This band is evaluated only on writes addressed to the registration itself or created directly beneath it — registration maintenance, nexus evaluation, completion, withdrawal, the calendar roll, and the creation of activity records, elections, returns, remittances and disclosure drafts. An operation addressed to a child resource by that child's own id — revoking or reinstating an election, anything done to a return, a modification, a credit claim, a remittance, an extension, a notice or a disclosure — does not re-check the registration and is not refused merely because the registration has since been withdrawn.
15. Nexus has been established before apportionment work is done (`ERR_NEXUS_NOT_ESTABLISHED`).
16. The state is not solicitation-protected when a return is frozen (`ERR_PL86272_PROTECTED`).
17. The registration is complete before a return is frozen (`ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_COMPLETE`).
18. Segregation of duties on approval (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`).
19. The referenced member entity belongs to the registration's own group (`ERR_ENTITY_NOT_IN_GROUP`).
20. Tax-year plausibility on activity records and disclosure windows (`ERR_ACTIVITY_YEAR_MISMATCH`).
21. A primary member entity is named when the basis requires one (`ERR_PRIMARY_ENTITY_REQUIRED`).
22. The referenced election actually governs the return being opened (`ERR_ELECTION_NOT_GOVERNING`).
23. The return's basis still matches its governing election at transmission time (`ERR_ELECTION_BASIS_MISMATCH`).
24. The remittance and the target return belong to the same registration (`ERR_FILING_REGISTRATION_MISMATCH`).
25. The remittance and the target return cover the same tax year (`ERR_PAYMENT_TAX_YEAR_MISMATCH`).
26. No return in the disclosure's lookback window has already been filed (`ERR_VDA_YEAR_ALREADY_FILED`).
27. The group has no other registration for the same state (`ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION`).
28. Activity exists for the year and members being apportioned (`ERR_NO_ACTIVITY_RECORDS`).
29. At least one weighted factor survives exclusion (`ERR_FACTOR_DENOMINATOR_ZERO`).
30. The computed factors are current (`ERR_FACTORS_STALE`).
31. The proposed election period does not overlap another (`ERR_ELECTION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`).
32. The proposed disclosure window does not overlap another (`ERR_VDA_WINDOW_OVERLAP`).
33. The installment slot is not already occupied (`ERR_DUPLICATE_INSTALLMENT`).
34. The filing deadline has not passed (`ERR_FILING_DEADLINE_PASSED`).
35. The extension window is still open (`ERR_EXTENSION_WINDOW_CLOSED`).
36. The notice response deadline has not passed (`ERR_RESPONSE_DEADLINE_PASSED`).
37. The appeal deadline has not passed (`ERR_APPEAL_DEADLINE_PASSED`).
38. The election's binding period permits revocation (`ERR_BINDING_PERIOD_ACTIVE`).
39. The extension prepayment threshold is met (`ERR_EXTENSION_PREPAYMENT_INSUFFICIENT`).
40. There is money available to refund (`ERR_REFUND_NOT_AVAILABLE`).
41. The disclosure's lookback length is permitted for its variant (`ERR_VDA_EXPOSURE_MISMATCH`).

Four consequences of this ordering are worth stating explicitly, because each of them is a case where two rules can fail on the same request. An extension requested on a `DRAFT` return whose prepayment is also insufficient fails at band 7 with `ERR_INVALID_FILING_STATE`, not at band 39. A factor computation run against a registration with no nexus whose sales denominator is also zero fails at band 15 with `ERR_NEXUS_NOT_ESTABLISHED`, not at band 29. A return whose basis contradicts its governing election *and* which is being transmitted after its due date fails at band 23 with `ERR_ELECTION_BASIS_MISMATCH`, not at band 34. And a refund claimed on a return that is neither `OVERPAID` nor backed by applied remittances fails at band 8 with `ERR_INVALID_FILING_STATE`, not at band 40.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Group and Its State Footprint

Everything in this system hangs off a **taxpayer group** — the combined-reporting family whose books supply the everywhere denominators of the apportionment formula. A `TaxManager` opens one by supplying its legal `name`, its `base_state_code` (the two-letter commercial-domicile state, exactly two uppercase letters), and the three everywhere totals: `everywhere_sales`, the group's total gross receipts everywhere and the base denominator of the sales factor; `everywhere_property`, its total average owned and capitalized-rented property everywhere; and `everywhere_payroll`, its total compensation everywhere. None of the three may be negative. An optional `fiscal_year_end_month` records the calendar month in which the group's tax year ends; it must be between 1 and 12 inclusive and defaults to 12. A newly created group starts with `open_registration_obligations` at 0 and `compliance_posture` at `COMPLIANT`, and its `nowhere_sales_pool` — described in Section 3.4 — starts at 0.

Member entities are added to the group one at a time. Each `LegalEntity` carries a `name`, an `incorporation_state_code` giving the two-letter state of incorporation or organization, an `entity_type` drawn from `CORP`, `LLC`, `PARTNERSHIP` and `DISREGARDED` and defaulting to `CORP`, and an `is_unitary_member` flag, defaulting to true, that declares whether this member is inside the group's unitary business. The member's `group_id` records the owning group. A member also carries `vda_shield_until`, which is null until a voluntary disclosure naming that member is executed; Section 3.9 explains what writes it.

A **state registration** is the group's profile for one taxing jurisdiction, and it is where almost all of the per-state law lives. Opening one requires the `state_code` (again exactly two uppercase letters), the `factor_formula` that names the state's weighting scheme (`EQUAL_WEIGHTED_THREE`, `DOUBLE_WEIGHTED_SALES` or `SINGLE_SALES_FACTOR`), the `tax_rate` applied to state taxable income (a fraction between 0 and 1 inclusive), the `throwback_rule` governing origin sales whose purchaser is not taxable in the destination state (`THROWBACK`, `THROWOUT` or `NONE`), and the `service_sourcing_rule` (`MARKET_BASED` or `COST_OF_PERFORMANCE`). Optional configuration fills in the rest of the state's regime: `economic_nexus_sales_threshold` (defaulting to 500,000) and `economic_nexus_employee_days_threshold` (defaulting to 15) set the two nexus tripwires, and neither may be negative; `due_month` (1 to 12, defaulting to 4) and `due_day` (1 to 28, defaulting to 15) set the original return due date; `extension_months` (0 to 12, defaulting to 6) sets how far a granted extension moves it; `estimated_installment_count` (1 to 4, defaulting to 4) says how many estimated installments the state requires; `safe_harbor_basis` selects the estimated-payment variant from `PRIOR_YEAR_100`, `PRIOR_YEAR_110` and `CURRENT_YEAR_90`, defaulting to `PRIOR_YEAR_100`; `prior_year_tax` and `current_year_estimated_tax` (both defaulting to 0, and neither of which may be negative) supply the two safe-harbor bases; and `state_permits_early_revocation`, defaulting to false, records whether the state tolerates revoking an election inside its binding period. The registration's `group_id` records the owning group. A group may hold at most one registration per state: a second registration carrying a `state_code` the group already has is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION`.

A fresh registration begins with `nexus_status` at `NONE`, `registration_status` at `NOT_REQUIRED`, a null `state_account_number`, a null `registered_on_date`, `calendar_rolled_through_year` at 0, and both money pools — `total_payments_received` and `unapplied_payment_balance` — at 0.

Registration maintenance is a first-class activity, because state law changes and because last year's estimates become this year's history. A `TaxManager` may adjust `safe_harbor_basis`, `prior_year_tax`, `current_year_estimated_tax`, `tax_rate`, `due_month`, `due_day`, `extension_months`, `economic_nexus_sales_threshold`, `economic_nexus_employee_days_threshold`, `state_permits_early_revocation`, `factor_formula`, `throwback_rule` and `service_sourcing_rule` in place; each such update advances the registration's `version` by 1, and the update advances it even when the payload names no recognized field and therefore changes nothing. Maintenance is not retroactive: estimated-payment rows already written keep the figures computed at their own creation instant, and the new configuration governs only later writes.

The raw facts underneath every apportionment computation are **activity records** — one row per member entity, per state, per tax year. A record names the `registration_id` of the state it occurred in, the `legal_entity_id` of the group member that conducted it, and the `tax_year` it belongs to, and then carries the economics: `destination_sales`, tangible-goods receipts delivered to purchasers in this state; `origin_sales`, tangible-goods receipts shipped *from* this state to out-of-state purchasers; `purchaser_taxable_in_state`, defaulting to true, saying whether the purchaser of those origin sales is taxable where the goods landed; `service_receipts`, receipts from services connected to this state; `cost_of_performance_state_code`, the state where the greater cost of performing those services occurred, read only when the registration sources services by cost of performance; `property_average_value`, the average value of owned real and tangible personal property in the state; `rented_property_annual_rent`, the annual rent paid for property in the state; `payroll_compensation`, compensation paid for services performed in the state; `employee_days_in_state`, the count of employee-days physically spent there; and `protected_solicitation_only`, defaulting to false, marking a member whose only in-state activity was P.L. 86-272 protected solicitation of orders. All of the numeric fields default to 0 when omitted. A record's `legal_entity_id` must name a member of the very same group that owns the registration in the path, or the write is rejected with `ERR_ENTITY_NOT_IN_GROUP`; and its `tax_year` must be no earlier than 2000, or the write is rejected with `ERR_ACTIVITY_YEAR_MISMATCH`. Activity records are immutable: writing one changes no derived figure anywhere until a nexus evaluation or a factor computation is run over it.

### 3.2. Nexus Determination and Registration Maintenance

A state registration carries two status axes that move independently. `nexus_status` answers "does this state have a claim on us?" and ranges over `NONE`, `PROTECTED_PL86272`, `ECONOMIC`, `PHYSICAL` and `VOLUNTARY`. `registration_status` answers "have we done the paperwork?" and ranges over `NOT_REQUIRED`, `REGISTRATION_REQUIRED`, `REGISTERED` and `WITHDRAWN`, of which `WITHDRAWN` is terminal.

A `TaxManager` drives the nexus axis by running a nexus evaluation for one tax year, supplying `as_of_tax_year`. The evaluation reads **every** activity record whose `registration_id` matches this registration and whose `tax_year` equals `as_of_tax_year` — all member entities participate, and there is no status filter, because activity records have no lifecycle. From that candidate set it derives four quantities. The **total state receipts** is the sum, over every candidate record, of that record's `destination_sales` plus its sourced service receipts, where a record's sourced service receipts equal its `service_receipts` outright when the registration's `service_sourcing_rule` is `MARKET_BASED`, and equal its `service_receipts` only when its `cost_of_performance_state_code` equals the registration's own `state_code` (and zero otherwise) when the rule is `COST_OF_PERFORMANCE`. The **maximum employee days** is the largest `employee_days_in_state` across the candidate records, or zero when the candidate set is empty. **Unprotected activity exists** when at least one candidate record has `protected_solicitation_only` false and has either positive `payroll_compensation` or positive `property_average_value`. **Protected activity exists** when at least one candidate record has `protected_solicitation_only` true.

Those four quantities feed a four-rung ladder whose first match wins, evaluated strictly from the top:

1. The result is `PHYSICAL` when unprotected activity exists **and** the maximum employee days is greater than or equal to the registration's `economic_nexus_employee_days_threshold`.
2. Otherwise the result is `ECONOMIC` when the total state receipts is greater than or equal to the registration's `economic_nexus_sales_threshold`. Receipts are compared at 2 decimal places, and the comparison is inclusive: a figure exactly equal to the threshold **does** attach economic nexus.
3. Otherwise the result is `PROTECTED_PL86272` when protected activity exists and the total state receipts is greater than zero.
4. Otherwise the result is `NONE`.

Because the ladder is ordered, a state with heavy physical presence resolves to `PHYSICAL` even when its receipts also clear the economic threshold, and a state whose only activity is protected solicitation resolves to `PROTECTED_PL86272` only when neither of the two rungs above it matched. An empty candidate set — no activity recorded for that year — yields `NONE`.

The evaluation writes the result into `nexus_status` and then runs a four-link cascade. When the result is `ECONOMIC` or `PHYSICAL` — whether the axis moved `NONE → ECONOMIC`, `NONE → PHYSICAL` or `PROTECTED_PL86272 → ECONOMIC` — the first link promotes a `registration_status` of `NOT_REQUIRED` to `REGISTRATION_REQUIRED`; the second link, caused by the first, increments the group's `open_registration_obligations` by 1; the third link, caused by the second, re-derives the group's `compliance_posture`, which turns from `COMPLIANT` to `EXPOSED` once the counter reaches 1; and the fourth link, caused by the third, demotes every `APPROVED` return of that group to `IN_REVIEW` and clears each one's `approver_user_id`. A result of `PROTECTED_PL86272` writes the nexus axis and stops there — protected solicitation creates no filing obligation, so `registration_status` is left exactly as it was. Every nexus evaluation advances the registration's `version` by 1.

Nexus is cyclic. A later year with thinner activity can move the axis `ECONOMIC → NONE`, and when it does, the registration axis is deliberately **not** rolled back: an obligation once opened is closed only by completing the registration or by withdrawing it. An evaluation may not be run at all against a withdrawn registration; that write, like every other write against such a row, is rejected with `ERR_REGISTRATION_WITHDRAWN`.

Completing a registration is how the obligation is discharged. A `TaxManager` supplies the `state_account_number` the revenue department issued; the axis moves `REGISTRATION_REQUIRED → REGISTERED`, `registered_on_date` is stamped from the logical clock, the group's `open_registration_obligations` decrements by 1, the group's `compliance_posture` is re-derived — returning `EXPOSED` to `COMPLIANT` once the counter reaches 0 — and the registration's `version` advances. No source-state precondition guards the call: a registration already at `REGISTERED`, or one still at `NOT_REQUIRED`, may be completed too, in which case the account number and `registered_on_date` are simply rewritten and the `version` advances. The counter and the posture move only when the row being completed was actually sitting at `REGISTRATION_REQUIRED`.

Withdrawal retires a state profile permanently. A `TaxManager` supplies a `reason`, and the axis moves to `WITHDRAWN` from `NOT_REQUIRED`, from `REGISTRATION_REQUIRED` or from `REGISTERED`. Withdrawal is refused while any return on that registration is still in flight — that is, while any of them has a `preparation_status` of `DRAFT`, `FACTORS_LOCKED`, `IN_REVIEW` or `APPROVED`; that refusal carries `ERR_INVALID_FILING_STATE`. The obligation counter is adjusted only when the row being withdrawn was actually counted: withdrawing a `REGISTRATION_REQUIRED` row decrements `open_registration_obligations` by 1 and re-derives the posture, while withdrawing a `NOT_REQUIRED` or `REGISTERED` row leaves the counter untouched. Either way the registration's `version` advances by 1. The withdrawn row stays readable forever; it simply accepts no further work.

Rolling the filing calendar is the last piece of registration maintenance. A `TaxManager` supplies a `through_tax_year`; the registration's `calendar_rolled_through_year` is set to that value, every `ELECTED` election on the registration whose `expires_after_tax_year` is strictly less than `through_tax_year` moves to `EXPIRED` with its own `version` advanced, and the registration's `version` advances by 1.

### 3.3. Reporting Elections: Combined, Separate and Consolidated

States differ on whether a group reports as one unitary business or entity by entity, and most of them bind the choice for several years once it is made. A `FilingElection` records that choice for one registration. A `TaxManager` files one by supplying the `election_basis` — `COMBINED_UNITARY`, `SEPARATE_ENTITY` or `CONSOLIDATED` — together with the `effective_tax_year` that is the first year covered and the `binding_years` count of tax years the election binds, counting the effective year itself. `binding_years` must be at least 1. The registration the election binds is recorded as its `registration_id`, and the service computes `expires_after_tax_year` as the effective year plus the binding count, less one; a five-year election effective in 2024 therefore expires after 2028. A new election starts in `ELECTED` with `reinstatement_count` at 0, a null `revocation_tax_year` and a null `reason`.

An election occupies a closed interval of integer tax years on its registration, and two elections on the same registration may never occupy overlapping intervals. The proposed interval runs from `effective_tax_year` through `effective_tax_year` plus `binding_years` less one; if it intersects the occupied interval of any other election on that registration, the request is rejected with `ERR_ELECTION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. An `ELECTED` election occupies the whole interval from its `effective_tax_year` through its `expires_after_tax_year`, and so does an `EXPIRED` one — expiry frees no years for a competing election. Only a `REVOKED` election contracts its interval, to `effective_tax_year` through `revocation_tax_year` less one. Every other election on the registration is tested whatever its status, and the intervals are compared as closed integer ranges: they intersect when the proposed start is at or before the other's end and the other's start is at or before the proposed end.

Revocation is the escape hatch, and it is fenced. A `TaxManager` supplies the `revocation_tax_year` that is the first tax year no longer covered, plus an optional free-text `reason`, and the election moves `ELECTED → REVOKED`. The revocation year must be later than the election's `effective_tax_year` — an election cannot be unmade before it began, and a value that is not later is rejected as a malformed payload with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Beyond that, the revocation year must be later than the election's `expires_after_tax_year`, *unless* the registration's `state_permits_early_revocation` is true; a revocation year that falls inside the binding period in a state that does not permit early revocation is rejected with `ERR_BINDING_PERIOD_ACTIVE`. A successful revocation records the `revocation_tax_year` and the `reason`, advances the election's `version`, and contracts the election's occupied interval to run from its `effective_tax_year` through the year immediately preceding `revocation_tax_year` — which is precisely what frees the later years for a different election.

Election status is cyclic rather than terminal. An election in `REVOKED` may be re-elected over a fresh binding period, and may be revoked and re-elected any number of times. Reinstatement takes a new `effective_tax_year` and a new `binding_years`, moves the election `REVOKED → ELECTED`, replaces both fields, recomputes `expires_after_tax_year` from them, clears `revocation_tax_year` back to null, increments `reinstatement_count` by 1 and advances the election's `version`. The proposed interval is checked for intersection against every other election on the registration exactly as at creation, and an intersection is again rejected with `ERR_ELECTION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. The one status that is genuinely final is `EXPIRED`, which only the calendar roll of Section 3.2 produces.

### 3.4. Opening a Return and Computing the Apportionment Factors

A `StateFiling` is one state's return for one tax year. Opening one requires the `tax_year`, the `filing_basis` actually used on the return (again `COMBINED_UNITARY`, `SEPARATE_ENTITY` or `CONSOLIDATED`), and the `federal_taxable_income` that is the starting point before any state modification, which carries no sign restriction and may be negative. The return's `registration_id` records the state and calendar it belongs to. Two optional foreign keys refine it: `election_id` names the `FilingElection` that governs this return's basis and is left null when the state has no election on file, and `primary_legal_entity_id` names the member entity the return is filed for. The primary member is **required** when `filing_basis` is `SEPARATE_ENTITY` and ignored otherwise; omitting it on a separate-entity return is rejected with `ERR_PRIMARY_ENTITY_REQUIRED`, and naming a member that belongs to some other group is rejected with `ERR_ENTITY_NOT_IN_GROUP`. A supplied `election_id` must belong to the same registration, must still be `ELECTED`, and must actually cover the return's year — that is, the return's `tax_year` must fall at or after the election's `effective_tax_year` and at or before its `expires_after_tax_year`. An election that fails any of those three tests is rejected with `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_GOVERNING`.

A newly opened return begins at `preparation_status` `DRAFT` and `payment_status` `UNPAID`, with `apportionment_variant` null and every derived money field at 0: `apportionment_percentage`, `modification_total`, `state_taxable_income`, `tax_before_credits`, `credits_applied_total`, `estimated_credits_applied`, `assessment_added_total`, `balance_due` and `amendment_count`. Its `original_due_date` is computed as midnight UTC on the registration's `due_month` and `due_day` in the year following the return's `tax_year`; a 2025 return on a registration configured for the fifteenth of the fourth month is therefore due at midnight UTC on 15 April 2026. Its `extended_due_date` stays null until an extension is granted, and `preparer_user_id`, `approver_user_id`, `confirmation_number` and `filed_on_date` all start null.

The heart of the return is the factor computation, run by a `StateAnalyst` or a `TaxManager` while the return is in `DRAFT` or `AMENDED`. The caller supplies exactly one input, the `apportionment_variant`, choosing between the `JOYCE` and `FINNIGAN` member-inclusion doctrines. The computation refuses to run at all against a registration whose `nexus_status` is still `NONE`, rejecting with `ERR_NEXUS_NOT_ESTABLISHED`.

The computation first fixes its **included members**. On a `SEPARATE_ENTITY` return the included set is the single member named by `primary_legal_entity_id`. On a `COMBINED_UNITARY` or `CONSOLIDATED` return it is every `LegalEntity` of the group whose `is_unitary_member` is true. The records in scope are then the activity records whose `registration_id` matches the return's registration, whose `tax_year` matches the return's tax year, and whose `legal_entity_id` is one of the included members. If that set is empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_ACTIVITY_RECORDS`.

The doctrine switch decides which of those records contribute to the **numerators**. A member is *taxable in the state* when at least one of the in-scope records for that member has `protected_solicitation_only` false. Under `JOYCE`, a record counts toward the numerators only if its own member is taxable in the state — a P.L. 86-272 protected member contributes nothing to the sales, property or payroll numerators while still sitting inside the everywhere denominators. Under `FINNIGAN`, every in-scope record counts regardless of its member's own taxability, so a protected member's destination sales, market-sourced services, property and payroll all flow into the numerators. On identical activity data, `FINNIGAN` therefore yields a strictly larger apportionment percentage than `JOYCE` whenever any included member is protected-only.

The **sales factor** is built from three pieces. A record's sourced service receipts are its `service_receipts` outright when the registration's `service_sourcing_rule` is `MARKET_BASED`, and are its `service_receipts` only when its `cost_of_performance_state_code` equals the registration's `state_code` (zero otherwise) when the rule is `COST_OF_PERFORMANCE`. A record's *nowhere* amount is its `origin_sales` when `purchaser_taxable_in_state` is false, and zero when that flag is true. Summing the nowhere amounts across the counting records gives the adjustment the state's `throwback_rule` decides what to do with: under `THROWBACK` that sum becomes `throwback_added` and is added to the sales numerator; under `THROWOUT` it becomes `throwout_removed` and is subtracted from the sales denominator; under `NONE` both are zero. Both `throwback_added` and `throwout_removed` are zero on the property and payroll rows, which have no such adjustment. The sales numerator is therefore the sum over the counting records of `destination_sales` plus sourced service receipts, plus `throwback_added`; the sales denominator is the group's `everywhere_sales` less `throwout_removed`.

The **property factor** numerator is the sum, over the counting records, of `property_average_value` plus eight times `rented_property_annual_rent` — the standard UDITPA convention of capitalizing rented property at eight times annual rent — and its denominator is the group's `everywhere_property`. The **payroll factor** numerator is the sum, over the counting records, of `payroll_compensation`, and its denominator is the group's `everywhere_payroll`.

Each of the three factors is then evaluated for inclusion and ratio. A factor is `included` when its denominator is greater than zero, and excluded when its denominator is exactly zero. An included factor's `ratio` is its numerator divided by its denominator, rounded half up to 6 decimal places; an excluded factor's `ratio` is 0. Base weights come from the registration's `factor_formula`: `EQUAL_WEIGHTED_THREE` gives sales, property and payroll a base weight of 1 each; `DOUBLE_WEIGHTED_SALES` gives sales a base weight of 2 and property and payroll 1 each; `SINGLE_SALES_FACTOR` gives sales a base weight of 1 and gives property and payroll a base weight of 0. The weight denominator is the sum of the base weights of the *included* factors only. If that sum is zero — no factor carrying a non-zero base weight survived exclusion — the request is rejected with `ERR_FACTOR_DENOMINATOR_ZERO`. Otherwise each included factor's `weight` is its base weight divided by that weight denominator, rounded half up to 6 decimal places, and every excluded factor's `weight` is 0. Renormalization is what makes a dropped factor harmless: on an equal-weighted state where the payroll denominator is zero, sales and property each end up weighted at 0.500000 rather than 0.333333.

The order of rounding here is pinned and is not negotiable. Each factor's `ratio` is rounded to 6 decimal places **first**; each rounded ratio is then multiplied by its `weight`; the three products are summed; and the sum is rounded half up to 6 decimal places once more to produce the return's `apportionment_percentage`. Summing unrounded ratios and rounding once at the end gives a different number and is not what this system does.

Each run of the computation creates or replaces exactly three `ApportionmentFactor` rows for the return — one with `factor_type` `SALES`, one `PROPERTY`, one `PAYROLL` — each carrying its own `filing_id`, `numerator`, `denominator`, `ratio`, `weight`, `included`, `throwback_added` and `throwout_removed`. The stored `numerator`, `denominator`, `throwback_added` and `throwout_removed` are money figures rounded half up to 2 decimal places, while the `ratio` is divided out of the *unrounded* numerator and denominator before being rounded to 6 decimal places. A run that replaces existing rows overwrites them in place, keeping their `id`s, and the three rows are always listed in the fixed order `SALES`, `PROPERTY`, `PAYROLL` rather than in any sorted order. The run also rewrites one group-level figure: `nowhere_sales_pool` becomes the sum of the nowhere amounts over the counting records when the registration's `throwback_rule` is `NONE`, and 0 otherwise. Sales that neither the origin state throws back nor the destination state taxes are, in that case, assigned to no state at all, and the pool is where the group can see how much income that is. The pool is rewritten in full by each run rather than accumulated across runs.

Running the computation sets the return's `apportionment_variant` to the doctrine that was used and then drives a four-link cascade through the whole liability chain, in this order: the first link writes `apportionment_variant` and `apportionment_percentage`; the second link, caused by the first, recomputes `modification_total`, `state_taxable_income` and `tax_before_credits`; the third link, caused by the second, re-runs the credit waterfall over every credit claim on the return, rewriting each claim's `applied_amount`, `carryforward_amount`, `expired_amount` and `status` along with the return's `credits_applied_total`; and the fourth link, caused by the third, recomputes `balance_due` and re-derives `payment_status`. The return's `version` advances by 1. Sections 3.5 and 3.6 give the arithmetic of each link.

### 3.5. State Modifications, the Credit Waterfall and the Balance

Federal taxable income is only the starting point; every state adds some things back and subtracts others. A `StateAnalyst` or a `TaxManager` records each of those adjustments as a `Modification` line on a return that is still in `DRAFT` or `AMENDED`. A line carries the return's `filing_id`, a `modification_code` naming the state's adjustment (`STATE_TAX_ADDBACK`, for example), a `direction` of either `ADDITION` or `SUBTRACTION` saying whether the line increases or decreases apportionable income, a `raw_amount` giving the unallocated federal-basis figure (which may not be negative), and an `allocation_ratio` between 0 and 1 inclusive giving the portion of that raw amount attributable to this particular return.

Each line's `line_amount` is its `raw_amount` multiplied by its `allocation_ratio`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and its `sequence_number` is its 1-based position among the return's modification lines ordered by `created_at` ascending. The return's `modification_total` is the signed sum of those per-line amounts: an `ADDITION` line contributes its `line_amount` positively, a `SUBTRACTION` line contributes it negatively, and the signed sum is itself rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Every modification line of the return participates — there is no status filter, because a modification has no lifecycle of its own. The rounding order is pinned: **round at the line, then sum**. The alternative discipline of summing the exact products and rounding once at the end is not what this system does, and generally differs by whole cents.

From there the liability follows in two rounded steps. Apportionable income is the return's `federal_taxable_income` plus its `modification_total`. The return's `state_taxable_income` is that apportionable income multiplied by the return's `apportionment_percentage`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The return's `tax_before_credits` is `state_taxable_income` multiplied by the registration's `tax_rate`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Both roundings are mandatory and ordered: state taxable income is rounded to cents *before* the rate is applied to it, and applying the rate to the unrounded product yields a different number. Adding a modification line recomputes `modification_total`, then `state_taxable_income` and `tax_before_credits`, then the credit waterfall, then the balance — in that order — and advances the return's `version` by 1.

State credits are claimed against the return and applied through an ordered waterfall. A `CreditClaim` carries the return's `filing_id`, a `credit_code` naming the state's credit (`RD_CREDIT`, for example), a `granted_amount` that must be greater than zero, a `priority_order` of at least 1 giving the claim's position in the waterfall, an optional `refundable` flag defaulting to false, and an optional `expires_after_years` defaulting to 5 that sets the carryforward life measured from the return's `tax_year`. Because every new claim re-prices every claim that came before it, the create is guarded by optimistic concurrency: `expected_filing_version` must accompany the payload and must equal the return's current `version`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FILING_VERSION` as described in Section 2.8. Claims may only be added while the return is in `DRAFT` or `AMENDED`, and that state check follows the concurrency guard. The waterfall runs inside the create, so the 201 response carries the claim as the waterfall has just priced it — already `FULLY_APPLIED`, `PARTIALLY_APPLIED`, `CARRIED_FORWARD` or `EXPIRED` rather than `PENDING` — while the new claim's own `version` remains 1, because that first pricing is part of its creation and not a later mutation of it.

The waterfall runs over **every** credit claim on the return from scratch on each execution — there is no status filter, and each run recomputes each claim from its `granted_amount`. Claims are ordered by `priority_order` ascending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending. A running remainder starts at the return's `tax_before_credits`. For each claim in order: a non-refundable claim's `applied_amount` is the lesser of its `granted_amount` and the current remainder, floored at zero so that a remainder driven negative by a negative `tax_before_credits` applies nothing, while a refundable claim's `applied_amount` is its full `granted_amount`, because a refundable credit pays out past the liability; the remainder then becomes the greater of zero and the previous remainder less that claim's `applied_amount`; the claim's `carryforward_amount` becomes its `granted_amount` less its `applied_amount`; and its `expired_amount` starts at zero. If the return's `tax_year` plus the claim's `expires_after_years` is strictly less than the calendar year of the current logical clock, the carryforward life has lapsed: the claim's `expired_amount` takes over the whole `carryforward_amount` and `carryforward_amount` drops to zero.

Each claim's `status` then follows from those three numbers. It is `FULLY_APPLIED` when `applied_amount` equals `granted_amount`; `PARTIALLY_APPLIED` when `applied_amount` is greater than zero but less than `granted_amount`; `EXPIRED` when `applied_amount` is zero and `expired_amount` is greater than zero; and `CARRIED_FORWARD` when `applied_amount` is zero and `carryforward_amount` is greater than zero. A claim begins life at `PENDING` and only the waterfall moves it out. The return's `credits_applied_total` is the sum of every claim's `applied_amount`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — a no-op rounding, since each applied amount already sits at 2 decimal places. One invariant is conserved across every run and across the amendment reversal of Section 3.7: for each claim, `granted_amount` equals `applied_amount` plus `carryforward_amount` plus `expired_amount`, rounded to 2 decimal places. A return's credit claims are listed in waterfall order — `priority_order` ascending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending — rather than in plain creation order, and its modification lines are listed in `sequence_number` order.

The settlement axis of a return is derived, never set by hand. The return's `estimated_credits_applied` is the sum of the `amount` of every `EstimatedPayment` whose `applied_filing_id` names this return and whose `status` is `APPLIED`, rounded to 2 decimal places. Its `assessment_added_total` is the sum of the `assessment_final_amount` of every `Notice` on this return whose `status` is `APPEAL_DENIED`, `APPEAL_SUSTAINED` or `CLOSED`, likewise rounded to 2 decimal places. The return's `balance_due` is then its `tax_before_credits`, less `credits_applied_total`, less `estimated_credits_applied`, plus `assessment_added_total`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

`payment_status` follows from that balance by a ladder that is evaluated on every recomputation, and that is skipped entirely once the return has reached `REFUND_CLAIMED`. A negative `balance_due` yields `OVERPAID`. A `balance_due` of exactly zero yields `PAID`. A positive `balance_due` yields `PARTIALLY_PAID` when `estimated_credits_applied` is greater than zero, and `UNPAID` when `estimated_credits_applied` is zero. The zero rung is exact to the cent: a balance of 0.00 is `PAID`, and a balance of 0.01 is `UNPAID`. The permitted movements along this axis are `UNPAID → PARTIALLY_PAID`, `PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID`, `UNPAID → PAID`, `PARTIALLY_PAID → OVERPAID`, `PAID → OVERPAID`, `PARTIALLY_PAID → UNPAID` when a remittance is detached and nothing remains applied against a still-positive balance, and finally `OVERPAID → REFUND_CLAIMED`, which is terminal.

### 3.6. Estimated Remittances, Safe Harbors and Extensions

States collect most of their money before the return is ever filed, and they penalize a taxpayer who underpays along the way. An `EstimatedPayment` records one installment sent to one state. A `Controller` or a `TaxManager` creates it with the `tax_year` the installment belongs to, the `installment_number` (a 1-based index from 1 to 4), the remitted `amount` (which must be greater than zero), the `paid_on_date` instant the remittance was made, and the `expected_registration_version` that guards the write as described in Section 2.8. The registration the money went to is recorded as `registration_id`, and `applied_filing_id` starts null because the money has not yet been pointed at a return. No two remittances may occupy the same installment slot: a second payment carrying a `registration_id`, `tax_year` and `installment_number` that already exist together is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_INSTALLMENT`.

The installment's `due_date` is midnight UTC on the fifteenth day of the fourth, sixth, ninth or twelfth month of its own `tax_year`, according to whether its `installment_number` is 1, 2, 3 or 4 — the familiar April, June, September and December calendar. `is_timely` is true when `paid_on_date` is at or before that `due_date`, and false otherwise.

The safe-harbor arithmetic is where the state's chosen variant bites. The **annual required amount** depends entirely on the registration's `safe_harbor_basis`. Under `PRIOR_YEAR_100` it is the registration's `prior_year_tax` multiplied by 1.00 and rounded to 2 decimal places. Under `PRIOR_YEAR_110` it is `prior_year_tax` multiplied by 1.10 and rounded to 2 decimal places — the ten-percent uplift larger states impose on bigger taxpayers. Both of those variants are anchored to last year's settled liability and are completely blind to the current year's economics: `current_year_estimated_tax` is not read at all. Under `CURRENT_YEAR_90` the annual required amount is instead the registration's `current_year_estimated_tax` multiplied by 0.90 — ninety percent of the annualized current-year estimate — rounded to 2 decimal places, and `prior_year_tax` is ignored entirely. Two registrations holding identical `prior_year_tax`, identical `current_year_estimated_tax`, identical `estimated_installment_count` and identical remittance histories will therefore produce different figures purely because their `safe_harbor_basis` differs.

From the annual requirement, this installment's `safe_harbor_required_amount` is the annual required amount multiplied by this installment's number and divided by the registration's `estimated_installment_count`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — a cumulative figure, so the third of four installments must have covered three quarters of the year's requirement. The cumulative amount actually remitted is the sum of the `amount` of every payment sharing this payment's `registration_id` and `tax_year` whose `installment_number` is less than or equal to this one and whose `status` is `RECEIVED`, `APPLIED` or `UNAPPLIED`, including the payment being written; refunded remittances are excluded from that sum. The payment's `underpayment_amount` is the greater of zero and the `safe_harbor_required_amount` less that cumulative figure, rounded to 2 decimal places, and its `underpayment_penalty` is the `underpayment_amount` multiplied by the five-percent underpayment rate, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Recording a remittance also increases the registration's `total_payments_received` and its `unapplied_payment_balance` by the remitted `amount`, and advances the registration's `version` by 1. Those two registration-level pools are defined by what they hold rather than by how they got there: `total_payments_received` is the sum of the `amount` of every estimated payment on the registration whose `status` is `RECEIVED`, `APPLIED`, `UNAPPLIED` or `REFUNDED` — every remittance the state ever received — while `unapplied_payment_balance` is the sum of the `amount` of only those whose `status` is `RECEIVED` or `UNAPPLIED`, the money not currently pointed at any return.

A remittance starts at `status` `RECEIVED` and moves through a cyclic application lifecycle. Applying it points it at a return: the caller supplies the target `filing_id`, the payment moves `RECEIVED → APPLIED` (or `UNAPPLIED → APPLIED` if it had been detached earlier), `applied_filing_id` is set, the registration's `unapplied_payment_balance` decreases by the payment's `amount` while `total_payments_received` stays untouched, the return's `estimated_credits_applied` increases by that amount, and the return's `balance_due` and `payment_status` are recomputed. Both the payment's `version` and the return's `version` advance. Application is refused unless the target return belongs to the same registration as the payment — otherwise `ERR_FILING_REGISTRATION_MISMATCH` — and unless the target return's `tax_year` equals the payment's `tax_year` — otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_TAX_YEAR_MISMATCH`. Money may not be applied to a return that is still in `DRAFT`; that refusal carries `ERR_INVALID_FILING_STATE` and is the last of the four checks, evaluated only after the remittance's own `status`, the registration match and the tax-year match have all passed.

Detaching is the exact compensating action. A `Controller` or `TaxManager` supplies a `reason`; the payment moves `APPLIED → UNAPPLIED`, `applied_filing_id` is cleared to null, the registration's `unapplied_payment_balance` increases by the payment's `amount`, the return's `estimated_credits_applied` decreases by that amount, and `balance_due` and `payment_status` are recomputed. Across an apply-and-detach round trip the registration's `total_payments_received` is invariant — it counts money received by the state, not money allocated by the practice. Detaching is refused, with `ERR_INVALID_FILING_STATE`, when the return the money is applied to has already reached `REFUND_CLAIMED`; that check follows the remittance's own `status` check.

Extensions are the other half of the calendar story. A `StateAnalyst` or `TaxManager` requests one on a return that has reached `FACTORS_LOCKED`, `IN_REVIEW` or `APPROVED`, supplying `estimated_tax_paid` — the amount the taxpayer represents as already remitted toward this return, which may not be negative. The service stamps `requested_on_date` from the logical clock, records the return's `filing_id`, computes `required_prepayment` as the return's `tax_before_credits` multiplied by 0.90 — the ninety-percent prepayment nearly every state demands as the price of extra time — rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and initializes `status` to `REQUESTED` with `new_due_date` null and `state_confirmation` null. If `estimated_tax_paid` is less than `required_prepayment`, the request is rejected with `ERR_EXTENSION_PREPAYMENT_INSUFFICIENT`; the boundary is inclusive, so an `estimated_tax_paid` exactly equal to `required_prepayment` passes. An extension may also only be requested while the return's `original_due_date` has not yet passed: a request handled strictly after that instant is rejected with `ERR_EXTENSION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, and a request handled exactly at it succeeds.

Acknowledgement is a `TaxManager` action and forks into two branches whose consequences differ sharply. The caller supplies a `decision` of either `GRANTED` or `DENIED`, plus an optional `state_confirmation` reference, which is written onto the request on whichever branch is taken and left null when it is omitted. On the `GRANTED` branch, the request moves `REQUESTED → GRANTED`, its `new_due_date` is set to the return's `original_due_date` advanced by the registration's `extension_months` months, **and** the return's `extended_due_date` is set to that same instant — which is what actually buys the filing deadline more room. On the `DENIED` branch, the request moves `REQUESTED → DENIED`, its `new_due_date` stays null, and the return's `extended_due_date` is left untouched. Both branches are terminal and both advance the extension request's `version` by 1; only the `GRANTED` branch also advances the return's `version`.

### 3.7. Freezing, Review, Transmission and Amendment

The preparation axis of a return runs `DRAFT → FACTORS_LOCKED → IN_REVIEW → APPROVED → FILED`, with two loops back — a rejection loop and an amendment loop — and no terminal state at all.

Freezing a computed return is what ends the data-entry phase: a `StateAnalyst` or a `TaxManager` moves it from `DRAFT` (or from `AMENDED`, after an amendment reopened it) to `FACTORS_LOCKED`, after which no further modification lines and no further credit claims may be created against it. Four preconditions guard the freeze. The return's registration must have a `nexus_status` other than `NONE`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_NEXUS_NOT_ESTABLISHED`. It must have a `nexus_status` other than `PROTECTED_PL86272`, because a state whose only claim rests on protected solicitation cannot assess net income tax whatever the registration paperwork says; that rejection carries `ERR_PL86272_PROTECTED`. The registration's `registration_status` must be `REGISTERED`, or the rejection carries `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_COMPLETE`. And the factors must be both present and current: at least one `ApportionmentFactor` row must exist for the return, and no activity record for the return's registration and tax year may have been created after the return's most recent factor computation. A freeze attempted before the factors have ever been computed, or after fresh activity has landed underneath them, is rejected with `ERR_FACTORS_STALE`. A successful freeze advances the return's `version` by 1.

Submitting for review is where the audit trail begins. A `StateAnalyst` or a `Controller` moves the return `FACTORS_LOCKED → IN_REVIEW`, optionally attaching a `preparer_note`, and the service records that caller's `X-User-ID` as the return's `preparer_user_id`. Approval is then a `Controller` or `TaxManager` action moving the return `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED`, optionally attaching an `approval_note`, and recording the approving caller's `X-User-ID` as `approver_user_id`. The one thing approval will not tolerate is the same person doing both jobs: when the approving caller's `X-User-ID` equals the return's stored `preparer_user_id`, the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Rejection sends the work back: a `Controller` or `TaxManager` supplies a `rejection_reason`, the return moves `IN_REVIEW → DRAFT`, `approver_user_id` is cleared to null, and modification lines and credit claims become creatable again. The revision loop `DRAFT → FACTORS_LOCKED → IN_REVIEW → DRAFT` may repeat without limit. An approved return may also be knocked back to `IN_REVIEW` without anyone asking, by the compliance cascade of Section 2.9; when that happens `approver_user_id` is cleared and re-approval is required.

Transmission is the moment the return becomes the state's business. A `Controller` or `TaxManager` supplies the `confirmation_number` the state's e-file system issued, the return moves `APPROVED → FILED`, the confirmation is stored and `filed_on_date` is stamped from the logical clock. Two preconditions apply. When the return carries a non-null `election_id`, that election must still be `ELECTED` and the return's `filing_basis` must equal the election's `election_basis`; a mismatch or a lapsed election is rejected with `ERR_ELECTION_BASIS_MISMATCH`. And the current logical clock must be at or before the return's `extended_due_date` when one has been set, or at or before its `original_due_date` when none has; the boundary instant itself is allowed, and a strictly later instant is rejected with `ERR_FILING_DEADLINE_PASSED`.

Amendment reopens a filed return and is the compensating action for the credit waterfall. A `Controller` or `TaxManager` supplies a `reason` and may supply a replacement `federal_taxable_income`, which is stored exactly as given — `state_taxable_income` and `tax_before_credits` are **not** re-derived from it here, and continue to carry the figures the last factor computation produced until that computation is run again; the return moves `FILED → AMENDED` and its `amendment_count` increments by 1. The reversal is total: every credit claim on the return returns to an `applied_amount` of 0, a `carryforward_amount` equal to its full `granted_amount`, an `expired_amount` of 0 and a `status` of `PENDING`, and the return's `credits_applied_total` returns to 0. A claim sitting at `FULLY_APPLIED` or `PARTIALLY_APPLIED` therefore goes back to `PENDING`. The return's `balance_due` and `payment_status` are then recomputed and its `version` advances. The conserved quantity survives the round trip untouched: for each claim, `granted_amount` still equals `applied_amount` plus `carryforward_amount` plus `expired_amount`. An amended return is once again eligible for the freeze, which is how the `AMENDED → FACTORS_LOCKED` edge exists at all.

Claiming a refund closes the settlement axis. A `Controller` or `TaxManager` may claim one, optionally naming a `refund_method`, on a return whose `payment_status` is `OVERPAID` and whose `preparation_status` is `FILED` or `AMENDED`. There must also be money to give back: at least one `EstimatedPayment` with `status` `APPLIED` must point at the return, or the request is rejected with `ERR_REFUND_NOT_AVAILABLE`. On success the return moves `OVERPAID → REFUND_CLAIMED`, every estimated payment whose `applied_filing_id` names this return moves to `status` `REFUNDED` — leaving the registration's `total_payments_received` unchanged, since the state did receive that money, while its `unapplied_payment_balance` is likewise unmoved because a refunded remittance counts in neither pool — and the return's `version` advances by 1. The refund claim does not re-run the balance: the return's `estimated_credits_applied` and `balance_due` stay at the figures they carried at the moment it was claimed, even though the remittances behind them are no longer `APPLIED`. `REFUND_CLAIMED` is terminal on the settlement axis.

### 3.8. State Correspondence: Notices, Appeals and Assessments

Sooner or later a revenue department writes back. A `Notice` is recorded against a filed return by a `Controller` or a `TaxManager` and carries the return's `filing_id`, a `notice_type` drawn from `MATH_ERROR`, `NEXUS_INQUIRY`, `PROPOSED_ASSESSMENT` and `FINAL_ASSESSMENT`, the `state_notice_number` the department assigned, the `assessed_tax` it proposes to assess (which may not be negative), and the `notice_date` printed on the letter, which anchors both deadline windows and the interest accrual. `assessed_tax` may not be negative and `response_due_days` may not be negative. No `preparation_status` precondition guards the creation itself: although a notice is in practice recorded against a return the state has already received, a notice may be recorded against a return in any preparation state, and it is only the *appeal* that insists the return was transmitted. An optional `response_due_days`, defaulting to 45, sets how long the taxpayer has to answer. From those the service computes `response_due_date` as the `notice_date` advanced by `response_due_days` days, and `appeal_due_date` as the `notice_date` advanced by 90 days — the appeal window is fixed at ninety days regardless of how long the response window is. A new notice starts at `status` `OPEN` with `response_round` at 0, `contested_amount` at 0, `sustained_amount` at 0 and `penalty_amount`, `interest_amount` and `assessment_final_amount` all at 0.

Answering a notice is cyclic. A `Controller` or `TaxManager` files a response with a `response_summary` and an optional `contested_amount` naming the portion of the assessed tax the taxpayer disputes; the notice moves from `OPEN` or from `INFO_REQUESTED` to `RESPONDED`, its `response_round` increments by 1, the contested figure is recorded — rounded half up to 2 decimal places when supplied, and left at whatever the notice already carried when it is omitted, so a later round that names no figure does not erase an earlier one — and its `version` advances. A response is refused once the logical clock has passed the notice's `response_due_date`, with `ERR_RESPONSE_DEADLINE_PASSED`; the boundary instant itself is allowed. The state may then come back asking for more: a `TaxManager` records the department's `info_requested` text, the notice moves `RESPONDED → INFO_REQUESTED`, and the `response_due_date` is reset to the current logical clock advanced by 45 days, reopening the window. The cycle `RESPONDED → INFO_REQUESTED → RESPONDED` may repeat any number of times, and `response_round` counts the rounds.

Appealing escalates. A `Controller` or `TaxManager` supplies the `appeal_grounds` and the notice moves to `APPEAL_FILED` from `OPEN`, from `RESPONDED` or from `INFO_REQUESTED`. Two preconditions apply beyond the source state: the logical clock must be at or before the notice's `appeal_due_date`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_DEADLINE_PASSED`, and the linked return's `preparation_status` must be `FILED` or `AMENDED` — one cannot appeal an assessment on a return that was never transmitted; that refusal carries `ERR_INVALID_FILING_STATE`, and it is evaluated *after* the appeal-window check, so a notice on an untransmitted return that is also out of time fails with `ERR_APPEAL_DEADLINE_PASSED`.

The appellate decision is a `TaxManager` action, and its two branches diverge in exactly one place: how much tax survives. The caller supplies an `outcome` of either `SUSTAINED` or `DENIED`, plus an optional `sustained_amount` giving the amount abated in the taxpayer's favour. On a `DENIED` outcome the notice moves `APPEAL_FILED → APPEAL_DENIED`, nothing is abated, and the sustained amount is 0 — any `sustained_amount` the caller supplied on that branch is discarded without being checked against `assessed_tax`. On a `SUSTAINED` outcome the notice moves `APPEAL_FILED → APPEAL_SUSTAINED`, the supplied `sustained_amount` is recorded — rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and taken as 0 when omitted — and it may not exceed the notice's `assessed_tax`; a figure that does exceed it is a malformed payload rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, checked after the notice's source state rather than before it. In both branches the final tax is the notice's `assessed_tax` less the `sustained_amount` — which for a denial means the full assessed tax stands.

Penalty and interest are then resolved in a strictly pinned three-step order, because the dependency between them looks circular and is not: the penalty is capped on a pre-interest base, while the interest base already contains the penalty. **Step one**, the notice's `penalty_amount` is the lesser of two figures — the final tax multiplied by ten percent, and a hard cap of 2,500.00 — rounded half up to 2 decimal places. **Step two**, the days outstanding is the whole number of days from the notice's `notice_date` to the current logical clock, discarding any fractional day, and the notice's `interest_amount` is the sum of the final tax and the `penalty_amount`, multiplied by an annual rate of six percent, multiplied by the days outstanding, divided by 365, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Interest is never computed on the final tax alone. **Step three**, the notice's `assessment_final_amount` is the final tax plus the `penalty_amount` plus the `interest_amount`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

The decision then drives a three-link cascade out of the notice and into the group's compliance picture. The first link increases the linked return's `assessment_added_total` by the notice's `assessment_final_amount`. The second link, caused by the first, recomputes the return's `balance_due` and re-derives its `payment_status`. The third link, caused by the second, re-derives the group's `compliance_posture` — and a `FILED` or `AMENDED` return carrying both a positive `balance_due` and a positive `assessment_added_total` forces the posture to `DELINQUENT`. Both the notice's `version` and the return's `version` advance by 1.

Closing ends the correspondence. A `TaxManager` supplies a `closure_reason` and the notice moves to `CLOSED` from `RESPONDED`, from `APPEAL_SUSTAINED` or from `APPEAL_DENIED`, advancing its `version` by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal. Note that a `CLOSED` notice continues to count toward the return's `assessment_added_total` exactly as an `APPEAL_DENIED` or `APPEAL_SUSTAINED` one does — closing the file does not un-assess the tax.

### 3.9. Voluntary Disclosure of Past Exposure

When a group discovers it has been doing business in a state for years without filing, the remedy is a voluntary disclosure agreement: the taxpayer comes forward, the state limits how far back it will look, and some of the penalty is waived. A `TaxManager` drafts one against a state registration, naming the `legal_entity_id` of the member disclosing the exposure, the `anchor_tax_year` that is the last year covered by the lookback window, the `lookback_years` count of tax years the disclosure covers (at least 1, counting the anchor year), the `exposure_variant` of either `LOOKBACK_LIMITED` or `FULL_EXPOSURE`, and the `estimated_exposure_tax` that is the taxpayer's own estimate of full unlimited-lookback tax exposure (which may not be negative). The disclosure's `registration_id` records the state it is made to. The named member must belong to the same group as the registration, or the draft is rejected with `ERR_ENTITY_NOT_IN_GROUP`; and the `anchor_tax_year` must be at least as large as `lookback_years`, or it is rejected with `ERR_ACTIVITY_YEAR_MISMATCH`.

The variant decides both the window and the money. Under `LOOKBACK_LIMITED` the `effective_lookback_years` equals the submitted `lookback_years`, and that submitted figure may not exceed four years — a longer request is rejected with `ERR_VDA_EXPOSURE_MISMATCH`. Under `FULL_EXPOSURE` the `effective_lookback_years` is forced to seven regardless of what `lookback_years` was submitted. The `lookback_end_year` always equals the `anchor_tax_year`, and the `lookback_start_year` is the anchor year less the effective lookback years, plus one — so a four-year window anchored on 2024 runs from 2021 through 2024 inclusive.

The `computed_exposure_tax` follows the same fork. Under `LOOKBACK_LIMITED` it is the `estimated_exposure_tax` pro-rated to the negotiated window — multiplied by the effective lookback years and divided by seven — rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and the `waived_penalty_amount` is a quarter of that pro-rated figure, again rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Under `FULL_EXPOSURE` the `computed_exposure_tax` is simply the `estimated_exposure_tax` rounded to 2 decimal places, and the `waived_penalty_amount` is 0.00: coming forward for everything buys no penalty relief under this regime. On identical inputs the two variants therefore differ in both the years covered and the money owed. A drafted disclosure starts at `status` `DRAFT` with `negotiated_tax` null, `negotiated_interest` 0, `settlement_total` 0, `executed_on_date` null and `shield_expires_at` null.

Disclosure windows are exclusive per member entity, and they are fenced by a two-year cooling period on each side. The proposed window — running from the first day of `lookback_start_year` up to, but not including, the first day of the year after `lookback_end_year` — is widened by 24 months at each end, and that widened window must not intersect the *un-widened* window of any other disclosure for the same `legal_entity_id` whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `EXECUTED`. An intersection is rejected with `ERR_VDA_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

The disclosure lifecycle is short. Submitting moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and advances its `version`. Withdrawing moves it to `WITHDRAWN` from either `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`, takes a `reason`, advances its `version`, and releases the agreement's window so it no longer blocks new disclosures for that member entity; a withdrawn disclosure is terminal, and an executed one may never be withdrawn.

Execution is the consequential step. A `TaxManager` executes a `SUBMITTED` disclosure, optionally supplying a `negotiated_tax` figure agreed with the state and a `negotiated_interest` figure, neither of which may be negative; when `negotiated_tax` is not supplied, the `computed_exposure_tax` stands in its place and is written into the agreement's own `negotiated_tax` field, which therefore never stays null past execution, and an omitted `negotiated_interest` is recorded as 0.00. The agreement's `settlement_total` is that negotiated tax plus the negotiated interest, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Execution is refused when the disclosure's window covers ground the group has already given up: if any return on the disclosure's registration has a `preparation_status` of `FILED` or `AMENDED` and a `tax_year` falling anywhere between `lookback_start_year` and `lookback_end_year` inclusive, the request is rejected with `ERR_VDA_YEAR_ALREADY_FILED`.

On success the disclosure moves `SUBMITTED → EXECUTED`, its `executed_on_date` is stamped from the logical clock, and its `shield_expires_at` is set to that instant advanced by 24 months. The disclosing member's `vda_shield_until` is set to the same instant — a two-year period during which the member's past exposure in that state is settled. A three-link cascade then reshapes the registration and the group: the first link sets the registration's `nexus_status` to `VOLUNTARY` — unconditionally, whatever that axis previously read, so a `PHYSICAL` or `PROTECTED_PL86272` registration is overwritten too — and, when and only when its `registration_status` is `REGISTRATION_REQUIRED`, moves that axis to `REGISTERED` with `state_account_number` set to the literal prefix `VDA-` followed by the agreement's own `id` and `registered_on_date` stamped from the logical clock; the second link, caused by the first, decrements the group's `open_registration_obligations` by 1; and the third link, caused by the second, re-derives the group's `compliance_posture`. The second and third links fire only when the first actually moved the registration axis — executing against a registration that was already `REGISTERED` or still `NOT_REQUIRED` leaves the counter, the posture and the account number alone and changes nothing but the nexus axis. Both the disclosure's `version` and the registration's `version` advance by 1. Executing a disclosure is, in other words, the second way an open registration obligation gets closed — the state has agreed to treat the taxpayer as registered.
