# Bearing & Power-Transmission Rebuild API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A Motion Industries service shop lives or dies on two things it cannot see directly: how much life is left in a bearing or gearbox that a customer has just dropped across the counter, and how much that worn-out core is actually worth as an exchange credit. Today those two judgements are made on paper travellers that circulate between the receiving counter, the metrology bench, the condition-monitoring group and the quoting desk. By the time a quote reaches the plant's reliability contact, the clearance readings that justified it are frequently a week old, the exchange-core credit was struck from the customer's own optimistic self-assessment rather than from bench-verified metrology, and nobody can say whether the aftermarket substitution on line four was ever engineering-approved. This document specifies the API that replaces that paper trail.

The system this document describes governs the full arc of a re-manufacturing job: receipt and provisional valuation of an exchange core, capture of metrology readings against a published clearance standard, remaining-life grading in two competing flavours depending on how critical the driven machine is, failure-cause coding derived from route vibration data, OEM-versus-aftermarket part approval by the paying account, bench scheduling under finite parallel capacity, an expedited emergency lane with a tiered surcharge, delivery and in-service tracking by asset, and finally pro-rata warranty settlement when a rebuilt unit fails before its warranty clocks run out. Every monetary consequence of those steps lands in a single append-and-void credit ledger, so that the shop can always reconstruct why an account's balance is what it is.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. The first is that money is never adjusted incrementally: balances are always recomputed in full from the surviving ledger notes, which is what makes issuing a note and later voiding it exactly conservative. The second is that a rebuild order carries **two independent state dimensions on the same record** — one driven by the shop floor and one derived from the customer's part-line decisions — and neither may be inferred from the other. Implementations that collapse them into a single status field will fail the workflow gates described in §3.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies across the whole API surface. The rules here are contractual: they fix header names, envelope shapes, paging mechanics, rounding discipline, the order in which rejections are evaluated, and the concurrency guards. Domain behaviour — what a grade means, how a surcharge tier is chosen — lives in §3.

The entire API is mounted under the `/shop` prefix, with the single exception of the test-driving logical clock described in §2.4.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request, without exception, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before anything else — before the path is resolved, before the body is parsed.

The five roles are `ShopAdmin`, `Planner`, `Inspector`, `ReliabilityEngineer` and `Customer`, spelled exactly so.

A `ShopAdmin` is the service-centre manager. Reference data, core receipt, the credit ledger, installations and warranty adjudication all belong to this role. A `Planner` owns work orders, part-line proposals, bench scheduling and the emergency lane. An `Inspector` owns metrology capture, inspection submission and the four-eyes acceptance of a peer's inspection. A `ReliabilityEngineer` files route vibration data and holds the sole authority to return a submitted inspection to the bench. A `Customer` is the plant reliability contact acting as the account itself: it decides OEM-versus-aftermarket substitutions, logs run hours, and files warranty claims — but only against its own resources.

Once the caller is authenticated, the role is checked against the endpoint's authorised-role list from the table in §4. A caller whose role is absent from that list is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). Several endpoints take an `action` or `decision` discriminator in the body, and the role check goes one level deeper on those: a role may be permitted to reach the endpoint yet not permitted to invoke that particular action, in which case the rejection is `ERR_ACTION_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_ROLE` (HTTP 403). The action-level permission map is exactly this:

- On the rebuild-order advance endpoint, the actions `QUOTE`, `SCHEDULE`, `START`, `COMPLETE` and `DELIVER` are all open to `Planner` and `ShopAdmin`.
- On the inspection advance endpoint, `SUBMIT` is open only to `Inspector`; `ACCEPT` to `Inspector` and `ShopAdmin`; `RETURN` to `ReliabilityEngineer` and `ShopAdmin`; `REOPEN` only to `Inspector`.
- On the part-line advance endpoint, `SUBMIT` and `REVISE` are open to `Planner` and `ShopAdmin`.
- On the core-unit advance endpoint, `REINSPECT` is open to `Inspector` and `ShopAdmin`, while `SCRAP` and `RETURN_AS_IS` are open only to `ShopAdmin`.
- On the bench-reservation advance endpoint, `CONFIRM` and `RELEASE` are open to `Planner` and `ShopAdmin`.
- On the installation advance endpoint, `RECORD_HOURS` and `FAIL` are open to `ShopAdmin` and `Customer`, while `REMOVE` is open only to `ShopAdmin`.
- On the warranty-claim advance endpoint, `REVIEW`, `REQUEST_INFO` and `ISSUE_CREDIT` are open only to `ShopAdmin`, while `RESUBMIT` is open to `Customer` and `ShopAdmin`.

**How `X-User-ID` binds to the data.** When `X-User-Role` is `ShopAdmin`, `Planner`, `Inspector` or `ReliabilityEngineer`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow step demands it — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `accepted_by_user_id` on an `Inspection`, and as `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a `WarrantyClaim` — and it compares two such recorded identifiers against each other for segregation-of-duties purposes, but it never compares an opaque identifier to any row's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer` record; ownership checks on any resource that ultimately references a `Customer` compare the resource's owning `customer_id` to `X-User-ID`.

That ownership rule bites in four places. A `Customer` caller deciding a part line must be the customer of that line's rebuild order. A `Customer` caller filing, resubmitting or otherwise advancing a warranty claim must be the customer of that claim's installation's rebuild order. A `Customer` caller logging run hours or reporting a failure on an installation must be the customer of that installation's rebuild order. And every read a `Customer` performs is confined to resources whose owning `customer_id` equals `X-User-ID`. A violation of any of these yields `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). The other four roles are not scoped to any account and may read everything.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint in this API is paginated by the same two query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the 1-to-100 range, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped.

Unless a subsection in §3 says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`; `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the filter before paging is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page. Single-resource responses omit those four fields.

Most list endpoints also accept domain filters alongside the paging parameters — `status`, `work_status`, `approval_status`, `kind`, `iso_zone`, `feature_code`, `customer_id`, `core_unit_id`, `clearance_standard_id`, `replacement_core_unit_id`, `customer_asset_id`, `rebuild_order_id`, `linked_vibration_reading_id` and `warranty_claim_id` all appear as optional query parameters on the endpoints noted in §4. Filters combine conjunctively.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Shop Clock

Every resource the API returns carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; an `id` offered in a request body is ignored, not rejected. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same timestamp format, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Two record types are deliberately leaner: a `Measurement` and a `VibrationReading` are immutable observations, so each carries only `id` and `created_at` — neither `updated_at` nor `version` is present on those two, and clients must not expect them.

Alongside real time, the server maintains a **logical clock** used for every business instant it stamps itself. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to wall-clock time. A `ShopAdmin` may move it to any instant, forward or backward, by supplying a `to_iso8601` value to the clock endpoint; the response echoes the new value as `current_utc` and no entity is mutated. Every system-stamped instant in the domain comes from this clock: `in_service_start_at`, `removed_at`, `failed_at`, `submitted_at`, `voided_at` and `promoted_at`.

### 2.5. Resolving Nested Paths

Many resources are addressed through a nested path — an account's assets, a bench's reservations, an order's part lines, an inspection's measurements, an installation's claims. Identifiers embedded in a path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error. Resolving an inspection's measurements when the inspection id is unknown therefore yields `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, never a generic not-found or an empty list.

Each entity type has its own not-found code: `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BENCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CORE_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_READING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PART_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTALLATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_CREDIT_NOTE_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404. The same codes are reused for foreign keys supplied in a request body, but body foreign keys are resolved later in the ladder (see §2.8) and in the order the endpoint declares its input fields. Where a nested creation takes its parent from the path — registering an asset under an account, for instance — the parent's `customer_id` is taken from the path and any value of the same name in the body is ignored.

### 2.6. Cents, Micrometres and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is expressed as whole cents in integer fields; there are no floating-point currency amounts and no minor-unit conversion. All clearance metrology is expressed in whole micrometres. Where a computation produces a fractional amount of money, the result is rounded half up to whole cents. Where a computation produces a dimensionless ratio or fraction, the result is rounded half up to six decimal places.

Two deviations from that default are normative and must not be generalised away. First, the credit cap in the quoting waterfall is taken by truncation toward zero — a floor — not by rounding half up. Second, the whole-minute duration of a bench window and the whole-day elapsed span used in warranty pro-rata are also floors. Every other rounding in this document is half up.

Rounding order is itself part of the contract wherever it is stated. When this document says that individual lines are rounded and then summed, summing first and rounding once is not an acceptable substitute: the two procedures produce different cents, and the tests distinguish them.

### 2.7. Guarding Against Stale Views

Three write operations mutate a parent record whose state a client may have read some time earlier, and all three are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against that parent's `version`.

Adding a part line to a rebuild order requires `expected_order_version` in the request body. It is a mandatory field, not an optional one. Its value must equal the current `version` of the `RebuildOrder` named in the path. If any other writer has advanced that order's `version` since the client last read it, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a part-line creation legitimately supersedes the order's approval rollup and its `part_revision_counter`, so a writer working from a stale view of the order would silently reopen an approval gate a previous writer had just closed.

Posting a manual ledger adjustment against an account requires `expected_customer_version`, likewise mandatory, and likewise compared against the current `version` of the `Customer` named in the path; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Every cascade that posts or voids an exchange-core or warranty note moves that customer's balances and bumps its `version`, so an adjustment computed against a stale balance would over- or under-credit the account.

Creating an installation requires `expected_asset_version`, mandatory, compared against the current `version` of the `CustomerAsset` identified by the body's `customer_asset_id`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A prior installation on the same asset legitimately supersedes the writer's view of what is mounted, and the changeover arithmetic described in §3.9 depends on that view.

These three are the only version-bearing inputs in the API. No other endpoint accepts an expected-version field, and none should be invented.

### 2.8. The Order of Rejection

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined: rules are evaluated in ascending priority order and the **first** rule that fails decides the response. The ladder is:

1. Header presence and role validity — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. Endpoint-level role authorisation — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. Action-level or decision-level role authorisation — `ERR_ACTION_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_ROLE` (403).
4. Existence of every identifier embedded in the path, left to right — the per-entity 404 codes listed in §2.5.
5. Request-body shape: every required field present, every enum inside its declared value set, and every numeric range honoured. That includes `qty` at least 1, `unit_cost_cents` at least 0, `labor_hours` strictly greater than 0, `run_hours_delta` strictly greater than 0, `parallel_slots` at least 1, `baseline_amplitude_g` strictly greater than 0, `amount_cents` at least 0, each band's upper micrometre bound strictly greater than its lower bound, `window_end_at` strictly after `window_start_at`, `required_feature_codes` non-empty with every entry present in `feature_bands`, a submitted `feature_code` present in the governing standard's `feature_bands`, `limit` within 1 to 100 and `offset` at least 0. Violations are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Unknown body fields are ignored rather than rejected, and so are client attempts to write computed or system-owned fields.
6. Existence of every foreign key supplied in the request **body**, resolved in the order the endpoint declares its inputs — again the per-entity 404 codes.
7. Customer ownership — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
8. Optimistic concurrency on the parent's `version` — `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
9. The target record's own state admitting the requested action — `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CORE_UNIT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_INSPECTION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PART_LINE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_RESERVATION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_INSTALLATION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE` and `ERR_CREDIT_NOTE_INVALID_STATE`, all HTTP 409.
10. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
11. Cross-record referential business rules — `ERR_INSPECTION_ALREADY_ACCEPTED`, `ERR_VIBRATION_ASSET_MISMATCH`, `ERR_CORE_NOT_GRADED`, `ERR_CORE_SCRAPPED`, `ERR_PART_APPROVAL_PENDING`, `ERR_NO_CONFIRMED_BENCH`, `ERR_WARRANTY_NOT_ELIGIBLE` and `ERR_UNIT_NOT_DELIVERED`, all HTTP 409.
12. Safety rules — `ERR_VIBRATION_LINK_REQUIRED`, `ERR_MEASUREMENTS_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_CLEARANCE_OUT_OF_SPEC`, `ERR_REBUILD_LIMIT_REACHED`, `ERR_AFTERMARKET_NOT_PERMITTED` and `ERR_CLEANROOM_BENCH_REQUIRED`, all HTTP 422.
13. Temporal rules — `ERR_BENCH_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409), `ERR_INSTALL_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (409) and `ERR_WARRANTY_EXPIRED` (422).
14. Uniqueness and other flat business constraints — `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIAL`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM`, `ERR_ASSET_OCCUPIED`, `ERR_QUOTE_STALE` and `ERR_EXPEDITE_NOT_AVAILABLE`, all HTTP 409.
15. The fiscal ceiling — `ERR_CREDIT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422).

Two orderings inside this ladder are worth calling out because they are counter-intuitive. A stale-version rejection at step 8 pre-empts every state check at step 9, so a client holding an out-of-date order version learns that fact before it learns the order is in the wrong state. And on installation creation the changeover-buffer rule sits at step 13 while the asset-occupancy rule sits at step 14, so an asset that both still carries a live unit *and* lies inside the changeover window of an earlier removal reports the temporal failure, not the occupancy failure.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and the Credit-Conservation Invariant

Several actions in this system touch more than one record. When they do, every record they touch has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, including records the caller never named. The response body returns the primary resource of the endpoint; the secondary movements are observable only by reading the affected records afterwards, and §3 states each of them explicitly.

The credit ledger is the strictest of these. A `CreditNote` is never edited in place and never deleted: it is created `POSTED` and, if it must be undone, transitioned to `VOID`. A customer's four balance fields — `core_credit_balance_cents`, `warranty_credit_balance_cents`, `manual_credit_balance_cents` and `total_credit_balance_cents` — are always recomputed in full from that customer's surviving `POSTED` notes, never adjusted by a delta. `core_credit_balance_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the customer's `POSTED` notes whose `kind` is `CORE_EXCHANGE`; `warranty_credit_balance_cents` the same sum restricted to `kind` of `WARRANTY_PRORATA`; `manual_credit_balance_cents` the same sum restricted to `kind` of `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT`; and `total_credit_balance_cents` is the sum of those three. Notes whose `status` is `VOID` contribute nothing to any stream, which is exactly what makes issue-then-void conservative to the cent. Every recomputation of the four balances increments that `Customer` record's `version` by 1. A note's `kind` is always chosen by the server, never by the client: only a manual adjustment can be posted directly, and it always carries `kind` of `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT`. A note also carries two optional back-references, and each is populated only on its own stream: `core_unit_id` is set on `CORE_EXCHANGE` notes and null on every other kind, while `warranty_claim_id` is set on `WARRANTY_PRORATA` notes and null on every other kind. A note's `memo` is client-supplied on a manual adjustment and system-written otherwise, and its `voided_at` and `void_reason` stay null until it is voided.

Because a customer's spendable balance feeds directly into the quoting waterfall, any movement of that balance while an order sits in `QUOTED` must be reflected in the order's pricing. Whenever a cascade posts or voids a note for a customer, every `RebuildOrder` of that customer whose `work_status` is `QUOTED` recomputes `core_credit_applied_cents` and `quote_total_cents` — and only those two. The parts subtotal, the labour figure, the surcharge and the quoted revision snapshot are deliberately **not** re-taken, because the order's parts and labour have not changed; only the money available to draw down has. Each order so touched has its `version` incremented by 1.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The subsections below follow a core through the shop, from the moment it lands on the receiving counter to the moment a warranty claim on the unit that replaced it is settled. Rules that belong to a step are stated at that step, which means the complete specification of any single operation is assembled from more than one subsection.

### 3.1. Opening the Books: Accounts, Machines, Reman Specs and Benches

Before any physical work can be recorded, four kinds of reference record must exist, and all four are created by a `ShopAdmin`.

An account is opened with a legal `name` for the plant being billed and a short `account_code` of between 1 and 32 characters that must be unique across all accounts; a reliability `contact_email` may be supplied and defaults to null when it is not. A newly opened account starts with all four ledger balances at zero. Re-using an `account_code` that already exists is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

Under an account, each driven machine the shop services is registered as an asset. The registration carries an `asset_tag` — the plant's own tag for the machine, such as a conveyor gearbox designation — which must be unique within that account but may freely repeat across accounts; a `criticality` that is either `CRITICAL` or `STANDARD`; a `duty_class` that is one of `CONTINUOUS`, `INTERMITTENT` or `STANDBY` and is carried for reporting only, influencing no calculation in this document; and an optional `site_code` identifying the plant site, which defaults to `MAIN`. The owning `customer_id` comes from the path. A repeated `asset_tag` inside the same account is `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`. The `criticality` value is the single most consequential piece of reference data in the system: it selects which remaining-life variant governs bench grading (§3.5) and it decides whether an aftermarket substitution needs an engineering waiver (§3.7).

A reman spec — the shop calls it a clearance standard — is published per bearing or gearbox `model_code`, which must be unique across all specs. The spec fixes `base_core_value_cents`, the undamaged exchange-core reference value in cents from which every core credit is derived and which may not be negative; `max_rebuild_count`, the number of prior rebuilds a core may already carry and still be re-manufactured, which may not be negative; `warranty_hours` and `warranty_months`, both strictly positive, being the run-hour and calendar warranty granted to a unit rebuilt to this spec; and `cleanroom_required`, a boolean defaulting to false that says whether rebuilds of this model must occupy a cleanroom bench. The spec also carries `required_feature_codes`, a non-empty list of the feature codes that must each be measured at least once before an inspection may be submitted, and `feature_bands`, a non-empty list of band objects. Each band object names a `feature_code` — unique within the list, and every entry of `required_feature_codes` must appear as one — together with `min_um` and `max_um` giving the inclusive in-specification band in micrometres, where `max_um` must be strictly greater than `min_um`; `rework_extension_um`, which widens that band by the same amount on both sides into the reworkable zone; `deduction_pct`, a fraction between 0 and 1 inclusive giving the share of `base_core_value_cents` deducted when that feature comes back out of specification; and `weight`, a strictly positive composite-scoring weight. A repeated `model_code` is `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

Benches are registered with a `bench_code` unique across the shop, a `parallel_slots` count of at least 1 saying how many reservations may occupy the bench at the same instant, and a `cleanroom` boolean defaulting to false. A bench's `committed_minutes` starts at zero and is maintained by the system thereafter; it is never client-supplied. A repeated `bench_code` is `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

A `ShopAdmin` may also move an account's ledger directly, posting a manual adjustment with an `amount_cents` of at least zero and a free-text `memo`, guarded by `expected_customer_version` as described in §2.7. The resulting note is created with `kind` of `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT` and `status` of `POSTED`, the account's four balances are recomputed, and the account's `version` advances. Manual generosity has a ceiling: the shop will not let an account's `total_credit_balance_cents` stand above 2,000,000 cents, so an adjustment is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_CAP_EXCEEDED` when the balance the account *would* hold after the note was posted exceeds that figure. The projected post-posting total is what is tested, not the balance before.

### 3.2. Receiving an Exchange Core and Valuing It on Sight

A core arrives across the counter and a `ShopAdmin` books it in against the paying account's `customer_id`, the governing spec's `clearance_standard_id`, and the manufacturer's `serial_no` stamped on the housing. The `serial_no` must be unique within that account; a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIAL`. The number of prior rebuilds already on the core may be supplied as `rebuild_count` and defaults to zero when it is not. The counter clerk also records the customer's own `declared_condition_grade`, which is one of `GRADE_A`, `GRADE_B`, `GRADE_C`, `GRADE_D` or `SCRAP`. The core's `model_code` is copied from the referenced spec at receipt and is never accepted from the client. The core's `status` begins at `RECEIVED`, `condition_grade` and `final_core_credit_cents` are both null until the bench has graded it, and `replacement_core_unit_id` is null.

Receipt immediately produces a **provisional exchange-core credit**, written to `provisional_core_credit_cents`. It is computed from the spec's `base_core_value_cents` in three movements. First a tier: the base value is multiplied by the grade multiplier — 1.00 for `GRADE_A`, 0.75 for `GRADE_B`, 0.45 for `GRADE_C`, 0.20 for `GRADE_D`, and 0.00 for `SCRAP` — and that product is rounded half up to whole cents. Second a decay for prior service: eight percent of the base value, multiplied by the core's `rebuild_count`, rounded half up to whole cents, is subtracted. Third, deficiency deductions are subtracted — but at receipt no inspection exists, so the deduction total is zero and this movement contributes nothing. The tiered figure less the decay less the deductions is then clamped: anything below zero becomes zero, and anything above the full `base_core_value_cents` is capped at the base value. Note that the grade used at receipt is the customer's `declared_condition_grade`, not any bench verdict.

That provisional figure is posted to the ledger at once. A note is created with `kind` of `CORE_EXCHANGE`, `status` of `POSTED`, an `amount_cents` equal to the provisional credit, `core_unit_id` pointing at the core just received, and a system-written `memo`; the account's four balances are recomputed and the account's `version` advances by 1. From that instant the customer can spend the credit against a quote, even though the bench has not yet touched the core.

Cores can be listed and filtered by their `customer_id`, their `clearance_standard_id`, their `status`, and by `replacement_core_unit_id` — the last of which makes the self-referential replacement chain traversable, since a scrapped core may nominate the received core that takes its place.

### 3.3. Route Vibration Data and ISO Severity Zoning

Condition-monitoring data enters the system independently of the shop floor. A `ReliabilityEngineer` (or a `ShopAdmin`) files a route reading against a machine's `customer_asset_id`, stamping the instant the data was collected as `reading_at`, together with four numbers: `overall_velocity_mm_s`, the overall vibration velocity in millimetres per second RMS, which may not be negative; `bpfo_amplitude_g` and `bpfi_amplitude_g`, the ball-pass outer-race and inner-race amplitudes in g, neither of which may be negative; and `baseline_amplitude_g`, the healthy-baseline amplitude in g, which must be strictly greater than zero because it is the denominator of both defect ratios. A zero or negative baseline is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Filing the reading classifies it immediately. The `iso_zone` is banded from `overall_velocity_mm_s` against three floors: a velocity below 1.40 is zone `A`; from 1.40 up to but not including 2.80 is zone `B`; from 2.80 up to but not including 4.50 is zone `C`; and 4.50 or above is zone `D`. Each floor is inclusive at its lower edge, so a reading of exactly 2.80 is zone `C`, not zone `B`. Alongside the zone, `defect_ratio_outer` is the outer-race amplitude divided by the baseline amplitude and `defect_ratio_inner` is the inner-race amplitude divided by the same baseline, each rounded half up to six decimal places. Those two ratios and the zone are what the failure-cause ladder in §3.5 consults.

A vibration reading is a historical observation and is never revised: it carries only its `id` and `created_at`. Readings are listed per asset and may be filtered by `iso_zone`.

### 3.4. The Bench Inspection and Its Metrology

Opening a bench inspection is what moves a core off the receiving shelf: the core transitions `RECEIVED → INSPECTING`, its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances. An `Inspector` or a `ShopAdmin` opens the inspection against a `rebuild_order_id`, and the transition only fires when the core sitting on that order is still `RECEIVED` and the order itself is in `DRAFT` or `QUOTED`. Opening a *second* inspection on an order whose core is already `INSPECTING` is allowed and creates a second inspection record, but no core transition fires and the core's state is left exactly as it was.

At creation the inspection may already carry `linked_vibration_reading_id`, pointing at the route reading that will drive failure-cause coding, and it may carry a free-text `inspector_note`. It may also carry a `cause_code_override`, an engineering assertion of failure cause that pre-empts the derived ladder entirely; permitted values are `OUTER_RACE_SPALL`, `INNER_RACE_SPALL`, `MISALIGNMENT`, `LOOSE_FIT`, `LUBRICATION_FAILURE`, `NORMAL_WEAR`, `WORKMANSHIP` and `UNDETERMINED`, and it defaults to null. Whenever a vibration reading is linked — at creation, or later when the inspection is submitted — the reading must have been taken on the same machine the order is destined for: the reading's `customer_asset_id` must equal the order's `customer_asset_id`, and a cross-asset link is rejected with `ERR_VIBRATION_ASSET_MISMATCH`. A new inspection starts with `status` of `DRAFT` and `revision_count` of 0.

Metrology is captured one reading at a time, and only while the inspection is `DRAFT`; adding a measurement to an inspection in any other state is `ERR_INSPECTION_INVALID_STATE`. Each reading names a `feature_code` and a `measured_um` value in whole micrometres. The `feature_code` must appear in the governing spec's `feature_bands` — the spec is reached from the inspection through its order to that order's core and thence to the core's clearance standard — and an unrecognised code is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The system stamps each reading with its owning `inspection_id`, taken from the path rather than the body, and with a `sequence_no`, a monotonic per-inspection counter starting at 1, which exists to break ties when two readings on the same feature share a creation instant. Measurements, like vibration readings, are immutable observations carrying only `id` and `created_at`.

Every reading is banded against its feature's clearance band the moment it is recorded, writing `band_result`, `deviation_ratio` and `feature_score`. Take the band's lower bound `min_um` and upper bound `max_um`; the width of the band is the upper bound less the lower bound. If the measured value is at least the lower bound and at most the upper bound — both edges are inclusive, so a reading landing exactly on either bound is in specification — then `band_result` is `IN_SPEC` and `deviation_ratio` is exactly 0. Otherwise the excess is how far the reading falls outside the band: the measured value less the upper bound when it sits above the band, or the lower bound less the measured value when it sits below. If that excess is less than or equal to the band's `rework_extension_um`, `band_result` is `REWORKABLE`; if the excess is greater than the extension, `band_result` is `OUT_OF_SPEC`. The reworkable shoulder is therefore inclusive of its far edge: a reading exactly one extension beyond the upper bound is still reworkable, and one micrometre further out is out of specification. For any reading outside the band, `deviation_ratio` is the excess divided by the band width, capped at 1, rounded half up to six decimal places. Finally `feature_score` is one hundred multiplied by the quantity one minus `deviation_ratio`, rounded half up to six decimal places — so an in-specification reading scores exactly 100 and a reading a full band-width or more outside scores exactly 0.

### 3.5. Submitting the Inspection: Remaining Life and Failure Cause

Submission is the moment the bench commits to a verdict, and it is guarded by three checks in the ladder order of §2.8. The inspection must be in `DRAFT`, or the attempt is `ERR_INSPECTION_INVALID_STATE`. It must have a linked vibration reading — and the submitting call may itself supply `linked_vibration_reading_id` to satisfy this, so the test is against the value in force after the call's own input is applied; if none is in force the rejection is `ERR_VIBRATION_LINK_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). And every `feature_code` listed in the governing spec's `required_feature_codes` must carry at least one measurement on this inspection; any shortfall, including an inspection with no measurements at all, is `ERR_MEASUREMENTS_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). Only an `Inspector` may submit. On success the caller's `X-User-ID` is recorded as `submitted_by_user_id`, both computations below run, and `status` becomes `SUBMITTED`.

Both computations read the same **governing set** of measurements, and that set must be selected identically in both places. For each distinct `feature_code` that has at least one measurement on *this* inspection, the governing measurement is the one with the greatest `created_at`; where two share that instant, the one with the greater `sequence_no` governs. The record's `id` is never consulted as a tie-break. Measurements belonging to a sibling inspection on the same order are excluded entirely.

**Remaining-life scoring** then computes two competing aggregations over that same governing set. The composite aggregation multiplies each governing measurement's `feature_score` by its band's `weight`, sums those products, and divides by the sum of the weights — a weighted average, in which one bad feature is diluted by healthy siblings. The conservative aggregation simply takes the smallest `feature_score` among the governing measurements, so the worst feature alone decides. Which one governs is pinned by the destination machine: when the order's asset has a `criticality` of `CRITICAL`, `life_score_variant` is `CONSERVATIVE` and the conservative aggregation is the base; otherwise `life_score_variant` is `COMPOSITE` and the weighted average is the base. On identical metrology the conservative variant yields a score less than or equal to the composite one, and can drop a core a whole grade band.

The rounding order in the next step is normative. The base aggregation is first rounded half up to a whole number, and only then are two integer penalties subtracted from it. The first penalty comes from the linked reading's `iso_zone`: zero for zone `A`, five for zone `B`, fifteen for zone `C`, and thirty for zone `D`. The second penalises prior service at six points for every rebuild the core has already had, that is, six multiplied by the core's `rebuild_count`. The result is clamped into the range zero to one hundred inclusive and written as `remaining_life_score`. That score is then banded into `remaining_life_grade` against four inclusive floors: a score of 80 or more is `GRADE_A`, 60 or more is `GRADE_B`, 35 or more is `GRADE_C`, 15 or more is `GRADE_D`, and anything below 15 is `SCRAP`.

**Failure-cause coding** runs on the same submission and writes `failure_cause_code` together with `cause_confidence`. It is a ladder evaluated strictly from the top, and the first clause that matches wins. First: if the inspection carries a `cause_code_override`, that value becomes the code and the confidence is `ASSERTED`. Second: if the linked reading's `defect_ratio_outer` is at least 3.0 and is also greater than or equal to its `defect_ratio_inner`, the code is `OUTER_RACE_SPALL` with confidence `HIGH`. Third: if the reading's `defect_ratio_inner` is at least 3.0, the code is `INNER_RACE_SPALL` with confidence `HIGH`. The ordering of those two clauses is the pinned tie-break — when the two ratios are exactly equal and both clear the floor of 3.0, the outer race wins. Fourth: if the reading's `iso_zone` is `D`, the code is `MISALIGNMENT` with confidence `MEDIUM`. Fifth: if the inspection has a governing measurement for the feature code `HOUSING_FIT` and that measurement's `band_result` is anything other than `IN_SPEC`, the code is `LOOSE_FIT` with confidence `MEDIUM`. Sixth: if the reading's `iso_zone` is `C`, the code is `LUBRICATION_FAILURE` with confidence `MEDIUM`. Seventh: if the core's `rebuild_count` is 2 or more, the code is `NORMAL_WEAR` with confidence `LOW`. If nothing above matches, the code is `UNDETERMINED` with confidence `LOW`.

A submitted inspection is not necessarily right, and the condition-monitoring group can say so. A `ReliabilityEngineer` — and only that role, apart from a `ShopAdmin` — may send a `SUBMITTED` inspection back with a `return_reason`, moving it `SUBMITTED → RETURNED`. Returning grades nothing: no core is graded, no credit note is written, and no balance moves. An `Inspector` then reopens the returned inspection, moving it `RETURNED → DRAFT` so that fresh measurements can be taken. Reopening increments `revision_count` by 1, clears `submitted_by_user_id` back to null, and wipes all five computed grading outputs — `remaining_life_score`, `remaining_life_grade`, `life_score_variant`, `failure_cause_code` and `cause_confidence` are all set back to null so that the next submission recomputes them from scratch.

### 3.6. Four-Eyes Acceptance, Final Valuation, and Undoing a Grade

Acceptance is the shop's four-eyes control and the single most consequential action in the system. Only an `Inspector` or a `ShopAdmin` may accept, the inspection must be `SUBMITTED`, and the accepting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `submitted_by_user_id` recorded at submission — an attempt to accept one's own work is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Because an order may accumulate several inspections, acceptance is also exclusive: if any sibling inspection under the same `rebuild_order_id` already sits in `ACCEPTED`, the attempt is `ERR_INSPECTION_ALREADY_ACCEPTED` (HTTP 409). Note the ladder order between these two — the duty check is evaluated before the exclusivity check.

Acceptance then unwinds as a five-hop cascade, and every hop is observable. The inspection itself moves `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` and records the caller as `accepted_by_user_id`. Caused by that, the core takes on the bench verdict: its `condition_grade` is set from the inspection's `remaining_life_grade`, its status moves `INSPECTING → GRADED`, and its `final_core_credit_cents` is computed. Caused by *that*, the ledger is corrected: the core's existing `POSTED` note of `kind` `CORE_EXCHANGE` — there is at most one, and if several somehow exist the latest by `created_at`, then by the greater `version`, is the one chosen — transitions to `VOID` with a `void_reason` of "superseded by bench grading", and a fresh note is created with `kind` of `CORE_EXCHANGE`, `status` of `POSTED`, an `amount_cents` equal to the core's `final_core_credit_cents`, `core_unit_id` pointing at this core, and a `memo` of "final exchange-core credit". Caused by that, the account's `core_credit_balance_cents` and `total_credit_balance_cents` are recomputed and the account's `version` advances. And caused by that in turn, every rebuild order of that same account whose `work_status` is `QUOTED` re-derives `core_credit_applied_cents` and `quote_total_cents` — those two figures only, as §2.9 requires — with each such order's `version` advancing. The customer's balance therefore moves by the difference between the final and provisional valuations, which is frequently negative when the bench disagrees with the drop-off declaration.

The **final exchange-core credit** uses the same three-movement shape as the provisional one but with two substitutions. The grade in use is the bench-verified `remaining_life_grade` from the accepted inspection rather than the customer's `declared_condition_grade`, run through the same multipliers: 1.00 for `GRADE_A`, 0.75 for `GRADE_B`, 0.45 for `GRADE_C`, 0.20 for `GRADE_D`, 0.00 for `SCRAP`, with the product rounded half up to whole cents. The rebuild decay is unchanged: eight percent of `base_core_value_cents` multiplied by `rebuild_count`, rounded half up to whole cents, subtracted. And the deduction loop is no longer empty. It runs over the governing measurements of the accepted inspection — one per `feature_code`, selected by exactly the rule of §3.5 — and for each one takes that feature's `deduction_pct` from the spec's bands. A measurement whose `band_result` is `OUT_OF_SPEC` contributes `base_core_value_cents` multiplied by that percentage, **rounded half up to whole cents on that line alone**. A measurement whose `band_result` is `REWORKABLE` contributes the same product taken at half strength — the base value multiplied by the percentage multiplied by one half — again rounded half up on that line alone. A measurement that is `IN_SPEC` contributes nothing. Those already-rounded lines are then added together. Summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule and produces different cents. The tiered figure less the decay less the summed deductions is finally clamped below at zero and above at the full `base_core_value_cents`.

A grade is not irreversible. An `Inspector` or `ShopAdmin` may order a re-inspection, moving the core `GRADED → INSPECTING` again, provided the core is currently `GRADED` and no rebuild order referencing that core has reached `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED` or `DELIVERED`; a violation of either condition is `ERR_CORE_UNIT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Re-inspection sends the accepted inspection back to `RETURNED` with a `return_reason` of "core re-inspection ordered", clears the core's `condition_grade` and `final_core_credit_cents` back to null, voids the core's `POSTED` `CORE_EXCHANGE` note, recomputes the account's balances, and re-derives `core_credit_applied_cents` and `quote_total_cents` on the account's `QUOTED` orders.

A `ShopAdmin` may scrap a graded core outright, moving it `GRADED → SCRAPPED`. Scrapping is permitted when the core's `condition_grade` is `SCRAP`; when it is any other grade, the request must carry a non-empty `override_reason` to justify the decision, and without one the attempt is `ERR_CORE_UNIT_INVALID_STATE`. The request may also nominate a `replacement_core_unit_id`, the core taking the scrapped one's place: the nominated core must exist — otherwise `ERR_CORE_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` — and must currently be `RECEIVED`, belong to the same `customer_id`, and be governed by the same `clearance_standard_id`, failing which the attempt is `ERR_CORE_UNIT_INVALID_STATE`. When nothing is nominated, `replacement_core_unit_id` stays null. Scrapping cascades widely: the core's `POSTED` `CORE_EXCHANGE` note goes to `VOID` with a `void_reason` of "core scrapped", the account's balances are recomputed and its `QUOTED` orders re-priced, and every rebuild order referencing that core whose `work_status` is `DRAFT`, `QUOTED` or `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED` with a `cancel_reason` of "core scrapped" — each of those orders in turn releasing its `HELD` and `CONFIRMED` bench reservations, after which the affected benches recompute `committed_minutes` and run queue promotion exactly as described in §3.8.

Finally, a core the customer would rather have back untouched can be returned as-is by a `ShopAdmin`, from either `RECEIVED` or `GRADED`, moving it to `RETURNED_AS_IS`. In both cases the core's `POSTED` `CORE_EXCHANGE` note transitions to `VOID` with a `void_reason` of "core returned as-is" and the account's balances are recomputed; when the return is from `GRADED`, the account's `QUOTED` orders additionally re-derive `core_credit_applied_cents` and `quote_total_cents`. Attempting either scrap or return-as-is from a state that does not admit it — for instance scrapping a core still in `RECEIVED` — is `ERR_CORE_UNIT_INVALID_STATE`. `SHIPPED`, `SCRAPPED` and `RETURNED_AS_IS` are the core's three terminal states, and no action moves a core out of them.

### 3.7. The Work Order and Its Two Dimensions

A rebuild order is opened by a `Planner` or a `ShopAdmin` against the paying account's `customer_id`, the physical core's `core_unit_id`, the destination machine's `customer_asset_id`, and an estimate of bench `labor_hours` that must be strictly greater than zero. A new order starts with `work_status` of `DRAFT`, `approval_status` of `PENDING`, `part_revision_counter` at 0, `expedited` false, and `expedite_reason`, `emergency_ref`, `quoted_part_revision`, `cancel_reason` and every quote figure — `parts_subtotal_cents`, `labor_cents`, `pre_credit_subtotal_cents`, `expedite_surcharge_cents`, `gross_total_cents`, `core_credit_applied_cents`, `quote_total_cents` and `covered_base_cents` — all null. There is one hard gate at creation: the core's `rebuild_count` must be strictly less than the governing spec's `max_rebuild_count`. A core that has already reached the ceiling is not re-manufacturable, and the attempt is `ERR_REBUILD_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 422).

The two state dimensions on that record evolve independently and must be stored independently. `work_status` is driven by shop-floor actions and runs `DRAFT → QUOTED → SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED → DELIVERED`, with `CANCELLED` reachable from any of the first four; `DELIVERED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. `approval_status` is never set directly by anyone: it is re-derived from the order's part lines after every part-line mutation, moving among `PENDING`, `PARTIAL`, `APPROVED` and `REJECTED` with no terminal state at all. Neither dimension constrains the other's storage, though the scheduling gate in §3.8 reads both.

Part lines are proposed by a `Planner` or a `ShopAdmin` and require `expected_order_version` (§2.7). The order must be in `DRAFT` or `QUOTED` to accept a new line; otherwise the attempt is `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`. A line names a `part_no`, a `source_class` that is either `OEM` or `AFTERMARKET`, a `qty` of at least 1, and a `unit_cost_cents` of at least 0; its `line_total_cents` is simply the quantity multiplied by the unit cost. A new line begins at `approval_status` of `PROPOSED` with `revision_count` at 0 and `decision_note` null. Creating the line increments the order's `part_revision_counter` by 1, re-derives the order's `approval_status`, and increments the order's `version` by 1.

Substitutions are where engineering authority bites. A line whose `source_class` is `AFTERMARKET` on an order whose destination asset has a `criticality` of `CRITICAL` is only permitted when the line carries a non-empty `engineering_waiver_ref`; without one the request is `ERR_AFTERMARKET_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 422). This rule guards two operations, not one: it applies both when the line is first proposed and when a rejected line is revised into an aftermarket part. On a `STANDARD` asset, aftermarket lines need no waiver, and `engineering_waiver_ref` defaults to null everywhere.

A proposed line is put in front of the account by a `Planner` or `ShopAdmin`, moving it `PROPOSED → SUBMITTED`; this is only possible while the parent order is in `DRAFT` or `QUOTED`, and the line must actually be `PROPOSED`, failing which the attempt is `ERR_PART_LINE_INVALID_STATE`. The account then decides. Deciding is the `Customer` role's exclusive action, it is only available on a `SUBMITTED` line, and the deciding `X-User-ID` must be the order's `customer_id` or the request is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The `decision` is either `APPROVE`, moving the line `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, or `REJECT`, moving it `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`; an optional `decision_note` is recorded either way. A rejected line is not dead: a `Planner` or `ShopAdmin` may revise it back `REJECTED → PROPOSED`, optionally rewriting any of `part_no`, `source_class`, `qty`, `unit_cost_cents` and `engineering_waiver_ref` on the same call. Revision recomputes `line_total_cents` from whatever quantity and unit cost are then in force and increments the line's own `revision_count` by 1, and it too requires the parent order to be in `DRAFT` or `QUOTED`. `APPROVED` is the line's only terminal state.

Every one of those four part-line mutations — creation, submission, decision and revision — increments the order's `part_revision_counter` by 1 and re-derives the order's `approval_status`. The derivation looks at **all** of the order's part lines with no status filter, and reads as follows. An order with no part lines at all is `PENDING`. An order in which every line is either `PROPOSED` or `SUBMITTED` is `PENDING`. An order in which every line is `APPROVED` is `APPROVED`. An order in which no line is `PROPOSED` or `SUBMITTED` and at least one line is `REJECTED` is `REJECTED`. Every other combination is `PARTIAL`.

The consequences of that rollup are worth spelling out, because they run in both directions. Deciding the first of several lines moves an order `PENDING → PARTIAL`; deciding the last outstanding line favourably moves it `PENDING → APPROVED` or `PARTIAL → APPROVED`; a single-line order whose only line is rejected moves `PENDING → REJECTED`, and an order whose remaining undecided lines all get rejected moves `PARTIAL → REJECTED`. Running backwards, adding a fresh `PROPOSED` line to an order whose existing lines are all approved drags it `APPROVED → PARTIAL`, and revising a rejected line back to `PROPOSED` moves the order `REJECTED → PARTIAL` when at least one sibling is still approved or rejected, or all the way back to `REJECTED → PENDING` when the revision leaves every line of the order in `PROPOSED` or `SUBMITTED`. In every one of those backward moves the order's `work_status` is left completely untouched — so an order that was comfortably `QUOTED` and fully approved can silently lose its approval and then fail the scheduling gate, even though nothing about its `work_status` changed.

### 3.8. Pricing the Job, the Emergency Lane, and Bench Capacity

Quoting is a `Planner` or `ShopAdmin` action that moves an order `DRAFT → QUOTED`, and it may be repeated on an order already in `QUOTED` — a re-quote leaves `work_status` where it is and simply recomputes everything from current data. The evaluation order below looks circular, because the credit cap is a percentage of a total that already contains a surcharge whose tier was chosen from a subtotal; it is not circular, and the sequence is normative.

First, the parts subtotal. For each part line of the order whose `approval_status` is `APPROVED` — and only those — the line's `qty` is multiplied by its `unit_cost_cents`, and those per-line products are summed into `parts_subtotal_cents`. Lines still `PROPOSED` or `SUBMITTED`, and lines that were `REJECTED`, contribute nothing. Second, the labour: the order's `labor_hours` is multiplied by the shop rate of $145.00 per hour and rounded half up to whole cents, giving `labor_cents`. Third, `pre_credit_subtotal_cents` is the parts subtotal plus the labour.

Fourth comes the emergency surcharge, and its tier is chosen on `pre_credit_subtotal_cents` — never on the gross total and never on the quote total. An order that is not expedited has an `expedite_surcharge_cents` of exactly 0. An expedited order takes a percentage of the pre-credit subtotal: thirty percent when that subtotal is below $1,000.00, twenty-two percent when it is at least $1,000.00 but below $5,000.00, and fifteen percent when it is $5,000.00 or above. Both tier floors are inclusive, which makes the schedule deliberately non-monotonic across the first edge: a subtotal of exactly $1,000.00 is charged at twenty-two percent while one cent less is charged at thirty percent. That percentage of the subtotal is rounded half up to whole cents and a flat emergency callout of $250.00 is added on top; the sum is `expedite_surcharge_cents`. Fifth, `gross_total_cents` is the pre-credit subtotal plus the surcharge.

Sixth, the exchange-core credit is capped. The cap is forty percent of `gross_total_cents`, truncated downward to a whole cent rather than rounded — this is the one place in the document where a floor replaces round-half-up on money — and it is surfaced on the quote read as `credit_cap_cents`. Seventh, the draw-down: `core_credit_applied_cents` is the smaller of the account's `total_credit_balance_cents` at that instant and the cap, and `quote_total_cents` is the gross total less the applied credit. Eighth and last, the warranty coverage base is snapshotted. `covered_base_cents` is `labor_cents` plus, for each `APPROVED` part line, that line's `qty` multiplied by its `unit_cost_cents` multiplied by 1 when the line's `source_class` is `OEM` or by one half when it is `AFTERMARKET`, with each such line rounded half up to whole cents before the lines are summed. Quoting also snapshots `quoted_part_revision` from the order's current `part_revision_counter`.

The order's pricing chain is readable at any time as a computed view that reports `work_status`, `approval_status`, `parts_subtotal_cents`, `labor_cents`, `pre_credit_subtotal_cents`, `expedite_surcharge_cents`, `gross_total_cents`, `credit_cap_cents`, `core_credit_applied_cents`, `quote_total_cents`, `covered_base_cents`, `quoted_part_revision`, `part_revision_counter` and a live `quote_stale` boolean, the last being true exactly when the snapshotted revision and the live counter disagree.

Expediting is how the emergency lane is entered, and it is available only once per order and only while the order is in `DRAFT` or `QUOTED` with `expedited` still false; any other attempt is `ERR_EXPEDITE_NOT_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409). A `Planner` or `ShopAdmin` supplies an `expedite_reason` and may supply the customer's `emergency_ref`, its emergency work authorisation reference. The flag `expedited` becomes true and both texts are recorded. What happens next depends on where the order stands: expediting an order still in `DRAFT` writes no quote figures at all, because none exist yet, whereas expediting an order already in `QUOTED` re-runs the entire eight-step chain so that `expedite_surcharge_cents`, `gross_total_cents`, `core_credit_applied_cents` and `quote_total_cents` all move together, and re-snapshots `quoted_part_revision`. In both cases the order's `work_status` stays where it was.

Bench windows are held by a `Planner` or `ShopAdmin` against a `rebuild_order_id` and a `bench_id`, with a `window_start_at` and a `window_end_at` that must be strictly later. A `queue_if_full` boolean, defaulting to false, says what to do when the bench is full. The reservation's `window_minutes` is the whole number of minutes between the two instants, truncated downward. Before capacity is even considered, cleanliness is: if the spec governing the order's core sets `cleanroom_required`, the reservation must target a bench whose `cleanroom` is true, and a reservation on an ordinary bench is `ERR_CLEANROOM_BENCH_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422).

Capacity uses **half-open** window semantics, with the window running from `window_start_at` inclusive to `window_end_at` exclusive. Two reservations overlap exactly when each one's start is strictly before the other one's end; exact adjacency, where one window's end equals the next window's start, is therefore not an overlap and is always admissible. For the requested window, count at each instant how many of the bench's existing reservations with `status` of `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` cover that instant, and take the peak of that count across the window; reservations that are `QUEUED`, `CONSUMED` or `RELEASED` are invisible to this count. If the peak is strictly less than the bench's `parallel_slots`, the new reservation is created with `status` of `HELD`. If it is not, and `queue_if_full` was true, the reservation is created directly with `status` of `QUEUED` — a creation-time landing, not a later transition. If it is not and `queue_if_full` was false, the request is `ERR_BENCH_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

A bench's `committed_minutes` is always the full sum of `window_minutes` over that bench's reservations whose `status` is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`; `QUEUED`, `CONSUMED` and `RELEASED` rows contribute zero, and the figure is recomputed from scratch on every reservation creation and every reservation transition rather than adjusted by a delta. Confirming a held window, which a `Planner` or `ShopAdmin` does to move it `HELD → CONFIRMED`, therefore leaves `committed_minutes` untouched, since both states count. Releasing a window moves it to `RELEASED` from `QUEUED`, `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`; releasing from `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` drops the bench's committed minutes by that window's `window_minutes`, while releasing a `QUEUED` row changes nothing because it never contributed. A confirmed window may not be released by hand while its order is `IN_PROGRESS`; the only way such a window leaves `CONFIRMED` early is as a consequence of the order being cancelled. Confirming or releasing a reservation whose state does not admit the action is `ERR_RESERVATION_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). `CONSUMED` and `RELEASED` are the reservation's terminal states.

Whenever a reservation leaves `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`, capacity has been freed and the queue is examined. The candidates are that bench's `QUEUED` reservations whose admission would now keep the peak occupancy strictly below `parallel_slots` under the same half-open rule. If there are no candidates, nothing at all happens. Otherwise exactly one winner is promoted: the candidate with the earliest `created_at`, ties broken by the earliest `window_start_at`, and any remaining tie broken by the smaller `window_minutes`; the record's `id` is never consulted. The winner moves `QUEUED → HELD`, its `promoted_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the bench's `committed_minutes` is recomputed to include the newly held window. The routine promotes at most one reservation per freed slot and does not loop.

Scheduling is the gate all of this feeds. A `Planner` or `ShopAdmin` moving an order `QUOTED → SCHEDULED` must satisfy four conditions, and they are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.8. The order must be in `QUOTED`, or the attempt is `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`. Its `approval_status` must be `APPROVED`, or the attempt is `ERR_PART_APPROVAL_PENDING` (HTTP 409). At least one of its bench reservations must be `CONFIRMED`, or the attempt is `ERR_NO_CONFIRMED_BENCH` (HTTP 409). And the quote must be current: `quoted_part_revision` must still equal `part_revision_counter`, so that any part-line mutation since the last quote makes the quote stale and the attempt `ERR_QUOTE_STALE` (HTTP 409). Because the staleness check sits at the bottom of that group, an order that is both unapproved and stale reports the approval failure. Re-quoting clears the stale condition.

### 3.9. Execution, Delivery, Service Life and Warranty Settlement

Starting work moves an order `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS` and drags the core `GRADED → REBUILDING` with it. Four conditions guard the start, again in ladder order. The order must be `SCHEDULED`, or the attempt is `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`. The order's core must be in `GRADED`, or the attempt is `ERR_CORE_NOT_GRADED` (HTTP 409). The core must not be scrap — neither a `condition_grade` of `SCRAP` nor a core `status` of `SCRAPPED` is acceptable — or the attempt is `ERR_CORE_SCRAPPED` (HTTP 409). And no governing measurement of the order's accepted inspection may have a `band_result` of `OUT_OF_SPEC`; a core with an out-of-specification feature cannot be re-manufactured to this spec, and the attempt is `ERR_CLEARANCE_OUT_OF_SPEC` (HTTP 422). Reworkable features do not block the start; only out-of-specification ones do.

Completing the work moves the order `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and the core `REBUILDING → REBUILT`, and it is the moment the core's `rebuild_count` is incremented by 1 — not at start, and never on a cancellation. Completion also frees the bench: every reservation of that order sitting in `CONFIRMED` transitions to `CONSUMED`, which causes the bench's `committed_minutes` to be recomputed, which in turn causes the queue-promotion routine of §3.8 to run on that bench. Delivering then moves the order `COMPLETED → DELIVERED` and the core `REBUILT → SHIPPED`.

Cancellation is available from `DRAFT`, `QUOTED`, `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, takes a `cancel_reason`, and unwinds in four hops. The order moves to `CANCELLED` with the reason recorded and every reservation of that order in `QUEUED`, `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` transitions to `RELEASED`. Caused by that, each touched bench recomputes `committed_minutes`. Caused by that, queue promotion runs on each touched bench, moving the winning `QUEUED` reservation to `HELD` and stamping its `promoted_at`. Caused by that, `committed_minutes` is recomputed once more so that the promoted window's minutes are included. Cancelling from `IN_PROGRESS` additionally compensates the core, reverting it `REBUILDING → GRADED`; because the completion step never ran, the core's `rebuild_count` is left exactly where it was.

Putting a rebuilt unit into service is a `ShopAdmin` action naming the unit's `core_unit_id`, the machine's `customer_asset_id`, the producing `rebuild_order_id` and the asset's `expected_asset_version` (§2.7). The unit must genuinely be finished and matched: the core's `status` must be `SHIPPED`, the order's `work_status` must be `DELIVERED`, and the order's own core and asset references must agree with the body's, failing any of which the attempt is `ERR_UNIT_NOT_DELIVERED` (HTTP 409); a scrapped core is separately rejected with `ERR_CORE_SCRAPPED`.

Two admission rules then apply to the asset, and their order matters. First the **changeover buffer**: gather the asset's installations whose `status` is `REMOVED_PLANNED` or `FAILED_IN_SERVICE`, and if there are any, take the latest of their off-service instants — a planned removal's `removed_at` or a failure's `failed_at`, whichever that record carries — with ties broken by the greater `created_at`. The new installation's moment, taken from the logical clock, must be at least 24 hours after that instant; the edge is inclusive, so exactly 24 hours later is admissible while anything earlier is `ERR_INSTALL_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409). Only then is **occupancy** examined: if the asset already carries an installation whose `status` is `INSTALLED` or `RUNNING`, the attempt is `ERR_ASSET_OCCUPIED` (HTTP 409). Because the buffer test runs first, an asset that both still carries a live unit and lies inside the changeover window reports the buffer violation, not the occupancy one.

A successful installation stamps `in_service_start_at` from the logical clock, sets `status` to `INSTALLED` and `run_hours` to 0, leaves `removed_at` and `failed_at` null, and copies three snapshots that freeze the warranty terms at that instant: `warranty_hours_snapshot` and `warranty_months_snapshot` from the core's governing spec, and `covered_base_cents_snapshot` from the producing order's `covered_base_cents`. The asset's `version` is incremented by 1. Later changes to the spec or to the order's pricing do not disturb these snapshots.

In-service tracking is then driven by a `ShopAdmin` or by the account itself. Logging run hours supplies a `run_hours_delta` that must be strictly greater than zero; the delta is added to the installation's `run_hours`, and the first such log moves the record `INSTALLED → RUNNING`. Subsequent logs are a self-loop: the record stays `RUNNING` and only `run_hours` grows. Reporting an in-service failure, which either a `ShopAdmin` or the account may do, moves the record to `FAILED_IN_SERVICE` from either `INSTALLED` or `RUNNING` and stamps `failed_at` from the logical clock. A planned removal, which only a `ShopAdmin` may perform, moves the record to `REMOVED_PLANNED` from either `INSTALLED` or `RUNNING` and stamps `removed_at`. Both of those are terminal, and every action attempted against a terminal installation — or against a state that does not admit it — is `ERR_INSTALLATION_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Once a record is terminal the asset is free again, subject only to the 24-hour changeover buffer measured from the off-service instant.

A warranty claim is filed by the account or by a `ShopAdmin` against an `installation_id` with a `claimed_reason`. The installation must be `FAILED_IN_SERVICE`; a claim against a running or removed unit is `ERR_WARRANTY_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409). Neither warranty clock may have run out: the installation's `run_hours` must be strictly less than its `warranty_hours_snapshot` **and** the elapsed whole days must be strictly less than `warranty_months_snapshot` multiplied by 30, both edges being exclusive — so exactly 8,000 hours against an 8,000-hour warranty is expired, and the claim is `ERR_WARRANTY_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). And an installation may carry only one open claim at a time: if a sibling claim on the same installation is in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`, the new filing is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM` (HTTP 409). Note that expiry is tested before duplication.

Filing the claim sets `status` to `SUBMITTED`, stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock, and **freezes the entire pro-rata working then and there**. The `elapsed_days` figure is the whole number of days between the installation's `in_service_start_at` and its `failed_at`, truncated downward from the elapsed seconds. The `hours_consumed_fraction` is the installation's `run_hours` divided by its `warranty_hours_snapshot`, capped at 1, rounded half up to six decimal places. The `calendar_consumed_fraction` is `elapsed_days` divided by the product of `warranty_months_snapshot` and 30 — a warranty month is exactly thirty days for this purpose — likewise capped at 1 and rounded half up to six decimal places.

Which basis governs is then decided. If the accepted inspection of the claim's rebuild order coded a `failure_cause_code` of `WORKMANSHIP`, the shop accepts that it built the unit badly: `governing_basis` is `GOODWILL` and `remaining_fraction` is forced to exactly 1, so the entire covered base is credited regardless of how much of the warranty was consumed. Otherwise the basis that consumed *more* warranty governs, so that the customer is never credited on the more generous clock: `governing_basis` is `HOURS` when `hours_consumed_fraction` is greater than or equal to `calendar_consumed_fraction` — ties go to hours — and `CALENDAR` when the calendar fraction is strictly the greater. In that case `remaining_fraction` is 1 less the greater of the two fractions, rounded half up to six decimal places. Finally `pro_rata_credit_cents` is `covered_base_cents_snapshot` multiplied by `remaining_fraction`, rounded half up to whole cents. All of this is computed once, at filing, and later run-hour logs do not change it.

The claim then moves through review. A `ShopAdmin` opens it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, and from there may ask the account for more detail, moving it `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED` with an `info_note`. The account (or a `ShopAdmin` acting for it) resubmits, moving it `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and overwriting `info_note` with the account's reply; a `Customer` caller doing so must own the claim. Adjudication is `ShopAdmin`-only, available only from `UNDER_REVIEW`, and carries a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` plus an optional `adjudication_note`; the caller is recorded as `adjudicated_by_user_id`. Segregation of duties applies here too: the adjudicating `X-User-ID` must differ from the `accepted_by_user_id` of the accepted inspection on the claim's rebuild order, and a match is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Approving moves the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` and merely re-affirms the frozen `pro_rata_credit_cents`; it does not recompute it against run hours logged since filing. Denying moves the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`, writes no credit note, and leaves the account's balances exactly as they were. Any action attempted from a state that does not admit it is `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

Issuing the credit is the final hop and is `ShopAdmin`-only, available from `APPROVED`. The claim moves to `CREDITED`; caused by that, a note is created with `kind` of `WARRANTY_PRORATA`, `status` of `POSTED`, an `amount_cents` equal to the claim's `pro_rata_credit_cents`, `warranty_claim_id` pointing at this claim, and a `memo` of "warranty pro-rata credit"; and caused by that, the account's `warranty_credit_balance_cents` and `total_credit_balance_cents` are recomputed and the account's `version` is incremented by 1. `DENIED` and `CREDITED` are the claim's terminal states.

The ledger's compensating half is available to a `ShopAdmin` at any time: any note may be voided with a `void_reason`. The note must currently be `POSTED`; voiding a note whose `status` is already `VOID` is `ERR_CREDIT_NOTE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Voiding sets `status` to `VOID`, stamps `voided_at` from the logical clock, recomputes the account's four balances from the surviving posted notes with the account's `version` advancing, and re-derives `core_credit_applied_cents` and `quote_total_cents` on every order of that account whose `work_status` is `QUOTED`.
