# Audio Post-Production API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An audio post-production facility lives or dies by the fidelity of its paperwork. A feature arrives with a picture cut that is declared locked, and on the strength of that declaration the facility commits dub-stage hours, books union voice talent for automated dialogue replacement, cuts licensed sound-effects material into stems, and coordinates conform passes with the composer. When a new cut arrives and the lock breaks, every one of those commitments becomes suspect at once: the conformed stems no longer match the picture, the deliverables cut from those stems are worthless, and the stage slots reserved to finish them should return to the pool rather than quietly bill the client. Facilities that track this on spreadsheets discover the divergence at delivery time, which is the most expensive possible moment to discover it.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces those spreadsheets. It governs the whole arc of a title through post — intake with its contractual delivery deadline and turnaround band, picture lock, dialogue editorial and ADR scheduling, sound design with licensed-library usage tracking, music editing and stem conforming, mix-stage booking with mixer assignment and client attendance (on stage or remote), deliverable creation against platform loudness specifications, quality control with a bounded remediation loop, delivery, and the final archival seal of session files and stems for future versions. Three concerns pervade the design. First, **money and hours are ledgers, not annotations**: every reservation, cancellation, release, supersession and completion must leave the project's stage-hour and billing figures exactly consistent, including after compensating reversals. Second, **licensing exposure is scored continuously**, in two variants that deliberately disagree, so that a comfortable intake snapshot can never authorise a delivery that a rights audit would fail. Third, **quality control is segregated**: the operator who prepared a deliverable may not be the operator who submits its measurements, and the operator who submits a round may not be the one who signs off on it.

Because the service is exercised by automated clients and by an integration test harness, it exposes a **logical clock** rather than reading wall-clock time. Every deadline comparison, licence-expiry check, session-window check and "now" stamp in this specification reads that logical clock. All endpoints are mounted at the service root — there is no versioned or namespaced URL prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every business request carries two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user; `X-User-Role` names the capacity in which that user is acting. If `X-User-ID` is absent, is an empty string, or holds nothing but whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` is absent, is not one of the six role names defined below, or names a role that the target endpoint does not authorise, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403); the role name is matched exactly and case-sensitively, so `mixer` is not `Mixer` and is treated as an unknown role. A `X-User-ID` that is present and non-blank is otherwise accepted verbatim, with no trimming and no format constraint. Authentication is checked before authorisation, so a request missing both headers reports `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. The two logical-clock endpoints described in §2.4 are the sole exception: they are test-driving utilities and are exempt from both header checks, and all six roles may call them.

The six roles are `FacilityAdmin`, `PostSupervisor`, `SoundEditor`, `Mixer`, `QcOperator` and `ClientReviewer`.

A `FacilityAdmin` runs facility operations — registering platform specifications, mix stages, mixers, voice talent and library assets, and revoking licences. A `PostSupervisor` owns the project spine: picture lock and lock breaking, stem-package creation, stage booking and release, deliverable preparation, remediation, delivery and archival. A `SoundEditor` owns editorial artefacts: music cues, conform passes, library-asset usage, and ADR session confirmation and recording. A `Mixer` is the re-recording mixer who accepts, opens and closes their own stage bookings. A `QcOperator` performs quality control, submitting measurements and evaluating rounds. A `ClientReviewer` is the client-side reviewer named on a project, who signs off remote reviews and reads the project's commercial ledger.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they are not interchangeable:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Mixer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Mixer` record — the identifier returned when the mixer was registered. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `Mixer` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`: a booking may only be accepted, opened or closed by the mixer whose `id` equals that booking's `mixer_id`, and any other mixer is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_MIXER` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `FacilityAdmin`, `PostSupervisor`, `SoundEditor` or `QcOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit anchor — as `prepared_by_user_id` on a deliverable at preparation, as `submitted_by_user_id` and `evaluated_by_user_id` on a QC report, and as `sealed_by_user_id` on an archive package — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ClientReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier and not an entity `id`. It is compared instead to the opaque `client_user_id` string recorded on the project at intake. A `ClientReviewer` whose `X-User-ID` does not equal the `client_user_id` of the project in question — the project reached directly when reading a project or its ledger, or reached through a booking when signing off a remote review — is rejected with `ERR_NOT_PROJECT_CLIENT` (HTTP 403). No entity anywhere carries `client_user_id` as its `id`; it is a bare string the facility is told at intake.

Segregation of duties adds one further constraint on top of role membership, described in full in §3.7: a `QcOperator` acting on a deliverable is barred from certain QC operations by comparison against `prepared_by_user_id` and `submitted_by_user_id`, and violations report `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the upper-case Latin letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9` — a 24-character string in total, freshly randomised on every response, in which lower-case letters never appear.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints only. On every other response — single-resource reads, creations, state-transition actions and all error responses alike — `meta` carries exactly `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp` and nothing else.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 when omitted and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` that is not an integer or falls outside 1 through 100 inclusive, or an `offset` that is not an integer or is negative, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`. `total_count` is the number of records matching the request's filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when records matching the filters remain beyond the current window. A window that begins past the end of the matching set is not an error: `data` is an empty array while `total_count` still reports the full match count and `has_more` is false. Every list filter named in §3 is an exact, case-sensitive comparison of the raw query-string value against the stored field value; a filter value that is not a member of the field's declared vocabulary is **not** a validation error and simply matches no records, an empty filter value likewise matches nothing rather than being ignored, and where a query-string parameter is supplied more than once only its first occurrence is read.

Ordering is fixed per collection and is never client-selectable. The top-level collections of projects, library assets, asset usages, stem packages, ADR sessions, mix bookings and deliverables are all returned **newest first** — ordered by `created_at` descending. The two nested read-side collections invert this because they are read as chronologies rather than as feeds: the music cues of a stem package are ordered by `cue_number` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending, and the QC reports of a deliverable are ordered by `round` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending. A project's archive packages — a collection that holds at most one row — are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock advances only when it is explicitly set, records created in the same test scenario routinely share an identical `created_at`, so ordering always carries a second, decisive key: the server stamps every record with a monotonically increasing creation sequence number at insertion, and ties on the leading key are broken by that sequence — descending for the newest-first collections, so that the more recently inserted row sorts first, and ascending for the three read-as-chronology collections. The sequence number is internal bookkeeping and is never serialised in any payload.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and Instant Formats

Nothing in this service reads the host machine's wall clock. A single server-wide **logical clock** supplies the value of "now" for every deadline comparison, licence-expiry evaluation, session-window guard and system-written timestamp. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it must not fall back to real time under any circumstance. An administrative capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and a companion capability reads it back, both returning the clock's value under the field name `current_utc`. Setting the clock mutates no entity and recomputes no derived field — it merely moves the reference instant forward or backward for subsequent requests.

Instants supplied by clients and instants emitted by the service in domain fields use ISO-8601 UTC at whole-second precision with a trailing `Z`, for example `2026-09-14T10:00:00Z`. Fractional seconds are neither required on input nor emitted on output for domain fields such as `delivery_deadline_utc`, `window_start_utc`, `window_end_utc`, `expires_at_utc`, `picture_locked_at_utc`, `conformed_at_utc`, `cleared_at_utc`, `actual_start_utc`, `actual_end_utc`, `client_signoff_at_utc`, `delivered_at_utc`, `evaluated_at_utc` and `sealed_at_utc`. A malformed instant in any request body is a schema violation and reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The accepted **input** grammar is deliberately wider than the emitted one, so that ordinary ISO-8601 spellings are not turned away: surrounding whitespace is trimmed; the date and time parts may be separated by `T`, `t` or a single space; the zone designator may be `Z`, `z` or a numeric UTC offset written `+HH:MM`, `-HH:MM`, `+HHMM` or `-HHMM`, which is converted to the equivalent UTC instant; a fractional-second part of any number of digits is accepted and kept internally at microsecond resolution for every comparison the service makes; and a bare calendar date `YYYY-MM-DD` is accepted and read as midnight UTC on that day. Everything else is the schema violation just described — in particular a non-string value, a time with no zone designator at all, and an out-of-range calendar or clock field. Emission never widens to match: writing an instant back out **truncates** any fractional part rather than rounding it, so a value supplied as `2026-09-14T10:00:00.750Z` is stored and compared with its 750 milliseconds intact yet reads back as `2026-09-14T10:00:00Z`. The envelope's own `api_request_timestamp` is the one exception and keeps the three-fractional-digit form shown in §2.2.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Revision Counters and Immutable Records

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix — a randomly generated version-4 UUID in the canonical lower-case hyphenated 8-4-4-4-12 form, 36 characters long — and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC. Clients never supply either. Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp, which is set equal to `created_at` at creation and refreshed to the logical clock's current instant on every mutation of that resource, and an integer `version` that begins at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource — including mutations caused by cascades originating elsewhere. Projects, mix stages, mixers, voice talents, library assets, asset usages, stem packages, music cues, ADR sessions, mix stage bookings and deliverables all carry the full set.

Three record types are deliberately immutable and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — they expose **no** `updated_at` and **no** `version` field at all, and no endpoint exists to update them. A **platform specification** is facility configuration: once registered it is never edited, so a deliverable's measurements are always judged against the same numbers. A **QC report** is an append-only evaluation record: one row per evaluated round, never rewritten, so the audit trail of a rejected mix survives its remediation. An **archive package** is the seal at the end of a project's life: written once and never touched again.

The `version` field is more than bookkeeping — it is the token clients present to prove they are not writing over a state they never saw. §2.7 defines that mechanism.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Reference Checking Order

URL path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, before any body content is examined. A request to a path of the form `/projects/{id}` or any path beginning `/projects/{id}/` whose identifier does not resolve to a project is rejected with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). The same pattern holds for the other five addressable spines: `/stem-packages/{id}` and anything beneath it reports `ERR_STEM_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`; `/deliverables/{id}` and anything beneath it reports `ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_FOUND`; `/mix-bookings/{id}/...` reports `ERR_MIX_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`; `/adr-sessions/{id}/...` reports `ERR_ADR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`; and `/library-assets/{id}/...` reports `ERR_LIBRARY_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`. All six are HTTP 404. Those six spines are the only path parameters with a dedicated code. The remaining addressable records are facility configuration and junction rows rather than spines — `/platform-specs/{id}`, `/mix-stages/{id}`, `/mixers/{id}`, `/voice-talents/{id}` and `/asset-usages/{id}` — and an identifier of theirs that does not resolve reports the catalogue's generic 404, `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`. Route matching itself tolerates a trailing slash, so `/projects` and `/projects/` are one and the same route. A request whose path matches no declared route at all is likewise answered with `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and a request to a declared path using a method that path does not declare is answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` carrying **HTTP 405** — the one place in this service where that error id does not carry the 400 status listed against it in §5.

Only after every path parameter has resolved does the server examine foreign keys carried in the request body. Those are checked in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, and the first one that does not resolve to an existing record is reported as `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Six write operations carry body foreign keys and are therefore subject to this rule: stem-package creation (`project_id`), music-cue creation on a stem package, asset-usage creation (`library_asset_id` then `stem_package_id`), ADR session creation (`project_id` then `voice_talent_id`), booking creation (`project_id`, then `mix_stage_id`, then `mixer_id`), and deliverable creation (`project_id`, then `platform_spec_id`, then `stem_package_id`, then `mix_booking_id`). Reference resolution only ever inspects a body field that is present and holds a non-empty string. A body foreign key that is absent, explicitly null, of some other JSON type, or the empty string is not a reference failure at all: it falls through to the schema rung and reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A payload naming a `project_id` of `""` or of `42` therefore answers with the schema code rather than `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`, even though the reference rung sits earlier than the schema rung in the §2.11 ladder.

Two nested resources take their parent identifier from the URL rather than from the body. A music cue's `stem_package_id` is supplied by the stem-package path segment it is created under, and a QC report's `deliverable_id` is supplied by the deliverable path segment its evaluation runs under. Likewise an archive package's `project_id` comes from the project path segment being sealed. Clients do not repeat these identifiers in the payload.

### 2.7. Stale-View Protection on Project and Stem-Package Writes

Three write operations can silently destroy work that the calling client has never observed, because each of them supersedes a sibling record as a matter of design. All three are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the **parent** record's `version`, and in all three the version field is a required, non-optional input.

A booking-creation request against `POST /mix-bookings` must carry `expected_project_version` holding the `version` of the target project as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the project's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because creating a booking supersedes the project's prior non-terminal booking of the same booking type; without it, a client working from a stale view of the project would discard a reservation it never saw.

A deliverable-creation request against `POST /deliverables` must likewise carry `expected_project_version` matching the target project's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here the destructive act is supersession of the prior non-terminal deliverable for the same platform specification, which would throw away QC progress the client has not observed.

A music-cue-creation request against `POST /stem-packages/{id}/music-cues` must carry `expected_stem_package_version` matching the current `version` of the stem package named in the path. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STEM_PACKAGE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A cue carrying an existing cue number supersedes the prior active cue at that number and can push a conformed package back to stale, so a stale-view create would revise a cue set the client never saw.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field. In particular, conform, confirm, start, complete, release, sign-off, QC submission, QC evaluation, remediation, delivery, revocation, picture lock, lock breaking and archival take no expected-version input, and supplying one is simply ignored rather than validated.

### 2.8. Money, Hours and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this service are integer counts of cents; there are no floating-point currency fields and no sub-cent residues. Every field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer. Hours, minutes and loudness quantities are decimal numbers carried to exactly **2 decimal places**. Wherever a decimal or monetary result must be reduced to its stated precision, the rounding method is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. This applies to stage hours, ADR minutes, stem minutes, loudness deviations, licence-risk scores and every cents figure alike. On the wire both kinds are JSON numbers and never strings: a `_cents` field is a JSON integer, and a two-decimal quantity is a JSON number whose textual form does not preserve trailing zeros, so 2.00 is serialised as `2.0` and 0.00 as `0.0` — the two-decimal discipline governs the value, not the number of characters printed. A two-decimal field that is not yet populated is emitted as `null`. The same discipline applies on the way **in**: a decimal a client supplies is quantised to two decimal places half up before it is stored, so a supplied 3.005 is stored as 3.01, while the field's stated range bounds are checked against the value exactly as supplied, before that quantisation.

Two operations in this specification are explicitly **not** round-half-up and must not be confused with it. Stage billing quantises elapsed time upward to the next quarter-hour increment — a ceiling, never a rounding. Licensed-material billing quantises usage duration upward to the next whole 30-second unit — again a ceiling. Both are described where they are used, in §3.6 and §3.4 respectively. One further arithmetic convention governs every duration this service computes: although instants are retained and compared at microsecond resolution (§2.4), a *duration* is always taken as the **whole-second** difference between its two instants, the residual microseconds being truncated toward zero before that difference is divided into minutes or hours. A window running from `10:00:00.900Z` to `10:45:00.100Z` therefore measures 2699 seconds, not 2699.2, and every minute, hour, quarter-hour and cents figure derived from it starts from that integer. Sub-second precision matters for ordering and for boundary comparisons; it never reaches an hours or cents figure.

Where a computation has several steps, the order of those steps is pinned in this document and is part of the contract. Reordering rounding relative to summation, or reordering a discount relative to a surcharge, changes the answer, and the pinned order is the correct one.

### 2.9. Project Posture: the Derived Write Gate

Each project exposes a third status field alongside its two lifecycle dimensions. `posture` is **derived**, never written by a client: any value a client supplies for it in a request body is ignored, and the field is recomputed after every mutation that could change the project's picture status, its count of stale stem packages, or its count of unlicensed asset usages.

The derivation reads two counts over the project's children. `stale_stem_count` is the number of stem packages belonging to the project whose status is `STALE`. `unlicensed_usage_count` is the number of asset usages whose clearance status is `UNLICENSED` and whose stem package belongs to the project with a status of `DRAFT`, `CONFORMED` or `STALE` — usages hanging off a `RETIRED` package are not counted. Given those, posture resolves in a strict order of dominance: if the project's `picture_status` is `LOCK_BROKEN`, posture is `FROZEN`; otherwise, if `unlicensed_usage_count` is at least 1 or `stale_stem_count` is at least 1, posture is `RESTRICTED`; otherwise posture is `OPEN`. Lock breaking dominates, so a project with both a broken lock and stale stems is `FROZEN` and not `RESTRICTED`.

Posture is a gate on child writes, and its two non-open values gate different amounts. A `FROZEN` project rejects every child write named in the following list, and that list is **exhaustive**: conform, music-cue creation, ADR session confirmation and recording, booking creation, confirmation, start and completion, deliverable creation, QC submission, QC evaluation, remediation, delivery and archival. A child write absent from that list carries no posture gate at all, so neither a `FROZEN` nor a `RESTRICTED` posture can be the thing that rejects it — stem-package creation and asset-usage creation among them, since opening a fresh reel and documenting the licensed material already cut into one are part of how a facility works its way back out of a frozen condition. A `RESTRICTED` project still permits conform, music-cue creation, ADR operations and booking operations — that is exactly how a facility digs itself out of a stale or unlicensed condition — but rejects the four operations that would let compromised material escape the building: QC submission, QC evaluation, delivery and archival. In every case the rejection code is `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN` (HTTP 409), for both the frozen and the restricted variety. An `OPEN` project applies no posture gate at all.

### 2.10. The Invalidation Cascade

A single fact — the picture changed, a licence was withdrawn, a cue was revised — can invalidate a chain of downstream commitments. This service performs that invalidation eagerly and completely, in one synchronous request, and the shape of the chain is identical wherever it originates. Whenever a stem package becomes `STALE`, three consequences follow in order.

First, the package's own row is updated: its `status` becomes `STALE`, its `stale_reason` is set to the code describing why, and the owning project's `stale_stem_count`, `conformed_stem_count` and `posture` are recomputed while the project's `version` increases by 1.

Second — **because** that stem package became stale — every deliverable whose `stem_package_id` is that package and whose `status` is one of `PREPARED`, `QC_SUBMITTED`, `QC_REJECTED`, `REMEDIATION` or `QC_PASSED` moves to `INVALIDATED` with `invalidation_reason` set to `STEM_STALE`. Deliverables already in `DELIVERED`, `INVALIDATED` or `SUPERSEDED` are left untouched — the cascade is conditional on the target being non-terminal, and a shipped deliverable is not retroactively unshipped.

Third — **because** each of those deliverables became invalidated — the mix stage booking identified by that deliverable's `mix_booking_id` moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to the string `DELIVERABLE_INVALIDATED`, but only if that booking's `status` is `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED`. Bookings in `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`, `RELEASED` or `SUPERSEDED` are left untouched. Each such release returns the booking's hours to the pool, so the project's `stage_hours_reserved`, `stage_hours_available`, `stage_cost_subtotal_cents` and `billing_total_cents` are recomputed.

Every cascade step is a real mutation of a real record: each touched entity refreshes its `updated_at` and increases its own `version` by 1, exactly as if it had been written directly. The **project** row is the one exception to that per-hop accounting, and the exception is deliberate: however many stem packages, deliverables and bookings a single request stales, invalidates and releases, each project drawn into the request has its derived fields recomputed once and its own `updated_at` and `version` moved exactly **once** for the whole request. Breaking a lock that stales three conformed packages, each carrying an open deliverable and a reserved booking, therefore leaves the project's `version` one higher than it was, not three — while every one of those nine child rows is individually bumped. Where a cascade reaches a *second* project, that project too is recomputed and bumped exactly once. §3.3, §3.4 and §3.2 each identify the events that fire this chain.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence

A single request frequently violates more than one rule. The service does not report the first violation it happens to notice, nor the most severe HTTP status: it evaluates checks in one **globally ordered ladder** and returns the error of the earliest violated rung, stopping there. The ladder is numbered below from the earliest rung to the latest. Two consequences of a single ordered ladder deserve emphasis. First, ordering is by rung number, not by status code, so an earlier rung returning HTTP 422 wins over a later rung returning HTTP 409 — the mixer-certification rung and the stage-hours rung, rungs 24 and 42, are the canonical example, and an uncertified mixer is reported even when the same reservation would also blow the project's hour budget. Second, a rung placed after a broader gate can be permanently shadowed by it, which is why the unlicensed-material guard at rung 25 is scoped to deliverable **preparation** only: an unlicensed usage already drives posture to `RESTRICTED`, and the posture gate at rung 16 already blocks QC submission, QC evaluation, delivery and archival on a restricted project, so attaching rung 25 to those operations would make it unreachable. Deliverable creation is not blocked by `RESTRICTED`, and that is precisely where the licensing guard has to bite.

1. **Rung 1** — `X-User-ID` absent or empty, on every endpoint except the two logical-clock capabilities: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401.
2. **Rung 2** — `X-User-Role` absent, not one of the six declared roles, or not authorised for this endpoint, on every endpoint except the two logical-clock capabilities: `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, 403.
3. **Rung 3** — the project path parameter does not resolve, on `/projects/{id}` and every path beneath it: `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, 404.
4. **Rung 4** — the stem-package path parameter does not resolve, on `/stem-packages/{id}` and every path beneath it: `ERR_STEM_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, 404.
5. **Rung 5** — the deliverable path parameter does not resolve, on `/deliverables/{id}` and every path beneath it: `ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_FOUND`, 404.
6. **Rung 6** — the mix-booking path parameter does not resolve, on every path of the form `/mix-bookings/{id}/...`: `ERR_MIX_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, 404.
7. **Rung 7** — the ADR-session path parameter does not resolve, on every path of the form `/adr-sessions/{id}/...`: `ERR_ADR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, 404.
8. **Rung 8** — the library-asset path parameter does not resolve, on every path of the form `/library-assets/{id}/...`: `ERR_LIBRARY_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, 404.
9. **Rung 9** — a foreign key in the request body does not resolve, checked in the order the endpoint declares its inputs, on stem-package creation, music-cue creation, asset-usage creation, ADR session creation, booking creation and deliverable creation: `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`, 404.
10. **Rung 10** — the acting `Mixer`'s `X-User-ID` does not equal the booking's `mixer_id`, on booking confirmation, booking start and booking completion: `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_MIXER`, 403.

12. **Rung 12** — a required input field is missing or null, of the wrong type, an unknown enum member, a negative quantity where a non-negative one is required, or a malformed ISO-8601 instant, on every endpoint that takes a request body: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400. A field present with an explicit JSON `null` is treated exactly as absent — a violation where the field is required, and the field's stated default where it is optional. The body is parsed without consulting `Content-Type`; an absent or zero-length body is read as the empty object `{}`, which succeeds on an endpoint whose every field is optional and fails at this rung on one with a required field; and a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object — an array, a string, a number, or malformed JSON — is a violation here. Fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected. Typing is strict in both directions: a field declared as an integer count must be a JSON integer, so `90.0` and `"90"` are both violations, whereas a two-decimal quantity accepts any JSON number, integral or fractional, but never a numeric string; a JSON boolean is never accepted where a number is expected, nor a number where a boolean is; a string field must be a string and is length-checked against its stated bounds, so an empty string fails a field whose minimum is one character; and enum members and array entries must be strings drawn from the declared vocabulary, with an array field rejected outright when it is not a JSON array or holds fewer than its required minimum number of entries. The three uniqueness constraints §3 declares live on this rung too and carry this rung's code rather than a conflict code of their own: a mix stage whose `room_code` is already taken, a library asset whose `asset_code` is already taken, and a stem package whose `reel_label` is already used within the same project are each rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Facility: Specifications, Rooms, People and the Sound Library

Before a single title can be taken in, a `FacilityAdmin` populates the facility's standing configuration. None of these records belong to a project; they are the fixed furniture against which projects are measured.

A **platform specification** encodes what one distribution channel will accept. Its `platform` names the channel and is one of `THEATRICAL`, `BROADCAST`, `STREAMING` or `INTERNATIONAL_ME`; `spec_label` is a human label of 1 to 120 characters such as "ATSC A/85 5.1"; `target_lufs` is the required integrated loudness in LUFS, a negative decimal from -40.00 through 0.00 carried to two decimal places; `tolerance_lu` is the permitted absolute deviation from that target in loudness units, at least zero and carried to two decimal places; `true_peak_max_dbtp` is the highest permitted true peak in dBTP, from -20.00 through 0.00; and `channel_layout` states the required layout of the delivered mix as one of `STEREO`, `5_1`, `7_1` or `ATMOS`. Two boundary conventions are baked into the specification and are stated here because they govern every QC verdict later: a deviation **exactly equal** to `tolerance_lu` passes, and a measured true peak **exactly equal** to `true_peak_max_dbtp` passes. The flag `requires_me_stems` declares whether an international music-and-effects stem set must accompany a submission; it defaults to false when omitted. The integer `max_remediation_rounds` bounds how many remediation cycles a deliverable governed by this specification may consume and defaults to **2** when omitted, with zero being a legal value that forbids remediation entirely. Finally `qc_turnaround_hours` records the contractual QC turnaround in whole hours, must be greater than zero, and defaults to **24** when omitted; it is informational and surfaced in the specification only. A specification is immutable once registered — there is no way to edit it — and it exposes neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

A **mix stage** is a physical dub stage. Its `room_code` is a short code of 1 to 32 characters, unique across all stages, and `display_name` is the room's human name of 1 to 120 characters. Its `hourly_rate_cents` is the stage rate per billable hour before any turnaround multiplier and must be greater than zero. The boolean `supports_remote_review`, false when omitted, records whether the room is wired for client remote review; a stage that is not so wired cannot host a booking whose client attendance is remote. The integer `changeover_minutes` is the mandatory buffer between two occupations of the same room, at least zero, defaulting to **45** when omitted — and it applies on **both** sides of every occupied window, not just after it, which is why an hour-long teardown effectively costs an hour and a half of room availability at each end.

A **mixer** record is registered with `full_name` (1 to 120 characters) and `certified_platforms`, a non-empty array drawn from `THEATRICAL`, `BROADCAST`, `STREAMING` and `INTERNATIONAL_ME`. The `max_daily_hours` figure caps the reserved hours a mixer may carry across the bookings that begin on any single UTC calendar day; it is carried to two decimal places, its accepted minimum is **0.01** as supplied — the bound is checked before the two-decimal quantisation of §2.8, so a supplied 0.004 is rejected rather than quantised down to zero — and it defaults to **10.00** when omitted. The identifier returned for a newly registered mixer is that mixer's `X-User-ID` for every subsequent call made in the `Mixer` role.

**Voice talent** is registered with `full_name` (1 to 120 characters) and `union_status`, either `UNION` or `NON_UNION`. Union terms are carried on the talent record itself, and both are **whole-minute integers** rather than two-decimal quantities, so a JSON `240.0` or `"240"` is a schema violation: `daily_cap_minutes` caps the total billable minutes the talent may carry across sessions beginning on one UTC calendar day, is an integer of at least 1, and defaults to **240** — four hours of recording — when omitted; `min_call_minutes` is the union minimum call, is likewise an integer of at least 1, defaults to **60**, and is ignored entirely for non-union talent — ignored in the sense that it never enters a non-union cost calculation, not in the sense that it disappears: a `NON_UNION` talent record still stores the supplied or defaulted figure and still returns it in its payload alongside `daily_cap_minutes`.

The **sound library** holds licensed effects, foley and library music. Each asset carries an `asset_code` of 1 to 64 characters that is unique across the library, a `kind` of `SFX`, `FOLEY` or `MUSIC_LIBRARY`, a `license_type` of `ROYALTY_FREE` or `RIGHTS_MANAGED`, a `territory_scope` of `WORLDWIDE` or `DOMESTIC`, a non-negative `fee_cents`, and an optional `expires_at_utc` expiry instant that is null — meaning perpetual — when omitted. A freshly registered asset has `status` set to `ACTIVE` and `revocation_reason` null. The two licence types accrue money differently, described in §3.4. Licence expiry is evaluated **lazily**, by comparing `expires_at_utc` against the logical clock at the moment a usage is recorded; expiry never rewrites the asset's `status`, so an expired asset still reads as `ACTIVE`. Only a deliberate revocation, which carries a mandatory `revocation_reason`, moves an asset `ACTIVE → REVOKED`. The library can be listed with an optional `status` filter and an optional `license_type` filter.

### 3.2. Project Intake and Two Independent Status Dimensions

A project is taken in by a `FacilityAdmin` or a `PostSupervisor` with a `title` and `client_name` of 1 to 200 characters each, a `client_user_id` of 1 to 64 characters naming the opaque user who will act as this project's `ClientReviewer`, and a contractual `delivery_deadline_utc`. Two commercial terms are optional: `turnaround_class` is one of `STANDARD`, `EXPEDITED` or `RUSH` and defaults to `STANDARD`; `stage_hours_authorized` is the mix-stage hours the client has authorised, is carried to two decimal places, has an accepted minimum of **0.01** as supplied — checked before the two-decimal quantisation of §2.8, exactly as the mixer's `max_daily_hours` is — and defaults to **40.00** when omitted. A newly created project sits in `picture_status` `NOT_LOCKED` with `picture_locked_at_utc` null and `picture_lock_revision` 0, in `pipeline_stage` `INTAKE`, with `posture` `OPEN`. Its ledgers start empty: `stage_hours_reserved` and `stage_hours_consumed` are 0.00 while `stage_hours_available` equals `stage_hours_authorized`; `stage_cost_subtotal_cents`, `adr_cost_cents`, `license_fee_accrued_cents` and `billing_total_cents` are 0; `license_risk_score_intake` and `license_risk_score_audit` are 0.00; and `unlicensed_usage_count`, `stale_stem_count` and `conformed_stem_count` are 0. Those are exactly the ledger figures the project resource itself carries, and the boundary is deliberate: the project payload exposes `stage_cost_subtotal_cents`, `adr_cost_cents`, `license_fee_accrued_cents` and the final `billing_total_cents`, but **not** `conform_fee_total_cents` and **not** the roll-up intermediates `pre_discount_total_cents`, `volume_discount_cents`, `discounted_base_cents` or `rush_surcharge_cents`, which surface only on the ledger read of §3.8. Projects can be listed with an optional `pipeline_stage` filter and an optional `posture` filter.

What makes a project unusual is that it carries **two independent status dimensions on one row**, and neither drives the other mechanically. `picture_status` tracks the cut: `NOT_LOCKED`, `LOCKED` and `LOCK_BROKEN`, and it is cyclic — a locked picture can break and a broken lock can be re-declared, indefinitely. `pipeline_stage` tracks production progress through `INTAKE`, `EDITORIAL`, `PREMIX`, `FINAL_MIX`, `QC`, `DELIVERED` and `ARCHIVED`, and it is strictly monotonic: it only ever advances, one step at a time, and it never regresses. Each pipeline advance fires only when `pipeline_stage` currently equals that advance's starting stage; if the triggering event occurs while the project sits at some other stage, `pipeline_stage` is simply left untouched, with no skip and no regression. `ARCHIVED` is the only terminal stage; `picture_status` has no terminal state.

Declaring picture lock is a `PostSupervisor` action that accepts an optional `picture_lock_note` of 0 to 2000 characters. The note is validated and then discarded: no project field records it and it appears in no response, so its only observable effect is the schema violation a longer or non-string value produces. It is permitted from `NOT_LOCKED` and from `LOCK_BROKEN` — the two transitions `NOT_LOCKED → LOCKED` and `LOCK_BROKEN → LOCKED` — and in both cases the project's `pipeline_stage` must not be `ARCHIVED`. Both stamp `picture_locked_at_utc` with the logical clock's current instant, increase `picture_lock_revision` by 1, recompute `posture`, and increase the project's `version` by 1. They differ in exactly two respects. The first lock, arriving out of `NOT_LOCKED`, additionally bubbles the other dimension: when `pipeline_stage` is `INTAKE` at that moment it advances `INTAKE → EDITORIAL`. A re-lock out of `LOCK_BROKEN` leaves `pipeline_stage` exactly where it is, and its posture recomputation is the interesting one — a re-locked project leaves `FROZEN` behind but only reaches `OPEN` if it is otherwise clean, becoming `RESTRICTED` instead when `stale_stem_count` is at least 1 or `unlicensed_usage_count` is at least 1. Re-locking does **not** re-conform anything: stem packages that went stale when the lock broke stay stale until a fresh conform pass runs on each of them.

Breaking picture lock is the most destructive single action in the system. A `PostSupervisor` supplies a mandatory `break_reason` of at least one character, which — like `picture_lock_note` — is validated and then discarded rather than persisted: the project payload exposes no field carrying it. The project must currently be `LOCKED`, and any other `picture_status` has no transition here and reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; its `pipeline_stage` must not be `ARCHIVED`. The transition `LOCKED → LOCK_BROKEN` sets `picture_status`, forces `posture` to `FROZEN` because a broken lock dominates every other posture input, and increases the project's `version` by 1. Then, **because** the picture status became `LOCK_BROKEN`, every stem package of the project whose status is `CONFORMED` moves to `STALE` with `stale_reason` set to `PICTURE_LOCK_BROKEN`, and the project's `stale_stem_count` and `conformed_stem_count` are recomputed. Then, **because** those packages became stale, the full invalidation chain of §2.10 runs on each of them: their non-terminal deliverables move to `INVALIDATED` with `invalidation_reason` `STEM_STALE`, and each such deliverable's booking, if it is `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED`, moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` `DELIVERABLE_INVALIDATED`, after which `stage_hours_reserved`, `stage_hours_available`, `stage_cost_subtotal_cents` and `billing_total_cents` are all recomputed. What breaking the lock explicitly does **not** do is move `pipeline_stage`: a project three quarters of the way to delivery does not fall back to editorial because a new cut arrived. The dimensions are independent.

The remaining pipeline advances are all system-driven consequences of work happening elsewhere, and each is described along the workflow that causes it: `INTAKE → EDITORIAL` on the first successful picture lock; `EDITORIAL → PREMIX` when a stem package conforms and the project's `conformed_stem_count` goes from 0 to 1; `PREMIX → FINAL_MIX` when a booking whose `booking_type` is `FINAL_MIX` is confirmed by its mixer; `FINAL_MIX → QC` when that same kind of booking completes; `QC → DELIVERED` when the project's first deliverable is delivered; and `DELIVERED → ARCHIVED` when the project is sealed. Each of these advances increases the project's `version` by 1 as part of the same mutation that caused it — the picture-lock bubble in particular is not a second, separate version bump.

### 3.3. Editorial: Stem Packages, Music Cues and the Conform Pass

Editorial work is organised one reel at a time. A `SoundEditor` or a `PostSupervisor` opens a **stem package** for a reel by naming the owning `project_id`, a `reel_label` of 1 to 32 characters that must be unique among that project's packages, and a `stem_layout` of `STEREO`, `5_1`, `7_1` or `ATMOS`. The optional boolean `includes_me_stems` records whether a music-and-effects stem set will be prepared for international delivery and is false when omitted. A new package lands in `DRAFT` with `conform_round` 0, `cue_count` 0, `total_stem_minutes` 0.00, `conform_fee_cents` 0, `conformed_at_utc` null and `stale_reason` null. Packages can be listed with optional `project_id` and `status` filters.

Music cues arrive next. A `SoundEditor` adds a cue under a stem package, supplying the `expected_stem_package_version` demanded by §2.7, a `cue_number` of 1 to 32 characters such as "1M3", a `duration_seconds` greater than zero, and a `source` of either `COMPOSER_ORIGINAL` or `LIBRARY`. When the cue comes from the composer, `composer_delivery_ref` carries the composer's session or stem name of 0 to 200 characters; it is optional and null when omitted. Every new cue is created `ACTIVE`, and cue creation always increases the parent stem package's `version` by exactly 1 — **exactly** 1 even when the same request also pushes that package from `CONFORMED` to `STALE`, because the stale transition and the cue insertion are one mutation of one row and the cascade of §2.10 adds no second bump to the package itself.

Cue revision is expressed through the cue number rather than through an edit operation. When an incoming cue's `cue_number` matches that of an existing `ACTIVE` cue on the same package, that existing cue moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` — a terminal state — while the incoming cue is created `ACTIVE` in its place. And revising a cue set under a package that had already been conformed invalidates that conform: when the package's `status` is `CONFORMED` at the moment of the revision, it moves `CONFORMED → STALE` with `stale_reason` set to `CUE_REVISED`, which fires the full chain of §2.10 — the package's non-terminal deliverables become `INVALIDATED` with `invalidation_reason` `STEM_STALE`, their `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` bookings become `RELEASED` with `release_reason` `DELIVERABLE_INVALIDATED`, and the project's `stale_stem_count`, `conformed_stem_count`, `posture`, stage-hour ledger and `billing_total_cents` are all recomputed. A cue added to a `DRAFT` or already-`STALE` package changes no status, and it leaves the owning project's row entirely alone: no derived project field is recomputed, the project's `updated_at` does not move, and its `version` does **not** increase. The project is recomputed and bumped only on the branch where the package actually went `CONFORMED → STALE`. The cues of a package can be listed with an optional `status` filter, in the `cue_number`-ascending order of §2.3 — an ordinary case-sensitive string comparison of the raw label, not a numeric one, so `"10M1"` sorts ahead of `"2M1"`.

The **conform pass** is the editorial milestone, performed by a `SoundEditor`. It is permitted out of `DRAFT` and out of `STALE` — the transitions `DRAFT → CONFORMED` and `STALE → CONFORMED` — and both require that the package hold at least one `ACTIVE` music cue, that the owning project's `posture` is not `FROZEN`, and that the project's `pipeline_stage` is not `ARCHIVED`. A package with zero `ACTIVE` cues is rejected with `ERR_EMPTY_STEM_SET`; a project whose lock is broken must be re-locked before any conform can succeed, because a `FROZEN` posture rejects the attempt with `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN`. The conform pass takes no request body at all.

Both conform transitions pin exactly the same three totals, and the **order in which they are computed is load-bearing**. The input set is every music cue on the package whose status is `ACTIVE`; superseded cues are excluded entirely. First, `cue_count` is the number of cues in that input set. Second, each cue's contribution is converted to minutes individually, by dividing that cue's `duration_seconds` by 60 and rounding the result half up to two decimal places. Third, `total_stem_minutes` is the sum of those already-rounded per-cue minute values, with no further rounding applied to the sum. Fourth, `conform_fee_cents` is the conform labour charge: `total_stem_minutes` multiplied by the facility's conform rate of **1500 cents per stem minute** — fifteen dollars a minute — with the product rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Summing raw seconds first and dividing once at the end is **forbidden** and produces a different, wrong answer: three cues of 95 seconds each give 1.58 plus 1.58 plus 1.58, which is 4.74 minutes and a `conform_fee_cents` of 7110, whereas summing to 285 seconds and dividing once yields 4.75 minutes and 7125 cents. That fifteen-cent gap is the observable difference between the correct order and the forbidden one. As a second reference point, a single cue of 90 seconds gives `total_stem_minutes` 1.50 and `conform_fee_cents` 2250.

Beyond pinning those totals, every successful conform increases `conform_round` by 1, stamps `conformed_at_utc` with the logical clock's current instant, clears `stale_reason` back to null, and recomputes the project's `conformed_stem_count` and `stale_stem_count`. A re-conform out of `STALE` decrements the stale count and increments the conformed count, and its posture recomputation can lift a project from `RESTRICTED` back to `OPEN` — but only once `stale_stem_count` has reached 0 **and** `unlicensed_usage_count` is also 0. When the conformed count moves from 0 to 1 and the project sits at `EDITORIAL`, `pipeline_stage` advances to `PREMIX`. Either way the project's `version` increases by 1. One thing a re-conform pointedly does **not** do is resurrect deliverables that the invalidation chain destroyed: those remain `INVALIDATED` forever, and a fresh deliverable must be prepared.

A stem package's last state is `RETIRED`, which is terminal and reachable only through archival, described in §3.8.

### 3.4. Licensed Material: Clearance, Accrual and Withdrawal

Cutting a library asset into a reel creates an **asset usage** — a junction record joining one library asset to one stem package. A `SoundEditor` records it by supplying the `library_asset_id`, the `stem_package_id`, a `usage_seconds` duration greater than zero measuring the on-screen use, and a `territory_declared` value of `WORLDWIDE` or `DOMESTIC` stating where the deliverable will be exploited for this particular use. Those two identifiers are the request's whole reference set — a usage names **no** project, taking its owning project implicitly from the stem package, so there is nothing here for the cross-project guard of rung 29 to compare and asset-usage creation never reports `ERR_CROSS_PROJECT_REFERENCE`. The one project-level gate that does apply is the archival gate: a usage recorded beneath a project whose `pipeline_stage` is `ARCHIVED` is rejected with `ERR_PROJECT_ARCHIVED`, while posture, picture status and the project's `version` are not consulted at all.

The clearance verdict is decided once, at creation, and pinned onto the usage row. A usage clears only if all three of the following hold: the asset's `status` is `ACTIVE`; the asset's `expires_at_utc` is either null or strictly later than the logical clock's current instant; and the asset's `territory_scope` is `WORLDWIDE`, or else its `territory_scope` equals the usage's `territory_declared`. Note the strictness of the expiry test — an asset whose `expires_at_utc` is exactly the current logical-clock instant has **expired** and does not clear. A cleared usage has `clearance_status` set to `CLEARED`, `cleared_at_utc` stamped with the logical clock's current instant, and `unlicensed_reason` null. A usage that fails any of the three conditions has `clearance_status` set to `UNLICENSED`, `cleared_at_utc` null, `accrued_fee_cents` 0, and `unlicensed_reason` set by testing three causes **in this order** and taking the first that applies: `ASSET_REVOKED` when the asset's status is `REVOKED`; otherwise `ASSET_EXPIRED` when `expires_at_utc` is at or before the current logical-clock instant; otherwise `TERRITORY_OUT_OF_SCOPE`. An asset that is both revoked and expired therefore reports `ASSET_REVOKED`.

Licence money is accrued only by cleared usages, and the two licence types accrue it differently. The usage's duration is first quantised into **billable units of 30 seconds each**, taking the ceiling — a 100-second use is four units, and any use of at least one second is at least one unit. A `RIGHTS_MANAGED` asset accrues `accrued_fee_cents` equal to its `fee_cents` multiplied by that unit count; a `ROYALTY_FREE` asset accrues a flat `accrued_fee_cents` equal to its `fee_cents`, with the unit count ignored entirely. So a rights-managed asset with a `fee_cents` of 25000 used for 100 seconds accrues 100000 cents, while a royalty-free asset with a `fee_cents` of 17890 used for the same 100 seconds accrues 17890. Recording a usage recomputes the owning project's `license_fee_accrued_cents`, `unlicensed_usage_count`, `license_risk_score_intake`, `license_risk_score_audit`, `billing_total_cents` and `posture`, and increases the project's `version` by 1 — which means an unlicensed usage on a live package immediately drags that project's posture down to `RESTRICTED`. Usages can be listed with optional `library_asset_id`, `stem_package_id` and `clearance_status` filters.

**Licensing exposure** is scored on every project as a number from 0.00 through 100.00, carried to two decimal places, and it is scored **twice** with deliberately divergent weights. The input set for both scores is every asset usage whose stem package belongs to the project and whose stem package's `status` is `DRAFT`, `CONFORMED` or `STALE`; usages under a `RETIRED` package are excluded from both. The facility's weights are fixed: 20 points per rights-managed usage, 5 points per royalty-free usage, a 30-point penalty per unlicensed usage, and 10 points per cleared usage whose licence is close to expiring, where "close" means within a **30-day** window. Both scores are refreshed on exactly **two** events and on no others: recording an asset usage, and revoking a library asset. Every other mutation of the project — a conform pass, a cue revision, a picture lock or lock break, a booking, an ADR session, a delivery, an archival seal — recomputes the counts, the ledgers and the roll-up but leaves `license_risk_score_intake` and `license_risk_score_audit` holding the values they were last given, even when it changes which usages fall inside the input set. Two consequences are worth stating plainly, because they are what a naive continuous recomputation would get wrong. Advancing the logical clock past a licence's expiry does not move either score, consistent with §2.4's rule that setting the clock recomputes no derived field, so the near-expiry term reflects the clock as it stood at the last usage creation or revocation. And the archival seal, which retires the project's conformed packages and so drops every usage beneath them out of the input set, leaves both scores frozen at their pre-seal figures rather than recomputing them downward.

`license_risk_score_intake` is the intake snapshot, and it scores the licensed material mix only. It counts, among **cleared usages only**, how many reference a `RIGHTS_MANAGED` asset and how many reference a `ROYALTY_FREE` asset, multiplies those counts by 20 and 5 respectively, adds the two products, rounds the sum half up, and then caps the result at 100.00. It ignores unlicensed usages entirely and ignores expiry proximity entirely.

`license_risk_score_audit` is the delivery audit, recomputed on exactly the same events, and it diverges from the snapshot in two ways. First, its rights-managed and royalty-free counts are taken over **all** usages in the input set, cleared or unlicensed alike — this is the first divergence. Second, it adds two further terms: 30 points for each usage whose `clearance_status` is `UNLICENSED`, and 10 points for each usage whose `clearance_status` is `CLEARED` whose asset has a non-null `expires_at_utc` that lies strictly after the current logical-clock instant and no more than 30 days after it. The four products are summed, the sum is rounded half up, and the result is then capped at 100.00. The cap is applied once, after summing — never per term. Consider a project holding one cleared rights-managed usage whose licence expires 10 days out, plus one unlicensed royalty-free usage: the intake snapshot reads 20.00, counting only the cleared rights-managed usage, while the delivery audit reads 20 plus 5 plus 30 plus 10, or 65.00. That audit figure is at or above the facility's risk block threshold of **50.00**, so delivery is barred while the intake snapshot still looks comfortable. A delivery gate that consults the intake variant is simply wrong.

**Revoking** an asset is a `FacilityAdmin` action carrying a mandatory `revocation_reason` of at least one character, and it is the deepest cascade in the system. It is a facility-level write on a facility-level record, so it carries **no** project gate whatsoever: neither posture, nor picture status, nor `pipeline_stage` is consulted on any project the cascade will reach, and a revocation therefore drives its consequences into a `FROZEN` project and into a project already sealed at `ARCHIVED` alike, recomputing and version-bumping each of them. The asset must currently be `ACTIVE`, and a second revocation of an already-`REVOKED` asset has no transition and reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the transition `ACTIVE → REVOKED` stores the reason and increases the asset's own `version` by 1. Then, **because** the asset became revoked, every asset usage referencing it whose `clearance_status` is `CLEARED` moves `CLEARED → UNLICENSED`: its `accrued_fee_cents` is reset to 0, its `unlicensed_reason` is set to `ASSET_REVOKED`, and its `cleared_at_utc` is cleared back to null. Then, **because** those usages became unlicensed, each one's stem package, if its `status` is `CONFORMED`, moves `CONFORMED → STALE` with `stale_reason` set to `LICENSE_REVOKED`. Then, **because** those packages became stale, the chain of §2.10 runs in full: their non-terminal deliverables become `INVALIDATED` with `invalidation_reason` `STEM_STALE`, and each such deliverable's `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` booking becomes `RELEASED` with `release_reason` `DELIVERABLE_INVALIDATED`. Across every affected project, `license_fee_accrued_cents`, `unlicensed_usage_count`, `stale_stem_count`, `conformed_stem_count`, `license_risk_score_intake`, `license_risk_score_audit`, `stage_hours_reserved`, `stage_hours_available`, `stage_cost_subtotal_cents`, `billing_total_cents` and `posture` are all recomputed. The accrual reversal is a genuine compensating movement: the money the withdrawn licence had accrued leaves the project's licence ledger entirely, rather than being offset by a negative entry.

### 3.5. Automated Dialogue Replacement: Scheduling Talent

ADR sessions are booked against a picture-locked project — the whole point of an ADR pass is to replace lines against a cut that will not move again, so scheduling a session on a project whose `picture_status` is anything other than `LOCKED` is rejected with `ERR_PICTURE_NOT_LOCKED`, and so is confirming one. A `SoundEditor` or a `PostSupervisor` schedules a session by naming the owning `project_id`, the booked `voice_talent_id`, the session's `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`, and a `lines_scheduled` count greater than zero. Session windows are **half-open**: the start instant is inclusive and the end instant is exclusive. A window whose `window_end_utc` is at or before its `window_start_utc` is rejected with `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`, and a window whose `window_end_utc` falls later than the project's `delivery_deadline_utc` is rejected with `ERR_WINDOW_AFTER_DEADLINE`.

Cost is pinned at scheduling time from the talent's union terms. `scheduled_minutes` is the length of the requested window in minutes — its whole-second duration per §2.8 divided by 60, the quotient rounded half up to two decimal places — so it is not itself an integer count of minutes: a 2730-second window yields `scheduled_minutes` of 45.50, and a 95-second one yields 1.58. For `UNION` talent, `billable_minutes` is the greater of `scheduled_minutes` and the talent's `min_call_minutes`, and the union rate of **900 cents per billable minute** applies. For `NON_UNION` talent, `billable_minutes` is exactly `scheduled_minutes` — the minimum call is ignored — and the non-union rate of **600 cents per billable minute** applies. `session_cost_cents` is `billable_minutes` multiplied by the applicable rate. Union talent with a 60-minute minimum call booked for a 45-minute window therefore bills 60 minutes and 54000 cents; the same 45-minute window with non-union talent bills 45 minutes and 27000 cents; and union talent booked for only 15 minutes still bills 60 minutes and 54000 cents. It is `billable_minutes`, not `scheduled_minutes`, that counts against a talent's capacity.

Two capacity guards protect the talent, and they read deliberately different sets. The **overlap** guard rejects a requested window that strictly overlaps an existing session for the same `voice_talent_id` — any session of that talent anywhere in the facility, whichever project owns it, since a voice actor can only be in one booth at a time — whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `CONFIRMED` or `RECORDED`, reporting `ERR_TALENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`; sessions in `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW` are excluded from that occupancy set. Adjacency is explicitly allowed, and there is no buffer for talent: a session that begins at exactly another session's `window_end_utc` does not conflict. The **daily cap** guard sums `billable_minutes` across the talent's sessions — again facility-wide, across every project — whose `window_start_utc` falls on the same UTC calendar day as the requested `window_start_utc` and whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `CONFIRMED`, `RECORDED` **or** `NO_SHOW`, adds the incoming request's own `billable_minutes`, and rejects the request with `ERR_TALENT_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED` when that total exceeds the talent's `daily_cap_minutes`; only a strict excess is rejected, so a total landing exactly **on** the cap is accepted. The asymmetry is intentional: a no-show consumed the talent's day even though nobody was in the booth, so it stays in the cap set while leaving the overlap set.

A newly scheduled session lands in `PROPOSED` with `lines_recorded` 0 and `cancellation_reason` null, and it contributes nothing to the project's money yet — a proposed session's `session_cost_cents` is outside `adr_cost_cents`. Scheduling therefore leaves the owning project's row entirely alone: no derived project field is recomputed, the project's `updated_at` does not move, and its `version` does **not** increase. Confirming it is a `SoundEditor` action that requires the session to be `PROPOSED`, the project's `posture` not to be `FROZEN`, and the project's `pipeline_stage` not to be `ARCHIVED`. The transition `PROPOSED → CONFIRMED` brings the session's `session_cost_cents` into the project's `adr_cost_cents`, recomputes `billing_total_cents`, and increases the project's `version` by 1.

Recording is likewise a `SoundEditor` action, moving the session `CONFIRMED → RECORDED` while storing the supplied `lines_recorded`, a required integer of at least 0 — zero is legal and records a session in which no line was captured. Three preconditions apply: the session must be `CONFIRMED`, the supplied `lines_recorded` must not exceed the session's `lines_scheduled` — a larger figure is rejected with `ERR_ADR_LINES_EXCEEDED` — and the logical clock must be at or past the session's `window_start_utc`, since a session cannot be recorded before it opens; an attempt made before the window opens is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The project's `posture` must not be `FROZEN`. Recording leaves `adr_cost_cents` unchanged, because the session's cost entered the ledger back at confirmation and must not be counted twice; only the session row moves, so recording recomputes no project field and leaves the project's `updated_at` and `version` exactly where they were. `RECORDED` is terminal.

Cancellation is available to a `SoundEditor` or a `PostSupervisor`, requires the project's `pipeline_stage` not to be `ARCHIVED`, and branches on the mandatory `cancellation_reason`. A reason of `CLIENT_REQUEST` or `SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` moves the session to `CANCELLED`, which is permitted from `PROPOSED` and from `CONFIRMED`; the session's `cancellation_reason` is recorded and the session leaves both the talent overlap set and the talent daily-cap set. When the cancellation comes out of `CONFIRMED`, the session's cost also leaves the project's `adr_cost_cents`, `billing_total_cents` is recomputed and the project's `version` increases by 1; when it comes out of `PROPOSED` there is no money to remove, because a proposed session never contributed any, and the project's row is left entirely alone on that branch — nothing is recomputed, its `updated_at` does not move and its `version` does not increase. A reason of `TALENT_NO_SHOW` is a different transition with different economics: it is permitted only from `CONFIRMED`, additionally requires the logical clock to be at or past the session's `window_start_utc` — a no-show cannot be declared before the call time, and doing so is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and moves the session to `NO_SHOW`. A no-show session's `session_cost_cents` **stays** inside `adr_cost_cents`, because the day is still billed to the client, and the session **stays** in the talent's daily-cap set while leaving the overlap set. Because that money does not move, the no-show branch likewise writes only the session row and leaves the project's derived fields, `updated_at` and `version` untouched. Declaring a no-show on a merely `PROPOSED` session has no transition and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Both `CANCELLED` and `NO_SHOW` are terminal. ADR sessions can be listed with optional `project_id`, `voice_talent_id` and `status` filters.

### 3.6. The Mix Stage: Reservations, Occupancy and the Stage-Hour Ledger

Reserving stage time is a `PostSupervisor` action and the most heavily guarded write in the service. The request names the `project_id`, the `mix_stage_id` of the room, the `mixer_id` of the assigned re-recording mixer, a `booking_type` of `PREMIX`, `FINAL_MIX` or `PRINTMASTER`, the `platform` this session mixes for (one of `THEATRICAL`, `BROADCAST`, `STREAMING` or `INTERNATIONAL_ME`), and the occupation's `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`. It also carries the `expected_project_version` token demanded by §2.7. The optional `client_attendance` field is one of `ONSITE`, `REMOTE` or `NONE` and defaults to `NONE`.

Picture lock gates the booking type rather than the booking as such. A `FINAL_MIX` or `PRINTMASTER` reservation requires the project's `picture_status` to be `LOCKED` and is otherwise rejected with `ERR_PICTURE_NOT_LOCKED`; a `PREMIX` reservation is exempt from that requirement entirely, because premixing legitimately precedes the lock. The assigned mixer must be certified for the requested work: if the `platform` named on the request does not appear in that mixer's `certified_platforms`, the reservation is rejected with `ERR_MIXER_NOT_CERTIFIED`. Remote attendance requires a wired room: a `client_attendance` of `REMOTE` against a stage whose `supports_remote_review` is false is rejected with `ERR_STAGE_NO_REMOTE_REVIEW`. Booking windows follow the same half-open convention and the same two temporal guards as ADR sessions — `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID` when `window_end_utc` is at or before `window_start_utc`, and `ERR_WINDOW_AFTER_DEADLINE` when `window_end_utc` falls later than the project's `delivery_deadline_utc`.

Room occupancy is exclusive, and the exclusion zone is wider than the booked window. The requested half-open window, **extended by the stage's `changeover_minutes` on both sides**, must not overlap any existing booking on the same `mix_stage_id` whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`; a collision is reported as `ERR_STAGE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Comparison remains half-open at the boundary, so a new window that begins at exactly the existing window's `window_end_utc` plus that stage's changeover minutes does **not** conflict. Bookings in `RELEASED` or `SUPERSEDED` are excluded from the occupancy set — including a booking superseded by this very request, described next.

Booking creation supersedes a sibling. Any existing non-terminal booking of the project with the **same** `booking_type` — that is, any booking of that type in `RESERVED` or in `CONFIRMED` — moves to `SUPERSEDED`, releasing its hours: its `reserved_hours` leave `stage_hours_reserved`, and `stage_hours_available` and `stage_cost_subtotal_cents` are recomputed accordingly. Each superseded booking refreshes its own `updated_at` and increases its own `version` by 1, while its `release_reason` stays **null** — supersession is not a release and records no reason. Crucially, that superseded booking is excluded from **both** the occupancy check above and the stage-hours check below, so re-booking the same room for the same purpose over an overlapping window is legal and simply replaces the earlier reservation. This supersession is exactly why the project version token is mandatory: without it a client working from a stale view would silently discard a reservation it had never seen, and the mismatch is reported as `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION`.

Reservation cost is pinned immediately, from the **requested** window. First, `requested_hours` is the length of the requested window in hours, rounded half up to two decimal places. Then `reserved_hours`, the figure stored on the booking, is the greater of `requested_hours` and the facility's **two-hour minimum session** — a one-hour window still reserves and bills 2.00 hours. The base charge is `reserved_hours` multiplied by the stage's `hourly_rate_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. Finally `provisional_cost_cents` is that base multiplied by the project's turnaround multiplier and rounded half up to whole cents, where the multiplier is 1.00 for `STANDARD`, 1.25 for `EXPEDITED` and 1.60 for `RUSH`. A three-hour window on a stage billing 120000 cents an hour for a `STANDARD` project therefore reserves 3.00 hours and pins a `provisional_cost_cents` of 360000; the identical window on a `RUSH` project pins 576000; and a one-hour window on that stage pins 240000 against 2.00 reserved hours. The provisional figure never contains overtime — overtime is a completion-time concept only. Once the reservation lands, `status` is `RESERVED`, `actual_start_utc`, `actual_end_utc`, `billable_hours_final`, `overtime_hours`, `final_cost_cents`, `client_signoff_at_utc` and `release_reason` are all null, and the project's `stage_hours_reserved`, `stage_hours_available`, `stage_cost_subtotal_cents` and `billing_total_cents` are recomputed. That recomputation writes the project's own row too: its `updated_at` refreshes and its `version` increases by exactly 1 — once for the whole request, however many sibling bookings it supersedes — so a client reserving twice in a row must present the incremented figure as the second request's `expected_project_version`.

The last guard on reservation is fiscal. The booking's `reserved_hours` must not exceed the project's `stage_hours_available` as computed **after** excluding any booking this same request supersedes; exceeding it is rejected with `ERR_STAGE_HOURS_EXCEEDED`. That guard sits deliberately **after** the mixer-certification guard in the precedence ladder of §2.11, so a request that both names an uncertified mixer and blows the hour budget reports the certification defect.

The **stage-hour ledger** on the project is maintained continuously from the bookings themselves. `stage_hours_reserved` is the sum of `reserved_hours` over the project's bookings whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. `stage_hours_consumed` is the sum of `billable_hours_final` over the project's bookings whose `status` is `COMPLETED`. `stage_hours_available` is `stage_hours_authorized` minus `stage_hours_reserved` minus `stage_hours_consumed`. Bookings in `RELEASED` and `SUPERSEDED` contribute 0.00 to every one of those three terms. All three are carried to two decimal places, and the identity that `stage_hours_reserved` plus `stage_hours_consumed` plus `stage_hours_available` equals `stage_hours_authorized` must hold after **every** mutation. Releasing a booking must return `stage_hours_available` to exactly the value it held before that booking was created — no drift — and completing a booking moves hours from the reserved term to the consumed term rather than adding them a second time. `stage_hours_available` may legitimately go **negative**, but only through overtime recognised at completion; the fiscal guard on reservation prevents a reservation from ever driving it below zero.

Acceptance, opening and closing a booking all belong to the assigned mixer alone — any other mixer is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_MIXER`. **Accepting** moves the booking `RESERVED → CONFIRMED` and requires that the project's `posture` not be `FROZEN` and its `pipeline_stage` not be `ARCHIVED`. It also enforces the mixer's own workload: the sum of `reserved_hours` over that mixer's bookings whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED` and whose `window_start_utc` falls on the same UTC calendar day, plus this booking's `reserved_hours`, must not exceed the mixer's `max_daily_hours`; exceeding it is rejected with `ERR_MIXER_DAILY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Note that this daily check reads `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PROGRESS` and `COMPLETED` bookings and therefore ignores merely `RESERVED` ones — a mixer's day fills up only as they accept work. When the accepted booking's `booking_type` is `FINAL_MIX` and the project sits at `PREMIX`, `pipeline_stage` advances to `FINAL_MIX`; either way the project's `version` increases by 1.

**Opening** the stage moves the booking `CONFIRMED → IN_PROGRESS`, stamps `actual_start_utc` with the logical clock's current instant, and requires the project's `posture` not to be `FROZEN` and its `pipeline_stage` not to be `ARCHIVED`. It also requires the logical clock to be strictly before the booking's `window_end_utc`: a slot whose window has already run out cannot be opened, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_WINDOW_ELAPSED`. Opening moves neither money nor hours — an `IN_PROGRESS` booking reserves exactly as a `CONFIRMED` one did — so it writes the booking row alone: no project field is recomputed and the project's `updated_at` and `version` do not move.

**Closing** the stage moves the booking `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and stamps `actual_end_utc` with the logical clock's current instant. It requires the project's `posture` not to be `FROZEN` and its `pipeline_stage` not to be `ARCHIVED`, and it enforces the remote-review contract: if the booking's `client_attendance` is `REMOTE` and its `client_signoff_at_utc` is still null, the closure is rejected with `ERR_CLIENT_SIGNOFF_REQUIRED`. A booking whose attendance is `ONSITE` or `NONE` needs no sign-off at all.

Closing pins the **final** cost variant, and its step order differs from the provisional variant in ways that matter. First, `elapsed_hours` is the length of the actual occupation from `actual_start_utc` to `actual_end_utc` in hours — its whole-second duration per §2.8 divided by 3600, carried unrounded rather than reduced to two decimals, so no rounding of its own ever precedes the ceiling that follows. Should the logical clock have been moved backwards between opening and closing, so that `actual_end_utc` precedes `actual_start_utc`, that negative elapsed figure is clamped to zero rather than producing negative hours or a negative charge. That elapsed figure is then quantised **upward** to the next whole quarter-hour: divide by 0.25, take the ceiling, multiply back by 0.25. Only then is the two-hour minimum session applied, so `billable_hours_final` is the greater of that quarter-hour-quantised figure and 2.00 — applying the floor before the ceiling is **forbidden**. `overtime_hours` is `billable_hours_final` minus the booking's `reserved_hours`, or 0.00 when that difference would be negative. `regular_hours` is `billable_hours_final` minus `overtime_hours`. The base charge is then the sum of two separately rounded parts: `regular_hours` multiplied by the stage's `hourly_rate_cents` and rounded half up, plus `overtime_hours` multiplied by that same rate and by the facility's overtime factor of **1.5** and rounded half up. Finally `final_cost_cents` is that base multiplied by the project's turnaround multiplier — 1.00, 1.25 or 1.60 as before — and rounded half up. The multiplier is applied **once**, to the combined regular-plus-overtime base; multiplying each component separately and then summing is forbidden. Working the earlier example through: a booking that reserved 3.00 hours at 120000 cents an hour on a `STANDARD` project but actually ran from 10:00:00Z to 13:10:00Z has an elapsed 3.1667 hours, quantises up to 3.25, takes `billable_hours_final` 3.25 and `overtime_hours` 0.25 against `regular_hours` 3.00, giving a base of 360000 plus 45000, so `final_cost_cents` is 405000 — 45000 cents more than the provisional figure. A booking started and closed at the same instant has an elapsed 0.0000 hours, quantises to 0.00, is floored to `billable_hours_final` 2.00 with `overtime_hours` 0.00, and pins 240000.

Beyond the money, closing rearranges the ledger: the booking's hours leave `stage_hours_reserved` and enter `stage_hours_consumed` as `billable_hours_final`, `stage_hours_available` is recomputed, and `stage_cost_subtotal_cents` and `billing_total_cents` are recomputed with the final figure replacing the provisional one. When the closed booking's `booking_type` is `FINAL_MIX` and the project sits at `FINAL_MIX`, `pipeline_stage` advances to `QC`. The project's `version` increases by 1. `COMPLETED` is terminal.

**Remote-review sign-off** is the project's `ClientReviewer` recording that they watched and approved the session. It is permitted while the booking's `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, requires that the booking's `client_attendance` be `REMOTE` — a booking whose attendance is `ONSITE` or `NONE` has no sign-off transition at all and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than with a remote-review code — requires that the acting `X-User-ID` equal the project's `client_user_id`, and requires that the stage's `supports_remote_review` be true — a sign-off against an unwired room is rejected with `ERR_STAGE_NO_REMOTE_REVIEW`. Sign-off is a **field mutation only**: it stamps `client_signoff_at_utc` with the logical clock's current instant and leaves the booking's `status` exactly where it was, whether that was `CONFIRMED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Despite looking idempotent it is not: a second sign-off on a booking whose `client_signoff_at_utc` is already set is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_SIGNED_OFF`. Sign-off attempted on a booking in any other status has no transition and reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Sign-off is also the one write beneath a project that carries neither project gate: the project's `posture` and its `pipeline_stage` are not consulted at all, so the client may still record a sign-off beneath a `RESTRICTED`, a `FROZEN` or even an already-`ARCHIVED` project. And it writes the booking row alone — no project field is recomputed and the project's `updated_at` and `version` do not move.

**Releasing** a slot is a `PostSupervisor` action carrying a mandatory `release_reason` of at least one character with no upper length bound, permitted out of `RESERVED` and out of `CONFIRMED` and requiring that the project's `pipeline_stage` not be `ARCHIVED`. In both cases the reason is stored, the booking moves to the terminal `RELEASED`, its `reserved_hours` leave `stage_hours_reserved`, `stage_hours_available` returns to exactly the value it held before the booking was created, its `provisional_cost_cents` leaves `stage_cost_subtotal_cents`, `billing_total_cents` is recomputed, and the project's `version` increases by 1. The booking also leaves the stage occupancy set, freeing that window for a new reservation. A release out of `CONFIRMED` does **not** regress `pipeline_stage` — a project that reached `FINAL_MIX` stays there even if the booking that got it there is released. The system also releases bookings on its own initiative through the invalidation chain of §2.10, where the stored `release_reason` is the string `DELIVERABLE_INVALIDATED`. Bookings can be listed with optional `project_id`, `mix_stage_id`, `mixer_id` and `status` filters.

### 3.7. Preparing Deliverables and Running the Quality-Control Loop

A **deliverable** is one platform's cut, assembled from one conformed stem package off one completed stage session. A `PostSupervisor` prepares it by naming the owning `project_id`, the `platform_spec_id` of the governing specification, the `stem_package_id` the mix was built from, and the `mix_booking_id` of the stage session that produced it, alongside the `expected_project_version` token of §2.7.

Preparation is where four consistency guards bite. The project's `picture_status` must be `LOCKED`, otherwise `ERR_PICTURE_NOT_LOCKED`. The referenced stem package's `status` must be `CONFORMED`, otherwise `ERR_STEM_NOT_CONFORMED`. The specification's `platform` must equal the `platform` recorded on the referenced booking — a broadcast spec cut off a theatrical mix session is rejected with `ERR_SPEC_PLATFORM_MISMATCH`. And any referenced stem package or booking that belongs to a project other than the one named on the request is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_PROJECT_REFERENCE`. Preparation is additionally the **only** operation guarded by the standalone licensing check: if the project's `unlicensed_usage_count` is at least 1, preparation is rejected with `ERR_UNLICENSED_MATERIAL_PRESENT`. That guard lives here and nowhere else on purpose. An unlicensed usage already drags the project's posture to `RESTRICTED`, and a `RESTRICTED` posture already blocks QC submission, QC evaluation, delivery and archival with `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN`; deliverable preparation is the one step a `RESTRICTED` project may still take, which is exactly where a licensing guard has to sit to be reachable at all.

Like booking creation, preparation supersedes a sibling: any existing non-terminal deliverable of the project for the **same** `platform_spec_id` — one in `PREPARED`, `QC_SUBMITTED`, `QC_REJECTED`, `REMEDIATION` or `QC_PASSED` — moves to the terminal `SUPERSEDED`, frozen out of the QC loop, though its accumulated QC reports remain readable. Discarding QC progress on a stale view is precisely what the project version token prevents, and a mismatch reports `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION`. A newly prepared deliverable sits in `PREPARED` with `remediation_round` 0, `qc_rejection_codes` an empty array, `measured_integrated_lufs`, `measured_true_peak_dbtp`, `me_stems_present` and `deviation_lu` all null, `delivery_reference` and `delivered_at_utc` null, `invalidation_reason` null, and `prepared_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID` — the anchor for every segregation-of-duties comparison that follows. Preparation increases the project's `version` by 1. Deliverables can be listed with optional `project_id`, `platform_spec_id`, `stem_package_id`, `mix_booking_id` and `status` filters.

QC runs in two distinct steps performed by a `QcOperator`, and separating them is what makes segregation of duties enforceable. **Submission** carries the measurements: `measured_integrated_lufs`, the integrated loudness in LUFS as a decimal from -40.00 through 0.00; `measured_true_peak_dbtp`, the true peak in dBTP from -20.00 through 0.00; and the boolean `me_stems_present`, declaring whether a music-and-effects stem set accompanied the submission. Submission is permitted out of `PREPARED` and out of `REMEDIATION` — the transitions `PREPARED → QC_SUBMITTED` and `REMEDIATION → QC_SUBMITTED` — and both carry the same four preconditions: the acting `X-User-ID` must **not** equal the deliverable's `prepared_by_user_id`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the referenced booking's `status` must be `COMPLETED`, or `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_COMPLETED`; the referenced stem package's `status` must be `CONFORMED`, or `ERR_STEM_NOT_CONFORMED`; and the project's `posture` must be `OPEN`, so a merely `RESTRICTED` project is rejected with `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN` here just as a `FROZEN` one is. Both submissions store the three measurements and recompute `deviation_lu`; a submission out of `REMEDIATION` simply overwrites the previous round's stored values with the new ones. Both also remember the acting `X-User-ID` as the round's submitter, which the evaluation step will compare against; that submitter identity is internal bookkeeping and appears on no deliverable payload, surfacing only as the `submitted_by_user_id` of the QC report the evaluation writes. Submission writes the deliverable row alone: it recomputes no project field and leaves the project's `updated_at` and `version` untouched.

**Evaluation** takes no request body, and a body sent with it anyway is never parsed at all, so an array, a bare string or malformed JSON accompanying an evaluation is discarded unread rather than reported as a schema violation. Evaluation is permitted only out of `QC_SUBMITTED`, and requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the `submitted_by_user_id` of the round being evaluated — one operator may not both measure and judge a round, and doing so is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The project's `posture` must be `OPEN`. Evaluation compares the stored measurements against the governing specification and produces two things: a loudness deviation and an ordered list of rejection codes.

`deviation_lu` is the absolute difference between `measured_integrated_lufs` and the specification's `target_lufs`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Three independent conditions then contribute rejection codes. `LOUDNESS_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE` is contributed when `deviation_lu` is **strictly greater** than the specification's `tolerance_lu` — a deviation exactly equal to the tolerance passes. `TRUE_PEAK_EXCEEDED` is contributed when `measured_true_peak_dbtp` is **strictly greater** than the specification's `true_peak_max_dbtp` — a peak exactly at the ceiling passes. `ME_STEMS_MISSING` is contributed when the specification's `requires_me_stems` is true and the submitted `me_stems_present` is false. However many conditions fire, the codes are always emitted in one fixed order regardless of the order in which they were tested: `LOUDNESS_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE` first, then `TRUE_PEAK_EXCEEDED`, then `ME_STEMS_MISSING`. The `verdict` is `PASS` when the resulting list is empty and `FAIL` otherwise. Consider a specification with `target_lufs` -24.00, `tolerance_lu` 0.50, `true_peak_max_dbtp` -2.00 and `requires_me_stems` true. A submission of -24.50 LUFS, -2.00 dBTP with `me_stems_present` true yields a `deviation_lu` of 0.50, an empty code list and a `PASS`. A submission of -24.51 LUFS, -2.00 dBTP with stems present yields a `deviation_lu` of 0.51 and a single-code `FAIL` of `LOUDNESS_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`. A submission of -23.49 LUFS, -1.99 dBTP with `me_stems_present` false yields a `deviation_lu` of 0.51 and a three-code `FAIL` listing `LOUDNESS_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`, `TRUE_PEAK_EXCEEDED` and `ME_STEMS_MISSING` in exactly that order.

Every evaluation appends an immutable **QC report** — this is the one operation in the QC loop that creates a new resource rather than mutating one. The report records `round`, which is the deliverable's `remediation_round` at evaluation time and is therefore 0 for a first evaluation; the `verdict`; the ordered `rejection_codes`, which is an empty array on a pass; snapshots of `measured_integrated_lufs`, `measured_true_peak_dbtp` and the specification's `target_lufs` as they stood at evaluation time; the computed `deviation_lu`; the `submitted_by_user_id` remembered at submission; the `evaluated_by_user_id`, which is the acting `X-User-ID`; and `evaluated_at_utc`, the logical clock's current instant. Because the specification is immutable and the report snapshots its `target_lufs` anyway, a historical report can always be re-read against the numbers that produced it. Alongside the report, the deliverable itself moves `QC_SUBMITTED → QC_PASSED` with `qc_rejection_codes` set to an empty array when the code list came out empty, or `QC_SUBMITTED → QC_REJECTED` with `qc_rejection_codes` set to that same ordered list when it did not. The evaluation's 201 response body is that new report itself and not the updated deliverable, which must be re-read to observe its new status; the deliverable's own `updated_at` and `version` do move, while the project's row does not — evaluation recomputes no project field and leaves the project's `updated_at` and `version` alone. A deliverable's QC reports can be read back as a chronology of its rounds.

**Remediation** reopens a rejected deliverable and is a `PostSupervisor` action carrying an optional `remediation_note` of 0 to 2000 characters which — like the picture-lock note of §3.2 — is validated and then discarded, recorded on no field and returned in no response. It is permitted only out of `QC_REJECTED`, requires the project's `pipeline_stage` not to be `ARCHIVED`, and is bounded: the deliverable's `remediation_round` must be **strictly less than** the governing specification's `max_remediation_rounds`, and a deliverable that has already consumed its allowance is rejected with `ERR_REMEDIATION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. A specification whose `max_remediation_rounds` is 0 therefore forbids remediation outright, since the very first rejection already leaves `remediation_round` at 0. The transition `QC_REJECTED → REMEDIATION` increases `remediation_round` by 1 and resets `qc_rejection_codes` to an empty array, after which the loop repeats: `REMEDIATION → QC_SUBMITTED → QC_REJECTED → REMEDIATION` for as many rounds as the specification allows. Remediation too writes only the deliverable row: the project's derived fields, `updated_at` and `version` are untouched.

Two exits from the loop are involuntary. A deliverable in any of `PREPARED`, `QC_SUBMITTED`, `QC_REJECTED`, `REMEDIATION` or `QC_PASSED` moves to `INVALIDATED` with `invalidation_reason` `STEM_STALE` when the stem package beneath it goes stale, as described in §2.10, and a deliverable in any of those same five states moves to `SUPERSEDED` when a fresh deliverable is prepared for the same platform specification. `DELIVERED`, `INVALIDATED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal, and no re-conform, re-lock or fresh evaluation brings an invalidated deliverable back — a new one must be prepared from scratch.

### 3.8. Delivery, the Billing Roll-Up and the Archival Seal

**Delivering** a passed deliverable is a `PostSupervisor` action carrying a mandatory `delivery_reference` of 1 to 200 characters, the client-side identifier the platform acknowledges. It is permitted only out of `QC_PASSED`, and four further conditions must hold. The project's `posture` must be `OPEN`. The project's `unlicensed_usage_count` must be 0 — and note that an unlicensed usage on a live stem package is precisely what makes posture `RESTRICTED`, so this condition surfaces at delivery as `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN` rather than as the preparation-time licensing code. The project's `license_risk_score_audit` must be **strictly below** the facility's risk block threshold of 50.00; a project at or above it is rejected with `ERR_LICENSE_RISK_TOO_HIGH`, which is why the audit variant of the licence score, and not the intake snapshot, is the gate that matters. And the referenced stem package's `status` must still be `CONFORMED`, or the attempt is rejected with `ERR_STEM_NOT_CONFORMED`. On success the transition `QC_PASSED → DELIVERED` stores `delivery_reference`, stamps `delivered_at_utc` with the logical clock's current instant, advances the project's `pipeline_stage` from `QC` to `DELIVERED` when the project sits at `QC` — that is, for the project's first delivered deliverable — and increases the project's `version` by 1.

The project's commercial position is available as a **computed read** for a `FacilityAdmin`, a `PostSupervisor` or the project's own `ClientReviewer`. It exposes the stage-hour ledger as `stage_hours_authorized`, `stage_hours_reserved`, `stage_hours_consumed` and `stage_hours_available`; the four component ledgers as `stage_cost_subtotal_cents`, `adr_cost_cents`, `conform_fee_total_cents` and `license_fee_accrued_cents`; the pinned roll-up intermediates as `pre_discount_total_cents`, `volume_discount_cents`, `discounted_base_cents` and `rush_surcharge_cents`; the final `billing_total_cents`; both licence-risk variants as `license_risk_score_intake` and `license_risk_score_audit`; the three child counts `unlicensed_usage_count`, `stale_stem_count` and `conformed_stem_count`; and the project's `posture`, `pipeline_stage` and `picture_status`. That list is exhaustive: the ledger is a computed projection rather than a resource, so it repeats none of the project's descriptive fields and carries no `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` or `version` of its own.

The **billing roll-up** is computed in a pinned nine-step order, and that order resolves what would otherwise be a circular argument between the volume discount and the rush surcharge. Step one, `stage_cost_subtotal_cents` is the sum over the project's bookings of `final_cost_cents` for each booking whose `status` is `COMPLETED` and of `provisional_cost_cents` for each booking whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; bookings in `RELEASED` and `SUPERSEDED` contribute 0. Step two, `adr_cost_cents` is the sum of `session_cost_cents` over the project's ADR sessions whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, `RECORDED` or `NO_SHOW`; sessions in `PROPOSED` and `CANCELLED` contribute 0. Step three, `conform_fee_total_cents` is the sum of `conform_fee_cents` over the project's stem packages whose `status` is `CONFORMED` or `STALE`; packages in `DRAFT` and `RETIRED` contribute 0. Step four, `license_fee_accrued_cents` is the sum of `accrued_fee_cents` over the asset usages whose `clearance_status` is `CLEARED` and whose stem package belongs to this project with a `status` of `DRAFT`, `CONFORMED` or `STALE`; usages under a `RETIRED` package contribute 0. Note that step four and step three treat a `DRAFT` package differently on purpose: a draft package has no conform fee yet, but the cleared licences already cut into it **do** accrue, because the licence is owed the moment the material lands in the reel.

Step five, `pre_discount_total_cents` is the sum of those four component figures. Step six selects the volume-discount rate from the tier whose bounds contain `pre_discount_total_cents` — 0.00 for a subtotal from 0 through 499999 cents inclusive, 0.05 for a subtotal from 500000 through 999999 cents inclusive, and 0.08 for a subtotal of 1000000 cents or more with no upper bound — and `volume_discount_cents` is `pre_discount_total_cents` multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents. The tier is keyed on the **pre-surcharge** subtotal. Step seven, `discounted_base_cents` is `pre_discount_total_cents` minus `volume_discount_cents`. Step eight, `rush_surcharge_cents` is `discounted_base_cents` multiplied by the surcharge percentage for the project's `turnaround_class` — 0.00 for `STANDARD`, 0.10 for `EXPEDITED` and 0.20 for `RUSH` — rounded half up to whole cents; the surcharge is computed on the **post-discount** base. Step nine, `billing_total_cents` is `discounted_base_cents` plus `rush_surcharge_cents`. Applying the rush surcharge before the discount, or selecting the discount tier from the post-surcharge total, is **forbidden**: both alternatives change the answer whenever the surcharge would push the subtotal across a tier boundary. A worked example: a stage subtotal of 720000 plus ADR of 54000 plus conform fees of 7110 plus licence fees of 17890 gives a `pre_discount_total_cents` of 799000, which falls in the middle tier at 0.05, so `volume_discount_cents` is 39950 and `discounted_base_cents` is 759050. On a `STANDARD` project `rush_surcharge_cents` is 0 and `billing_total_cents` is 759050; on a `RUSH` project `rush_surcharge_cents` is 151810 and `billing_total_cents` is 910860.

**Archival** closes the project's life and is a `PostSupervisor` action. It supplies `session_file_count`, the number of DAW session files handed to the archive, which must be greater than zero, and an optional `retention_years` term which must be greater than zero and defaults to **10** when omitted. Five preconditions gate the seal. The project's `pipeline_stage` must be `DELIVERED`; an archival attempt against a project sitting at an earlier stage has no archival transition available and is reported at rung 19 of the §2.11 ladder as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, which therefore answers ahead of the deliverable gates at rungs 40 and 41 when those would fail on the same request — a project already at `ARCHIVED` is caught earlier still, by rung 17's `ERR_PROJECT_ARCHIVED`. Its `posture` must be `OPEN`, so any stale stem package, any unlicensed usage or a broken lock blocks the seal with `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN`. The project must have at least one deliverable whose `status` is `DELIVERED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_DELIVERED_DELIVERABLE`. It must have **no** deliverable left in `PREPARED`, `QC_SUBMITTED`, `QC_REJECTED`, `REMEDIATION` or `QC_PASSED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_DELIVERABLE_STILL_OPEN`. And no archive package may already exist for the project, or the request is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_ARCHIVED`.

The seal creates the immutable archive package, pinning `stem_manifest_count` as the number of the project's stem packages whose `status` is `CONFORMED` at seal time, `archived_stem_package_ids` as the identifiers of exactly those packages ordered by their `created_at` ascending, ties broken by the insertion sequence of §2.3, and `delivered_deliverable_count` as the number of the project's deliverables whose `status` is `DELIVERED` at seal time. It stamps `sealed_at_utc` with the logical clock's current instant and `sealed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. Every stem package that was `CONFORMED` then moves `CONFORMED → RETIRED`, which is terminal, each such package refreshing its own `updated_at` and `version`, and the project's `conformed_stem_count` is recomputed accordingly. That retirement is not cosmetic, and its effect on the money is the seal's least obvious consequence: a `RETIRED` package contributes nothing to step three of the roll-up and its asset usages fall outside the step-four input set, so the seal drops those packages' `conform_fee_cents` out of `conform_fee_total_cents` and their cleared usages' `accrued_fee_cents` out of `license_fee_accrued_cents`, then re-runs the whole roll-up — `pre_discount_total_cents`, `volume_discount_cents` at whatever tier the reduced subtotal now falls in, `discounted_base_cents`, `rush_surcharge_cents` and `billing_total_cents` — inside the same request. A sealed project's `billing_total_cents` is therefore normally **lower** than it was the instant before the seal. `unlicensed_usage_count` and `stale_stem_count` are recomputed over the same reduced set, while the two licence-risk scores, per §3.4, are left exactly where they stood. Finally the project's `pipeline_stage` advances `DELIVERED → ARCHIVED`, which is terminal, and its `version` increases by 1. From that instant every write beneath the project — conform, cue creation, asset usage, ADR scheduling, confirmation, recording or cancellation, booking creation, release or any mixer action, deliverable preparation, QC submission or evaluation, remediation, delivery and any further archival attempt — is rejected with `ERR_PROJECT_ARCHIVED`. The sealed package remains readable as a project sub-resource: a collection of zero or one row, depending on whether the project has been sealed.
