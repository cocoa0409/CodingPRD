# Nuclear Refueling Outage Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A refueling outage is the largest planned evolution a nuclear generating station ever undertakes. For six to eight weeks the unit earns no revenue, several thousand temporary craft workers pass through the access-authorization turnstiles, the reactor vessel head comes off, a third of the core is exchanged, and hundreds of maintenance, modification and inspection jobs compete for the same crane hooks, the same shutdown safety trains and the same collective dose budget. Every one of those competing demands is measured against a single committed breaker-to-breaker duration target, and the station's fleet reputation is settled on whether that target holds.

The system specified here is the station's outage system of record. It carries the outage from scope development — where work orders are scored, prioritized and either approved into the baseline or deferred out of it — through fuel reload design and spent fuel pool capacity planning, supplemental workforce onboarding, schedule development with a critical-path calculation and shutdown-safety-function protection, radiological work planning with dose estimates and as-low-as-reasonably-achievable review, contingency handling for conditions discovered in the field, the ordered startup test sequence with its regulatory hold points, and finally the post-outage performance review. Three derived station postures — a duration posture, a dose posture and a per-work-order priority band — sit at the centre of the design: they are never supplied by an operator, they are recomputed from stored data on every mutation that could move them, and they gate what the station is permitted to do next.

Because the consequences of a wrong answer here are radiological and regulatory rather than merely commercial, this specification is written to be deterministic. Where a rounding order, an interval boundary, a tie-break or an evaluation sequence could plausibly be read two ways, the intended reading is pinned explicitly. Implementers should treat the ordering statements in §2.8 and the cascade step numbering in §3 as normative, not illustrative.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Station Personnel and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting station user, and `X-User-Role`, naming that user's station role. If either header is absent, or if the value of `X-User-Role` is not one of the nine roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); a header present but empty or whitespace-only counts as absent, and the role name is matched exactly and case-sensitively. If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorized for the called endpoint (see the authorization column of §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The nine roles are `StationAdmin`, `OutageDirector`, `OutagePlanner`, `ReactorEngineer`, `ContractorCoordinator`, `AccessAuthority`, `RadProtectionPlanner`, `RadProtectionManager` and `RegulatoryAffairs`. They are peer roles: each owns a distinct slice of the outage, and none inherits another's authority.

- `StationAdmin` is the plant configuration authority. It registers spent fuel pools, radiological areas and shutdown safety functions, may open an outage, and drives the server's logical clock.
- `OutageDirector` is the single accountable owner of the outage: it freezes scope, opens the breaker, authorizes startup, approves and defers work-order scope, dispositions emergent work and signs the post-outage review.
- `OutagePlanner` builds the scope and the schedule: it plans work orders, creates and drives schedule activities, and reports emergent work found in the field.
- `ReactorEngineer` owns fuel reload design and spent fuel pool capacity: it submits core loading patterns and manages pool cell reservations across their whole lifecycle.
- `ContractorCoordinator` sponsors the supplemental workforce, requesting access authorization for temporary workers and recording their training progress.
- `AccessAuthority` is station access authorization: it records background screening results and issues badges. It may not add workers to the roster and has no authority over outage scope.
- `RadProtectionPlanner` drafts radiological work permits with their dose estimates, shielding credit and ALARA figures, and closes permits against actual dose. It may not approve a permit — neither one it drafted nor any other.
- `RadProtectionManager` is the radiation protection approval authority: it approves and voids permits, thereby committing and releasing dose against the outage budget.
- `RegulatoryAffairs` holds the regulatory interface: it satisfies startup tests and releases NRC hold points, and it may author tests.

Every role in this system is an operator role, so `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `StationAdmin`, `OutageDirector`, `OutagePlanner`, `ReactorEngineer`, `ContractorCoordinator`, `AccessAuthority`, `RadProtectionPlanner`, `RadProtectionManager` or `RegulatoryAffairs`, the server may record the supplied `X-User-ID` where a workflow needs an audit trail — as `created_by_user_id` on an outage, as `created_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a core loading pattern, and as `created_by_user_id` and `satisfied_by_user_id` on a startup test — but it never compares `X-User-ID` to any resource's `id`, and no endpoint performs an ownership check of the form "this row belongs to the calling user". The only comparisons ever made against `X-User-ID` are the two segregation-of-duties gates described in §2.10, and each of those compares the acting `X-User-ID` against an `X-User-ID` value recorded earlier on the same row.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is an envelope diagnostic rather than a stored field, and it alone is read from real wall-clock time — the station logical clock of §2.4 governs the timestamps persisted on rows, never this one.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and so is a `limit` or `offset` supplied as anything other than an integer literal — a fractional or non-numeric value, or the parameter present but empty.

Unless a specific list endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. When a list endpoint is served, the `meta` object of the success envelope carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows matching the filters remain beyond the returned page. Every declared filter is an exact string equality test against the stored value, filters combine conjunctively, and a filter value that matches no stored row — including one naming an identifier that does not exist, or an enumerated value outside the declared set — simply yields an empty page rather than a 404 or a validation error. Query parameters a list endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected; the no-unknown-fields rule of §2.8 governs request bodies only.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Station Logical Clock

Every resource carries an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers are bare UUIDs with no type prefix of any kind, and clients must never construct or infer one. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, formatted as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional-second digits.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. There is one exception: a dose ledger row is an immutable accounting record, so a `DoseTransaction` carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and neither field should appear in its representation.

Timestamps the server stamps itself — `created_at`, `updated_at`, an outage's `scope_locked_at`, and a schedule activity's `started_at` and `completed_at` — are all read from a server-side logical clock rather than from real wall-clock time. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; the server must never fall back to real wall-clock time. An administrative test-driving capability, restricted to `StationAdmin`, advances that logical clock to an instant supplied as `to_iso8601` and echoes the resulting clock reading back as `current_utc` — the sole member of that response's `data` object, which carries no `id` and no other field. The supplied instant may be any parseable ISO 8601 instant, with or without a `Z` suffix or a numeric UTC offset and with any number of fractional digits; it is converted to UTC, and the echoed `current_utc` is rendered in the canonical three-fractional-digit form. Setting the clock is an assignment rather than a monotonic advance: an instant earlier than the current reading is accepted and simply becomes the new reading. Every timestamp stamped after such a call uses the newly supplied instant.

### 2.5. Decimal Discipline for Dose, Hours, Money and Scores

Money, collective dose in mrem, hours, percentages and computed scores are all Decimal quantities carried to exactly 2 decimal places, and every rounding step is round-half-up. Cell counts, badged-worker counts, train counts, assembly counts and training-module counts are integers and are never rounded. Both families are carried in JSON as bare numbers rather than as quoted strings, so a two-decimal quantity whose hundredths and tenths are zero may serialise with fewer digits shown; the value, not its printed width, is what this specification pins.

Two consequences of this discipline recur throughout §3 and are worth stating once, up front. First, where a calculation is defined as a sequence of steps, the rounding is applied at each step the specification names and not merely at the end; the collective dose calculation of §3.7 and the cumulative path-length walk of §3.4 both accumulate already-rounded intermediate values, and rounding only once at the end produces a different and incorrect answer. Second, one comparison in this system deliberately escapes 2-decimal rounding: the dose-budget ratio that selects the ALARA posture in §3.7 is compared as an unrounded Decimal quotient, because rounding it first would misclassify postures at the boundary.

### 2.6. Nested Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Several resources are addressed through a nested path — a work order's radiological permits hang beneath the work order, a schedule activity is created beneath its outage — and path parameters are always resolved strictly left to right. The leftmost identifier is resolved first, and if it names no stored row the request fails immediately with that resource's own not-found code; only then is the next segment considered. The not-found codes are `ERR_OUTAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPENT_FUEL_POOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RAD_AREA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAFETY_FUNCTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCHEDULE_ACTIVITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CORE_LOAD_PATTERN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POOL_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORKER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RWP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EMERGENT_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_STARTUP_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, each carrying HTTP 404.

Identifiers supplied inside a request body are held to the same standard. A `safety_function_id`, `rad_area_id`, `spent_fuel_pool_id`, `outage_id`, `work_order_id` or `predecessor_activity_id` that names no stored row also yields the corresponding 404 code — `ERR_SAFETY_FUNCTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RAD_AREA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPENT_FUEL_POOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OUTAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_SCHEDULE_ACTIVITY_NOT_FOUND` respectively. Body-level foreign-key resolution applies to work-order creation, schedule-activity creation, activity re-sequencing, pool cell reservation creation and permit drafting.

Beyond mere existence, two graph-shape rules are enforced on the schedule and are described where they arise in §3.4: an activity may not be chained behind an activity belonging to a different outage, nor linked to a work order belonging to a different outage (`ERR_PREDECESSOR_OUTAGE_MISMATCH`, HTTP 422), and following the predecessor chain from an activity must terminate at a chain root rather than revisit an activity already seen (`ERR_ACTIVITY_CYCLE`, HTTP 422).

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Plant Aggregates

Two write operations in this system mutate an aggregate that several roles read and write, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the parent's `version`.

Submitting a core loading pattern beneath an outage requires the request body to carry `expected_outage_version`, a mandatory integer holding the `version` of the outage as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the outage's current stored `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_OUTAGE_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412) and no pattern is created. The guard exists because a newly submitted reload design supersedes the outage's live draft design; a writer holding a stale view of the outage would silently discard a design it had never seen. The check is purely a stale-version comparison on the outage's `version` — it is not a timing rule and says nothing about when requests arrive.

Creating a spent fuel pool cell reservation requires the request body to carry `expected_spent_fuel_pool_version`, a mandatory integer holding the `version` of the target pool as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the pool's current stored `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_POOL_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412) and no reservation is created. A reservation is admitted against the pool's headroom at the instant the client read it, and because both the reserved and the occupied cell counts move on every reservation, consumption and release, a writer whose `expected_spent_fuel_pool_version` lags the stored value has sized its request against capacity that no longer exists.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. In particular, work-order transitions, permit transitions, schedule transitions, badging, emergent-item disposition and startup-test satisfaction take no expected-version input and must not be rejected for its absence.

### 2.8. Order of Rejection

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the order below, and the first violated check decides the response; no later check is evaluated, and no earlier check is skipped because a later one also fails.

1. Authentication: both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present, and `X-User-Role` naming one of the nine declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Authorization: the acting role appears among the called endpoint's authorized roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Path resolution: every identifier embedded in the request path resolves to a stored row, validated left to right — otherwise the matching 404 code from §2.6.
4. Request shape: every required body field present, every value of its declared type, every enumerated value a declared member, no unknown fields, and every numeric within its declared bound (a permit's `worker_count` at least 1, a reservation's `cells_requested` at least 1, a permit's `shielding_factor` between 0 and 0.95 inclusive, `planned_duration_hours` strictly greater than zero, a startup test's `sequence_index` at least 1, and no negative money, hours or dose) — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Declared types are checked without coercion: a numeric field supplied as a quoted string, an integer field given a fractional number, and a boolean field given anything but `true` or `false` are each rejected. An absent or empty request body reads as an empty JSON object, so an endpoint that declares no body fields accepts an absent, empty or `{}` body and rejects any field whatsoever as unknown, while a body that fails to parse as JSON or parses to something other than an object is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Omitting an optional field takes its declared default; an explicit `null` is admitted only for the fields this specification declares nullable — a work order's `safety_function_id`, a schedule activity's `work_order_id`, `predecessor_activity_id`, `oos_safety_function_id` and `oos_train_label`, a permit approval's `alara_review_reference`, a startup-test satisfaction's `nrc_release_reference`, an emergent disposition's `disposition_note` and a post-outage review's `review_notes` — and a `null` supplied for any other field is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This rank also carries the cross-field bounds and natural-key uniqueness rules that no dedicated code covers: `cells_baseline_occupied` no greater than `cells_total`, `min_trains_required` no greater than `trains_total`, and a duplicate `area_code`, `function_code` or outage `designation` within the same `station_unit`, are all `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` here — unlike the badge-number and startup-index collisions, which have their own codes at ranks 18 and 19.
5. Body foreign keys: every identifier supplied in the body resolves to a stored row — otherwise the matching 404 code from §2.6.
6. Own-state precondition: the resource named in the path is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise that resource type's state code (`ERR_OUTAGE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_WORK_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ACTIVITY_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CORE_LOAD_PATTERN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_POOL_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_WORKER_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_RWP_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_EMERGENT_ITEM_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_STARTUP_TEST_STATE_INVALID`, each HTTP 409).
7. Cross-entity state guards: the owning outage is in an admissible status for the operation, and the related-resource guards hold — `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_CORE_LOAD_PATTERN_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_COMPLETE`, `ERR_OPEN_WORK_ORDERS_REMAIN`, `ERR_STARTUP_SEQUENCE_EMPTY` and `ERR_BADGED_WORKFORCE_INSUFFICIENT` all live at this rank, as does `ERR_OUTAGE_STATE_INVALID` when it is the owning outage rather than the path resource whose status is wrong. This rank governs permit approval, reservation creation, activity start, startup authorization, execution commencement, scope lock, and every other action whose declared preconditions include an outage-status test.
8. Optimistic concurrency: the submitted `expected_outage_version` or `expected_spent_fuel_pool_version` equals the stored parent version — otherwise `ERR_OUTAGE_VERSION_STALE` or `ERR_POOL_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412).
9. Shutdown margin: a submitted core loading pattern's computed reactivity margin is sufficient — otherwise `ERR_SHUTDOWN_MARGIN_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422).
10. ALARA posture: the owning outage's dose posture is not suspended when a permit is approved — otherwise `ERR_ALARA_POSTURE_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 409).
11. ALARA review: a permit that requires ALARA review carries its review reference at approval — otherwise `ERR_ALARA_REVIEW_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422).
12. Regulatory hold point: a regulatory hold-point startup test carries its NRC release reference at satisfaction — otherwise `ERR_REGULATORY_HOLD_POINT_NOT_RELEASED` (HTTP 422).
13. Shutdown safety function protection: the aggregate out-of-service train count over the new activity's window is within capacity — otherwise `ERR_SAFETY_FUNCTION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).
14. Schedule graph ownership: a supplied predecessor or work order belongs to the same outage — otherwise `ERR_PREDECESSOR_OUTAGE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).
15. Radiological area occupancy: the approving permit's buffered window does not overlap another approved permit in the same area — otherwise `ERR_RAD_AREA_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).
16. Schedule acyclicity: the predecessor chain terminates at a root — otherwise `ERR_ACTIVITY_CYCLE` (HTTP 422).
17. Startup sequencing: every lower-indexed startup test of the outage is already satisfied — otherwise `ERR_STARTUP_SEQUENCE_OUT_OF_ORDER` (HTTP 409).
18. Startup sequence uniqueness: the new test's `sequence_index` is unused in the outage — otherwise `ERR_STARTUP_SEQUENCE_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409).
19. Badge uniqueness: the submitted `badge_number` is unheld among the outage's badged workers — otherwise `ERR_BADGE_NUMBER_TAKEN` (HTTP 409).
20. Segregation of duties: the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the recorded author where §2.10 demands it — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).
21. Discretionary scope window: a discretionary work order's scope is approved only while the outage's duration posture is on track — otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409).
22. Pool capacity: the requested cells fit the headroom the reservation's variant selects — otherwise `ERR_POOL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).
23. Dose budget: the permit's committed dose fits the outage's remaining dose budget — otherwise `ERR_DOSE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).

The domain logic of this ordering is worth internalising: nuclear and radiological safety outranks referential integrity, referential integrity outranks temporal windows, temporal windows outrank administrative constraints, and fiscal and capacity budgets are evaluated last. A request that both breaches shutdown-safety-function capacity and overruns the spent fuel pool reports the safety breach, never the capacity breach.

### 2.9. Derived Postures, Cascades and Version Semantics

Three families of field in this system are computed, never supplied: the two outage postures (a duration posture and a dose posture), the roll-ups and ledgers on the outage and the spent fuel pool, and the per-activity path lengths. A client that submits any of them in a request body has submitted an unknown field and receives `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The server recomputes each of them from stored rows at every point §3 names, and the recomputation is a full reprojection over the current input set rather than an incremental adjustment — a distinction that matters because deferring an approved work order can lower a cost base by enough to drop a whole contingency tier.

Cascades are ordered and total. When one operation moves the stored state of several rows, each row so moved has its own `version` increased by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed, and where a cascade touches a set of sibling rows the set is always processed in ascending `created_at` order so that the resulting sequence of ledger rows and state changes is reproducible. Reprojecting a derived field is not by itself a mutation of that kind: a row's `version` and `updated_at` move only where §3 declares the operation in hand to move them, so a reprojection rewrites the per-activity `path_length_hours`, `risk_adjusted_path_length_hours` and `is_critical_path` in place without ever bumping the activity's `version` or refreshing its `updated_at`, and a cascade step that leaves a sibling's stored state exactly as it found it leaves that sibling's `version` alone as well. Identifiers never participate in ordering or in selection: wherever this specification names a tie-break, the tie-break is `created_at`, and wherever it names a selector, that selector ranks on business quantities with `created_at` as the tie-break. No decision in this system is ever made by comparing UUIDs. Because the logical clock holds still until it is next set, two sibling rows can share an identical `created_at`; where that happens the residual ordering is the order in which the rows were inserted, so the earlier-created row still sorts first, and identifiers remain outside the comparison.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Two decisions in the outage carry a two-person rule, and both are enforced by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against a user identifier recorded earlier on the same row.

A core loading pattern records the `X-User-ID` of the reactor engineer who submitted it as `created_by_user_id`. Approving that pattern records the approver as `approved_by_user_id`, and the approving `X-User-ID` must differ from `created_by_user_id`; an attempt to approve one's own reload design is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A startup test likewise records its author's `X-User-ID` as `created_by_user_id`. When that test's hold point is classified `REGULATORY`, the user who satisfies it must differ from its author, and the satisfying user is recorded as `satisfied_by_user_id`; a self-release of a regulatory hold point is rejected with the same `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. For hold points classified `STATION` or `NONE` no two-person rule applies, and the author may satisfy the test.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Plant Configuration: Pools, Rad Areas and Safety Functions

Before any outage can be planned, the station's physical configuration must be on record, and that registration is reserved to `StationAdmin`. Three kinds of plant asset are registered, each independent of any particular outage and each shared across both generating units. Every one of them declares the unit it belongs to through `station_unit`, which is either `UNIT_1` or `UNIT_2`.

Registering a spent fuel pool records `cells_total`, the number of storage cells in the pool rack, which must be greater than zero; `cells_baseline_occupied`, the cells already filled by legacy inventory at registration, which must be at least zero and no greater than `cells_total`; and `full_core_offload_reserve_cells`, the block of cells the station holds permanently open so that a full core can be offloaded in an unplanned condition, which must be at least zero. At registration the pool's live ledger is initialised: `cells_occupied` is set equal to `cells_baseline_occupied`, `cells_reserved` is set to 0, `cells_available` is computed as `cells_total` less `cells_occupied` less `cells_reserved`, and `protected_cells_available` is computed as `cells_available` less `full_core_offload_reserve_cells`. That last figure is a headroom number, not a count of physical cells, and it is permitted to be negative when the protected block has legitimately been drawn down.

Registering a radiological area records `area_code`, a station area code of 1 to 32 characters that must be unique within the area's `station_unit`; `dose_rate_mrem_per_hr`, the general-area dose rate used for every permit dose estimate in that area, which must be greater than zero; `occupancy_limit_workers`, the number of workers the area may hold, which must be at least 1 and against which planners size a permit's worker complement; and `survey_buffer_hours`, the post-work survey and decay buffer appended to the trailing edge of every occupied window in that area, which must be at least zero.

Registering a shutdown safety function records `function_code`, which is one of `DECAY_HEAT_REMOVAL`, `INVENTORY_CONTROL`, `ELECTRICAL_POWER`, `CONTAINMENT` or `REACTIVITY_CONTROL` and must be unique within its `station_unit`; `trains_total`, the number of redundant trains implementing the function, which must be at least 1; and `min_trains_required`, the number of trains that must stay in service throughout the outage, which must be at least zero and no greater than `trains_total`. From those two numbers the server computes and stores `max_concurrent_oos_trains` as `trains_total` less `min_trains_required` — the aggregate out-of-service capacity that §3.4 polices.

### 3.2. Opening an Outage and Scoring the Scope Baseline

An outage is opened by either `StationAdmin` or `OutageDirector` and begins life in `PLANNING`. Its creation payload sets the `station_unit`, a `designation` such as a station's 1R24 label — 1 to 32 characters, unique within the `station_unit` — and the three commitments against which everything else is measured: `duration_target_hours`, the committed breaker-to-breaker duration target, which must be greater than zero; `dose_budget_total_mrem`, the total collective dose budget, which must be greater than zero; and `cost_budget_usd`, the approved cost budget, which must be greater than zero. The payload also carries `scope_freeze_at`, a mandatory ISO 8601 instant recording the station's scope-freeze commitment — accepted in any parseable ISO 8601 form and stored converted to UTC with exactly three fractional digits — at or after which discretionary scope additions are barred as a matter of station policy; it is stored for planning and audit and is not itself a request-rejection gate, the enforceable discretionary-scope gate being the duration-posture check described later in this subsection. Two workforce parameters are optional: `required_training_modules`, the number of general-employee-training modules a temporary worker must finish before a badge may be issued, which must be at least zero and defaults to 4, and `required_badged_workers`, the minimum badged headcount needed before execution may commence, which must be at least zero and defaults to 0.

At creation the outage's `status` is `PLANNING`, its `schedule_posture` is `ON_TRACK` and its `alara_posture` is `NORMAL`. Every ledger and roll-up field starts at zero: `dose_committed_mrem`, `approved_scope_cost_usd`, `scope_contingency_usd`, `total_scope_cost_usd`, `approved_emergent_hours`, `projected_duration_hours` and `risk_adjusted_duration_hours` are each 0.00, `badged_worker_count` is 0, and `dose_budget_remaining_mrem` therefore equals `dose_budget_total_mrem`. `critical_path_activity_id` and `scope_locked_at` are both null. All post-outage fields — `actual_duration_hours`, `actual_cost_usd`, `review_notes`, `duration_variance_pct`, `adjusted_duration_variance_pct`, `dose_variance_pct`, `cost_variance_pct`, `outage_performance_index` and `adjusted_performance_index` — are null until the review of §3.9 runs. The acting `X-User-ID` is stored as `created_by_user_id` for audit only.

Scope arrives as work orders, added beneath the outage by either `OutagePlanner` or `OutageDirector`. A work order declares `wo_type`, one of `MAINTENANCE`, `MODIFICATION` or `INSPECTION`; a `title` of 1 to 200 characters; `estimated_hours`, greater than zero; `estimated_cost_usd`, at least zero; and `estimated_dose_mrem`, at least zero. Three further inputs are optional: `regulatory_commitment`, a flag defaulting to false that is true when the work discharges a docketed regulatory commitment; `safety_function_id`, defaulting to null, naming the shutdown safety function the work touches; and `requires_rad_work`, a flag defaulting to false that is true when the work will need at least one radiological work permit. A newly created work order lands in `status` `SCOPED` with `origin` `PLANNED_SCOPE`, and both `deferral_reason` and `return_reason` are null. Its `outage_id` is taken from the outage the creation path names. Creation carries no outage-status gate of any kind — scope may be added beneath an outage in any status, including one already past `SCOPE_LOCKED` — and it leaves the outage row itself untouched: no outage `version` bump and no roll-up reprojection, since a `SCOPED` work order sits outside every roll-up base.

Creation immediately scores the work order, writing `priority_score` and the band `priority`. The score starts from a type weight — 20 points for `MAINTENANCE`, 35 for `MODIFICATION` and 30 for `INSPECTION`. A bonus of 25 points is added when the work order names a shutdown safety function, and a further 20 points when `regulatory_commitment` is true. A duration-pressure term is then added: divide the work order's `estimated_hours` by the owning outage's `duration_target_hours`, multiply by 300, round the result half up to two decimal places, and take the smaller of that figure and a ceiling of 30 points. Note that this term is cross-entity — the very same work order scores higher against a 360-hour target than against a 720-hour target. A dose penalty is then subtracted: divide `estimated_dose_mrem` by 100, discard the fractional part entirely so that only completed 100-mrem blocks count (199 mrem penalises exactly as much as 100 mrem), and take the smaller of that whole number and a ceiling of 15 points. The type weight, the two bonuses and the duration-pressure term are summed, the dose penalty is subtracted, and the result is rounded half up to two decimal places to give `priority_score`. Only the single work order and its owning outage's duration target participate; no other work order affects the score.

The band follows from the rounded score: a score of at least 75 makes `priority` `CRITICAL`, a score of at least 45 makes it `HIGH`, and anything lower makes it `DISCRETIONARY`. Both comparisons are inclusive and are made on the rounded figure, so a score of exactly 75.00 is `CRITICAL` while 74.99 is `HIGH`.

A work order then walks a lifecycle with two revision loops in it. An `OutagePlanner` moves it `SCOPED → PLANNED`; the work order must be in `SCOPED`, and the owning outage must be in `PLANNING`, `SCOPE_LOCKED` or `EXECUTION`, otherwise `ERR_OUTAGE_STATE_INVALID` is returned. That move bumps the work order's own `version` by 1 and nothing else: no roll-up moves and the outage's `version` is left where it stood, because a `PLANNED` work order is still outside the cost base. Approval of scope is the `OutageDirector`'s decision and moves the work order `PLANNED → APPROVED`; the outage must be in `PLANNING` or `SCOPE_LOCKED`, and if the work order's `priority` is `DISCRETIONARY` the outage's `schedule_posture` must be exactly `ON_TRACK` — once the duration posture has slipped to `AT_RISK` or `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` the discretionary scope window is closed and the request is rejected with `ERR_SCOPE_FROZEN`. `CRITICAL` and `HIGH` work orders are unaffected by the posture. Approval recomputes the outage's scope cost roll-up and increments the outage's `version` by 1.

Reopening an approved work order for revision is the reverse move, `APPROVED → PLANNED`, and it carries a mandatory `return_reason` of 1 to 500 characters in its payload. It is refused with `ERR_ACTIVITY_STATE_INVALID` if any schedule activity of that work order has already reached `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETE` — work already touched in the field cannot be un-approved. The touched-activity test is the only precondition beyond the work order's own state: reopening imposes no outage-status gate, so an `APPROVED` work order may be pulled back to `PLANNED` whatever status its outage holds. The move recomputes the scope cost roll-up and increments the outage's `version` by 1, and the `APPROVED → PLANNED → APPROVED` loop may be walked any number of times. The same reason-bearing operation also serves as the reinstatement path `DEFERRED → PLANNED`: here the outage must be in `PLANNING`, `SCOPE_LOCKED` or `EXECUTION`, the supplied `return_reason` is stored, `deferral_reason` is cleared back to null, and the work order's own `version` increments by 1. Reinstatement is confined to the work-order row: it revives nothing the deferral cascade destroyed — activities cancelled and permits voided then stay cancelled and voided — and it reprojects neither the scope cost roll-up nor the critical path, the postures or the outage's `version`, all of which move again only when the reinstated work order is approved afresh.

Dropping a work order from the outage is deferral, and it carries a mandatory `deferral_reason` of 1 to 500 characters. From `PLANNED → DEFERRED` there is no further precondition beyond the state itself; the stored `deferral_reason` is taken from the payload, then two cascades run. Every schedule activity of that work order still in `NOT_STARTED` moves to `CANCELLED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, and every radiological permit of that work order in `DRAFT` or `HELD_FOR_ALARA` moves to `VOIDED` with its `void_reason` set to the literal `WORK_ORDER_DEFERRED`. Because a `PLANNED` work order can hold no approved permit, no ledger row is written on this path. The outage's `projected_duration_hours`, `risk_adjusted_duration_hours`, `critical_path_activity_id` and `schedule_posture` are then recomputed and the outage's `version` increments by 1.

Deferral from `APPROVED → DEFERRED` is the same operation but with a fuller cascade, and it is refused with `ERR_ACTIVITY_STATE_INVALID` when any activity of the work order is already `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETE`. Hop one cancels the work order's `NOT_STARTED` activities in ascending `created_at` order. Hop two voids the work order's permits in `DRAFT`, `HELD_FOR_ALARA` *and* `APPROVED`, each with `void_reason` set to `WORK_ORDER_DEFERRED`, processed in ascending permit `created_at` order; each permit that had actually reached `APPROVED` appends a dose ledger row whose `direction` is `RELEASE`, whose `amount_mrem` is that permit's `committed_dose_mrem` and whose `reason` is `WORK_ORDER_DEFERRED`. Hop three replays the ledger, refreshing `dose_committed_mrem` and `dose_budget_remaining_mrem`. Hop four recomputes `alara_posture` from the new remaining-to-total ratio, and if the posture has returned to `NORMAL` every permit of the outage sitting in `HELD_FOR_ALARA` returns to `DRAFT`, in ascending `created_at` order. Finally the scope cost roll-up, the critical path figures — `projected_duration_hours`, `risk_adjusted_duration_hours` and `critical_path_activity_id` — and `schedule_posture` are recomputed, and the outage's `version` increments by 1.

Two further work-order transitions are driven by the schedule rather than by an operator. When a schedule activity linked to an `APPROVED` work order is started, that work order moves `APPROVED → EXECUTING`. When an activity completion leaves every activity of a work order in either `COMPLETE` or `CANCELLED` with at least one of them `COMPLETE`, the work order moves `EXECUTING → COMPLETE`, which is its terminal state. Both bump the work order's `version` by 1.

The scope cost roll-up itself is a tiered contingency calculation over the outage's committed scope, and it re-runs on scope approval, on return-to-planning, on deferral, on scope lock, and inside the emergent-work cascade of §3.8. First, `approved_scope_cost_usd` is the sum of `estimated_cost_usd` over the outage's work orders whose status is `APPROVED`, `EXECUTING` or `COMPLETE`, rounded half up to two decimal places; work orders in `SCOPED`, `PLANNED` or `DEFERRED` are excluded entirely. A single contingency rate is then selected from that base: 8 percent when the base is below 5,000,000 US dollars, 12 percent when the base is below 20,000,000, and 15 percent otherwise. The rate is flat across the whole base, not marginal per band, and the tier is selected from the base alone — selecting it from the base plus contingency is a different and incorrect answer whenever the contingency would push the base across a boundary. `scope_contingency_usd` is the base multiplied by the selected rate, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `total_scope_cost_usd` is the base plus the contingency, likewise rounded. Because deferred work orders leave the base, deferring one approved job can drop the rate an entire tier.

Work orders can be listed with filters on `outage_id`, `safety_function_id`, `status`, `priority` and `origin`, alongside the usual `limit` and `offset`. That listing is the reverse-reachability channel for a work order's outage and safety function, and it is the only way to discover a work order that the emergent-work cascade minted rather than an operator created.

### 3.3. Fuel Reload Design and Spent Fuel Pool Capacity

Reload design is the `ReactorEngineer`'s work. A core loading pattern is submitted beneath an outage and declares `cycle_length_mwd_mtu`, the target cycle burnup in megawatt-days per metric ton of uranium, which must be greater than zero; `fresh_assembly_count`, the number of fresh assemblies the pattern loads, which must be at least 1; and `discharge_assembly_count`, the number of assemblies the pattern discharges to the spent fuel pool, which must be at least zero. The submission also carries `expected_outage_version` as described in §2.7. Submission carries no outage-status gate of any kind — a reload design may be submitted beneath an outage in any status, the `PLANNING` restriction biting only at approval.

Submission computes the pattern's `reactivity_margin_pcm`, the predicted shutdown margin in pcm. Start from a base shutdown margin of 800 pcm. Add 12 pcm for each fresh assembly the pattern loads. Then subtract a burnup penalty: divide `cycle_length_mwd_mtu` by 1000 to express the cycle in gigawatt-days per metric ton, multiply that quotient by 15 pcm, and subtract the product. Round the result half up to two decimal places. The pattern's `discharge_assembly_count` plays no part in this arithmetic. The margin is computed from `cycle_length_mwd_mtu` exactly as submitted, whereas the figure stored on the pattern row is that same input rounded half up to two decimal places. A core that cannot be held subcritical is never persisted: if the computed `reactivity_margin_pcm` falls below 1000 pcm, the submission is rejected with `ERR_SHUTDOWN_MARGIN_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422) and no pattern row is written.

An admitted pattern lands in `status` `DRAFT` with the submitting `X-User-ID` stored as `created_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` null. Because only one draft design per outage is ever live, the submission also moves any existing pattern of the same outage that is still in `DRAFT` to `SUPERSEDED`, bumping that pattern's own `version` by 1 — a design under revision is superseded rather than deleted. The outage's `version` increments by 1 as well.

Approval moves a pattern `DRAFT → APPROVED` and is open to `ReactorEngineer` and `OutageDirector` alike, subject to the two-person rule of §2.10. The pattern must be in `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_CORE_LOAD_PATTERN_STATE_INVALID`; the owning outage must still be in `PLANNING`, otherwise `ERR_OUTAGE_STATE_INVALID`; the pattern's `reactivity_margin_pcm` must still be at least 1000 pcm, otherwise `ERR_SHUTDOWN_MARGIN_INSUFFICIENT`; and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `created_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Those four checks are evaluated in exactly that order, which is the order §2.8 fixes — the margin re-check at rank 9 ahead of the two-person rule at rank 20. Approval stores the approving `X-User-ID` as `approved_by_user_id`, moves every *other* pattern of the same outage that was in `APPROVED` to `SUPERSEDED` in ascending `created_at` order, and increments the outage's `version` by 1. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal for a pattern. Patterns can be read individually and listed beneath their outage with a `status` filter alongside `limit` and `offset`, which is the reverse-reachability channel for the pattern's outage.

Pool capacity is booked through reservations, also a `ReactorEngineer` responsibility. A reservation names the consuming outage through `outage_id` and the pool through `spent_fuel_pool_id`, requests a whole number of cells as `cells_requested` (at least 1), declares a `purpose` of `DISCHARGE`, `FRESH_STAGING` or `CONTINGENCY`, and carries `expected_spent_fuel_pool_version` per §2.7. Of the two body identifiers, `spent_fuel_pool_id` is resolved first and `outage_id` second, so a request in which neither names a stored row reports `ERR_SPENT_FUEL_POOL_NOT_FOUND`. The `purpose` decides which of the pool's two headroom figures the request is admitted against, and the server records that decision as `availability_variant`: a `DISCHARGE` or `FRESH_STAGING` reservation is a planned, long-dwell occupancy and takes the `FULL_CORE_PROTECTED` variant, while a `CONTINGENCY` reservation — precisely the unplanned condition the reserve block exists to absorb — takes the `NOMINAL` variant.

Admission then compares `cells_requested` against the headroom the variant selects: `protected_cells_available` for `FULL_CORE_PROTECTED`, and `cells_available` for `NOMINAL`. The comparison has an inclusive upper bound, so a request for exactly the available headroom is admitted; a request for one cell more is rejected with `ERR_POOL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The practical difference between the two variants is exactly `full_core_offload_reserve_cells`: for identical pool state and an identical `cells_requested`, the `NOMINAL` variant admits requests the `FULL_CORE_PROTECTED` variant refuses, because a planned occupancy may never encroach on the block held open for an unplanned full-core offload while a contingency may. Only the requested cell count and the pool's two stored headroom figures participate; no other reservation is consulted directly. A `DISCHARGE` reservation carries one additional guard, evaluated ahead of both the pool's optimistic-concurrency check and the headroom comparison: the consuming outage must already own a core loading pattern in `APPROVED`, otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_CORE_LOAD_PATTERN_NOT_APPROVED`. Reservation creation carries no outage-status gate at all, so cells may be booked against an outage in any status.

An admitted reservation is written in `status` `RESERVED` with `release_reason` null, the pool ledger is reprojected, and the pool's `version` increments by 1. The consuming outage's row is not touched anywhere on this path: no outage field moves and its `version` stands still, here and equally on consumption and release. Reservations can be listed with filters on `outage_id`, `spent_fuel_pool_id`, `purpose` and `status` plus `limit` and `offset`, which is how a reservation's outage and pool are reached in reverse.

Consuming a reservation moves it `RESERVED → CONSUMED` and requires the owning outage to be in `SCOPE_LOCKED` or `EXECUTION`, otherwise `ERR_OUTAGE_STATE_INVALID`; a reservation that is not in `RESERVED` yields `ERR_POOL_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID`. Consumption is a ledger transfer rather than a new booking: the pool's `cells_reserved` falls by `cells_requested` while its `cells_occupied` rises by the same number, both headroom figures are recomputed, and the pool's `version` increments by 1. Releasing a reservation instead moves it `RESERVED → RELEASED`, storing the mandatory `release_reason` of 1 to 500 characters its payload supplies; release, unlike consumption, carries no outage-status gate whatsoever, so a reservation may be released whatever status its outage holds. The pool's `cells_reserved` falls by `cells_requested` and `cells_occupied` is left untouched, so both headroom figures return exactly to their pre-reservation values, and the pool's `version` increments by 1. Both `CONSUMED` and `RELEASED` are terminal.

The pool ledger itself is a reprojection, not an increment. `cells_occupied` is `cells_baseline_occupied` plus the sum of `cells_requested` over that pool's reservations in `CONSUMED`. `cells_reserved` is the sum of `cells_requested` over that pool's reservations in `RESERVED`. `cells_available` is `cells_total` less `cells_occupied` less `cells_reserved`. `protected_cells_available` is `cells_available` less `full_core_offload_reserve_cells`, and it may be negative. Reservations in `RELEASED` are excluded from the projection entirely, which is precisely what makes reserve-then-release an exact compensation. All four figures are integers.

### 3.4. Schedule Development: the Activity Chain, the Critical Path and Safety Function Protection

The schedule is the `OutagePlanner`'s instrument, and every schedule activity is created beneath its outage. An activity declares a `label` of 1 to 200 characters, a `planned_start_offset_hours` expressed as hours after breaker-open — outage hour zero — which must be at least zero, and a `planned_duration_hours` greater than zero. Four optional links follow. `work_order_id` names the work order the activity executes, or is null for a non-work evolution such as a plant manoeuvre. `predecessor_activity_id` names the single logic predecessor that chains this activity behind another, or is null for a chain root. `oos_safety_function_id` names a shutdown safety function whose train this activity takes out of service, or is null; when it is supplied, `oos_train_label` — a train label of 1 to 8 characters — must accompany it, and its absence is a malformed request carrying `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at rank 4 of §2.8. The converse pairing is deliberately not policed: an `oos_train_label` supplied alongside a null `oos_safety_function_id` is accepted and stored as given, and the activity simply takes no train out of service and is not subject to the capacity check below. The three body identifiers are resolved in a fixed order — `oos_safety_function_id` first, then `work_order_id`, then `predecessor_activity_id` — so when more than one of them names no stored row, the earliest in that order decides which 404 code is returned.

Two graph rules guard creation and re-sequencing. A supplied `predecessor_activity_id` must name an activity whose owning outage is the outage in the creation path, and a supplied `work_order_id` must name a work order whose owning outage is likewise that outage; either mismatch is rejected with `ERR_PREDECESSOR_OUTAGE_MISMATCH`. And the predecessor chain reachable from the activity must terminate at a root: a chain that revisits an activity already seen closes a loop and is rejected with `ERR_ACTIVITY_CYCLE`. At creation the chain is acyclic by construction, so in practice the loop rule bites on re-sequencing, where naming the activity itself, or naming an activity whose own predecessor chain already reaches back to it, closes the loop. For re-sequencing, the ownership rule compares the named predecessor's outage against the re-sequenced activity's own outage. Creation carries no outage-status gate of any kind: an activity may be added beneath an outage in any status. And although creation reprojects the outage's critical-path figures and its `schedule_posture` exactly as described below, it leaves the outage row's own bookkeeping alone — the outage's `version` and `updated_at` stand still, unlike re-sequencing and completion, which both bump them.

An activity carries two independent state dimensions that move separately and must not be conflated. `execution_status` tracks as-worked progress across `NOT_STARTED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETE` and `CANCELLED`. `constraint_status` tracks whether the activity's logic predecessor has cleared, and is either `CLEAR` or `BLOCKED`. At creation the predicate is evaluated exactly once: the activity is created `BLOCKED` when a predecessor is supplied whose `execution_status` is anything other than `COMPLETE`, and `CLEAR` otherwise — including the case where the supplied predecessor is already `COMPLETE`.

Every activity creation, every re-sequencing, every completion, every work-order deferral, every scope lock and both recompute steps of the emergent-work cascade reproject the outage's critical path. The input set is the outage's activities whose `execution_status` is `NOT_STARTED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETE`; a `CANCELLED` activity is invisible to the walk, so an activity whose predecessor was cancelled is treated as a chain root for length purposes even though its `constraint_status` remains `BLOCKED` — the two dimensions really are independent. Each activity in the input set is assigned a contingency factor: an activity with no `work_order_id` takes 10 percent, and an activity linked to a work order takes the factor of that work order's `priority` band — 25 percent for `CRITICAL`, 15 percent for `HIGH` and 5 percent for `DISCRETIONARY`.

The walk proceeds in topological order over the `predecessor_activity_id` edges, with ties in walk order resolved by ascending `created_at`. For each activity, let the predecessor be the row in the input set whose identity matches its `predecessor_activity_id`, if such a row exists. The activity's `path_length_hours` is the predecessor's `path_length_hours` — taken as 0.00 when there is no predecessor in the input set — plus the activity's own `planned_duration_hours`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Its `risk_adjusted_path_length_hours` is the predecessor's `risk_adjusted_path_length_hours` — again 0.00 when there is no such predecessor — plus the activity's own `planned_duration_hours` multiplied by one plus that activity's contingency factor, rounded half up to two decimal places. The rounding is applied at each hop of the walk rather than once at the end, so the risk-adjusted chain accumulates already-rounded hop values.

The outage's `projected_duration_hours` is then the largest `path_length_hours` in the input set, or 0.00 when the input set is empty, and its `risk_adjusted_duration_hours` is the largest `risk_adjusted_path_length_hours`, or 0.00 when the set is empty. `critical_path_activity_id` identifies the activity terminating the deterministic longest path: among the input set, the winner is the activity with the greatest `path_length_hours`, ties broken by ascending `created_at`, and the field is null when the input set is empty. It is an identity only and is never itself an input to any decision. Each activity's `is_critical_path` flag is true for that winner and for every ancestor reached by following `predecessor_activity_id` from the winner back to a chain root, and false for every other activity in the input set and for every `CANCELLED` activity. That ancestor walk runs only through the input set, so it halts the moment a `predecessor_activity_id` points at a `CANCELLED` activity — that cancelled row is a chain root for this purpose too, and neither it nor anything further up its chain is flagged.

The duration posture follows immediately from the projection. `schedule_posture` is `ON_TRACK` when `projected_duration_hours` is less than or equal to the outage's `duration_target_hours`; otherwise it is `AT_RISK` when the projection is less than or equal to the target multiplied by 1.10 and rounded half up to two decimal places; otherwise it is `RECOVERY_REQUIRED`. Both boundaries are inclusive: a projection exactly equal to the target is `ON_TRACK`, and a projection exactly equal to the rounded 110-percent ceiling is `AT_RISK`. The posture reads only those two outage duration fields — with one exception described in §3.9, where a failed reactivity measurement forces `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` outright, bypassing this comparison until the next schedule reprojection runs.

Re-sequencing an activity sets its `predecessor_activity_id` from the payload, and supplying null detaches the activity and makes it a chain root; the field is mandatory in that payload — it may be sent explicitly null but it may not be omitted, and omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The activity must itself still be in `NOT_STARTED`, otherwise `ERR_ACTIVITY_STATE_INVALID`; beyond that own-state test re-sequencing carries no outage-status gate whatever, and shutdown-safety-function capacity is not re-checked, the activity's out-of-service window being untouched by the move. The activity's `constraint_status` is then re-evaluated on the same predicate as at creation — `CLEAR` when the new predecessor is null or already `COMPLETE`, `BLOCKED` otherwise — every activity's `path_length_hours`, `risk_adjusted_path_length_hours` and `is_critical_path` are reprojected across the outage, the outage's `projected_duration_hours`, `risk_adjusted_duration_hours`, `critical_path_activity_id` and `schedule_posture` are recomputed, and the outage's `version` increments by 1.

Starting an activity moves it `NOT_STARTED → IN_PROGRESS` and has three guards beyond its own state. Its `constraint_status` must be `CLEAR`, otherwise `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_COMPLETE`; the owning outage must be in `EXECUTION`, otherwise `ERR_OUTAGE_STATE_INVALID`; and either the activity has no `work_order_id` at all, or the named work order's status is `APPROVED` or `EXECUTING`, otherwise `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_APPROVED`. The start stamps `started_at` with the current logical-clock instant, and when the activity does name a work order that is still in `APPROVED`, that work order moves `APPROVED → EXECUTING` in the same request. Each row so moved bumps its own `version` by 1, and the outage row is left entirely alone: starting an activity reprojects nothing and does not increment the outage's `version`, the cost base being unmoved because `APPROVED` and `EXECUTING` work orders both sit inside it.

Completing an activity moves it `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETE` and stamps `completed_at` from the logical clock; beyond the activity's own state there is no gate here at all, and in particular no outage-status test. Its first cascade hop works the other state dimension: every activity whose `predecessor_activity_id` names the just-completed activity has its `constraint_status` recomputed to `CLEAR`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, and their `execution_status` values are left entirely alone. That hop reaches such a successor whatever its own `execution_status` — a `CANCELLED` successor is cleared on the constraint dimension along with the rest, the two dimensions being independent — but it touches only successors that actually move: one already `CLEAR` is left completely alone, its `version` and `updated_at` unchanged, so only a successor going `BLOCKED → CLEAR` is bumped. The second hop bubbles upward: when the activity names a work order and every activity of that work order now sits in either `COMPLETE` or `CANCELLED`, the work order moves `EXECUTING → COMPLETE`. The outage's critical path figures and `schedule_posture` are then reprojected and the outage's `version` increments by 1. Note carefully that the critical path consumes `planned_duration_hours` and never a recorded actual — `started_at` and `completed_at` never enter the arithmetic — so completing an activity on time leaves `projected_duration_hours` unchanged.

An activity can also leave `NOT_STARTED` for `CANCELLED` without an operator acting on it: whenever the owning work order is deferred, whether by an operator, by the scope-lock auto-deferral of §3.6 or by the posture auto-deferral of §3.8, that work order's `NOT_STARTED` activities are cancelled. Only activities that actually name a work order can be cancelled this way. A cancelled activity drops out of the critical-path input set, so the outage's `projected_duration_hours` and `risk_adjusted_duration_hours` are reprojected over the surviving activities. Its successors, however, keep `constraint_status` `BLOCKED`: a cancelled predecessor never satisfies the clear predicate, and only a genuinely `COMPLETE` predecessor — or a re-sequencing onto a different predecessor — releases them. Both `COMPLETE` and `CANCELLED` are terminal on the execution dimension.

Shutdown safety function protection is checked at activity creation whenever `oos_safety_function_id` is supplied, and it is the reason the station may take one train out of service but not two. The activity's out-of-service window is a **closed** interval running from `planned_start_offset_hours` to `planned_start_offset_hours` plus `planned_duration_hours`, with both endpoints inclusive — deliberately the opposite of the radiological window semantics of §3.7, so two out-of-service windows that merely touch at an endpoint *do* conflict. The server collects every schedule activity naming the same `oos_safety_function_id` whose `execution_status` is not `CANCELLED`, excluding the incoming activity itself, and whose window intersects the incoming window. Critically, that collection spans **all** outages, because a safety function is a plant asset shared across units, so out-of-service windows aggregate across concurrent outages. It then forms the set of distinct `oos_train_label` values among those overlapping activities, unioned with the incoming activity's own `oos_train_label`, and compares the size of that set against the safety function's `max_concurrent_oos_trains`. If the count exceeds the capacity the creation is rejected with `ERR_SAFETY_FUNCTION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because the aggregate is over *distinct* labels, two activities taking the same train out at overlapping times consume one unit of capacity rather than two.

Schedule activities can be listed with filters on `outage_id`, `work_order_id`, `predecessor_activity_id`, `oos_safety_function_id`, `execution_status` and `constraint_status`, plus `limit` and `offset`. That listing is the reverse-reachability channel for all four of an activity's foreign keys and the only way to discover the activity minted by the emergent-work cascade.

### 3.5. The Supplemental Workforce Pipeline

Several thousand temporary craft workers pass through a refueling outage, and each of them walks the same onboarding pipeline: a request, two parallel qualification branches, a join, and finally a badge. A `ContractorCoordinator` adds a worker beneath the outage, supplying `full_name` — the name as it will be printed on the badge, 1 to 120 characters — a `craft` classification drawn from `PIPEFITTER`, `ELECTRICIAN`, `INSULATOR`, `SCAFFOLD_BUILDER`, `RAD_TECH` and `LABORER`, and a `sponsoring_contractor` of 1 to 120 characters sponsoring the worker's site access. The worker is written in `status` `REQUESTED` with `background_status` `PENDING`, `training_status` `PENDING`, `training_modules_completed` 0 and `badge_number` null. Creation carries no outage-status gate of any kind — a worker may be requested beneath an outage in any status — and it leaves the outage row itself untouched: no outage `version` bump and no `badged_worker_count` reprojection, since only badging ever moves that count.

The first branch of the fork is access-authorization screening, recorded by `AccessAuthority` with an `outcome`. An `outcome` of `CLEAR` sets `background_status` to `CLEAR` and moves a worker in `REQUESTED` to `SCREENING`, completing branch A of the fork. An `outcome` of `ADVERSE` instead sets `background_status` to `ADVERSE` and moves the worker to `DENIED`, whether the worker was in `REQUESTED` or already in `SCREENING`; the training branch is abandoned and no badge can ever be issued. A background result may only be recorded for a worker in `REQUESTED` or `SCREENING` — recording one for a worker already `DENIED` or `BADGED` is rejected with `ERR_WORKER_STATE_INVALID`. Note the asymmetry between the two branches deliberately: the `ADVERSE` path never touches the outage's `badged_worker_count`, whereas the `CLEAR` path eventually will.

The second branch is training, and it may be recorded by either `AccessAuthority` or `ContractorCoordinator` through a `modules_completed` figure — an integer of at least zero, with no upper bound, so a count exceeding the outage's requirement is accepted and stored exactly as submitted. That figure is stored as the worker's `training_modules_completed`, and the derived `training_status` is then recomputed: it becomes `COMPLETE` when `training_modules_completed` is greater than or equal to the owning outage's `required_training_modules`, and stays `PENDING` otherwise. Recording training also moves a worker in `REQUESTED` to `SCREENING`, and like the background result it is only accepted for a worker in `REQUESTED` or `SCREENING`, otherwise `ERR_WORKER_STATE_INVALID`. Branch B of the fork is complete only when `training_status` actually reaches `COMPLETE` — submitting a module count below the outage's requirement records progress without completing the branch.

The join is evaluated by the server at the end of every background-result request and every training-completion request. A worker in `SCREENING` whose `background_status` is `CLEAR` and whose `training_status` is `COMPLETE` moves `SCREENING → BADGE_READY`. The branch write and the join are two separate mutations of the same worker row: the branch update bumps the worker's `version` by 1 and the join transition bumps it by 1 again, so a single request that trips the join raises the `version` by 2 in total while leaving `updated_at` at that request's one logical-clock instant. A request that records a branch without tripping the join bumps the `version` by exactly 1. Neither branch alone reaches `BADGE_READY`; whichever branch lands second is the one that trips the join.

Badging is `AccessAuthority`'s act and moves the worker `BADGE_READY → BADGED`. The worker must be in `BADGE_READY`, otherwise `ERR_WORKER_STATE_INVALID` — badging a worker still in `SCREENING` is exactly this rejection. The submitted `badge_number`, 1 to 24 characters, must not already be held by another worker of the same outage whose status is `BADGED`, otherwise `ERR_BADGE_NUMBER_TAKEN`; badge numbers held by denied or not-yet-badged workers do not block reuse. The badge number is stored, the outage's `badged_worker_count` is reprojected as the count of the outage's workers in `BADGED`, and the outage's `version` increments by 1. `BADGED` and `DENIED` are both terminal.

Workers can be read individually and listed beneath their outage with filters on `status` and `craft` plus `limit` and `offset`, which is the reverse-reachability channel for a worker's outage.

### 3.6. Freezing Scope, Opening the Breaker and Authorizing Startup

The outage advances through five operator-driven gates and one system-driven gate, and the `OutageDirector` owns all five of the operator gates. The lifecycle runs `PLANNING → SCOPE_LOCKED → EXECUTION → STARTUP → ONLINE → REVIEWED`, with `REVIEWED` terminal.

Scope lock moves the outage `PLANNING → SCOPE_LOCKED` and is the point at which the baseline stops moving. Two things must be true, and at this gate they are evaluated in the order stated here. First, the outage must own at least one work order in `APPROVED`, otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_STATE_INVALID` — an outage with nothing approved has no baseline to freeze. Second, the outage must own at least one core loading pattern in `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_CORE_LOAD_PATTERN_NOT_APPROVED`; an outage that owns neither an approved work order nor an approved pattern therefore reports `ERR_WORK_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`, the missing pattern surfacing only once the first precondition is satisfied. On success `scope_locked_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant, and the auto-deferral cascade runs: every work order of the outage still sitting in `SCOPED` moves `SCOPED → DEFERRED` with its `deferral_reason` set to the literal string `SCOPE_LOCK_AUTO_DEFERRAL`, processed in ascending work-order `created_at` order. For each work order deferred by that step, its activities in `NOT_STARTED` move to `CANCELLED` and its permits in `DRAFT` or `HELD_FOR_ALARA` move to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `WORK_ORDER_DEFERRED`; because a `SCOPED` work order can hold no approved permit, no dose ledger row is appended anywhere in this cascade. The outage's `approved_scope_cost_usd`, `scope_contingency_usd` and `total_scope_cost_usd` are then reprojected, followed by `projected_duration_hours`, `risk_adjusted_duration_hours`, `critical_path_activity_id` and finally `schedule_posture`. The outage's `version` increments by 1.

Breaker-open moves the outage `SCOPE_LOCKED → EXECUTION`, and it has exactly one substantive gate: the outage's `badged_worker_count` must be greater than or equal to its `required_badged_workers`, otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_BADGED_WORKFORCE_INSUFFICIENT`. The comparison is inclusive, so a headcount exactly equal to the requirement admits. The outage's `version` increments by 1. Until this gate is passed no schedule activity can be started; once it is passed, activities become startable.

Startup authorization moves the outage `EXECUTION → STARTUP` and carries three gates evaluated on top of the outage's own state. The outage must own at least one startup test, otherwise `ERR_STARTUP_SEQUENCE_EMPTY` — an outage with an empty startup sequence cannot be authorized into startup. No work order of the outage may still be in `SCOPED`, `PLANNED`, `APPROVED` or `EXECUTING`, otherwise `ERR_OPEN_WORK_ORDERS_REMAIN`; only `COMPLETE` and `DEFERRED` work orders are acceptable at this gate. And every activity of the outage that names an `oos_safety_function_id` must have reached `COMPLETE` or `CANCELLED`, otherwise `ERR_ACTIVITY_STATE_INVALID` — shutdown safety functions must be restored to service before the unit is taken to startup. The outage's `version` increments by 1, and the startup tests become satisfiable in `sequence_index` order.

The move `STARTUP → ONLINE` is the one gate no operator drives. It is a compound transition: the outage's status is a function of its startup-test children, and the outage moves to `ONLINE` automatically at the moment the last of its startup tests reaches `SATISFIED`, provided the outage was in `STARTUP` and every one of its tests is then `SATISFIED`. The outage's `version` increments by 1 as part of that same request. The final gate, `ONLINE → REVIEWED`, is the post-outage review described in §3.9.

### 3.7. Radiological Work Permits, the Dose Ledger and ALARA Posture

Radiological work planning is where the outage's collective dose budget is actually spent, and it is split deliberately between two roles: a `RadProtectionPlanner` drafts and closes permits, while only a `RadProtectionManager` may approve or void one. A permit is drafted beneath the work order it covers, and the server copies that work order's `outage_id` onto the permit so that the permit is reachable from the outage as well as from the work order. The draft payload names the area through `rad_area_id`, the number of workers the permit covers as `worker_count` (at least 1), the fractional dose reduction credited to temporary shielding as `shielding_factor` (at least 0 and at most 0.95), and the permit's planned window through `planned_start_offset_hours` (at least zero, expressed as hours after outage hour zero) and `planned_duration_hours` (greater than zero).

Drafting computes four dose figures and one flag, and the rounding order is pinned. First, the per-worker estimate: multiply the area's `dose_rate_mrem_per_hr` by the permit's `planned_duration_hours`, then by one minus the permit's `shielding_factor`, and round the product half up to two decimal places to give `per_worker_dose_mrem`. Second, multiply that **already rounded** per-worker figure by `worker_count` and round half up to two decimal places to give `collective_dose_mrem`; multiplying the unrounded per-worker figure and rounding the product afterwards is a different and incorrect answer for most inputs. Third, `contingency_dose_mrem` is `collective_dose_mrem` multiplied by 20 percent, rounded half up to two decimal places. Fourth, `committed_dose_mrem` is `collective_dose_mrem` plus `contingency_dose_mrem`, rounded half up to two decimal places; this is the amount that will be debited from the outage's dose budget if the permit is approved. The flag `alara_review_required` is true when `collective_dose_mrem` is greater than or equal to 300 mrem — note that the ALARA gate reads the pre-contingency collective figure, never the post-contingency committed figure. The area's dose rate is read once, at draft time, and is not re-read if the area is later re-registered with a different rate.

A drafted permit lands in `status` `DRAFT` with `alara_review_reference` null, `actual_dose_mrem` null and `void_reason` null, and drafting commits no dose whatsoever to the outage. Drafting carries no status gate on either side of the permit's parentage: neither the covering work order's status nor the owning outage's status is tested, so a permit may be drafted against a `SCOPED` work order beneath a `PLANNING` outage, and the outage row is left entirely untouched — its `version` stands still and nothing is reprojected. Permits can be read individually and listed with filters on `outage_id`, `work_order_id`, `rad_area_id` and `status` plus `limit` and `offset`, which is the reverse-reachability channel for all three of a permit's foreign keys.

Approval moves a permit `DRAFT → APPROVED` and is the single most heavily guarded operation in this system. The permit must be in `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_RWP_STATE_INVALID` — approving a permit already parked in `HELD_FOR_ALARA` is exactly this rejection. The owning outage must be in `SCOPE_LOCKED` or `EXECUTION`, otherwise `ERR_OUTAGE_STATE_INVALID`; drafting is permitted earlier, but committing dose against a `PLANNING` outage is not. The covering work order must itself be in `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_APPROVED`. The outage's `alara_posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_ALARA_POSTURE_SUSPENDED` — a fully consumed dose budget blocks every further dose commitment. When the permit's `alara_review_required` is true, the approval payload must supply a non-empty `alara_review_reference` of 1 to 64 characters, otherwise `ERR_ALARA_REVIEW_REQUIRED`; when the flag is false the reference is optional and, if supplied, is simply recorded. The permit's window must not conflict with another approved permit in the same area, otherwise `ERR_RAD_AREA_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. And finally the permit's `committed_dose_mrem` must be less than or equal to the outage's `dose_budget_remaining_mrem`, otherwise `ERR_DOSE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`; the comparison is inclusive, so a permit that consumes exactly the remaining budget is admitted — and drives the posture straight to `SUSPENDED`. These seven checks are evaluated in the order just given, which is the order §2.8 fixes: a suspended posture surfaces as `ERR_ALARA_POSTURE_SUSPENDED` rather than being masked by the later window or dose-budget checks.

Area occupancy deserves its own statement because its interval semantics differ from the safety-function rule of §3.4. A permit's occupied window is **half-open**: it begins inclusively at `planned_start_offset_hours` and ends exclusively at `planned_start_offset_hours` plus `planned_duration_hours` plus the area's `survey_buffer_hours`, so the survey and decay buffer is appended to the trailing edge only. A conflict exists when that window intersects the window of any *other* permit sharing the same `rad_area_id` whose status is `APPROVED`. Permits in `DRAFT`, `HELD_FOR_ALARA`, `CLOSED` or `VOIDED` never block. Exact adjacency is explicitly admitted: a permit beginning precisely at another permit's buffered end offset does not conflict. Because the buffer is read from the area rather than from the permit, two areas with different `survey_buffer_hours` return different verdicts for otherwise identical permits.

Approval's side effects are a four-hop cascade. Hop one appends a dose ledger row whose `rwp_id` is the permit, whose `direction` is `COMMIT`, whose `amount_mrem` is the permit's `committed_dose_mrem` and whose `reason` is `RWP_APPROVED`. Hop two replays the ledger to refresh the outage's `dose_committed_mrem` and `dose_budget_remaining_mrem`. Hop three recomputes the outage's `alara_posture` from the new remaining-to-total ratio. Hop four is conditional: if the posture moved out of `NORMAL` during this very request, then every permit of the same outage that is in `DRAFT` and whose `committed_dose_mrem` exceeds 200 mrem moves `DRAFT → HELD_FOR_ALARA`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, each such permit's own `version` incrementing by 1. Draft permits at or below that 200-mrem threshold are left in `DRAFT`. The outage's `version` increments by 1.

The dose ledger is append-only and is always reprojected rather than adjusted. Starting from 0.00, the server replays every ledger row reachable from the outage through its permits, in ascending row `created_at` order with ties broken by `created_at` alone and never by identity; a row whose `direction` is `COMMIT` adds its `amount_mrem` to the running total and a row whose `direction` is `RELEASE` subtracts it. The result, rounded half up to two decimal places, is the outage's `dose_committed_mrem`, and `dose_budget_remaining_mrem` is `dose_budget_total_mrem` less `dose_committed_mrem`, likewise rounded; it is permitted to go negative when actual dose overruns the commitment. No status filter is applied to the replay — a voided permit's rows stay in the ledger forever, and it is the offsetting `RELEASE` row rather than any deletion that neutralises them. Permits that never reached `APPROVED` contribute nothing simply because no `COMMIT` row was ever written for them. Every ledger row carries an unsigned `amount_mrem` greater than zero; the sign lives entirely in `direction`, and the `reason` records which transition appended the row, drawn from `RWP_APPROVED`, `RWP_CLOSED_UNDERRUN`, `RWP_CLOSED_OVERRUN`, `RWP_VOIDED` and `WORK_ORDER_DEFERRED`. A permit's ledger rows can be read in ascending `created_at` order with an optional `direction` filter plus `limit` and `offset`; that read is the reverse-reachability channel for a ledger row's `rwp_id` and the only way to discover rows appended by a cascade.

The dose posture is recomputed immediately after every ledger reprojection. Let the remaining budget be the outage's `dose_budget_remaining_mrem` and the ratio be that remaining figure divided by `dose_budget_total_mrem`, evaluated as an unrounded Decimal quotient. `alara_posture` is `SUSPENDED` when the remaining budget is less than or equal to zero; otherwise it is `RESTRICTED` when the ratio is less than or equal to 0.25; otherwise it is `NORMAL`. The unrounded comparison matters at the boundary: a remaining-to-total position of 250 against 1000 is exactly one quarter and is `RESTRICTED`, while 251 against 1001 is very slightly above one quarter and is `NORMAL`. Every posture move drives the hold cascade in whichever direction it went. A move from `NORMAL` into `RESTRICTED` or `SUSPENDED` parks the outage's over-threshold draft permits in `HELD_FOR_ALARA`; a move from `RESTRICTED` or `SUSPENDED` back to `NORMAL` returns every permit of the outage in `HELD_FOR_ALARA` to `DRAFT`, in ascending `created_at` order, each incrementing its own `version` by 1. That closes the posture-driven cycle `DRAFT → HELD_FOR_ALARA → DRAFT`, which may be walked any number of times.

Closing a permit reconciles the estimate against reality and may be done by either `RadProtectionPlanner` or `RadProtectionManager`. It moves the permit `APPROVED → CLOSED`; a permit not in `APPROVED` yields `ERR_RWP_STATE_INVALID`, so closing a permit still in `DRAFT` is exactly that rejection. The owning outage must be in `EXECUTION` or `STARTUP`, otherwise `ERR_OUTAGE_STATE_INVALID`. The payload supplies `actual_dose_mrem`, the as-worked collective dose, which must be at least zero, and it is stored on the permit. The ledger consequence is three-way and each branch is distinct: when `actual_dose_mrem` is less than `committed_dose_mrem` a `RELEASE` row is appended whose `amount_mrem` is `committed_dose_mrem` less `actual_dose_mrem` and whose `reason` is `RWP_CLOSED_UNDERRUN`; when `actual_dose_mrem` is greater than `committed_dose_mrem` a `COMMIT` row is appended whose `amount_mrem` is `actual_dose_mrem` less `committed_dose_mrem` and whose `reason` is `RWP_CLOSED_OVERRUN`; and when the two are exactly equal no ledger row is appended at all. The outage's `dose_committed_mrem`, `dose_budget_remaining_mrem` and `alara_posture` are then recomputed and the hold cascade runs in whichever direction the posture moved, and the outage's `version` increments by 1.

Voiding a permit is `RadProtectionManager`'s act, carries a mandatory `void_reason` of 1 to 500 characters, and is admissible from three states — with sharply different ledger consequences. Beyond the permit's own state there is no gate here at all: voiding imposes no outage-status test, so a permit may be voided whatever status its outage holds. Voiding from `DRAFT` or from `HELD_FOR_ALARA` stores the `void_reason` and appends no ledger row at all, because an unapproved permit holds no committed dose; the permit's own `version` increments by 1 and the outage row is left entirely untouched — its `version`, its two dose figures and its `alara_posture` all stand still, and no hold cascade runs. Voiding from `APPROVED` stores the `void_reason` and then runs a three-hop compensation: hop one appends a `RELEASE` row whose `amount_mrem` is the permit's `committed_dose_mrem` and whose `reason` is `RWP_VOIDED`, exactly reversing the `COMMIT` written at approval; hop two reprojects the outage's `dose_committed_mrem` and `dose_budget_remaining_mrem`; hop three recomputes `alara_posture`, and if it returns to `NORMAL` every `HELD_FOR_ALARA` permit of the outage returns to `DRAFT` in ascending `created_at` order. The outage's `version` increments by 1. A permit can also be voided without anyone acting on it, by the work-order deferral cascade of §3.2 and §3.6: a permit in `DRAFT`, `HELD_FOR_ALARA` or `APPROVED` whose work order is deferred moves to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to the literal `WORK_ORDER_DEFERRED`, and a `RELEASE` row with `reason` `WORK_ORDER_DEFERRED` is appended only when the prior status was `APPROVED`. Both `CLOSED` and `VOIDED` are terminal.

### 3.8. Emergent Work: Discovery in the Field and Disposition

No outage survives contact with the plant. Conditions found once insulation comes off or an inspection scope is opened are reported as emergent work items, and either an `OutagePlanner` or a `RadProtectionPlanner` may report one against the work order during which it was discovered — that work order becomes the item's `discovered_from_work_order_id`. The report carries a `description` of the discovered condition, 1 to 500 characters; `estimated_hours` to correct, greater than zero; `estimated_cost_usd`, at least zero; `estimated_dose_mrem`, at least zero; and an optional `discovered_during_inspection` flag, defaulting to true, which records for audit whether the condition surfaced during inspection work. A reported item lands in `status` `REPORTED` with `disposition_note` null and `created_work_order_id` null, and nothing on the outage changes until the item is dispositioned. Reporting carries no status gate whatsoever: neither the discovering work order's status nor the owning outage's status is tested, so an item may be reported against a work order in any status beneath an outage in any status, and the outage's `version` stands still. Items can be read individually and listed beneath their discovering work order with a `status` filter plus `limit` and `offset`, which is the reverse-reachability channel for `discovered_from_work_order_id`.

Disposition is the `OutageDirector`'s decision alone. It supplies a `disposition` of `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` or `ABSORBED` and an optional `disposition_note`, which defaults to null when omitted, may be sent explicitly null, and carries no length bound at all — an empty string is accepted and stored as an empty string. The item must be in `REPORTED`, otherwise `ERR_EMERGENT_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`, and the owning outage — the outage of the discovering work order — must be in `SCOPE_LOCKED` or `EXECUTION`, otherwise `ERR_OUTAGE_STATE_INVALID`. All three dispositions are terminal for the item. Whichever branch runs, the item row itself is mutated exactly once: its `version` increments by exactly 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed once, even on the eight-step `APPROVED` cascade, which writes `created_work_order_id` onto the item in a later step without bumping it a second time.

Two of the three dispositions need a **terminal-activity selector** over the discovering work order, and that selector is deterministic and pinned. Eligible candidates are the schedule activities whose `work_order_id` is the discovering work order and whose `execution_status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`. Candidates are ranked by the sum of `planned_start_offset_hours` and `planned_duration_hours`, descending — that is, the activity that finishes latest wins — with ties broken by ascending `created_at`. When no candidate is eligible the selector returns nothing. Activity identities never participate in this ranking.

An `APPROVED` disposition is the heaviest cascade in the system, and its eight steps run in this order. **Step 1** sets the item's `status` to `APPROVED` and stores the supplied `disposition_note`. **Step 2** mints a work order: its `outage_id` is the discovering work order's outage, its `origin` is `EMERGENT`, its `status` is `APPROVED` immediately, its `wo_type` is `MAINTENANCE`, its `title` is the literal prefix `EMERGENT: ` followed by the item's `description`, its `estimated_hours`, `estimated_cost_usd` and `estimated_dose_mrem` are copied from the item, its `regulatory_commitment` is false, and its `safety_function_id` and `requires_rad_work` are copied from the discovering work order. Its `priority_score` and `priority` are computed by exactly the same scoring rule an operator-created work order uses, described in §3.2. The item's `created_work_order_id` is set to the new work order — an identity only, never itself a decision input. **Step 3** mints a schedule activity for that new work order: its `label` is the literal prefix `EMERGENT: ` followed by the item's `description`, its `planned_duration_hours` is the item's `estimated_hours`, its `predecessor_activity_id` is the activity the terminal-activity selector returns over the *discovering* work order, and its `planned_start_offset_hours` is that predecessor's `planned_start_offset_hours` plus the new activity's own `planned_duration_hours`. When the selector returns no candidate, the new activity's `predecessor_activity_id` is null and its `planned_start_offset_hours` is 0. Its `oos_safety_function_id` and `oos_train_label` are both null, its `execution_status` is `NOT_STARTED`, and its `constraint_status` is evaluated on exactly the predicate §3.4 applies at creation — `CLEAR` when the selector returned no predecessor or when the predecessor it returned is already `COMPLETE`, `BLOCKED` otherwise.

**Step 4** reprojects, on the strength of the new activity and the new work order, the outage's `projected_duration_hours`, `risk_adjusted_duration_hours`, `critical_path_activity_id`, `approved_emergent_hours`, `approved_scope_cost_usd`, `scope_contingency_usd` and `total_scope_cost_usd`. **Step 5** recomputes `schedule_posture` from the `projected_duration_hours` that step 4 produced. **Step 6** is the posture auto-deferral: if `schedule_posture` was `ON_TRACK` before this request and is `AT_RISK` or `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` after step 5, then every work order of the outage whose `priority` is `DISCRETIONARY` and whose status is `SCOPED` or `PLANNED` moves to `DEFERRED` with `deferral_reason` set to the literal `SCHEDULE_POSTURE_AUTO_DEFERRAL`, processed in ascending work-order `created_at` order. **Step 7** cascades from each work order deferred in step 6: its `NOT_STARTED` activities move to `CANCELLED`, and its permits in `DRAFT`, `HELD_FOR_ALARA` or `APPROVED` move to `VOIDED`, with each formerly `APPROVED` permit appending a `RELEASE` ledger row whose `reason` is `WORK_ORDER_DEFERRED`. **Step 8** reprojects a final time: `projected_duration_hours`, `risk_adjusted_duration_hours`, `critical_path_activity_id`, `approved_scope_cost_usd`, `scope_contingency_usd`, `total_scope_cost_usd`, `dose_committed_mrem`, `dose_budget_remaining_mrem` and `alara_posture`. Crucially, `schedule_posture` is **not** re-evaluated after step 7 — the value fixed in step 5 is the value this request returns, even though step 7 has meanwhile removed scope that would have shortened the projection. The outage's `version` increments by 1.

A `REJECTED` disposition is deliberately inert. It stores the `disposition_note` and moves the item to `REJECTED`; `created_work_order_id` stays null, and no work order, no schedule activity and no outage field changes anywhere. There is no roll-up, no reprojection and no posture move on this branch.

An `ABSORBED` disposition takes a third path: the extra hours are absorbed into existing schedule rather than becoming new scope. It requires the terminal-activity selector over the discovering work order to return a candidate, otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_ACTIVITY_STATE_INVALID`. On success the item's `estimated_hours` are added to that selected activity's `planned_duration_hours`, and that activity's own `version` increments by 1 with its `updated_at` refreshed; no work order and no schedule activity are created, and `created_work_order_id` stays null. The outage's `projected_duration_hours`, `risk_adjusted_duration_hours`, `critical_path_activity_id` and `schedule_posture` are then recomputed and the outage's `version` increments by 1. What the absorbed branch pointedly does *not* do is increase `approved_emergent_hours`.

That last exclusion is the reason the outage carries two performance numbers. `approved_emergent_hours` is the sum of `estimated_hours` over the emergent work items whose `discovered_from_work_order_id` points at a work order of this outage and whose status is `APPROVED`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Items in `REPORTED`, `REJECTED` or `ABSORBED` are excluded, so an absorbed item lengthens real work in the schedule without ever earning the crew relief in the adjusted performance index of §3.9.

### 3.9. Startup Testing, Reactivity Management and the Post-Outage Review

The startup sequence is declared during the outage and executed after startup authorization. Either a `ReactorEngineer` or a `RegulatoryAffairs` user may add a test beneath the outage, supplying a `test_code` procedure code of 1 to 32 characters, a `sequence_index` of at least 1 that fixes its position in the sequence, a `hold_point_type` of `REGULATORY`, `STATION` or `NONE`, and a `predicted_reactivity_pcm` figure for the test point, which is unbounded and may be positive, negative or zero. The `sequence_index` must be unused among the outage's existing tests, otherwise the creation is rejected with `ERR_STARTUP_SEQUENCE_DUPLICATE`. Declaration carries no outage-status gate of any kind — a test may be added beneath an outage in any status, including one already past startup authorization — and it leaves the outage row untouched, its `version` standing still. A new test lands in `status` `PENDING` with `measured_reactivity_pcm`, `reactivity_deviation_pcm`, `nrc_release_reference` and `satisfied_by_user_id` all null, and the creating `X-User-ID` stored as `created_by_user_id`. Tests can be read individually, and the outage's tests can be listed with a `status` filter plus `limit` and `offset`; that listing is ordered by ascending `sequence_index` rather than by `created_at`, and it is the reverse-reachability channel for a test's outage.

Satisfying a test is `RegulatoryAffairs`'s exclusive act. The payload carries `measured_reactivity_pcm` and, optionally, `nrc_release_reference`. Five guards apply, and they are evaluated in exactly the order §2.8 fixes, which is the order stated here. The test must be in `PENDING` or `FAILED`, otherwise `ERR_STARTUP_TEST_STATE_INVALID` — a test already `SATISFIED` cannot be re-measured. The owning outage must be in `STARTUP`, otherwise `ERR_OUTAGE_STATE_INVALID`. When the test's `hold_point_type` is `REGULATORY` the payload must supply a non-empty `nrc_release_reference` of 1 to 64 characters, otherwise `ERR_REGULATORY_HOLD_POINT_NOT_RELEASED`; this check sits at rank 12 and so precedes the sequencing check, meaning a regulatory test submitted without its release reference reports the missing reference even when a lower-indexed test of the same outage is also still outstanding. Every test of the same outage with a strictly lower `sequence_index` must already be `SATISFIED`, otherwise `ERR_STARTUP_SEQUENCE_OUT_OF_ORDER` at rank 17; the sequence is walked in order and cannot be skipped. And when the `hold_point_type` is `REGULATORY` the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the test's `created_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at rank 20 — last of the five, so a self-release attempt that is also out of sequence reports the sequencing failure. For `STATION` and `NONE` hold points neither the release reference nor the two-person rule applies.

The measurement itself decides the outcome. `reactivity_deviation_pcm` is the absolute value of `measured_reactivity_pcm` less `predicted_reactivity_pcm`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The deviation is unsigned by design: a core 150 pcm more reactive than predicted and a core 150 pcm less reactive than predicted are equally unacceptable. When the deviation is less than or equal to 100 pcm the test moves to `SATISFIED`; the comparison is inclusive, so a deviation of exactly 100.00 pcm satisfies while 100.01 pcm does not. Satisfaction stores `measured_reactivity_pcm`, `reactivity_deviation_pcm`, `nrc_release_reference` and the acting user as `satisfied_by_user_id`, and then bubbles upward: once every test of the outage is `SATISFIED`, the outage moves `STARTUP → ONLINE` and its `version` increments by 1.

When the deviation exceeds 100 pcm the test lands in `FAILED` — and this is emphatically **not** an error response. The request succeeds with HTTP 200, the measurement is recorded in `measured_reactivity_pcm` — which, exactly like the prediction, is unbounded and may be positive, negative or zero — and in `reactivity_deviation_pcm`, `satisfied_by_user_id` stays null, any `nrc_release_reference` the payload supplied is pointedly *not* recorded on this branch and the field keeps whatever value it already held, and the outage's `schedule_posture` is forced to `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` regardless of what `projected_duration_hours` says, remaining there until the next schedule reprojection runs; the outage's `version` increments by 1. A failed test may be retested: the same operation, applied to a test in `FAILED`, is subject to the same outage-status and lower-index sequencing guards, and overwrites `measured_reactivity_pcm` and `reactivity_deviation_pcm` with the retest values. A retest within tolerance moves the test `FAILED → SATISFIED` and bubbles the outage to `ONLINE` if it was the last outstanding test; a retest still out of tolerance leaves the test in `FAILED`, overwrites the two measurement fields, and leaves `schedule_posture` at `RECOVERY_REQUIRED`. `SATISFIED` is the test's terminal state.

The post-outage review closes the outage, moving it `ONLINE → REVIEWED`, and it is the `OutageDirector`'s signature. The payload supplies `actual_duration_hours`, the as-worked breaker-to-breaker duration, which must be greater than zero; `actual_cost_usd`, the as-spent outage cost, which must be at least zero; and an optional `review_notes` commentary of up to 2000 characters. All three are stored on the outage, and six performance figures are then computed.

Three of them are simple gross variances, each expressed as a percentage and each rounded half up to two decimal places. `duration_variance_pct` is `actual_duration_hours` less `duration_target_hours`, divided by `duration_target_hours`, multiplied by 100. `dose_variance_pct` is `dose_committed_mrem` — as the dose ledger last left it — less `dose_budget_total_mrem`, divided by `dose_budget_total_mrem`, multiplied by 100. `cost_variance_pct` is `actual_cost_usd` less `cost_budget_usd`, divided by `cost_budget_usd`, multiplied by 100. The fourth figure is the emergent-adjusted duration variance: subtract the outage's `approved_emergent_hours` from `actual_duration_hours` to obtain the adjusted hours, then compute `adjusted_duration_variance_pct` as the adjusted hours less `duration_target_hours`, divided by `duration_target_hours`, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places.

The two composite indices share one formula and differ only in which duration variance they consume. The penalty is one-sided: only overruns cost points, and an underrun contributes zero rather than earning credit. Take the greater of zero and the duration variance percentage in play and multiply it by a weight of 0.5; take the greater of zero and `dose_variance_pct` and multiply it by a weight of 0.3; take the greater of zero and `cost_variance_pct` and multiply it by a weight of 0.2. The rounding order is pinned: each variance percentage is rounded to two decimal places first, each rounded percentage is then multiplied by its weight, the three products are summed without intermediate rounding, and the single subtraction of that sum from 100 is rounded half up to two decimal places once at the end. `outage_performance_index` is that index computed with `duration_variance_pct` — the headline number reported to the fleet, in which every hour spent on approved emergent work counts against the crew. `adjusted_performance_index` is the same index computed with `adjusted_duration_variance_pct` — the number used for crew performance review, in which hours the crew could not have foreseen are excluded. Both indices are floored at zero. For identical stored data the two figures diverge whenever `approved_emergent_hours` is greater than zero, and the sign of the duration term can flip from overrun to underrun, zeroing the duration penalty entirely. The outage's `version` increments by 1, and `REVIEWED` is terminal.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Creating a new resource returns HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`; every other successful operation, including every state transition and every read, returns HTTP 200 with the affected resource — or the requested collection — in `data`.
