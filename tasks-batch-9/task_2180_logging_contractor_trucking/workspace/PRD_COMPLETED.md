# Logging Contractor Trucking API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A logging contractor lives or dies on the gap between what the woods boss plans and what the mill scale actually says. Timber is cruised by the acre, sorted by species and grade at the landing, hauled over posted roads that close when the frost comes out, weighed on somebody else's scale, and paid for by the ton after a defect deduction that nobody in the woods gets to argue with. Between the stump and the settlement check sit four independent organisations — the contractor who owns the tract, the trucking carrier who owns the wheels, the mill that owns the quota, and the auditor who owns the right to shut all of them down — and every one of them holds a piece of the record that the others need.

This document specifies the REST service that reconciles those four records. The system tracks harvest tracts from the moment a unit is opened through mobilization, production, weather suspension and close-out; it prices crew and equipment move-ins by lowboy trip and tiered mileage; it holds mill quota by species and grade and refuses to over-commit it; it dispatches individual truck loads against that quota subject to legal weight, permit class, seasonal access and carrier compliance; it captures the mill's scale ticket and turns the certified tonnage into a payable haul figure; and it rolls those figures into a periodic settlement statement that a second accountant must approve before anyone is paid.

Two design commitments shape everything downstream. First, **money is never estimated twice the same way**: the provisional haul pay written at dispatch off the woods estimate and the final haul pay written at certification off the scaled tonnage are deliberately different numbers, both retained, and only the final one is ever settled. Second, **time is a server-controlled variable**: frost-law postings, insurance expiry, seasonal access windows and settlement periods all read a logical clock that an administrator can set, so that a seasonal system can be exercised deterministically rather than waiting for December.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below spelled exactly, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `OperationsManager`, `HaulContractor`, `MillScaler`, `SafetyOfficer` and `SettlementAccountant`.

A `PlatformAdmin` is the back-office operator who seeds reference data — mills, quota contracts, carriers, trucks, crews, haul routes — and who drives the logical clock. An `OperationsManager` is the logging contractor's woods boss: they open tracts, compile and approve production plans, allocate crews and equipment, run the tract through its lifecycle, declare and lift shutdowns, and dispatch loads. A `HaulContractor` is the trucking company acting as itself. A `MillScaler` is a scale-house operator acting on behalf of exactly one mill. A `SafetyOfficer` is an independent compliance auditor who records safety findings, revokes insurance certificates, and suspends or reinstates carriers, but who never dispatches, scales or settles. A `SettlementAccountant` compiles, submits, approves, disputes, revises and pays settlement statements.

Identity binding differs by role, and it is what makes ownership checks meaningful:

- When `X-User-Role` is `HaulContractor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `HaulContractor`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `HaulContractor` compare the resource's `haul_contractor_id` to `X-User-ID`. A carrier that acts on a load, truck, insurance certificate or settlement statement belonging to a different carrier is refused with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_OWNER` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `MillScaler`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Mill`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Mill` compare the resource's `mill_id` to `X-User-ID`. A scaler acting on a ticket, load or mill whose mill identity differs from its own is refused with `ERR_SCALER_MILL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `OperationsManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `SafetyOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `SettlementAccountant`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it as `compiled_by_user_id` on a statement the accountant compiles and as `approved_by_user_id` on a statement the accountant approves, but it does not compare it to any entity's `id`.

Segregation of duties is enforced on one operation only: the accountant approving a settlement statement must present an `X-User-ID` different from the `compiled_by_user_id` recorded when that statement was compiled. Self-approval is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty upper-case alphanumeric characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the host's real wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.4; it is the one timestamp in the system that does not read the logical clock, and it is therefore unaffected by any clock-setting call.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A value outside those bounds, or a `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer at all, is a schema failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a subsection below says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock is frozen unless an administrator moves it, many records legitimately share a single `created_at`; such ties are broken by insertion order — the sequence in which the server created the records — and never by `id`. The `meta` object on a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filter before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when records remain beyond the current window.

Filter parameters are additive: supplying several narrows the result set to records matching all of them. The specific filters each collection accepts are named in the workflow narratives of §3 and summarised in the endpoint table of §4.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID string with no type prefix — and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with exactly three fractional-second digits. Clients never supply either; a client-supplied `id` is ignored.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. One entity is the exception: a safety finding is an immutable audit record, so a `SafetyFinding` carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and neither field appears in its representation. Every other entity in the system — `Mill`, `MillContract`, `HaulContractor`, `InsuranceCertificate`, `Truck`, `Tract`, `ProductionPlan`, `Crew`, `CrewAssignment`, `HaulRoute`, `ShutdownOrder`, `Load`, `DeliveryTicket` and `SettlementStatement` — carries the full set of four common properties.

Time in this system is not wall-clock time. The server maintains a **logical clock** whose value at startup is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and every date-sensitive rule in this document — insurance effectivity and expiry, frost-law postings, seasonal access months, mill-contract periods, settlement periods, the 180-day safety lookback, and every timestamp the server stamps onto a resource — reads that logical clock rather than the host's real time. A `PlatformAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability — a required field whose value must parse as an ISO 8601 instant, failing which the call is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`; the response's `data` carries a single `current_utc` field reporting the resulting instant. Moving the clock is not a passive act: it immediately re-derives the `status` of every `InsuranceCertificate` and recomputes the `compliance_score` of every carrier under the rolling-180-day window, so a jump across an expiry date can suspend nobody but can certainly drop scores below the dispatch threshold. Both of those quantities are derived on read rather than stored, so moving the clock writes to no resource: no `updated_at` is refreshed and no `version` is incremented by the clock call itself.

Where a rule speaks of "the clock date" it means the calendar date, in UTC, of the current logical-clock instant. Date-valued fields are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings; instant-valued fields are full ISO 8601 UTC timestamps.

### 2.5. Money, Tonnage and Rounding Conventions

All money in this system is carried in **whole cents** as integers. There are no fractional cents anywhere: every intermediate money figure named in §3 is an integer before any sum is taken over it, and rounding is always **half-up** — a value exactly halfway between two integers rounds away from zero.

Tonnage is carried as a decimal number with **two decimal places**, likewise rounded half-up. Weights are whole pounds. Percentages supplied by clients (`defect_deduction_pct`, `overrun_tolerance_pct`) are fractions between 0 and 1, not percentage points. Every numeric input named in this document is supplied as a JSON number; a numeric value presented as a string, as a boolean, or as a non-finite value is a schema failure refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

The placement of a rounding step is part of the specification, not an implementation detail. Two rules recur and both are load-bearing. First, a rounding step that this document places on a single load's figure happens on that load, before any aggregate sums it — summing unrounded quantities and rounding the aggregate is a different and incorrect answer. Second, where a conversion and a deduction both apply, the order given in the narrative governs; converting first and deducting afterwards produces a different result and is forbidden.

### 2.6. Version Pinning on Tract and Carrier Writes

Two write operations in this system can silently destroy a decision the caller never saw, so both require the caller to pin the version of the parent resource it believes it is writing against.

Compiling a production plan for a tract requires a non-optional `expected_tract_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `Tract`. If it does not equal the tract's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is concrete: creating a plan supersedes any prior plan on the tract, so a writer working from a stale view of the tract would overwrite a supersede decision it never observed.

Compiling a settlement statement requires a non-optional `expected_haul_contractor_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `HaulContractor` being paid. If it does not equal the carrier's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_HAUL_CONTRACTOR_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new statement voids a prior draft statement covering an overlapping period, and every compliance event on the carrier also advances the carrier's `version`, so a stale view can void a statement whose contents the writer never inspected.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Endpoints not named in this subsection take no `expected_*_version` input, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.7. The Admission Ladder

Requests are admitted through a strictly ordered ladder of checks. The first check that fails determines the response; later checks are never reached, and their failure conditions are therefore invisible on that request. When two conditions on a single request would both fail, the earlier rung wins. The ladder, in ascending order, is:

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present, `X-User-Role` one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorisation.** The role appears in the called endpoint's authorised list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Request schema.** On every write endpoint: every required input field present, every enum-typed value a legal member of its enumeration, every number inside its declared range, `window_end` strictly after `window_start`, `scheduled_arrive_at` strictly after `scheduled_depart_at`, `period_end` on or after `period_start`, and any attempt to set a derived-only status value (specifically a `MillContract` `status` of `FILLED`) refused. Fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than refused, and an omitted optional field takes its declared default. The free-text justification fields carry declared bounds of their own: the `reason` on a certificate revocation, a carrier suspension and a load cancellation is required and runs 1 to 500 characters; the `reason` on a mill-contract patch, the `note` on a safety finding and the `note` on a carrier reinstatement are all optional and run to 500 characters; a statement's `dispute_reason` is required and runs 1 to 1,000 characters, a revision `note` is optional and runs to 1,000, and a `payment_reference` is required and runs 1 to 200. Failure is `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).
4. **Existence.** Path resources resolve left to right along the URL, and only then do body foreign keys resolve, in the order they appear in the endpoint's declared input list. The 404 raised names the resource type that failed to resolve: `ERR_MILL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MILL_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HAUL_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSURANCE_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRUCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCTION_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREW_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HAUL_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHUTDOWN_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOAD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DELIVERY_TICKET_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`.
5. **Resource ownership.** The three ownership rules of §2.1 — scaler-to-mill (`ERR_SCALER_MILL_MISMATCH`), carrier-to-carrier (`ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_OWNER`) and accountant segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`) — share this rung and are evaluated after existence but before any state check.
6. **Version pinning.** The two optimistic-concurrency checks of §2.6, yielding `ERR_STALE_TRACT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_HAUL_CONTRACTOR_VERSION`.
7. **Duplicate shutdown order.** On a shutdown declaration only, the check that the tract carries no shutdown order still in `ACTIVE` status, yielding `ERR_ACTIVE_SHUTDOWN_ORDER` (HTTP 409). This rung sits deliberately **above** the tract-state rung that follows it, so a second shutdown declared on an already-suspended tract reports the existing order rather than the tract's `SUSPENDED` status.
8. **Own-resource state.** The status of the resource named in the path admits the requested transition: `ERR_INVALID_TRACT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_LOAD_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_TICKET_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_SHUTDOWN_ORDER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE` and `ERR_INVALID_MILL_CONTRACT_STATE` all live on this rung.
9. **Carrier compliance status.** On load dispatch, the owning carrier's `compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT` or `WARNED`, yielding `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_COMPLIANT` (HTTP 422) otherwise. This rung sits deliberately **above** the truck-status rung, so a suspended carrier is reported as a compliance failure rather than as an equipment failure, even though the suspension is also what put its trucks out of service.
10. **Approved production plan.** On mobilization, the tract has at least one production plan in `APPROVED` status, yielding `ERR_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409). This is checked before the crew-eligibility rung below it.
11. **Cross-entity state.** The related resources are in states that admit the operation: `ERR_TRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_INVALID_MILL_CONTRACT_STATE` for a dispatch against a contract that is not `OPEN`, `ERR_MILL_NOT_RECEIVING`, `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_TRUCK_OUT_OF_SERVICE`, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CREW` and `ERR_LOADS_STILL_OPEN`.
12. **Safety and compliance thresholds.** `ERR_COMPLIANCE_SCORE_TOO_LOW`, `ERR_INSURANCE_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_WET_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET` and `ERR_WET_THRESHOLD_STILL_MET`.
13. **Temporal windows.** `ERR_SEASONAL_ACCESS_CLOSED`, `ERR_CREW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_TRUCK_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_CONTRACT_PERIOD_CLOSED` and `ERR_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.
14. **Physical and referential constraints.** `ERR_PERMIT_CLASS_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_ROUTE_TRACT_MILL_MISMATCH`, `ERR_SPECIES_GRADE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_OVERWEIGHT_LOAD` and `ERR_ZERO_NET_TONS`.
15. **Fiscal limits.** `ERR_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_OVERRUN_TOLERANCE_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_NO_SETTLEABLE_LOADS`.

Within rungs 11, 13 and 14 the ordering above also holds where a single endpoint is subject to more than one of the listed rules — for example a load dispatch checks tract activity, then mill-contract openness, then mill receiving status, then route openness, then truck serviceability.

### 2.8. Nested Paths and Referential Coherence

Several collections are addressed beneath a parent — a tract's production plans, a tract's crew assignments, a tract's shutdown orders, a carrier's insurance certificates, a carrier's safety findings, a carrier's trucks, a load's delivery tickets. Path parameters resolve strictly left to right: the parent must exist before the child is looked up, and a missing parent produces the parent's own 404 code rather than the child's. A child that exists but hangs off a different parent than the one named in the path is treated as not found under that path.

Foreign keys carried in a request body — `mill_id`, `tract_id`, `crew_id`, `mill_contract_id`, `haul_route_id`, `truck_id`, `haul_contractor_id`, `load_id` — resolve after all path segments have resolved, in the order the endpoint declares them.

Referential coherence goes beyond existence. A haul route is a directed pairing of one tract with one mill, and a load may only travel a route whose `tract_id` equals the load's own `tract_id` and whose `mill_id` equals the `mill_id` of the mill contract the load draws against; a route that connects some other pair is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_TRACT_MILL_MISMATCH`.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this system mutate more than one resource. When they do, the full cascade is part of the operation's contract: it either happens completely or, if the operation is refused, not at all. Each resource touched by a cascade has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, exactly as if it had been mutated directly — a tract suspended by a shutdown order, a crew stood down by that suspension, and a mill contract that releases reserved tons in the same cascade all advance their versions. A cascade releases loads one at a time and recomputes each load's contract as it goes, so a single contract that releases two loads inside one cascade advances its `version` twice, once per load.

Derived statuses are a distinct mechanism from cascaded statuses. A `Truck` `status`, an `InsuranceCertificate` `status` and a `MillContract` `status` of `FILLED` are all *derived*: they are recomputed from current facts whenever those facts change, and no client may set them directly. Cascaded statuses — a `Tract` moving to `SUSPENDED`, a `CrewAssignment` moving to `STOOD_DOWN` — are *written* by the cascade and persist until another transition rewrites them.

### 2.10. Enumerations of the Woods and the Yard

Four enumerations recur throughout this specification and their exact spellings are contractual.

Log **species** is one of `DOUGLAS_FIR`, `WESTERN_HEMLOCK`, `RED_ALDER` or `PONDEROSA_PINE`. Log **grade** is one of `PEELER`, `SAWLOG_1`, `SAWLOG_2` or `PULP`. Both appear twice on every load: once as sorted in the woods and once as determined at the mill scale.

Truck **permit class** is one of `STANDARD`, `DIVISIBLE_LOAD_PERMIT` or `LONGER_COMBINATION`, and the three are strictly ranked in that order — `STANDARD` ranks 1, `DIVISIBLE_LOAD_PERMIT` ranks 2 and `LONGER_COMBINATION` ranks 3. Wherever this document compares a truck's permit class to a route's requirement, it compares those ranks, and a higher-ranked permit satisfies a lower-ranked requirement.

Tract **access class** is one of `ALL_SEASON`, `SUMMER_ONLY` or `FROZEN_ONLY`. A tract whose access class is `ALL_SEASON` may be worked in any calendar month. A `SUMMER_ONLY` tract may be worked only when the logical clock's calendar month is May, June, July, August or September. A `FROZEN_ONLY` tract may be worked only when the logical clock's calendar month is December, January or February. A request that violates this gate is refused with `ERR_SEASONAL_ACCESS_CLOSED` (HTTP 422), and the gate is applied both when a tract is activated and when a load is dispatched from it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Network: Mills, Quotas, Carriers, Fleets, Crews and Roads

Before a single stick of wood moves, a `PlatformAdmin` seeds the reference network. Registering a receiving mill captures its trade `name` (1 to 120 characters, required) and the `state_code` it sits in, which must be exactly two upper-case letters. A mill also carries a `receiving_status` of either `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, defaulting to `OPEN` when the registration omits it; the yard may be opened or closed later by either a `PlatformAdmin` or the mill's own `MillScaler` through a patch whose body carries a required `receiving_status` and nothing else, and closing the yard blocks new dispatch against every quota contract that mill owns.

A **mill contract** is the quota a mill commits to buy. It names the owning mill through a payload carrying the `mill_id`, and it is specific to exactly one `species` and one `grade` — a mill buying peeler Douglas-fir and pulp hemlock holds two separate contracts. Its `quota_tons` is the total tonnage committed for the period and must be greater than zero with at most two decimal places; `base_rate_cents_per_ton` is the mill's pay rate in whole cents per delivered ton and must be greater than zero. The contract is live only between `period_start` and `period_end`, two `YYYY-MM-DD` dates that form a closed interval — a load dispatched on a clock date before `period_start` or after `period_end` is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_PERIOD_CLOSED`. An `overrun_tolerance_pct` between 0 and 0.25 inclusive, defaulting to 0.05 when omitted, expresses how far certified deliveries may run past the committed quota before the scale house must stop certifying.

Three tonnage figures are maintained on every contract and none of them is client-supplied. `reserved_tons` is the sum of `estimated_net_tons` over every load on this contract whose `dispatch_status` is `DISPATCHED`, `IN_TRANSIT` or `AT_MILL`. `delivered_tons` is the sum of `net_tons` over every certified delivery ticket whose load draws on this contract and whose load has reached `DELIVERED`. `available_tons` is the committed quota less the reserved tonnage less the delivered tonnage, rounded half-up to two decimal places; it may legitimately go negative inside the overrun tolerance. Cancelled and rejected loads contribute to neither sum, and there is no date filter on either sum — the status filters just given are the complete inclusion rule. All three initialise to a state where nothing is reserved, nothing is delivered, and the whole quota is available. Any event that moves a load into or out of a reserving state recomputes all three.

A contract's `status` is one of `OPEN`, `FILLED`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED`. `FILLED` is derived, never requested: the moment `available_tons` is recomputed to zero or below, an `OPEN` contract becomes `FILLED` and further dispatch against it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MILL_CONTRACT_STATE`; the moment a cancellation, a rejection or a shutdown cascade recomputes `available_tons` back above zero, a `FILLED` contract returns to `OPEN` and the freed tons become dispatchable again. A `PlatformAdmin` may move a contract that is `OPEN` or `FILLED` to `SUSPENDED`, supplying an optional `reason`, which blocks dispatch while leaving existing loads untouched; moving a `SUSPENDED` contract back to `OPEN` recomputes `available_tons` on resume and, if the recomputed figure is zero or below, immediately auto-transitions the contract to `FILLED`. A contract that is `OPEN`, `FILLED` or `SUSPENDED` may be moved to `CLOSED`, which removes it permanently from the rehaul selector's candidate set. Requesting a status change the contract's current status does not admit is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MILL_CONTRACT_STATE`; requesting `FILLED` at all is caught earlier, at the schema rung, and is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` because `FILLED` is derived and never settable by a client. Contracts may be listed with filters on `mill_id`, `species`, `grade` and `status`.

Carriers are registered with a legal `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `dot_number` of 1 to 32 characters that must be unique across all carriers — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` — and a two-letter `base_state_code`. A newly registered carrier starts with `compliance_status` of `COMPLIANT` and a `compliance_score` of 25, because at the moment of registration all three required insurance coverages are missing and each missing coverage costs 25 points against a base of 100. A carrier also accumulates `lifetime_settled_cents`, the running sum of `net_pay_cents` across every settlement statement of that carrier that has reached `PAID`.

Trucks belong to a carrier and may be added either by a `PlatformAdmin` or by the carrier acting as itself. A truck carries a `unit_number` of 1 to 32 characters, unique within its owning carrier and refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` when it collides with a unit already in that fleet; an `axle_config` of `FIVE_AXLE`, `SIX_AXLE` or `SEVEN_AXLE`; a `tare_weight_lbs` between 10,000 and 60,000 pounds inclusive, being the empty weight of tractor plus trailer; and a `permit_class`. Its `status` is entirely derived and never set: a truck reads `OUT_OF_SERVICE` whenever its owning carrier's `compliance_status` is `SUSPENDED`; otherwise it reads `DISPATCHED` whenever at least one load on that truck has a `dispatch_status` of `DISPATCHED`, `IN_TRANSIT` or `AT_MILL`; otherwise it reads `AVAILABLE`. A carrier's fleet may be listed with a filter on `status`.

Crews are the other half of the production capacity. A crew has a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `crew_type` of `HAND_FALLING`, `MECHANIZED_GROUND` or `CABLE_YARDING`, an `equipment_pieces` count between 1 and 24 inclusive counting every yarder, processor and skidder that must be lowboyed to a tract, and a `daily_rate_cents` day rate greater than zero. A crew's `status` walks `IDLE`, `MOBILIZING`, `ON_TRACT` and `STOOD_DOWN` under the control of its assignments, never by direct client action; a freshly registered crew is `IDLE`.

Haul routes are the roads. Each route is declared by a `PlatformAdmin` and pairs one `tract_id` with one `mill_id` — the two body keys resolve in exactly that order, so a request naming neither an existing tract nor an existing mill reports `ERR_TRACT_NOT_FOUND` — recording the one-way `distance_miles` as a whole number between 1 and 400 inclusive and the posted `legal_gross_weight_lbs` for that road outside frost season, between 40,000 and 120,000 pounds inclusive. A route also declares the minimum `permit_class_required` a truck must hold to run it. Two optional dates, `frost_law_start_on` and `frost_law_end_on`, delimit a seasonal frost-law posting as a closed interval; both are null on a road that is never posted. The posting is anchored on the start date alone: a road carrying a `frost_law_start_on` but no `frost_law_end_on` is posted from that date onward with no end, while a road carrying only a `frost_law_end_on` is never posted at all. Neither date is validated against the other. A route's `status` is `OPEN`, `POSTED_RESTRICTED` or `CLOSED`, defaulting to `OPEN`; a route sitting in `POSTED_RESTRICTED` behaves as though the frost-law posting were in force on every date regardless of the window. Routes may be listed with filters on `tract_id`, `mill_id` and `status`.

### 3.2. Carrier Compliance: Insurance, Findings, Suspension and Reinstatement

Three coverage types are required of every carrier: `GENERAL_LIABILITY`, `AUTO_LIABILITY` and `WORKERS_COMP`. A certificate is filed against a carrier — by a `PlatformAdmin`, or by the carrier acting as itself — naming its `coverage_type`, the closed date range it covers through `effective_on` and `expires_on`, and a per-occurrence `limit_cents` greater than zero. The two dates are not validated against one another; a certificate whose `expires_on` precedes its `effective_on` is accepted and simply derives a non-`ACTIVE` status. A certificate's `status` is derived, in this order of precedence: it is `REVOKED` if `revoked_at` is not null; otherwise `PENDING` if the clock date falls before `effective_on`; otherwise `EXPIRED` if the clock date falls after `expires_on`; otherwise `ACTIVE`. Certificates may be listed for a carrier with filters on `coverage_type` and `status`. Filing a certificate recomputes the carrier's `compliance_score` and increments the carrier's `version` by 1 — which matters, because that `version` is the one a settlement compile must pin.

A `SafetyOfficer` may revoke a filed certificate, supplying a `reason`. Revocation stamps `revoked_at` with the logical clock so the certificate derives `REVOKED`, and the call returns that certificate — not the carrier — in `data`. The carrier's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented by exactly 1 on every revocation, whether or not the cascade below fires. If that revocation leaves any of the three required coverage types with no `ACTIVE` certificate, the four-hop insurance cascade runs: the carrier's `compliance_status` becomes `SUSPENDED`; because the carrier is suspended, every truck it owns derives `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, each truck having its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1; because those trucks are out of service, every load on them whose `dispatch_status` is `DISPATCHED`, `IN_TRANSIT` or `AT_MILL` moves to `CANCELLED`; and because those loads were cancelled, each affected mill contract loses that load's `estimated_net_tons` from `reserved_tons`, recomputes `available_tons`, and returns from `FILLED` to `OPEN` if the recomputed figure rose above zero.

Safety findings are the auditor's other instrument. A `SafetyOfficer` records a finding against a carrier with a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`, a demerit `points` value between 0 and 100 inclusive, an `occurred_on` date that places the finding inside the scoring windows, and an optional free-text `note` of up to 500 characters. Findings may be listed for a carrier with a filter on `severity`. A `MINOR` finding costs points but changes no status. Recording a finding of any severity refreshes the carrier's `updated_at` and increments its `version` by exactly 1, even when the carrier's `compliance_status` is left where it was; the finding itself comes back with HTTP 201 carrying only `id`, `created_at` and its own fields, since it is the one entity with neither `updated_at` nor `version`. A `MAJOR` finding recorded against a carrier that is `COMPLIANT` moves it to `WARNED` — dispatch remains permitted while the score holds at 70 or above. A `CRITICAL` finding recorded against a carrier that is `COMPLIANT` or `WARNED` drives the same four-hop suspension cascade as an insurance revocation: `compliance_status` becomes `SUSPENDED` and the score is recomputed, the carrier's trucks derive `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, their non-terminal loads move to `CANCELLED`, and the affected mill contracts release reserved tons with a `FILLED` contract returning to `OPEN`. A `SafetyOfficer` may also suspend a carrier administratively with a supplied `reason`, driving that identical four-hop cascade. That endpoint applies no precondition on the carrier's current `compliance_status`: suspending a carrier that is already `SUSPENDED` is admitted and simply re-runs the cascade rather than being refused.

The **compliance score** is a weighted deduction from a base of 100 points. From that base the system subtracts the sum of `points` over every safety finding on the carrier whose `occurred_on` falls inside the scoring window, both endpoints inclusive; subtracts 25 points for each of the three required coverage types that has no certificate deriving `ACTIVE` at the clock date; and subtracts 3 points for each load of that carrier whose `dispatch_status` is `REJECTED` and whose `rejected_on` falls inside the scoring window, again with both endpoints inclusive. The result is then clamped: anything above 100 is held at 100 and anything below 0 is held at 0. Every input is an integer, so no rounding is required.

That formula is evaluated under two different windows, and the two windows are the only difference between them. The **rolling window** runs from 180 days before the clock date through the clock date, both endpoints inclusive, with missing coverage assessed at the clock date; this is the variant written to the carrier's `compliance_score` field and the variant consulted at the dispatch gate, where a score below 70 refuses the load with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_SCORE_TOO_LOW`. The **period-to-date window** runs from a settlement statement's `period_start` through its `period_end`, both endpoints inclusive, with missing coverage still assessed at the clock date; this is the variant frozen onto a statement as `compliance_score_at_compile` and the only variant the insurance-holdback tier reads. Because a settlement period and a rolling 180-day window cover different spans, the very same history of findings and rejections routinely scores differently under the two, and that divergence is intentional.

The score is recomputed whenever a finding is recorded, a certificate is filed or revoked, a carrier is suspended or reinstated, the logical clock is moved, or a load transitions into `REJECTED`.

Reinstatement closes the loop. A `SafetyOfficer` returns a carrier to `COMPLIANT` by reinstating it, supplying an optional `note`. Coverage is the only precondition: each of `GENERAL_LIABILITY`, `AUTO_LIABILITY` and `WORKERS_COMP` must have at least one certificate deriving `ACTIVE`, and attempting it while any required coverage is uncovered is refused with `ERR_INSURANCE_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE`. The carrier's own `compliance_status` carries no precondition — a carrier already `COMPLIANT` or sitting at `WARNED` may be reinstated, and simply lands on `COMPLIANT`. On success the score is recomputed and every truck the carrier owns derives `AVAILABLE` again, each truck having its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1. Loads cancelled by the suspension cascade are **not** resurrected — the wood is off the truck and the reservation is gone.

### 3.3. Opening a Tract: Production Plans, Crew Allocation and Move-In Pricing

An `OperationsManager` opens a harvest unit by supplying its `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `legal_description` of 1 to 200 characters carrying the township, range and section, the `gross_acres` inside the unit boundary as a number greater than zero, the `planned_volume_tons` the operator expects to remove as a number greater than zero, and the `access_class` that governs which months the ground will carry equipment. A new tract opens in `PLANNED` status with a `suspension_count` of 0, a `move_in_cost_cents` of 0 and a `produced_tons` of 0. The last two are maintained by the system: `move_in_cost_cents` is the sum of the move-in charge over every crew assignment ever made to the tract, regardless of what status those assignments have since reached, and `produced_tons` is the sum of `net_tons` over every certified delivery ticket whose load came off this tract and reached `DELIVERED`.

A **production plan** schedules the tract's output. The manager supplies a `target_tons_per_week` greater than zero and a `plan_horizon_weeks` between 1 and 52 inclusive, together with the `expected_tract_version` described in §2.6. The system multiplies the weekly target by the horizon and rounds the product half-up to two decimal places, storing the result as the plan's own `planned_volume_tons`. A plan is created in `DRAFT` status; creating one supersedes the tract's history, so every prior plan on that tract whose status is `DRAFT` or `APPROVED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and the tract's `version` is incremented by 1. Plans on a tract may be listed with a filter on `status`. A `DRAFT` plan is promoted to `APPROVED` by the manager, which is what unlocks mobilization of its tract; attempting to approve a plan that is no longer `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`.

Crews are allocated to a tract one assignment at a time. The manager names the `crew_id` being allocated, the one-way lowboy distance from the crew's last landing as `move_in_miles` — a whole number between 0 and 600 inclusive — and the crew's occupation window as `window_start` and `window_end`, both `YYYY-MM-DD` dates, where `window_end` is exclusive and must fall strictly after `window_start`. An assignment is created in `SCHEDULED` status. Neither allocating a crew nor compiling a production plan carries any precondition on the tract's own `status` — both are admitted against a tract in any status — and a `SCHEDULED` assignment leaves its crew `IDLE` and otherwise untouched: the crew's own `updated_at` and `version` move only when a later tract-level call drives the assignment. Crew assignments may be read individually and listed with filters on `crew_id`, `tract_id` and `status`.

A crew cannot be in two places at once, and a lowboy does not teleport. Occupation windows are therefore treated as half-open intervals that include their start date and exclude their end date, and every existing assignment is widened by a two-day move buffer appended to its tail before the comparison is made. Concretely, a proposed assignment conflicts with an existing one — considering only that crew's assignments whose status is anything other than `RELEASED` — when the proposed `window_start` falls before the existing `window_end` plus two days **and** the proposed `window_end` falls strictly after the existing `window_start`. A conflict is refused with `ERR_CREW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

**Pricing the move-in** happens exactly once, at the moment the assignment is created, and the figure is pinned forever after — no later event recomputes it. The calculation runs in a pinned order. First, the number of lowboy trips is the crew's `equipment_pieces` divided by two and rounded **up** to a whole number, since one lowboy carries two pieces; that count is stored on the assignment as `lowboy_trips`. Second, a tiered mileage charge is accumulated across the one-way distance: 900 cents for each of the first 25 miles, 725 cents for each mile beyond 25 up to and including 75, and 610 cents for each mile beyond 75. Third, that tiered mileage total is multiplied by the trip count to give the base charge. Fourth, the base charge is multiplied by an access multiplier taken from the tract's `access_class` — 1.00 for `ALL_SEASON`, 1.15 for `SUMMER_ONLY` and 1.35 for `FROZEN_ONLY` — and the product is rounded half-up to whole cents *before* anything else is added. Fifth, a flat crew-type surcharge is added to that rounded figure: nothing for `HAND_FALLING`, 120,000 cents for `MECHANIZED_GROUND`, and 275,000 cents for `CABLE_YARDING`. Sixth, the subtotal is raised to a floor of 185,000 cents if it falls below that, and only then lowered to a ceiling of 1,250,000 cents if it exceeds it — the floor is applied before the cap, so a subtotal below the floor and a cap below the floor can never both bind. The result is stored as the assignment's `move_in_cost_cents`.

Only three things feed that number: the crew named by `crew_id` (its `equipment_pieces` and `crew_type`), the tract's `access_class`, and the request's `move_in_miles`. No other crew assignment on the tract influences the result. Creating the assignment adds the computed charge to the tract's own `move_in_cost_cents` and increments the tract's `version` by 1.

### 3.4. Running the Tract: Mobilizing, Activating and Closing Out

The tract lifecycle is a five-state machine — `PLANNED`, `MOBILIZING`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `COMPLETED` — with `COMPLETED` terminal and a revision loop between `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`.

**Mobilizing** advances a tract from `PLANNED` to `MOBILIZING`. Three conditions must hold: the tract is genuinely in `PLANNED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TRACT_STATE`), at least one production plan on the tract has reached `APPROVED` (otherwise `ERR_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`), and at least one crew assignment on the tract is still `SCHEDULED` (otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CREW`). The plan check is evaluated before the crew check. On success the cascade runs two hops deep: every crew assignment on the tract in `SCHEDULED` moves to `MOVED_IN`, and because an assignment moved to `MOVED_IN`, its crew moves `IDLE → MOBILIZING`. The tract's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented by 1.

**Activating** advances the tract from `MOBILIZING` to `ACTIVE` and starts production. The tract must be in `MOBILIZING`; at least one crew assignment on it must have reached `MOVED_IN`, or the call is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CREW`; and the seasonal access gate of §2.10 must pass for the current logical-clock month, failing which the call is refused with `ERR_SEASONAL_ACCESS_CLOSED`. No shutdown order on a `MOBILIZING` tract can still be `ACTIVE` — orders are declarable only against an `ACTIVE` tract — so activation performs no explicit shutdown-order check of its own. On success, every crew assignment on the tract in `MOVED_IN` moves to `WORKING`, and because an assignment moved to `WORKING`, its crew moves `MOBILIZING → ON_TRACT`. Again `updated_at` is refreshed and `version` is incremented by 1.

**Closing out** advances the tract from `ACTIVE` to the terminal `COMPLETED`. The tract must be `ACTIVE`, and no load off that tract may still be in flight — if any load on the tract has a `dispatch_status` of `DISPATCHED`, `IN_TRANSIT` or `AT_MILL`, the close-out is refused with `ERR_LOADS_STILL_OPEN`. On success every crew assignment on the tract whose status is `MOVED_IN`, `WORKING` or `STOOD_DOWN` moves to the terminal `RELEASED`, and because each of those assignments was released, its crew returns to `IDLE`. Every production plan on the tract still in `APPROVED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`.

The crew-assignment machine therefore mirrors the tract's: `SCHEDULED → MOVED_IN` on mobilization, `MOVED_IN → WORKING` on activation, `WORKING → STOOD_DOWN` when the tract is suspended, `STOOD_DOWN → WORKING` when the suspension is lifted, and `WORKING → RELEASED`, `MOVED_IN → RELEASED` or `STOOD_DOWN → RELEASED` on close-out — the three release paths behave identically, each returning the crew to `IDLE`. Every one of those transitions is a consequence of a tract-level call, never an independently addressable operation.

### 3.5. Wet Weather, Frost Law and the Suspension Loop

Ground goes soft, roads get posted, fire danger closes the woods, and a fatality stops everything. A `ShutdownOrder` records any of those as a `reason` of `WET_WEATHER`, `FROST_LAW`, `FIRE_DANGER` or `SAFETY_STAND_DOWN`, together with a `soil_moisture_index` field-moisture reading between 0 and 100 inclusive taken when the order was declared. The order is stamped with `declared_at` from the logical clock and opens in `ACTIVE` status; `lifted_at` and `lift_soil_moisture_index` stay null while it is active. Orders on a tract may be listed with filters on `status` and `reason`.

Declaring an order requires the tract to be `ACTIVE` and requires that the tract carry no other order still `ACTIVE` — and as §2.7 sets out, the duplicate-order check runs *first*, so a second declaration against an already-suspended tract reports `ERR_ACTIVE_SHUTDOWN_ORDER` rather than `ERR_INVALID_TRACT_STATE`. A `WET_WEATHER` order carries one further precondition: the supplied `soil_moisture_index` must be **at least 72**, since below that reading the ground is not wet enough to justify a shutdown, and a declaration below the threshold is refused with `ERR_WET_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`. Orders declared for the other three reasons carry no moisture threshold.

Declaring an order drives a four-hop cascade. Hop one creates the order and moves the tract `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, incrementing the tract's `suspension_count` by 1. Hop two-a: because the tract became `SUSPENDED`, every crew assignment on it in `WORKING` moves to `STOOD_DOWN`, and hop three-a follows — because an assignment stood down, its crew moves `ON_TRACT → STOOD_DOWN`. Hop two-b runs in parallel over the wood: because the tract became `SUSPENDED`, every load off that tract whose `dispatch_status` is `DISPATCHED` or `IN_TRANSIT` moves to `CANCELLED`. Loads already `AT_MILL` are deliberately left alone, because that wood is already sitting on the mill scale and the mill will decide its fate. Hop three-b: because those loads were cancelled, each mill contract they drew against loses the cancelled load's `estimated_net_tons` from `reserved_tons` and recomputes `available_tons`. Hop four-b: because that recomputed `available_tons` rose above zero, a contract that had gone `FILLED` returns to `OPEN`.

**Lifting** the order reverses the ground half of the cascade. The manager supplies a `soil_moisture_index` reading taken at lift time. The order must still be `ACTIVE`, or the call is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SHUTDOWN_ORDER_STATE`. The tract's own status carries no precondition and is never checked: the lift itself always succeeds, and the ground half of the reversal below simply does not run unless the tract is in fact `SUSPENDED`. For a `WET_WEATHER` order, the supplied reading must be **strictly below 72**; a lift attempted while the reading is still 72 or above is refused with `ERR_WET_THRESHOLD_STILL_MET`. On success the order's `status` becomes `LIFTED`, `lifted_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `lift_soil_moisture_index` stores the supplied reading. Because the order was lifted and no other `ACTIVE` order remains on the tract, the tract returns `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`; because the tract returned to `ACTIVE`, every crew assignment on it in `STOOD_DOWN` returns to `WORKING`; and because those assignments returned to `WORKING`, their crews return `STOOD_DOWN → ON_TRACT`. Loads cancelled by the shutdown are not restored.

This is a genuine loop. A tract may run `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` as many times as the weather demands, and `suspension_count` records how many laps were run.

### 3.6. Dispatching a Load Against Mill Quota

A load is one truckload of sorted logs committed to one mill quota over one road. An `OperationsManager` dispatches it by naming the `tract_id` the wood came off, the `mill_contract_id` the quota is drawn against, the `haul_route_id` the truck will run, the `truck_id` hauling it, the `declared_species` and `declared_grade` as sorted in the woods, the `estimated_net_tons` the woods believes are payable — a number greater than zero with at most two decimal places — and the planned occupation interval as `scheduled_depart_at` and `scheduled_arrive_at`, the latter strictly after the former. Both instants are stored and echoed back on the load exactly as the client supplied them, without renormalisation to any canonical form.

Dispatch is the most heavily guarded operation in the system, and the order of its guards is specified in §2.7. Working down that ladder: the owning carrier must have a `compliance_status` of `COMPLIANT` or `WARNED`, failing which the dispatch is refused with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_COMPLIANT`. Then the cross-entity states are checked in this order — the tract must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_TRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`), the mill contract must be `OPEN` (`ERR_INVALID_MILL_CONTRACT_STATE`), the mill owning that contract must have `receiving_status` of `OPEN` (`ERR_MILL_NOT_RECEIVING`), the haul route must be `OPEN` or `POSTED_RESTRICTED` and never `CLOSED` (`ERR_ROUTE_NOT_OPEN`), and the truck must not derive `OUT_OF_SERVICE` (`ERR_TRUCK_OUT_OF_SERVICE`). Then the carrier's rolling-window `compliance_score` must be at least 70 (`ERR_COMPLIANCE_SCORE_TOO_LOW`). Then the temporal gates: the tract's seasonal access class must admit the current logical-clock month (`ERR_SEASONAL_ACCESS_CLOSED`), the truck must be free over the proposed interval (`ERR_TRUCK_WINDOW_CONFLICT`), and the clock date must fall inside the contract's closed period (`ERR_CONTRACT_PERIOD_CLOSED`). Then the physical and referential constraints: the truck's permit rank must be at least the route's required permit rank (`ERR_PERMIT_CLASS_INSUFFICIENT`), the route must connect this tract to this contract's mill (`ERR_ROUTE_TRACT_MILL_MISMATCH`), and the load's declared species and grade must equal the contract's `species` and `grade` exactly (`ERR_SPECIES_GRADE_MISMATCH`). Last comes the fiscal gate.

Truck occupation is treated as a **closed** interval with no buffer, unlike the half-open crew window with its two-day tail. A proposed load conflicts with an existing one on the same truck — considering only that truck's loads whose `dispatch_status` is `DISPATCHED`, `IN_TRANSIT` or `AT_MILL` — when the proposed `scheduled_depart_at` falls at or before the existing load's `scheduled_arrive_at` **and** the proposed `scheduled_arrive_at` falls at or after the existing load's `scheduled_depart_at`. Intervals that merely touch at an endpoint therefore conflict.

The fiscal gate is strict but admits exact equality: the requested `estimated_net_tons` must not be greater than the contract's `available_tons`. A request for more is refused with `ERR_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`; a request for exactly the available figure is admitted and drives `available_tons` to exactly 0.00, which immediately sends the contract `OPEN → FILLED`.

On admission, the load is created with `dispatch_status` of `DISPATCHED`, `settlement_status` of `UNSETTLED`, and `rehaul_status` of `NONE`. The truck's owning carrier is copied onto the load as `haul_contractor_id` and never changed thereafter, which is what makes ownership checks on departure and arrival, and eligibility for settlement, resolvable from the load alone. The load's `rehaul_of_load_id` stays null unless the system itself later creates the load as a rehaul.

Dispatch also books the money the operator expects to owe. A per-ton-mile rate is selected from the **whole** route distance — an average-rate selection, not a marginal waterfall: 26 cents per ton-mile when `distance_miles` is 30 or less, 22 cents when it is more than 30 but no more than 70, and 19 cents beyond 70. That rate is stored on the load as `rate_cents_per_ton_mile` and is reused unchanged by the final calculation at certification. The line-haul component is the tonnage multiplied by that rate multiplied by the route distance, rounded half-up to whole cents. A fuel surcharge of 7.5 percent of that line-haul figure is computed and rounded half-up to whole cents on this single load, before any aggregate ever sums it. Demurrage is the wait beyond a free window of 45 minutes, charged at 150 cents for each minute of excess, with no negative result. The three are added to a subtotal, and a volume incentive is applied to that subtotal: 4 percent when the subtotal is at least 120,000 cents, otherwise 2 percent when the subtotal is at least 60,000 cents, otherwise nothing; the incentive is rounded half-up to whole cents. A regrade penalty of 5 percent of the line-haul figure, rounded half-up to whole cents, is subtracted when the mill regraded the wood. The payable figure is the subtotal plus the incentive minus the regrade penalty. The incentive tier is keyed on the subtotal of line haul, fuel and demurrage — never on the payable figure — so this is a single pass, not a fixed-point solve.

At dispatch that machinery runs in its **provisional** form: the tonnage is the woods estimate `estimated_net_tons`, taken before any defect deduction; the wait is zero so demurrage is always zero; and no regrade penalty ever applies, because nothing has been scaled. The result is written to `provisional_haul_pay_cents` and is **never revised**, not even after certification. Of the load's money block only `rate_cents_per_ton_mile` and `provisional_haul_pay_cents` carry a value at dispatch; `wait_minutes`, `line_haul_cents`, `fuel_surcharge_cents`, `demurrage_cents`, `incentive_cents`, `regrade_adjustment_cents` and `final_haul_pay_cents` are all null on the load until certification writes them, as are `departed_at`, `arrived_at`, `rejected_on`, `settlement_event_on` and `rehaul_of_load_id`. Dispatch also adds the estimated tonnage to the contract's `reserved_tons`, recomputes `available_tons`, increments the contract's `version` by 1, and may auto-transition the contract to `FILLED` as described above.

Loads may be read individually — the representation carries both status axes and every money component — and listed with filters on `tract_id`, `mill_contract_id`, `haul_route_id`, `truck_id`, `haul_contractor_id`, `rehaul_of_load_id`, `dispatch_status` and `settlement_status`.

### 3.7. The Haul: Departure, Arrival, Cancellation and the Scale Ticket

A load carries **two independent status axes on one row**, and confusing them is the most expensive mistake an implementer can make here. The `dispatch_status` axis tracks the physical wood: `DISPATCHED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `AT_MILL`, and then one of the terminal values `DELIVERED`, `REJECTED` or `CANCELLED`. The `settlement_status` axis tracks the money: `UNSETTLED`, `ACCRUED`, `STATEMENTED` and the terminal `PAID`. They advance on different triggers and never gate one another. A load may legitimately sit at `DELIVERED` with `ACCRUED`, at `DELIVERED` with `STATEMENTED`, or at `DELIVERED` with `PAID`; one `REJECTED` load may be `ACCRUED` while another `REJECTED` load stays `UNSETTLED` forever because it was rehauled.

The carrier drives the physical axis for itself. Recording the loaded truck leaving the landing advances the load `DISPATCHED → IN_TRANSIT` and stamps `departed_at` with the logical clock; it requires that the caller's `X-User-ID` equal the load's `haul_contractor_id` and that the truck not derive `OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Recording arrival at the mill advances the load `IN_TRANSIT → AT_MILL` and stamps `arrived_at` with the logical clock; the demurrage clock starts running from that instant, and this call likewise requires the caller to be the load's own carrier. Attempting either while the load is in the wrong `dispatch_status` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LOAD_STATE`.

An `OperationsManager` may cancel a load that is still `DISPATCHED` or `IN_TRANSIT`, supplying a `reason`; a load that has already reached `AT_MILL` or any terminal state cannot be cancelled. Cancellation moves the load to the terminal `CANCELLED`, causes its mill contract to lose the load's `estimated_net_tons` from `reserved_tons` and recompute `available_tons`, and returns a `FILLED` contract to `OPEN` when the freed tons push the available figure above zero. A cancelled load's `settlement_status` stays `UNSETTLED` forever — cancelled wood never enters a settlement statement.

**The scale ticket** is written by the `MillScaler` of the receiving mill while the load stands `AT_MILL`. The scaler supplies the `gross_weight_lbs` read off the mill scale, a whole number between 0 and 200,000; a `defect_deduction_pct` between 0 and 0.50 inclusive expressing the cull deduction as a fraction of net weight; and the `scaled_species` and `scaled_grade` as determined at the mill, which may differ from what the woods declared. A load may carry only one live ticket: an attempt to write a second ticket for a load that already has one in `DRAFT` or `CERTIFIED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LOAD_STATE`, as is an attempt to write a ticket for a load that is not `AT_MILL`. The ticket opens in `DRAFT` status with `certified_at`, `certified_by_user_id` and `reject_reason` all null. Tickets may be listed with filters on `load_id`, `mill_id` and `ticket_status`.

Two fields on a ticket are copied by the server and never taken from the client. The ticket's `mill_id` is copied from the `mill_id` of the load's mill contract, which is also what the scaler's `X-User-ID` is compared against. The ticket's `tare_weight_lbs` is copied from the truck's own `tare_weight_lbs`; a client-supplied tare is ignored outright.

Weight is then checked against what the road and the rig can legally carry, and the resulting ceiling is stored on the ticket as `max_allowed_gross_lbs`. The frost-law posting is considered active when the route's `status` is `POSTED_RESTRICTED`, or when the route has a non-null `frost_law_start_on` and the clock date falls on or after `frost_law_start_on` and on or before `frost_law_end_on` — a closed interval on both endpoints. When frost law is active, the effective legal weight is the route's `legal_gross_weight_lbs` multiplied by 0.80 — a twenty-percent reduction — and rounded **down** to whole pounds, never rounded to nearest; when it is not active, the effective legal weight is the posted figure unchanged. A permit uplift of 10,000 pounds is granted only when the truck's `permit_class` is `LONGER_COMBINATION` **and** the route's `permit_class_required` is also `LONGER_COMBINATION`; in every other combination the uplift is zero. The ceiling is then the smaller of the effective legal weight plus that uplift, and the axle cap for the truck's configuration — 80,000 pounds for `FIVE_AXLE`, 91,000 for `SIX_AXLE` and 105,500 for `SEVEN_AXLE`. Rejection is strict but admits equality: a `gross_weight_lbs` strictly greater than the ceiling is refused with `ERR_OVERWEIGHT_LOAD`, while a gross weight exactly equal to the ceiling passes.

The payable tonnage is then computed in a pinned order that must not be rearranged. The net weight is the gross weight less the truck's tare weight, stored as `net_weight_lbs`. The defect deduction is that net weight multiplied by `defect_deduction_pct`, rounded half-up to whole **pounds**, stored as `defect_deducted_lbs`. The payable weight is the net weight less the rounded deduction, stored as `payable_lbs`. Only that integer pound figure is then divided by 2,000 pounds per ton and rounded half-up to two decimal places, giving `net_tons` — the scaled tonnage that every downstream money figure uses. Converting to tons first and deducting afterwards is a different answer and is forbidden. If the resulting `net_tons` is not strictly greater than 0.00, the ticket is refused with `ERR_ZERO_NET_TONS`.

### 3.8. Certification, Rejection and the Rehaul Selector

A ticket sitting in `DRAFT` has exactly two futures, and both are the scaler's to choose. In either case the ticket must still be `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TICKET_STATE` — its load must still be `AT_MILL`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_LOAD_STATE`, and the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the ticket's `mill_id`. Both calls return HTTP 200 carrying the updated ticket itself in `data`, never the load or the contract the cascade also moved.

**Certifying** the ticket carries one further precondition, the overrun gate: the contract's `delivered_tons` *after* adding this ticket's `net_tons` must not exceed the committed `quota_tons` multiplied by one plus the contract's `overrun_tolerance_pct`, that ceiling itself rounded half-up to two decimal places. Exceeding it is refused with `ERR_OVERRUN_TOLERANCE_EXCEEDED`.

Certification then runs a four-hop cascade. Hop one: the ticket's `ticket_status` becomes `CERTIFIED`, `certified_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and `certified_by_user_id` records the certifying scaler. Hop two: because the ticket was certified, the load advances `AT_MILL → DELIVERED`; its `wait_minutes`, `rate_cents_per_ton_mile`, `line_haul_cents`, `fuel_surcharge_cents`, `demurrage_cents`, `incentive_cents`, `regrade_adjustment_cents` and `final_haul_pay_cents` are all written from the **final** form of the haul-pay calculation; its `settlement_event_on` is set to the certification date; and on the independent money axis its `settlement_status` moves `UNSETTLED → ACCRUED`. Hop three: because the load became `DELIVERED`, its mill contract loses the load's `estimated_net_tons` from `reserved_tons`, gains the ticket's `net_tons` in `delivered_tons`, and recomputes `available_tons`, its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1; the tract's `produced_tons` is recomputed the same way, and the tract too has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1. Hop four: because the recomputed `available_tons` fell to zero or below, a contract still `OPEN` moves `OPEN → FILLED`.

The final form of the haul-pay calculation differs from the provisional form of §3.6 in exactly three inputs, and in nothing else. The tonnage is the ticket's `net_tons`, scaled *after* the defect deduction, rather than the woods estimate. The wait is real: `wait_minutes` is the elapsed time from the load's `arrived_at` to the ticket's `certified_at`, expressed in seconds, divided by 60 and rounded **down** to a whole number of minutes, with a negative elapsed time — the clock having been wound backwards between arrival and certification — clamped to 0; the free window of 45 minutes and the 150-cents-per-minute rate then apply as described. And the regrade penalty applies exactly when the ticket's `scaled_grade` differs from the load's `declared_grade`. The route distance and the rate band are the same ones pinned at dispatch. Identical wood over an identical road therefore yields a different number from the provisional figure whenever the scale disagrees with the woods estimate, whenever the truck waited past the free window, or whenever the mill regraded the load — and both numbers are retained, the provisional one untouched.

**Rejecting** the ticket takes a `reject_reason` of `SPECIES_MISMATCH`, `EXCESS_DEFECT`, `CONTAMINATION` or `OVERWEIGHT`, and drives a five-hop cascade. Hop one: the ticket's `ticket_status` becomes `REJECTED` and `reject_reason` stores the supplied value. Hop two: because the ticket was rejected, the load advances `AT_MILL → REJECTED` and its `rejected_on` is set to the logical clock's calendar date. Hop three: because the load became `REJECTED`, its mill contract loses the load's `estimated_net_tons` from `reserved_tons` and recomputes `available_tons`, and a `FILLED` contract whose available figure rose above zero returns to `OPEN`. Hop four: because the load became `REJECTED`, the owning carrier's `compliance_score` is recomputed — the rejection now counts against both scoring windows at three points apiece. That score is derived on read rather than stored, so this hop writes nothing to the carrier: its `compliance_status` is unchanged and its `version` is not incremented by the rejection. Hop five runs the rehaul selector.

The **rehaul selector** looks for somewhere else to take the wood, and it is a strict ranking over an explicit candidate set. A candidate is a pairing of a mill contract with a haul route where every one of the following holds: the contract's `status` is `OPEN`; the contract's `species` equals the ticket's `scaled_species` and its `grade` equals the ticket's `scaled_grade`; the clock date falls inside the contract's closed period from `period_start` through `period_end`; the contract's `available_tons` is at least the ticket's `net_tons`; the contract belongs to a mill other than the one that just rejected the load; that mill's `receiving_status` is `OPEN`; the route's `tract_id` is the rejected load's tract and its `mill_id` is the candidate contract's mill; the route's `status` is `OPEN` or `POSTED_RESTRICTED`; and the hauling truck's permit rank is at least the route's required permit rank.

Among candidates, the winner is the one maximising net yield in whole cents, where net yield is the contract's `base_rate_cents_per_ton` multiplied by the ticket's `net_tons` and rounded half-up to whole cents — the revenue term is the only term that rounds — less the route's `distance_miles` multiplied by 85 cents of cost per mile. Ties are broken in this order: the candidate with the smaller `distance_miles` wins, and if that is still tied, the candidate contract with the earlier `created_at` wins; should two candidate contracts share a `created_at` as well, the one the server created first wins. No ranking, tie-break or ordering rule anywhere in this system consults an `id`.

When a winner exists, the original load's `rehaul_status` becomes `REHAULED`, its `settlement_event_on` stays null, and no reject deduction ever accrues against it. The system then creates a fresh load itself: same tract, the winning contract as its `mill_contract_id`, the winning route as its `haul_route_id`, the same `truck_id`, a `rehaul_of_load_id` pointing at the load the mill rejected, `declared_species` and `declared_grade` taken from the ticket's scaled values, `estimated_net_tons` set to the ticket's `net_tons`, a `scheduled_depart_at` equal to the current logical clock, and a `scheduled_arrive_at` that many minutes later where the minute count is the route's `distance_miles` multiplied by 60 and divided by an assumed 45 miles per hour, rounded **up** to a whole minute. The rehaul load opens with `dispatch_status` of `DISPATCHED` and `settlement_status` of `UNSETTLED`, and its `provisional_haul_pay_cents` is written by the provisional form of the haul-pay calculation exactly as any other dispatch would be. Because the eligibility filter above already proved quota, route, permit and mill availability, this system-created load is exempt from the truck-occupation overlap check and from the carrier compliance-score gate — and, in fact, from every other dispatch guard of §3.6 as well, the tract-`ACTIVE`, mill-receiving, seasonal-access, contract-period, species/grade and quota checks included, since the selector's own filter is the complete admission test for a rehaul. It does still add its `estimated_net_tons` to the winning contract's `reserved_tons`, recompute `available_tons`, increment that contract's `version` by 1 and fill the contract if the figure falls to zero or below, exactly as a manual dispatch would.

When no candidate survives the filter, the original load's `rehaul_status` becomes `NO_ALTERNATE`, its `settlement_event_on` is set to its `rejected_on`, and its `settlement_status` moves `UNSETTLED → ACCRUED` so that a reject deduction can accrue against the carrier at settlement time.

### 3.9. Settlement: Compiling, Submitting, Approving, Disputing and Paying

A `SettlementAccountant` compiles a statement for one carrier over one closed date period, naming the `haul_contractor_id`, the `period_start` and `period_end` (the latter on or after the former), and the `expected_haul_contractor_version` of §2.6. Before compiling, the system checks for period collisions: if any other statement for the same carrier whose `status` is anything other than `DRAFT` or `VOID` covers a period intersecting the requested closed interval, the compile is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. An intersecting statement still in `DRAFT` is not a collision — it is instead **voided**: its lines are cleared, every money field resets to 0, and its `status` becomes the terminal `VOID`. Compiling also increments the carrier's `version` by 1, and records the compiling accountant's `X-User-ID` as `compiled_by_user_id`. Creation and roll-up are one call, so the statement comes back with HTTP 201 at `version` 1 with its `lines` array and every money field already populated; a period holding no eligible load compiles successfully rather than being refused, returning an empty `lines` array and 0 in `gross_pay_cents`, `reject_deduction_cents`, `holdback_cents`, `deduction_cents` and `net_pay_cents`, with `compliance_score_at_compile` and `holdback_pct` nonetheless computed and stored.

Eligibility for a statement is an explicit filter with no discretion in it. A load is eligible when its `haul_contractor_id` is the statement's carrier, its `settlement_status` is `ACCRUED`, its `dispatch_status` is `DELIVERED` or `REJECTED`, its `settlement_event_on` is not null, and that date falls on or after `period_start` and on or before `period_end`. Everything else is excluded: cancelled loads, loads already `STATEMENTED` or `PAID`, rejected loads whose `rehaul_status` is `REHAULED` (their `settlement_event_on` stays null by construction), and loads whose settlement date falls outside the closed period. The eligible loads are then ordered by their `created_at` ascending — never by `id`.

Lines are emitted per load, in that order, and in a fixed order within each load. Each entry of the `lines` array records a `line_type`, the `load_id` it derives from, the `tons` and `distance_miles` behind it, and its `amount_cents`. On a `HAUL`, `FUEL_SURCHARGE` or `DEMURRAGE` line the `tons` is the certified delivery ticket's `net_tons`; on a `REJECT_DEDUCTION` line, where no ticket was ever certified, it is the load's `estimated_net_tons`. On all four the `distance_miles` is that of the load's own haul route. For a load that reached `DELIVERED`, the system emits a `HAUL` line whose amount is that load's `line_haul_cents` plus its `incentive_cents` less its `regrade_adjustment_cents`; then a `FUEL_SURCHARGE` line whose amount is that load's already-rounded `fuel_surcharge_cents`; then, **only when that load's `demurrage_cents` is greater than zero**, a `DEMURRAGE` line for that amount. For a load that reached `REJECTED` with `rehaul_status` of `NO_ALTERNATE`, the system instead emits a single `REJECT_DEDUCTION` line whose amount is the negative of 35 percent of that load's `provisional_haul_pay_cents`, the percentage rounded half-up to whole cents before the sign is applied.

The money then rolls up in a pinned order. `gross_pay_cents` is the sum of the amounts over every `HAUL`, `FUEL_SURCHARGE` and `DEMURRAGE` line. `reject_deduction_cents` is the absolute value of the sum of the amounts over every `REJECT_DEDUCTION` line. The fuel surcharge is never recomputed at this level: each load's already-rounded whole-cent figure is summed as-is, and summing unrounded ton-miles to take 7.5 percent of the aggregate is forbidden because it produces a different total.

The insurance holdback is next, and it is deliberately not circular. The statement's `compliance_score_at_compile` is frozen from the period-to-date variant of the compliance score described in §3.2. A `holdback_pct` is selected from that frozen score: nothing at all when the score is 90 or above, 2 percent when it is at least 80 but below 90, 5 percent when it is at least 70 but below 80, and 10 percent below 70. The base the percentage applies to is pinned to the **settleable base** — gross pay less the reject deduction — computed before the holdback exists, so there is no fixed point to solve. `holdback_cents` is that settleable base multiplied by the holdback fraction, rounded half-up to whole cents, and it is emitted as a single `INSURANCE_HOLDBACK` line carrying the negative of that amount, always as the last line of the array, with a null `load_id`, a null `tons` and a null `distance_miles`. That line is emitted whenever the array holds at least one load line, even when the tier put the holdback at zero; when no load line was emitted at all, the holdback line is omitted entirely and `holdback_cents` is 0. Finally `deduction_cents` is the reject deduction plus the holdback, and `net_pay_cents` is gross pay less that total deduction.

A statement's `status` walks `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, `DISPUTED`, `REVISED`, and terminates at `PAID` or `VOID`. **Submitting** moves a statement from `DRAFT` or `REVISED` to `SUBMITTED`; it requires at least one line, and submitting a statement whose `lines` array is empty is refused with `ERR_NO_SETTLEABLE_LOADS`. On submission every load referenced by a `HAUL`, `FUEL_SURCHARGE`, `DEMURRAGE` or `REJECT_DEDUCTION` line moves `ACCRUED → STATEMENTED` on its settlement axis, with its `dispatch_status` untouched.

**Approving** moves a `SUBMITTED` statement to `APPROVED` and records the approver's `X-User-ID` as `approved_by_user_id`, subject to the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.1. **Disputing** may be raised against a statement that is `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`, by an accountant or by the carrier itself — and when the caller is the carrier, its `X-User-ID` must equal the statement's `haul_contractor_id`. A dispute stores the supplied `dispute_reason`, clears `approved_by_user_id` back to null, and leaves the attached loads at `STATEMENTED` until the statement is actually revised.

**Revising** moves a `DISPUTED` statement to `REVISED`, optionally recording a `note`. Every attached load returns `STATEMENTED → ACCRUED` on the settlement axis, with no money field on any load altered, and the statement's `lines`, `compliance_score_at_compile`, `holdback_pct`, `gross_pay_cents`, `reject_deduction_cents`, `holdback_cents`, `deduction_cents` and `net_pay_cents` are all recompiled from scratch over the same eligibility filter. A `REVISED` statement may then be submitted again, moving its loads `ACCRUED → STATEMENTED` once more — that is the negotiation loop, and it may run as many times as the carrier and the accountant need.

**Paying** moves an `APPROVED` statement to the terminal `PAID`, storing the supplied `payment_reference`. Every attached load moves `STATEMENTED → PAID` on its settlement axis, and the carrier's `lifetime_settled_cents` — a figure derived by summing `net_pay_cents` over that carrier's `PAID` statements rather than stored — increases by this statement's `net_pay_cents`, the carrier's `updated_at` being refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1 in the same call. Any of submit, approve, dispute, revise or pay attempted from a status that does not admit it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE`. Statements may be read individually with their full line array and listed with filters on `haul_contractor_id` and `status`.
