# Brewing Ingredient Distribution API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

BSG CraftBrewing sits between the hop farm, the maltings, the yeast bench and several hundred craft breweries, and almost every commercial problem it faces is a problem of *promises made against a crop that has not been harvested yet*. A brewery signs a crop-year commitment in January for hops that will not be picked until September; the distributor sells the same warehouse floor to spot buyers in the meantime; the maltster ships lots whose analysis sheet may or may not match what the brewer's recipe assumes; and the yeast bench needs a fortnight of lead time to grow slurry that decays from the moment it is harvested. When the harvest comes in short, every one of those promises has to be re-cut in a defensible order, and the money has to follow.

This document specifies the API that runs that business. It governs the full arc of a crop-year program — opening contracting, writing fixed and spot commitments, reporting the realised harvest, draining the shortfall down a strict allocation waterfall, offering substitute varieties, rolling undrawn poundage forward, and settling defaults with a resale market — alongside the physical side of the operation: malt receipt and analysis, yeast propagation scheduling, order assembly and pricing, temperature-controlled staging and shipping, and the accounts-receivable and credit-exposure ledger that ultimately decides whether a brewery may order at all.

Two properties of the system deserve emphasis up front, because they shape nearly every rule that follows. First, **poundage is conserved**: a contract's allocation is partitioned into remaining, committed, carried-forward, defaulted and resold buckets, and no operation may create or destroy pounds — it may only move them between buckets. Second, **operations cascade**: reporting a harvest can trim a confirmed order line, which reprices the order, which moves a brewery's credit exposure, which can freeze a staged reefer load. The specification treats those cascades as first-class behaviour rather than incidental bookkeeping, and each link is described where the business narrative reaches it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity and audit fields, rounding discipline, concurrency guards, the precedence in which validations fire, and the cross-cutting invariants that the domain workflows in §3 depend on.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent or empty the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Five roles exist and their spelling is exact: `Brewer`, `AllocationManager`, `CreditManager`, `WarehouseOperator` and `PlatformAdmin`. A value of `X-User-Role` that is not one of those five is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ROLE` (HTTP 403). A syntactically valid role that is not among the roles authorised for the requested operation — see the authorised-role column in §4 — is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles divide the business along segregation-of-duties lines. A `Brewer` is a brewery customer: it draws on its own crop-year commitments, builds and confirms its own orders, responds to substitution offers, asks for carry-forward, and requests yeast propagation. An `AllocationManager` is the hop desk: it opens crop-year programs, writes contracts, reports harvest, reprices spot, runs shortfall substitution, and decides carry-forwards, defaults and resales — but it cannot approve credit, and it cannot pick or ship. A `CreditManager` is the credit desk: it onboards breweries, sets limits and payment terms, posts cash receipts, decides each order's credit standing and releases credit-held reefer loads — but it cannot write contracts, declare defaults or move fulfilment. A `WarehouseOperator` runs the floor and the cold chain: receiving and analysing malt lots, releasing them, propagating yeast, picking, shipping and delivering orders, staging reefer loads and logging temperature readings — but it cannot approve credit or write contracts. A `PlatformAdmin` seeds reference data and drives the logical clock, and deliberately holds **no** business decision rights: it cannot confirm an order, approve credit, report a harvest, declare a default, analyse or release a lot, or move fulfilment.

Identity binding differs by role, and it matters because ownership checks are performed against `X-User-ID` directly. When `X-User-Role` is `Brewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Brewer` entity; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Brewer` compare that resource's brewer reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Brewer` actor may only address contracts, orders, order lines, recipes, propagation jobs, substitution offers and payments belonging to the brewery whose `id` equals `X-User-ID`; reaching for another brewery's resource is refused with `ERR_BREWER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). The `Brewer` entity itself sits outside that list and is not scope-checked: a `Brewer` actor may fetch any brewery through `GET /brewers/{id}`, and an id there that resolves to no brewery reports `ERR_BREWER_NOT_FOUND` rather than a scope violation. When `X-User-Role` is `AllocationManager`, `CreditManager`, `WarehouseOperator` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — it is stored as `defaulted_by_user_id` on a hop contract when a default is declared, and as `analyzed_by_user_id` and `released_by_user_id` on a malt lot when the lot is analysed and released — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`. Those three recorded operator identifiers are what the segregation-of-duties gates in §2.9 compare.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Collection endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0. Both are echoed in the envelope's `meta` object alongside `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page).

Unless a specific listing states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two listings are called out explicitly elsewhere because they read differently: the crop-year program listing is presented newest `crop_year` first, and a reefer load's temperature series is returned in `recorded_at` ascending order.

### 2.4. Collection Filters

Every collection endpoint accepts filters named after the field they filter on, and each filter is optional. Crop-year programs may be filtered by `status` and by `crop_year`. Hop contracts may be filtered by `brewer_id`, `variety_id`, `crop_year_program_id`, `carried_forward_from_contract_id`, `carry_forward_target_program_id`, `contract_type` and `status`. Substitution offers may be filtered by `hop_contract_id`, `substitute_variety_id`, `created_contract_id` and `status`. Malt lots may be filtered by `malt_product_id` and `status`. Brewer recipes may be filtered by `brewer_id`, `malt_product_id` and `yeast_strain_id`. Propagation jobs may be filtered by `brewer_id`, `recipe_id`, `yeast_strain_id`, `replaces_job_id` and `status`; the `replaces_job_id` filter is the only channel through which a replacement job minted by a cold-chain excursion can be discovered. Sales orders may be filtered by `brewer_id` and, independently, by `fulfillment_status` and by `credit_status`. Order lines may be filtered by `sales_order_id`, `hop_contract_id`, `malt_lot_id`, `yeast_propagation_job_id`, `hop_variety_id`, `item_type`, `allocation_source` and `status`, and this listing is the only discovery channel for lines that a cascade trimmed or quarantined. Reefer loads may be filtered by `sales_order_id`, `cold_storage_zone_id` and `status`, and it is likewise the only way to find a load that a cascade superseded or held.

Any identifier supplied as a filter value must resolve to an existing row; an unresolvable filter identifier is reported with the not-found code for the entity it names, exactly as a body reference would be.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix, generated by the server and never accepted from the client. Alongside it, all entities except one carry the full audit triple — `created_at` and `updated_at` as ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits, and `version` as an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that resource, including mutations that only rewrite derived fields such as a recomputed `total_cents` or `credit_status`. The single exception is the temperature reading: a `TemperatureReading` is an append-only telemetry event and carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has **no** `updated_at` and **no** `version`, because nothing ever modifies a reading once it is filed.

Every other instant the server stamps or derives for itself — a program's `harvest_reported_at`, an order's `invoiced_at` and `delivered_at`, an offer's `responded_at`, a job's `ready_at`, a lot's `received_at` when the caller omitted one, a carried-forward successor's shifted delivery window, a replacement job's pushed-out `pitch_date` — is rendered in that same ISO 8601 UTC form, with three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`. An instant that arrived in a request body is instead stored as supplied and echoed back verbatim, character for character, wherever it is reported, including where the server copies it onto another field: a reefer load's `last_reading_at` repeats its newest reading's `recorded_at` exactly as that reading carried it.

Time in this system is a *logical* clock rather than wall-clock time. Every delivery window test, propagation lead-time test, viability decay, trailing-volume window, staging interval and "current instant" stamp reads the logical clock. Before the clock is ever set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; the server must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A `PlatformAdmin`-only test-driving capability moves the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and echoes that instant back, and moving it changes the instant against which the freight trailing window, the yeast pitch advisory, the zone occupancy projection and every temporal validation are evaluated.

### 2.6. Money, Poundage and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are **integer counts of cents** — there is no fractional cent anywhere in a stored field or a response. Wherever a monetary amount is derived from a multiplication or a percentage, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent at the step where the formula says to round, and not later. Poundages and litre volumes are decimals: a line `quantity` carries up to three decimal places, propagation volumes up to three, and analysis and temperature figures two. Two derived decimals are explicitly two-place values rounded half up: `credit_utilization_pct` and a lot's specification match score. Yeast volumes (`recommended_slurry_l`, `propagation_volume_l`) are three-place values rounded half up, and the intermediate viability fraction is carried to four decimal places before use.

Three places in the system use **floor** rather than rounding, and the distinction is load-bearing: the allocatable pool of a harvested crop year floors to a whole pound, the carry-forward cap floors to a whole pound, and both `interval_minutes` and `cumulative_excursion_minutes` on temperature telemetry are whole minutes truncated toward zero. A partial pound is never allocated and a partial minute never accrues.

Where a formula both rounds per item and aggregates, the order is normative and is stated with the formula in §3. The general principle: per-row money is rounded to whole cents first, and the already-rounded rows are then summed. Summing unrounded products and rounding once at the end is incorrect and produces a different answer whenever two or more rows each carry a half-cent residue.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Contended Records

Three write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency against a *parent* record's `version`. In each case the version field is a **required, non-optional** input on the request, and it must carry the `version` value the client last observed on that parent. When the supplied value does not equal the parent's current `version`, the write is rejected and nothing is mutated. Operations not named below take no version field at all.

- Creating a substitution offer against a hop contract requires `expected_hop_contract_version` matching that `HopContract`'s current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_HOP_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists precisely because a new offer legitimately supersedes the contract's prior `OPEN` offer, so nothing in the offer state machine would refuse a second create; the version test is what tells an allocation desk that the shortfall picture its offer was based on has already moved.
- Staging a reefer load requires `expected_sales_order_version` matching the target `SalesOrder`'s current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SALES_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Re-staging is a valid repeat operation — a new load supersedes the order's prior staged load and releases its pallets — so the version test is what catches a dock planner working from an order snapshot that predates a shortfall trim, a credit hold or an earlier re-stage.
- Updating a brewery's credit profile requires `expected_brewer_version` matching that `Brewer`'s current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BREWER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Repeated credit-profile updates are legitimate in themselves, so the version test is what prevents a credit desk from overwriting a limit that a default settlement or a cash receipt has already repriced.

The concurrency check runs **after** the state preconditions on the target resource have been evaluated. An operation attempted from a status that does not admit it therefore reports the state error even when the supplied version is also stale.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence

Priority is a total order over the whole rule set. When one request violates several rules, the response carries the error belonging to the **lowest** priority number, and only that error. The bands, from first-evaluated to last, are:

1. **Authentication and authorisation** (priorities 1–3): header presence, then role validity, then endpoint role permission.
2. **Request shape** (priority 4): every required body field present, every field of the declared type, every enum field carrying a declared member, every quantity, poundage, pallet count and money amount non-negative, no decimal exceeding its declared scale, and every version field an integer. Violations are reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). This applies to all creating, updating and deleting operations.
3. **Existence and ownership scope** (priorities 5–7): path identifiers resolved left to right, then the `Brewer` ownership scope, then identifiers supplied in the body or query string.
4. **Referential coherence** (priorities 8–11): whether the resources named in one request actually belong together.
5. **State preconditions on the target resource** (priorities 12–18): whether the requested transition is declared from the target's current status.
6. **Optimistic concurrency** (priority 19): the stale-version guards of §2.7.
7. **Safety and cold-chain compliance** (priorities 20–24): segregation of duties, cold-chain certification, malt release, yeast readiness and excursion flags.
8. **Temporal conflicts** (priorities 25–30): reading order, staging windows, delivery windows, propagation lead time and slot spacing, contract window overlap.
9. **Capacity, uniqueness and volume** (priorities 31–43): zone capacity, unique trade codes, order composition, minimum order quantities, carry-forward caps, substitute availability, resale limits and inventory sufficiency.
10. **Credit and money** (priorities 44–47): fiscal checks fire last, so a request that is both fiscally and structurally invalid always reports the structural problem.

Within band 3, path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right: an outer identifier is reported before an inner one. Deleting a line from an order, for instance, resolves the order first and reports `ERR_SALES_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` before it ever looks for the line; only once the order resolves does an unresolvable line segment — or a line that resolves but belongs to a different order — report `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`. Identifiers supplied in a request body or query string are checked at priority 7, after the `Brewer` scope check at priority 6, and each reports the not-found code for the entity it names: `ERR_BREWER_NOT_FOUND` for `brewer_id` or `buyer_brewer_id`, `ERR_CROP_YEAR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` for `crop_year_program_id` or `carry_forward_target_program_id`, `ERR_HOP_VARIETY_NOT_FOUND` for `variety_id`, `hop_variety_id` or `substitute_variety_id`, `ERR_HOP_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND` for a contract path identifier, `hop_contract_id`, `carried_forward_from_contract_id` or `created_contract_id`, `ERR_SUBSTITUTION_OFFER_NOT_FOUND` for an offer path identifier, `ERR_MALT_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND` for `malt_product_id`, `ERR_MALT_LOT_NOT_FOUND` for a lot path identifier or `malt_lot_id`, `ERR_BREWER_RECIPE_NOT_FOUND` for a recipe path identifier or `recipe_id`, `ERR_YEAST_STRAIN_NOT_FOUND` for `yeast_strain_id`, `ERR_YEAST_JOB_NOT_FOUND` for a job path identifier, `yeast_propagation_job_id` or `replaces_job_id`, `ERR_SALES_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` for an order path identifier or `sales_order_id`, `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND` for a line segment, `ERR_COLD_STORAGE_ZONE_NOT_FOUND` for a zone path identifier or `cold_storage_zone_id`, and `ERR_COLD_CHAIN_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND` for a shipment path identifier. All of these carry HTTP 404.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Two operations in this business may not be performed by the same human who performed the paired earlier step, and both gates compare the incoming `X-User-ID` against an operator identifier that the earlier step recorded. A malt lot may not be released by the user recorded in its `analyzed_by_user_id`, and a defaulted hop contract's poundage may not be resold by the user recorded in its `defaulted_by_user_id`. Either violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), evaluated at priority 20 — after the target's state precondition, so a release attempted on a lot that is not in a releasable status reports the lot-state error instead.

### 2.10. Uniqueness of Trade Codes

Seven identifiers are globally unique within their entity, and a collision is refused at priority 32 with HTTP 409. A duplicate `crop_year` on a crop-year program reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CROP_YEAR`; a duplicate `lot_code` on a malt lot reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOT_CODE`; a duplicate `brewery_license_no` on a brewery reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_BREWERY_LICENSE`; and a duplicate `zone_code` on a cold storage zone, `variety_code` on a hop variety, `sku` on a malt product or `strain_code` on a yeast strain all report `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_CODE`.

### 2.11. Conservation of Allocated Poundage

Contracted hop poundage is a conserved quantity. A hop contract's `allocated_lbs` — the poundage the crop actually supports — is partitioned at all times into exactly five buckets: `remaining_lbs`, `committed_lbs`, `carried_forward_out_lbs`, `defaulted_lbs` and `resold_lbs`. The invariant that must hold after every write to a contract is that `allocated_lbs` equals the sum of those five figures, with `remaining_lbs` computed as `allocated_lbs` less `committed_lbs`, less `carried_forward_out_lbs`, less `defaulted_lbs`, less `resold_lbs`.

Order activity never touches `allocated_lbs`. Confirming an order moves poundage out of `remaining_lbs` and into `committed_lbs`; cancelling the order moves exactly the same poundage back, so a confirm-then-cancel round trip leaves all five buckets where they started. A resale moves poundage out of `defaulted_lbs` and into `resold_lbs`, again leaving the sum unchanged. Only the harvest reallocation described in §3.3 rewrites `allocated_lbs` itself. The field `substitution_covered_lbs` sits deliberately **outside** this identity: it is a reporting figure recording how much of a contract's `shortfall_lbs` an accepted substitution offer has replaced, and the substitute poundage physically lives on a different contract.

### 2.12. Cascading Recomputation and Derived-Field Consistency

Because so many figures in this system are derived, a single request routinely mutates several resources. The system performs those propagations immediately and synchronously within the triggering request — there is no deferred or eventual reconciliation — and each affected resource independently refreshes its `updated_at` and increments its `version`, including resources the client never named. Three named cascades recur throughout §3 and are described in full where they arise: **cascade A** runs from a harvest report through contract reallocation, order-line trimming, order repricing and finally the brewery's credit posture; **cascade B** runs from a contract default through the receivable balance, the brewery's credit posture, its open orders' credit standing and finally its staged reefer loads; and **cascade C** runs from a temperature reading through the reefer load's excursion flag, the yeast jobs and hop lines it carried, and finally the replacement propagation job minted for the failed yeast.

A consequence worth stating plainly: because a cascade can move a resource the client did not touch, the `version` a client holds for a contract, an order or a brewery can be advanced by activity elsewhere. That is exactly the situation the stale-version guards of §2.7 are designed to surface.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding a Brewery, Its Credit Standing and Its Receivable Ledger

A brewery enters the platform when the credit desk registers it. Registration captures the brewery's trade `name` (1 to 120 characters) and its state alcohol licence identifier `brewery_license_no` (1 to 40 characters, globally unique), and optionally four commercial settings: `credit_terms_days`, the net payment terms granted, which must be one of `0`, `15`, `30` or `45` and defaults to 30; `credit_limit_cents`, the ceiling on credit exposure, which must be at least 1 and defaults to 500000; `cold_chain_certified`, true when the brewery's dock is certified to receive refrigerated freight, defaulting to false; and `legacy_trailing_lbs`, a migrated pre-platform shipped poundage that must be zero or greater and defaults to 0. A newly registered brewery immediately gets a freight-program rating computed over an empty delivered-order set plus its `legacy_trailing_lbs`, and a credit posture computed from nothing at all: `ar_balance_cents` 0, `open_order_exposure_cents` 0, `credit_exposure_cents` 0, `credit_utilization_pct` 0.00 and `credit_status` `GOOD_STANDING`.

**Freight program standing.** A brewery's `trailing_12mo_volume_lbs` is its `legacy_trailing_lbs` plus the sum of `chargeable_lbs` over its own orders whose `fulfillment_status` is `DELIVERED` and whose `delivered_at` falls inside the trailing 365-day window. That window is half-open on the older end and closed on the newer end: an order delivered exactly 365 days before the current logical clock has aged **out** and is excluded, while an order delivered at exactly the current logical clock is included. `legacy_trailing_lbs` is a seeded opening balance that never ages out. From that total, `freight_program_tier` is `TIER_3` when the trailing volume is at least 120,000 pounds, otherwise `TIER_2` when it is at least 40,000 pounds, otherwise `TIER_1`; the comparison is a plain "at least" on the summed poundage with no rounding. The only way to change `legacy_trailing_lbs` after registration is a volume adjustment, an `AllocationManager` or `PlatformAdmin` capability that rewrites the figure to a supplied `legacy_trailing_lbs` with an optional `reason`, then re-rates `trailing_12mo_volume_lbs` and `freight_program_tier`.

**Credit posture.** Three figures drive the brewery's credit standing and they are recomputed on every write that changes the receivable balance or any order total. `open_order_exposure_cents` is the sum of `total_cents` over the brewery's own orders whose `fulfillment_status` is `DRAFT`, `CONFIRMED` or `PICKED`. `credit_exposure_cents` is `ar_balance_cents` plus `open_order_exposure_cents`. `credit_utilization_pct` is the exposure divided by `credit_limit_cents`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places. From those, `credit_status` is `HOLD` when `credit_exposure_cents` is **strictly greater** than `credit_limit_cents`; otherwise `WATCH` when `credit_utilization_pct` is at least 85.00; otherwise `GOOD_STANDING`. Note two deliberate consequences. Exposure exactly equal to the limit is *not* a hold — a brewery sitting at exactly 100.00% utilisation lands in `WATCH` — and the hold test compares raw cents rather than the rounded percentage. A `SHIPPED` order is excluded from `open_order_exposure_cents` because its `total_cents` has already moved into `ar_balance_cents` at invoicing; counting it in both places would double-count the exposure, and `DELIVERED` and `CANCELLED` orders are excluded for the same family of reasons. `DRAFT` orders are inside the summed set but each carries `total_cents` 0 until its first fee assessment at confirmation, so they contribute nothing. `credit_terms_days` plays no part in this computation whatsoever; it is settlement metadata carried on the brewery.

Repricing a brewery is a `CreditManager` operation and is guarded by the stale-version rule of §2.7: the request carries `expected_brewer_version` and may adjust any of `credit_limit_cents`, `credit_terms_days` or `cold_chain_certified`. Because the limit is the denominator of the utilisation figure, the credit posture is recomputed against the new limit immediately, and `credit_status` can move in either direction as a result — a raised limit can lift a brewery off `HOLD`, and a lowered one can drop a brewery straight onto it.

**The receivable ledger.** `ar_balance_cents` starts at 0 and is moved only by the following events, replayed in the `created_at` order of the triggering operation and never re-sorted by amount or by entity: invoicing an order adds that order's `total_cents`; recording delivery adds the order's `final_freight_adjustment_cents`, which may be negative; cancelling an order that has already been invoiced posts a credit memo subtracting the sum of `total_cents` and any `final_freight_adjustment_cents` already booked; declaring a contract default adds that contract's `settlement_total_cents`; an authorised resale subtracts the contract's `resale_credit_cents`; approving a carry-forward adds the contract's `carry_forward_fee_cents`; and a cash receipt subtracts its `amount_cents`. Because the credit memo reverses exactly what was booked, including the delivery-time freight adjustment, an invoice-then-deliver-then-cancel sequence returns `ar_balance_cents` to its pre-invoice value with no residue.

A cash receipt is posted by the credit desk with the received `amount_cents` and optional `received_at` and `reference` values. A receipt whose `amount_cents` exceeds the brewery's current `ar_balance_cents` is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`; a receipt that exactly clears the balance is accepted. The balance can therefore never be driven negative by a payment, though it *can* transiently go negative through a credit memo on an order that had already been paid, and that is accepted and reported as-is. Every accepted receipt re-runs the credit posture, which is the normal route by which a brewery is lifted from `HOLD` back to `WATCH` or `GOOD_STANDING`.

### 3.2. Crop Years, Hop Varieties and Contract Commitments

Hop trading is organised around a **crop-year program**, opened by the hop desk with the harvest year `crop_year` it books commitments against (an integer between 2020 and 2100, unique across programs), the instant `contract_open_date` at which contracting opened, and the instant `harvest_report_due_date` by which the harvest report is expected. A freshly opened program sits in `status` `OPEN` with `total_contracted_lbs` 0, `allocatable_pool_lbs` 0, `unallocated_pool_lbs` 0 and `harvest_yield_pct` null. Its lifecycle is short and strictly forward: `OPEN → HARVEST_REPORTED → SETTLED`, with `SETTLED` terminal.

Varieties are reference data. Registering a hop variety captures its short trade code `variety_code` (1 to 20 characters, globally unique), its `name` (1 to 60 characters), the `substitution_group` naming the aroma or alpha family that forms its substitution candidate pool, and `alpha_acid_target_pct`, the nominal alpha acid content between 0.01 and 30.00 at two decimal places that drives substitution ranking. Four further settings are optional: `cold_storage_required`, true when lots of this variety must travel under refrigeration, defaulting to true; `pallet_quantity_lbs`, the full-pallet poundage, at least 1 and defaulting to 880; `min_order_lbs`, the hard per-line minimum order quantity, at least 1 and defaulting to 44; and `available_spot_lbs`, the uncommitted spot poundage on the floor, zero or greater and defaulting to 0. The posted market price `spot_price_cents_per_lb` is required at registration and must be greater than zero at two decimal places. Repricing a variety on the spot market is an `AllocationManager` capability that rewrites `spot_price_cents_per_lb` and optionally resets `available_spot_lbs` outright; it matters beyond the price list because the spot price is the market reference used by the `MARKET_SETTLED` default settlement variant and by automatic substitution ranking.

**Writing a contract.** The hop desk commits a brewery to crop-year poundage by naming the `brewer_id` holding the commitment, the `variety_id` contracted, the `crop_year_program_id` the commitment books against, a `contract_type` of either `FIXED_CROP_YEAR` or `SPOT_HOLD`, the `contracted_lbs` committed at signing, the `price_per_lb_cents` in cents per pound (greater than zero, two decimal places), and the inclusive delivery window bounded by `delivery_window_start` and `delivery_window_end`, where the end instant must be later than the start. The committed poundage must be at least the variety's `min_order_lbs`. A contract may only be written against a program whose `status` is `OPEN`; attempting it against a program that has already reported harvest or settled is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_OPEN`. Two contracts for the same brewery-and-variety pair may not have intersecting delivery windows: the check compares closed inclusive intervals against every existing contract for that pair whose `status` is anything other than `CARRIED_FORWARD` or `RESOLD`, and sharing a single endpoint date **is** an intersection and is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

A new contract lands in `status` `ACTIVE` with `allocated_lbs` set equal to `contracted_lbs` — a program that has not yet reported a harvest is assumed to deliver in full — and with `remaining_lbs` equal to `contracted_lbs` while `committed_lbs`, `carried_forward_out_lbs`, `defaulted_lbs` and `resold_lbs` are all 0, per the conservation identity of §2.11. `shortfall_lbs` is 0, `substitution_covered_lbs` is 0, and the settlement and carry-forward reporting fields — `settlement_variant`, `settlement_damages_cents`, `settlement_admin_fee_cents`, `settlement_total_cents`, `defaulted_by_user_id`, `carry_forward_requested_lbs`, `carry_forward_fee_cents`, `carry_forward_target_program_id` and `carried_forward_from_contract_id` — are all null, with `resale_credit_cents` at 0. Creating the contract also refreshes the owning program's `total_contracted_lbs`.

The distinction between the two contract types is a ranking, not a price: `FIXED_CROP_YEAR` is honoured ahead of `SPOT_HOLD` when a short crop forces the desk to re-cut its promises, which is the subject of the next subsection. The `carried_forward_from_contract_id` field is written by the system only on a successor contract minted by an approved carry-forward, and it is the field a client filters on to trace a commitment's lineage across crop years.

### 3.3. Reporting the Harvest: the Shortfall Waterfall and Its Downstream Trim

When the crop is in, an `AllocationManager` reports the realised yield, advancing the program `OPEN → HARVEST_REPORTED`. The request carries `harvest_yield_pct`, the realised harvest as a percentage of the total contracted poundage, which must be present and between 0.00 and 200.00 at two decimal places, plus an optional `reported_note`. Reporting from any status other than `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_STATE`. The report stamps `harvest_yield_pct` and sets `harvest_reported_at` to the current logical clock.

**The reallocation itself.** The program first recomputes `total_contracted_lbs` as the sum of `contracted_lbs` over the contracts in this program whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `SHORTFALL_ADJUSTED` or `CARRY_FORWARD_PENDING`. It then freezes `allocatable_pool_lbs` at the total contracted poundage multiplied by `harvest_yield_pct`, divided by 100, and **floored** to a whole pound — a partial pound is never allocated. That pool is then drained as a **strict waterfall, not pro rata**. Order the same contract set by `contract_type` rank ascending, where `FIXED_CROP_YEAR` has rank 1 and `SPOT_HOLD` has rank 2, and then by `created_at` ascending. Walking that order, each contract's `allocated_lbs` becomes the lesser of the poundage left in the pool and its own `contracted_lbs`; the pool is reduced by that amount; and the contract's `shortfall_lbs` becomes its `contracted_lbs` less its `allocated_lbs`. Whatever poundage survives the last contract becomes the program's `unallocated_pool_lbs`, which is 0 unless `harvest_yield_pct` exceeded 100. Every `FIXED_CROP_YEAR` contract is filled to its full `contracted_lbs` before **any** `SPOT_HOLD` contract receives a single pound: a shortfall therefore lands entirely on the spot holders until they are exhausted, and only then bites into fixed commitments. Distributing the shortfall proportionally across all contracts is incorrect.

**Cascade A, link by link.** The harvest report is the entry point of a five-link chain in which each link is caused by the preceding one rather than by the report itself.

*Link 1* is the reallocation above: it writes every contract's `allocated_lbs` and `shortfall_lbs`, and the program's `total_contracted_lbs`, `allocatable_pool_lbs` and `unallocated_pool_lbs`.

*Link 2*, caused by link 1, is a state change on every short contract: any contract whose newly written `allocated_lbs` is less than its `contracted_lbs` transitions `ACTIVE → SHORTFALL_ADJUSTED`, has its `shortfall_lbs` set to the difference between `contracted_lbs` and `allocated_lbs`, has `remaining_lbs` recomputed, and has its `version` incremented.

*Link 3*, caused by the `allocated_lbs` reduction on a contract, reconciles order lines that no longer have poundage behind them. While a contract's `committed_lbs` exceeds its newly reduced `allocated_lbs`, the system walks that contract's lines — those whose contract reference points at it, whose `status` is `OPEN`, and whose owning order has `fulfillment_status` `CONFIRMED` — in `created_at` **descending** order, newest first. Each line's `quantity` is reduced by the smallest amount that closes the gap; the reduction accumulates into `trimmed_lbs`; `extended_cents` and `pallet_break` are recomputed from the new quantity; and the line moves `OPEN → TRIMMED`. When a trim would drive a line's `quantity` to zero or below, the line instead moves `OPEN → CANCELLED_SHORTFALL` with `trimmed_lbs` set to its original `quantity`, `extended_cents` set to 0, and the line dropped from the order's `subtotal_cents`, `chargeable_lbs` and `line_count`. A line that has already been trimmed once and is caught by a second reallocation that exhausts its remaining quantity moves `TRIMMED → CANCELLED_SHORTFALL` with those same effects. Either way the poundage removed from the line is subtracted from the contract's `committed_lbs` and added back to its `remaining_lbs`, so `allocated_lbs` is untouched and the conservation identity holds. Lines whose owning order has already reached `PICKED`, `SHIPPED` or `DELIVERED` are immune: the ingredients are physically committed and are not clawed back.

*Link 4*, caused by the line trims in link 3, reprices every affected order. Each one re-runs its subtotal roll-up and then its fee assessment under the `PROVISIONAL` variant, rewriting `subtotal_cents`, `volume_discount_cents`, `chargeable_lbs`, `freight_cents`, `pallet_break_fee_cents`, `small_order_surcharge_cents` and `total_cents`.

*Link 5*, caused by the `total_cents` rewrite in link 4, re-runs the owning brewery's credit posture, rewriting `open_order_exposure_cents`, `credit_exposure_cents`, `credit_utilization_pct` and `credit_status`.

**Settling the crop year.** Once every carry-forward request against the program has been decided, the hop desk closes it, transitioning `HARVEST_REPORTED → SETTLED` with an optional `settlement_note`. Settlement is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_STATE` when the program is not in `HARVEST_REPORTED`, and equally when any contract belonging to the program is still sitting in `CARRY_FORWARD_PENDING` — an undecided carry-forward request blocks settlement of the crop year. No separate barrier against writing new contracts into a settled program is needed, since contract creation already requires an `OPEN` program.

### 3.4. Covering a Shortfall with a Substitute Variety

A contract that has been cut back by a short crop is the only contract eligible for a substitution offer: the hop desk may only open an offer against a contract sitting in `SHORTFALL_ADJUSTED`, and an attempt from any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE`. Because offer creation is guarded by optimistic concurrency (§2.7), the request also carries `expected_hop_contract_version`. Beyond that it names a `selection_mode` of either `EXPLICIT` or `AUTO_RANKED` and the poundage `offered_lbs`, which must be at least 1; when the caller omits the poundage it defaults to the contract's still-uncovered shortfall, that is, its `shortfall_lbs` less any `substitution_covered_lbs` already booked.

**The candidate pool.** Whichever selection mode is used, a variety is only a legitimate substitute when three things hold: its `substitution_group` equals the shorted variety's `substitution_group`, it is not the shorted variety itself, and its `available_spot_lbs` is at least the offered poundage.

Under `AUTO_RANKED`, the server picks the winner from that pool. For each candidate it computes `alpha_acid_delta_pct` as the absolute difference between the candidate's `alpha_acid_target_pct` and the shorted variety's `alpha_acid_target_pct`, carried at two decimal places, and then orders the pool by `alpha_acid_delta_pct` ascending, so the closest brewing character wins; ties are broken by `spot_price_cents_per_lb` ascending, so the cheaper variety wins a character tie; and any remaining tie is broken by `created_at` ascending. The first candidate in that order is selected, its `spot_price_cents_per_lb` is snapshotted onto the offer as `offered_price_cents_per_lb`, and `selection_mode` is recorded as `AUTO_RANKED`. A resource's `id` is never consulted as a tie-break — `created_at` ascending is the only sanctioned final ordering. When the candidate pool is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_SUBSTITUTE_AVAILABLE` and no offer row is created at all.

Under `EXPLICIT`, the caller supplies `substitute_variety_id` and `offered_price_cents_per_lb` directly and no ranking runs, but the candidate-pool membership test still applies in full: naming a variety from a different substitution group, or one without enough spot inventory to cover the offered poundage, is likewise refused with `ERR_NO_SUBSTITUTE_AVAILABLE`. In either mode the offer records `alpha_acid_delta_pct` against the shorted variety and lands in `status` `OPEN` with `responded_at` and `created_contract_id` null. Creating it also transitions any prior `OPEN` offer on the same contract `OPEN → SUPERSEDED`, incrementing that offer's `version`, and increments the shorted contract's own `version`.

**The brewery's response.** Only the owning brewery may respond, supplying a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE` and an optional `response_note`. An offer that is already `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED` or `SUPERSEDED` cannot be answered again and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`. Either decision stamps `responded_at` with the current logical clock and increments the offer's `version`.

Declining simply moves the offer `OPEN → DECLINED`. Accepting moves it `OPEN → ACCEPTED` and does four things. The substitute variety must still hold at least `offered_lbs` in `available_spot_lbs` at the moment of acceptance — inventory can have been drawn down since the offer was written — and if it does not, the acceptance is refused with `ERR_SPOT_INVENTORY_INSUFFICIENT`. Otherwise the system mints a fresh `SPOT_HOLD` contract on the substitute variety inside the shorted contract's own `crop_year_program_id`, with `contracted_lbs` equal to `offered_lbs`, `price_per_lb_cents` equal to `offered_price_cents_per_lb`, and `status` `ACTIVE`, and records the new contract in the offer's `created_contract_id`; it reduces the substitute variety's `available_spot_lbs` by `offered_lbs`; and it adds `offered_lbs` to the shorted contract's `substitution_covered_lbs`, which — as §2.11 notes — is a reporting figure outside the conservation identity, because the replacement poundage lives on the new contract rather than on the shorted one.

### 3.5. Carry-Forward, Default Settlement and Resale

Three exits exist for poundage a brewery has not drawn: roll it into next year, default on it, or — after a default — have the desk resell it.

**Requesting a carry-forward.** A brewery may ask to roll undrawn poundage forward, moving its contract `ACTIVE → CARRY_FORWARD_PENDING` (a contract in `SHORTFALL_ADJUSTED` uses the identical transition, with the same preconditions and the same effects, arriving at the same `CARRY_FORWARD_PENDING` state). The request names the `carry_forward_target_program_id` it wants to roll into and the `requested_lbs` it wants to move, with an optional `justification`. Six conditions gate it. The contract must be in `ACTIVE` or `SHORTFALL_ADJUSTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE`. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the contract's own brewery, or the request is refused with `ERR_BREWER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The contract's own crop-year program must already have reached `HARVEST_REPORTED` — there is nothing to roll forward before the crop is known — and a request against a program still `OPEN` or already `SETTLED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_STATE`. The named target program must exist, must carry a `crop_year` exactly one greater than the source program's `crop_year`, must itself be `OPEN`, and must not be the source program; any of those failing is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CARRY_FORWARD_TARGET`. The requested poundage must be within the eligible cap, described next, or the request is refused with `ERR_CARRY_FORWARD_EXCEEDS_CAP`. And finally the contract's `remaining_lbs` must be greater than zero — a fully drawn contract can be neither carried forward nor defaulted — with a violation refused as `ERR_NO_UNCOMMITTED_VOLUME`. Those last two are evaluated in that order, the cap test before the uncommitted-volume test, which is why a request against a fully drawn contract normally surfaces as the cap error.

The cap itself is computed as 25 percent of the contract's **original** `contracted_lbs`, floored to a whole pound, and the eligible poundage is the lesser of that cap and the contract's current `remaining_lbs`. Requesting exactly the eligible figure is accepted; anything above it is not. Basing the cap on the currently-remaining poundage instead of the original commitment is incorrect: a brewery that has already drawn most of a contract can still roll the full capped amount so long as that much remains undrawn. The accepted request writes `carry_forward_requested_lbs` equal to the requested poundage and `carry_forward_fee_cents` equal to the requested poundage multiplied by `price_per_lb_cents`, multiplied by 8 and divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents. It also stamps `carry_forward_target_program_id` and increments the contract's `version`.

**Deciding it.** The hop desk answers a pending request with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DECLINE` and an optional `decision_note`; a decision on a contract that is not in `CARRY_FORWARD_PENDING` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE`. Declining moves the contract `CARRY_FORWARD_PENDING → ACTIVE` and clears `carry_forward_target_program_id`, `carry_forward_requested_lbs` and `carry_forward_fee_cents` back to null — the revision loop that lets a brewery ask again with a different figure. Approving requires that the named target program is still `OPEN`, and moves the contract `CARRY_FORWARD_PENDING → CARRIED_FORWARD`, which is terminal. Approval sets `carried_forward_out_lbs` to `carry_forward_requested_lbs` and recomputes `remaining_lbs` accordingly; mints a successor contract in the target program carrying the same `brewer_id`, `variety_id`, `contract_type` and `price_per_lb_cents`, with `contracted_lbs` equal to the carried poundage, `carried_forward_from_contract_id` pointing back at the predecessor, a delivery window shifted forward by 365 days from the predecessor's `delivery_window_start` and `delivery_window_end`, and `status` `ACTIVE`; posts `carry_forward_fee_cents` onto the brewery's `ar_balance_cents`; and re-runs the brewery's credit posture. Both decisions increment the contract's `version`.

**Declaring a default.** When a brewery walks away from undrawn poundage, an `AllocationManager` declares the default with a `default_reason` and an optional `declared_at`, moving the contract `ACTIVE → DEFAULTED` (again, `SHORTFALL_ADJUSTED → DEFAULTED` is the identical transition with identical preconditions and identical effects, cascade included). The contract must be in `ACTIVE` or `SHORTFALL_ADJUSTED` and must have `remaining_lbs` greater than zero, refused respectively with `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE` and `ERR_NO_UNCOMMITTED_VOLUME`. Declaration sets `defaulted_lbs` to the whole of `remaining_lbs`, recomputes `remaining_lbs` to 0, and records `defaulted_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID` — the value the resale segregation-of-duties gate will later compare against.

*Pricing the settlement.* The defaulted poundage is the contract's `remaining_lbs` at the moment of default. Which of two settlement algorithms applies is decided by the crop-year program: `settlement_variant` is `MARKET_SETTLED` when the contract's program `status` is `HARVEST_REPORTED` or `SETTLED`, and `LIQUIDATED` otherwise, because with no harvest reported no market reference price is considered established. Under `MARKET_SETTLED`, `settlement_damages_cents` is the defaulted poundage multiplied by the amount by which the contract's `price_per_lb_cents` exceeds the variety's current `spot_price_cents_per_lb`, with that per-pound difference floored at zero, rounded half up to whole cents — when spot has risen above the contract price the distributor can resell at a profit and damages are zero. Under `LIQUIDATED`, `settlement_damages_cents` is the defaulted poundage multiplied by `price_per_lb_cents`, multiplied by 35 and divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents, with no reference to spot at all. Both variants then take the same administration fee. The fee percentage is 1.0 when `settlement_damages_cents` is at least 1,000,000 cents and 2.0 otherwise — note the inverted direction, in which *larger* settlements attract the *lower* administration percentage — and `settlement_admin_fee_cents` is the damages multiplied by that percentage and divided by 100, rounded half up. `settlement_total_cents` is `settlement_damages_cents` plus `settlement_admin_fee_cents`. The tier selection is normative in one respect: the fee tier is chosen from the **pre-fee** damages figure. Choosing the tier from the fee-inclusive total is incorrect and flips the rate whenever damages sit just below the 1,000,000-cent threshold while damages-plus-fee sit at or above it.

*Cascade B, link by link.* The default declaration is the entry point of a four-link chain. *Link 1* adds `settlement_total_cents` to the brewery's `ar_balance_cents`. *Link 2*, caused by that increase, re-runs the brewery's credit posture, rewriting `credit_exposure_cents`, `credit_utilization_pct` and `credit_status`. *Link 3*, caused by `credit_status` becoming `HOLD`, moves every order of that brewery whose `fulfillment_status` is `DRAFT` or `CONFIRMED` and whose own `credit_status` is `APPROVED` along the credit dimension `APPROVED → ON_HOLD`. *Link 4*, caused by each of those orders going on hold, moves every reefer load of those orders whose `status` is `STAGED` along `STAGED → HELD`, which releases that load's `pallet_count` from its zone's `occupied_pallets`. Every entity touched at every link refreshes its own `updated_at` and increments its own `version`, the contract included.

**Reselling defaulted poundage.** Defaulted poundage can be sold to another brewery, moving the contract `DEFAULTED → RESOLD`, which is terminal. The request names the `buyer_brewer_id`, the `resale_lbs` being sold and the `resale_price_cents_per_lb` achieved. Four conditions gate it: the contract must be in `DEFAULTED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE`; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the contract's `defaulted_by_user_id`, refused otherwise with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; `resale_lbs` must not exceed the contract's current `defaulted_lbs`, with exactly `defaulted_lbs` accepted and emptying the bucket, refused otherwise with `ERR_RESALE_EXCEEDS_DEFAULTED_VOLUME`; and `buyer_brewer_id` must differ from the contract's own `brewer_id`, since a brewery cannot buy back its own defaulted poundage, refused otherwise with `ERR_RESALE_BUYER_INVALID`. An accepted resale moves `resale_lbs` out of `defaulted_lbs` and into `resold_lbs`, leaving `allocated_lbs` untouched. It sets `resale_credit_cents` to the lesser of `settlement_total_cents` and the resale proceeds — the `resale_lbs` multiplied by `resale_price_cents_per_lb`, rounded half up to whole cents — and subtracts that credit from the defaulting brewery's `ar_balance_cents`. The brewery's credit posture is then re-run, which is the mechanism by which a resale can flip a brewery's `credit_status` from `HOLD` back to `WATCH` or to `GOOD_STANDING`. The contract's `version` increments.

### 3.6. Malt: Recipes, Receipt, Analysis Sheets and Specification Matching

**Malt products.** A malt SKU is reference data registered by the warehouse. It carries the distributor stock-keeping unit `sku` (1 to 30 characters, globally unique), a trade `name` (1 to 80 characters), a `malt_class` of either `BASE` or `SPECIALTY`, and a spot `list_price_cents_per_lb` greater than zero at two decimal places. Four optional settings define its physical and quality envelope: `max_moisture_pct`, the analysis moisture ceiling, greater than zero at two decimal places and defaulting to 5.0; `min_diastatic_power`, the diastatic power floor in degrees Lintner, zero or greater at two decimal places and defaulting to 60.0, which is enforced only for `BASE` malt because `SPECIALTY` malts are exempt from the enzyme requirement; `pallet_quantity_lbs`, the full-pallet poundage used for pallet-break assessment, at least 1 and defaulting to 2200; and `min_order_lbs`, the hard per-line minimum, at least 1 and defaulting to 55. A malt product also carries `cold_storage_required`, which is not supplied at registration and defaults to false.

**Brewer recipes.** A recipe is the specification a brewery brews to, and it is registered either by the brewery itself or by the hop desk on its behalf. It names the owning `brewer_id`, the `malt_product_id` it specifies as its base malt, and the `yeast_strain_id` it pitches, plus a `name` of 1 to 80 characters. Its brewing parameters are `batch_volume_bbl`, the brew length in US barrels, greater than zero at two decimal places; `original_gravity_plato`, the target original gravity in degrees Plato, greater than zero at two decimal places; and `requested_pitch_rate_million_cells_per_ml_per_plato`, the pitch rate the brewery intends to use, greater than zero at three decimal places, which is compared against the recommendation for advisory purposes only. Its malt specification targets are `target_color_lovibond`, `target_extract_pct` and `target_protein_pct`, each greater than zero at two decimal places. Optionally it carries `adjunct_pct`, the unmalted adjunct share of the grist between 0.00 and 60.00 at two decimal places, defaulting to 0; a non-zero adjunct share raises the diastatic-power requirement during specification matching, because adjunct brewing needs surplus enzyme.

**Receiving a lot.** The warehouse receives a physical lot against a product, naming its `malt_product_id`, the maltster's `lot_code` (1 to 40 characters, globally unique) and the `quantity_lbs` received, which must be at least 1. An optional `received_at` records the warehouse receipt timestamp; when omitted it defaults to the current logical clock. The lot lands in `status` `RECEIVED` with `analysis_count` 0, all five analysis figures — `color_lovibond`, `extract_pct`, `moisture_pct`, `protein_pct` and `diastatic_power` — null, `quarantine_reason`, `analyzed_by_user_id` and `released_by_user_id` all null, `committed_lbs` 0 and `available_lbs` equal to `quantity_lbs`.

**Availability.** A lot's `committed_lbs` is the sum of `quantity` over the malt lines pointing at it whose `status` is `OPEN` or `TRIMMED` **and** whose owning order has a `fulfillment_status` of `CONFIRMED`, `PICKED`, `SHIPPED` or `DELIVERED`, and `available_lbs` is `quantity_lbs` less `committed_lbs`. Lines on a `DRAFT` order are deliberately excluded — malt is not reserved until an order is confirmed — so two breweries can each hold draft lines against the same lot, and the second one to confirm is the one that fails with `ERR_MALT_LOT_INSUFFICIENT_QUANTITY`. Lines in `CANCELLED_SHORTFALL`, `REMOVED` or `QUARANTINED` are excluded too, so cancelling or trimming a line returns its poundage to `available_lbs`.

**The analysis sheet and its fork.** A `WarehouseOperator` files an analysis sheet against a lot in `RECEIVED`, supplying all five figures: `color_lovibond` in degrees Lovibond, `extract_pct` as fine-grind extract percentage, `moisture_pct`, `protein_pct` as total protein percentage, and `diastatic_power` in degrees Lintner. Filing against a lot in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`. Both outcomes of the sheet write all five values onto the lot, increment `analysis_count` by exactly 1, and record `analyzed_by_user_id` as the filing `X-User-ID`; both increment the lot's `version`.

The lot passes — `RECEIVED → PASSED` — when its `moisture_pct` is at or below the product's `max_moisture_pct` **and** either the product's `malt_class` is `SPECIALTY` or its `diastatic_power` is at or above the product's `min_diastatic_power`; passing also clears `quarantine_reason` to null. The lot is quarantined — `RECEIVED → QUARANTINED` — when its `moisture_pct` is strictly above `max_moisture_pct`, or when the product's `malt_class` is `BASE` and its `diastatic_power` is strictly below `min_diastatic_power`; quarantining sets `quarantine_reason` to `MOISTURE_ABOVE_MAX` or `DIASTATIC_POWER_BELOW_MIN`, with moisture evaluated first so that a lot failing both reports the moisture reason. Which branch a given sheet takes therefore depends entirely on the parent product's configuration.

A quarantined lot has two exits, both taken by the warehouse. A retest, carrying an optional `retest_reason`, moves it `QUARANTINED → RECEIVED` and clears `quarantine_reason` to null while leaving `analysis_count` and the previously recorded analysis values intact — the revision loop that lets a fresh sheet be filed against the same lot. A rejection, which requires a `reject_reason`, moves it `QUARANTINED → REJECTED` and is terminal.

A passed lot is released for picking by a `WarehouseOperator` with an optional `release_note`, moving `PASSED → RELEASED`, which is also terminal, and recording `released_by_user_id` as the releasing `X-User-ID`. The releasing user may not be the user recorded in `analyzed_by_user_id`, per §2.9.

**Specification matching.** A brewery, the hop desk, the warehouse or platform operations may score a lot's analysis sheet against a recipe's targets by naming a `recipe_id`; the credit desk is the one role excluded from this capability. The result echoes the `malt_lot_id` and `recipe_id` scored and reports `match_score`, `match_grade`, the three per-attribute deviations `deviation_color`, `deviation_extract` and `deviation_protein`, the `applicable_diastatic_power_floor` that was in force, and `hard_fail_reason`.

Two hard failures are evaluated first, in exactly this order, and when either fires the score is not computed at all and `match_grade` is `REJECTED`. The first is a lot whose `moisture_pct` is strictly above the product's `max_moisture_pct`. The second is a lot whose `diastatic_power` is strictly below the applicable floor, where that floor is the product's `min_diastatic_power` normally but is raised by 10.0 degrees Lintner when the recipe's `adjunct_pct` is greater than zero; the floor actually used is what `applicable_diastatic_power_floor` reports. `hard_fail_reason` names which hard specification failed, using the same vocabulary as quarantine — `MOISTURE_ABOVE_MAX` or `DIASTATIC_POWER_BELOW_MIN` — and is null when neither fired.

Otherwise the lot is graded on three attributes. For each attribute, the deviation is the absolute difference between the lot's value and the recipe's target, divided by that attribute's tolerance, and then capped at 1 — the pairs being `color_lovibond` against `target_color_lovibond` with a tolerance of 1.5 degrees Lovibond, `extract_pct` against `target_extract_pct` with a tolerance of 1.0 percentage point, and `protein_pct` against `target_protein_pct` with a tolerance of 0.8 percentage points. Each deviation is carried to four decimal places and is capped at 1 **individually, before weighting**: a wildly off-specification colour therefore costs at most its own weight and cannot drag the total negative. Capping the weighted sum instead of the individual deviations is incorrect. The weighted penalty is the colour deviation multiplied by 0.40, plus the extract deviation multiplied by 0.35, plus the protein deviation multiplied by 0.25 — three weights that sum to exactly 1.00, so a perfect lot scores 100.00. `match_score` is 100 multiplied by one minus that weighted penalty, rounded half up to two decimal places once at the end. `match_grade` is then `EXACT` when the score is at least 95.00, `ACCEPTABLE` when it is at least 80.00, `SUBSTITUTE_REQUIRED` when it is at least 60.00, and `REJECTED` otherwise.

### 3.7. Yeast: Strains, Propagation Slots and the Pitch-Rate Advisory

**Strains.** A yeast strain is registered by the warehouse or platform operations with its laboratory `strain_code` (1 to 20 characters, globally unique), trade `name` (1 to 60 characters), a `fermentation_type` of either `ALE` or `LAGER`, the whole-day `propagation_lead_time_days` of bench time required (at least 1), and a spot `list_price_cents_per_l` in cents per litre of slurry, greater than zero at two decimal places. Four optional parameters describe the bench: `viability_pct_at_ship`, the nominal cell viability at hand-off, between 50.00 and 100.00 at two decimal places, defaulting to 92.0; `cells_per_ml_slurry_billion`, the billions of cells per millilitre of harvested slurry, greater than zero at two decimal places, defaulting to 1.2; `min_batch_l`, the smallest batch the bench will run in litres, greater than zero at three decimal places, defaulting to 20.0; and `vessel_max_l`, the largest propagation vessel available in litres, at least `min_batch_l`, at three decimal places, defaulting to 2000.0. A strain also carries `cold_storage_required`, which is not supplied at registration and defaults to true, so slurry travels under refrigeration unless configured otherwise. The lead time serves double duty: besides being the bench time, it is also the minimum gap the platform enforces between two propagation slots on the same strain.

**Requesting a propagation.** A brewery, or the warehouse acting for it, requests a propagation by naming the `brewer_id` the slurry is for, the `recipe_id` supplying the batch parameters, the `yeast_strain_id` to propagate, and the `pitch_date` on which the brewery intends to pitch. Four business gates apply. The job's `yeast_strain_id` must equal the named recipe's own `yeast_strain_id`, and the recipe's `brewer_id` must equal the job's `brewer_id`; either mismatch is refused with `ERR_STRAIN_RECIPE_MISMATCH`. The `pitch_date` must fall at least `propagation_lead_time_days` whole days after the current logical clock, with exactly the lead time accepted and anything shorter refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PROPAGATION_LEAD_TIME`. No other job on the same `yeast_strain_id` whose `status` is `REQUESTED`, `PROPAGATING` or `READY` may have a `pitch_date` strictly closer than `propagation_lead_time_days` whole days to the requested date; a gap exactly equal to the lead time is permitted, because by then the propagation vessel has been turned over, and anything tighter is refused with `ERR_PROPAGATION_SLOT_CONFLICT`. Finally the owning brewery must not be on credit hold: a request from a brewery whose `credit_status` is `HOLD` is refused with `ERR_BREWER_CREDIT_HOLD`. An accepted request lands in `status` `REQUESTED` with `ready_at`, `failure_reason` and `replaces_job_id` all null, and with the advisory figures below computed immediately.

**The pitch-rate advisory.** The advisory turns the recipe's batch into a bench volume, and it runs on job creation, on a requeue, and on the automatic creation of a replacement job.

First, `recommended_pitch_rate_million_cells_per_ml_per_plato` is 0.75 when the strain's `fermentation_type` is `ALE` and 1.50 when it is `LAGER` — lagers are pitched at double the ale rate. Second, the batch is converted to litres by multiplying the recipe's `batch_volume_bbl` by 117.348 litres per US barrel, and `required_cells_billion` is the recommended rate multiplied by the recipe's `original_gravity_plato`, multiplied by that litre figure, rounded half up to two decimal places. Third, viability decays over the wait: `wait_days` is the number of whole days from the current logical clock forward to `pitch_date`, floored at 0, and the viability fraction is the strain's `viability_pct_at_ship` divided by 100, less 0.02 for each whole waiting day, but never allowed below a hard floor of 0.50 — a slurry sitting long enough to decay past that floor is still costed at the floor and never lower. That fraction is carried to four decimal places. Fourth, `recommended_slurry_l` is `required_cells_billion` divided by the product of the strain's `cells_per_ml_slurry_billion` and the viability fraction, then divided by 1000, rounded half up to three decimal places. Fifth, `propagation_volume_l` applies a safety factor and then clamps to the bench: take `recommended_slurry_l` multiplied by 1.15, rounded half up to three decimal places; raise the result to `min_batch_l` if it falls below that floor; and then lower it to `vessel_max_l` if it exceeds that ceiling. The nesting order is normative — the `min_batch_l` floor is applied first and the `vessel_max_l` ceiling second, so when a strain is configured with a `min_batch_l` above its `vessel_max_l`, the ceiling wins. Swapping the two yields a different answer for a tiny batch on a small vessel.

Sixth and last, `pitch_rate_advice` compares what the brewery asked for against the recommendation using a symmetric tolerance band of 10 percent. It is `UNDERPITCH` when the recipe's `requested_pitch_rate_million_cells_per_ml_per_plato` is strictly below the recommended rate multiplied by 0.9, `OVERPITCH` when it is strictly above the recommended rate multiplied by 1.1, and `OPTIMAL` otherwise — both band edges falling inside `OPTIMAL`. The advice never changes the computed volumes; it is guidance only.

**Running the bench.** The warehouse starts a propagation, moving the job `REQUESTED → PROPAGATING` and stamping `ready_at` as the current logical clock plus the strain's `propagation_lead_time_days` days; `ready_at` is written by this step alone and by nothing in the advisory. The warehouse then closes the propagation with an `outcome` of either `READY` or `FAILED` plus an optional `failure_reason`: the `PROPAGATING → READY` branch clears `failure_reason` to null, while the `PROPAGATING → FAILED` branch records the supplied `failure_reason`. Both increment the job's `version`, and a close attempted from any status other than `PROPAGATING` — like a start attempted from any status other than `REQUESTED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_JOB_STATE`.

A failed job can be re-booked. A requeue supplies a `new_pitch_date` and moves the job `FAILED → REQUESTED`, setting `pitch_date` to the new date and clearing both `failure_reason` and `ready_at` back to null. The same two temporal gates that guarded the original request are re-applied to `new_pitch_date`: it must be at least `propagation_lead_time_days` whole days after the current logical clock, and it must not sit strictly closer than that lead time to another live job on the same strain. Because the wait to the new date differs, the advisory is re-run and `propagation_volume_l` can change through viability decay.

Two further transitions on a job are driven by other parts of the system rather than by the bench. A `READY` job referenced by a yeast line of an order that ships moves `READY → SHIPPED`, which is terminal. And a `READY` job carried on a reefer load that suffers a temperature excursion moves `READY → FAILED` with `failure_reason` set to `COLD_CHAIN_EXCURSION`, which in turn mints a replacement job — described in §3.9.

### 3.8. Sales Orders: Lines, Pricing, the Two Status Dimensions and Fulfilment

**Opening an order.** A brewery, or the hop desk on its behalf, opens an order by naming the `brewer_id` it is for, the `requested_ship_date` on which the brewery wants the goods to leave the dock, and optionally its own `purchase_order_ref`, which is echoed back verbatim and never interpreted. A fresh order lands with `fulfillment_status` `DRAFT`, `credit_status` `PENDING_REVIEW`, `fee_variant` and `freight_tier_applied` null, `line_count` 0, `requires_cold_chain` false, `invoiced_at`, `delivered_at` and `cancel_reason` null, and every money field — `subtotal_cents`, `volume_discount_cents`, `freight_cents`, `pallet_break_fee_cents`, `small_order_surcharge_cents`, `total_cents` and `final_freight_adjustment_cents` — at 0, along with `chargeable_lbs` at 0.

An order carries **two independent status dimensions**. `fulfillment_status` is the physical dimension and runs `DRAFT`, `CONFIRMED`, `PICKED`, `SHIPPED`, `DELIVERED`, with `CANCELLED` as the other terminal state. `credit_status` is the financial dimension and moves among `PENDING_REVIEW`, `APPROVED` and `ON_HOLD`. The two evolve separately: an order can be `APPROVED` while still `DRAFT`, or still `PENDING_REVIEW` while already `CONFIRMED`. Neither dimension is derived from the other, and the credit dimension has no terminal state — it is cyclic, since an approved order can be put back on hold and then approved again.

**Adding lines.** A line is added to a `DRAFT` order only; an add attempted against any other `fulfillment_status` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. The line names its `item_type` — one of `HOP`, `MALT` or `YEAST` — the billable `quantity` in the line's own unit greater than zero at three decimal places, and the `uom`, either `LB` or `L`. Optionally it names an `allocation_source` of `CONTRACT` or `SPOT`, defaulting to `SPOT`; `CONTRACT` draws against a crop-year commitment while `SPOT` draws against floor inventory, and `MALT` and `YEAST` lines are always `SPOT`. Its four foreign keys — `hop_variety_id`, `hop_contract_id`, `malt_lot_id` and `yeast_propagation_job_id` — are each optional in the request but tightly constrained by `item_type`, and an optional `unit_price_cents` may override the price.

Exactly the reference set matching `item_type` must be populated, and the `uom` must agree with it: a `HOP` line requires a `hop_variety_id` plus, when its `allocation_source` is `CONTRACT`, a `hop_contract_id`, and must be in `LB`; a `MALT` line requires a `malt_lot_id` and must be in `LB`; a `YEAST` line requires a `yeast_propagation_job_id` and must be in `L`. A `HOP` line naming a `malt_lot_id`, or a `YEAST` line quoted in `LB`, is refused with `ERR_ITEM_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` — the `uom` is never silently corrected. A `CONTRACT`-sourced hop line carries **both** `hop_variety_id` and `hop_contract_id`, and the same rule refuses the line when the two disagree about which variety is being sold. Before that check, the referenced contract must belong to the same brewery as the owning order: a line pointing at another brewery's contract is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_BREWER_MISMATCH`, which outranks the reference-shape check — except when the actor is a `Brewer`, for whom the §2.1 ownership scope on the named contract is evaluated first and refuses the line with `ERR_BREWER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` instead. After both, the line's `quantity` must be at least the item's own minimum — the variety's `min_order_lbs` for `HOP`, the malt product's `min_order_lbs` for `MALT`, the strain's `min_batch_l` for `YEAST` — with exactly the minimum accepted and anything below it refused with `ERR_BELOW_MINIMUM_ORDER_QUANTITY`.

When `unit_price_cents` is omitted it is defaulted from the source of the goods: the contract's own `contract_price_cents_per_lb` for a `CONTRACT` hop line, the variety's `spot_price_cents_per_lb` for a `SPOT` hop line, the malt product's `list_price_cents_per_lb` for a `MALT` line, and the strain's `list_price_cents_per_l` for a `YEAST` line. The line's `cold_chain_required` is copied from the `cold_storage_required` flag of whatever it references — the variety for hops, the product for malt, the strain for yeast. A new line lands in `status` `OPEN` with `trimmed_lbs` 0.

Adding a line, and equally removing one, re-runs the order roll-up. Per line, in `created_at` ascending order, `extended_cents` is the line's `quantity` multiplied by its `unit_price_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. Then `subtotal_cents` is the sum of those per-line `extended_cents` values; `chargeable_lbs` is the sum of `quantity` over lines whose `uom` is `LB`; `line_count` is the number of lines in the set; and `requires_cold_chain` is true when any line in the set has `cold_chain_required` true. The input set for all four is the order's lines whose `status` is `OPEN` or `TRIMMED` — lines in `CANCELLED_SHORTFALL`, `REMOVED` or `QUARANTINED` are excluded, so a quarantined line silently stops contributing to the order's totals. **The rounding order is normative**: each line's `extended_cents` is rounded to whole cents first and the already-rounded per-line values are then summed. Summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end is wrong and produces a different `subtotal_cents` whenever two or more lines each carry a half-cent residue. Each line also gets a `pallet_break` flag, true when the line's applicable pallet quantity — the variety's `pallet_quantity_lbs` for hop lines, the malt product's `pallet_quantity_lbs` for malt lines, and absent for yeast lines — is greater than zero and the line's `quantity` is not an exact multiple of it.

Removing a line from a `DRAFT` order moves it `OPEN → REMOVED`, which is terminal, and re-runs the roll-up over what is left, rewriting `subtotal_cents`, `chargeable_lbs`, `line_count` and `requires_cold_chain`. Removal returns the order object rather than the line.

**Confirming.** Confirmation is the brewery's own act and moves the order `DRAFT → CONFIRMED`. Beyond the `DRAFT` requirement it imposes, in ascending precedence, a cold-chain gate, the delivery-window gate, the line-count gate, three volume gates and two fiscal gates. When the order's `requires_cold_chain` is true the owning brewery's `cold_chain_certified` must be true, otherwise `ERR_COLD_CHAIN_NOT_CERTIFIED`. For every `CONTRACT`-sourced hop line the order's `requested_ship_date` must fall inside the closed inclusive interval running from the contract's `delivery_window_start` to its `delivery_window_end`, otherwise `ERR_SHIP_DATE_OUTSIDE_DELIVERY_WINDOW`. The order must hold at least one line in `OPEN` or `TRIMMED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_HAS_NO_LINES`. Each contract must have `remaining_lbs` at least equal to the line quantity drawing on it, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_VOLUME_EXHAUSTED`; each malt lot must have `available_lbs` at least equal to the quantity drawing on it, otherwise `ERR_MALT_LOT_INSUFFICIENT_QUANTITY`; each variety must have `available_spot_lbs` at least equal to the `SPOT` quantity drawing on it, otherwise `ERR_SPOT_INVENTORY_INSUFFICIENT`. **All three volume gates are aggregate**, not per line: two individually affordable lines on the same contract, lot or variety that jointly overdraw it are refused. Finally the owning brewery's `credit_status` must not be `HOLD`, otherwise `ERR_BREWER_CREDIT_HOLD`, and the brewery's `credit_exposure_cents` plus this order's freshly computed `total_cents` must not exceed its `credit_limit_cents`, otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; exposure landing exactly on the limit is accepted. Note that a malt lot need **not** be in `RELEASED` to be confirmed against — release is only required at picking.

Confirmation commits poundage in three places. Every `CONTRACT` hop line adds its `quantity` to its contract's `committed_lbs`, reducing `remaining_lbs` by the same amount so `allocated_lbs` is conserved; every `MALT` line adds its `quantity` to its lot's `committed_lbs`, reducing `available_lbs`; and every `SPOT` hop line subtracts its `quantity` from its variety's `available_spot_lbs`. It then runs the roll-up followed by the fee assessment in its `PROVISIONAL` variant, and the owning brewery re-runs its credit posture, whose `open_order_exposure_cents` now includes this order's `total_cents`.

**The fee assessment and its two variants.** The fee assessment has two variants. `PROVISIONAL` is applied at confirmation and reads the brewery's freight program tier from a trailing volume that **excludes** this order's own `chargeable_lbs`, because the order has not shipped and has not yet earned volume credit. `FINAL` is applied at delivery and recomputes the tier with this order's own `chargeable_lbs` **included** in the trailing window, because a delivered order counts toward the program. A large order can therefore promote itself into a cheaper tier between confirmation and delivery. `fee_variant` records which variant produced the current fee fields and is null before confirmation.

**The evaluation order is normative — exactly these eleven steps, in this order.** First, `subtotal_cents` comes from the roll-up, with per-line rounding already applied. Second, the discount percentage is 3.0 when `subtotal_cents` is at least 5,000,000 cents, otherwise 1.5 when it is at least 1,000,000 cents, otherwise 0.0. Third, `volume_discount_cents` is `subtotal_cents` multiplied by that percentage divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents. Fourth, the discounted subtotal is `subtotal_cents` less `volume_discount_cents`. Fifth, the tier volume is the brewery's trailing volume, plus this order's `chargeable_lbs` when and only when the variant is `FINAL`. Sixth, `freight_tier_applied` is `TIER_3` when that tier volume is at least 120,000 pounds, otherwise `TIER_2` when it is at least 40,000 pounds, otherwise `TIER_1`. Seventh, the rated freight is `chargeable_lbs` multiplied by the rate for the applied tier — 12 cents per pound for `TIER_1`, 8 for `TIER_2`, 5 for `TIER_3` — rounded half up to whole cents. Eighth, `freight_cents` is the greater of the applied tier's freight minimum — 25,000 cents for `TIER_1`, 15,000 for `TIER_2`, 0 for `TIER_3` — and that rated freight. Ninth, when `requires_cold_chain` is true, a reefer surcharge of 18,000 cents is added to `freight_cents`. Tenth, `pallet_break_fee_cents` is 4,500 cents multiplied by the number of lines in the roll-up input set whose `pallet_break` is true. Eleventh, `small_order_surcharge_cents` is 7,500 cents when `chargeable_lbs` is strictly below the order minimum of 2,000 pounds, and 0 otherwise. `total_cents` is then the discounted subtotal plus `freight_cents` plus `pallet_break_fee_cents` plus `small_order_surcharge_cents`.

Two ordering points inside that sequence are load-bearing. The freight minimum in step eight is applied **before** the reefer surcharge in step nine, so the surcharge is never absorbed by the minimum; applying the minimum after the surcharge is wrong. And the volume discount in steps two and three is derived from `subtotal_cents` alone — freight, pallet break and small-order surcharge are never discounted. On the `FINAL` variant, `final_freight_adjustment_cents` is the new `total_cents` less the `total_cents` that the `PROVISIONAL` pass had stored, and is negative when the order promoted itself into a cheaper tier; it stays at its default under the `PROVISIONAL` variant.

**The credit dimension.** The credit desk records a decision on the order with a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `HOLD`, plus an optional `decision_note`. A decision may be recorded whenever the order's `fulfillment_status` is anything other than `CANCELLED` or `DELIVERED`; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. `APPROVE` moves `credit_status` `PENDING_REVIEW → APPROVED` or `ON_HOLD → APPROVED`, and requires that the owning brewery's own `credit_status` is not `HOLD`, otherwise `ERR_BREWER_CREDIT_HOLD` — a brewery over its limit cannot have its orders approved one at a time. `HOLD` moves `credit_status` `PENDING_REVIEW → ON_HOLD` or `APPROVED → ON_HOLD`, and additionally freezes the dock: every reefer load of this order in `STAGED` moves to `HELD`, releasing its `pallet_count` from its zone's `occupied_pallets`. The same `APPROVED → ON_HOLD` move, with the same freeze, is what cascade B link 4 performs when the owning brewery is driven onto credit hold, for orders whose `fulfillment_status` is `DRAFT` or `CONFIRMED`.

**Picking, shipping, delivering.** Picking is a warehouse act moving the order `CONFIRMED → PICKED`. It is guarded across three entity pairs: every `MALT` line's lot must be in `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_MALT_LOT_NOT_RELEASED`; every `YEAST` line's propagation job must be in `READY`, otherwise `ERR_YEAST_NOT_READY`; and the order's own `credit_status` must be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_CREDIT_NOT_APPROVED`. The malt and yeast gates are checked before the credit gate.

Shipping moves the order `PICKED → SHIPPED` and is where the money moves. When the order's `requires_cold_chain` is true, a reefer load for the order must be in `IN_TRANSIT`, and a load sitting in `EXCURSION_FLAGGED` blocks the ship with `ERR_COLD_CHAIN_EXCURSION`. Shipping sets `invoiced_at` to the current logical clock, adds `total_cents` to the brewery's `ar_balance_cents`, and moves every `READY` propagation job referenced by a `YEAST` line to `SHIPPED`. The brewery then re-runs its credit posture: because a `SHIPPED` order is excluded from `open_order_exposure_cents` at the same moment its total enters `ar_balance_cents`, `credit_exposure_cents` does not move at all on a ship. Counting it in both places would double-count the exposure.

Delivering moves the order `SHIPPED → DELIVERED`, which is terminal, and is again blocked by a flagged load with `ERR_COLD_CHAIN_EXCURSION`; when the order carries a reefer load in `IN_TRANSIT`, that load moves to `DELIVERED` alongside. Delivery sets `delivered_at` to the current logical clock, re-runs the fee assessment under the `FINAL` variant — rewriting `freight_cents`, `freight_tier_applied` and `total_cents`, setting `fee_variant` to `FINAL` and storing `final_freight_adjustment_cents` — and adds that adjustment, which may be negative, to the brewery's `ar_balance_cents`. The brewery then re-rates its freight program, since this order's `chargeable_lbs` is now inside the trailing window, and re-runs its credit posture.

**Cancelling.** Cancellation is available to the owning brewery or to the hop desk from `DRAFT`, `CONFIRMED`, `PICKED` or `SHIPPED`, and requires a `cancel_reason`. It moves the order to `CANCELLED`, moves every line in `OPEN` or `TRIMMED` to `REMOVED`, and unwinds every commitment: each `CONTRACT` hop line's `quantity` is subtracted from its contract's `committed_lbs` and added back to `remaining_lbs`, leaving `allocated_lbs` unchanged; each `MALT` line's `quantity` is subtracted from its lot's `committed_lbs`; and each `SPOT` hop line's `quantity` is added back to its variety's `available_spot_lbs`. When `invoiced_at` is not null, a credit memo for `total_cents` plus any `final_freight_adjustment_cents` already booked is subtracted from `ar_balance_cents`, restoring the pre-invoice balance exactly — a ship, then deliver, then cancel sequence leaves no residue. Every reefer load of the order in `STAGED` or `HELD` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, releasing its `pallet_count` from its zone. The brewery then re-runs its credit posture.

### 3.9. The Cold Chain: Zones, Reefer Loads and Temperature Telemetry

**Zones.** A temperature-controlled zone is commissioned by the warehouse or platform operations with a `zone_code` of 1 to 20 characters, globally unique across zones, a `temp_setpoint_c` between -40.00 and 25.00 degrees Celsius at two decimal places, and a `capacity_pallets` of at least 1. Optionally it carries a `tolerance_c`, the permitted band around the setpoint, greater than zero at two decimal places and defaulting to 2.0. It is commissioned with `occupied_pallets` 0.

**Staging a load.** A reefer load is staged by the warehouse against an order, naming the `sales_order_id`, the `cold_storage_zone_id` it will sit in, the `expected_sales_order_version` guarding the write, the `pallet_count` it occupies (at least 1), the half-open window `stage_from` to `stage_until`, and the trailer's `reefer_setpoint_c` between -40.00 and 25.00 degrees Celsius at two decimal places, with an optional `reefer_tolerance_c` greater than zero at two decimal places, defaulting to 2.0. A load may only be staged for an order whose `requires_cold_chain` is true, otherwise `ERR_COLD_CHAIN_NOT_CERTIFIED`. `stage_until` must be strictly after `stage_from`; a zero-length or inverted window is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STAGING_WINDOW`. An accepted load lands in `status` `STAGED` with `excursion_minutes` 0, `reading_count` 0 and `last_reading_at` null, and increments the owning order's `version`. Staging a new load moves any prior load on the same order in `STAGED` or `HELD` to `SUPERSEDED`, releasing its pallets — a legitimate re-stage, which is exactly why the write is version-guarded per §2.7.

**Zone capacity is an aggregate sweep.** Each `STAGED` load occupies its `pallet_count` over the **half-open** interval from `stage_from` inclusive to `stage_until` exclusive, so a load that vacates at exactly the instant another arrives does not overlap it and back-to-back staging with a zero gap is always permitted regardless of capacity. The admission test, run both when a load is staged and when a held load is returned to staging, merges the candidate interval into the zone's existing `STAGED` set: collect the boundary instants of the candidate plus every `STAGED` load in the zone; for each resulting sub-interval, sum `pallet_count` over every load whose half-open interval covers it; and refuse with `ERR_ZONE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when any sub-interval's sum exceeds `capacity_pallets`. A sum exactly equal to `capacity_pallets` is accepted; only a strict excess is refused. Testing the candidate pairwise against each existing load is **not** sufficient — three loads can each be individually admissible while their common overlap breaches capacity — so the check must be an aggregate sweep over merged boundaries.

Distinct from that test is the reported figure. A zone's `occupied_pallets` is the sum of `pallet_count` over `STAGED` loads in the zone whose interval covers the **current logical clock**, meaning `stage_from` at or before the clock and `stage_until` strictly after it; it is recomputed whenever a zone is fetched. A load staged entirely in the future therefore passes the admission test but contributes 0 to `occupied_pallets` until its window opens. A load leaving `STAGED` for `HELD`, `IN_TRANSIT` or `SUPERSEDED` drops out of the input set and releases its pallets immediately.

**Departure and holds.** The warehouse sends a `STAGED` load onto the road, moving it `STAGED → IN_TRANSIT` and releasing its `pallet_count` from the zone. The owning order must be in `PICKED`, and its `credit_status` must be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_CREDIT_NOT_APPROVED`. A load moved `STAGED → HELD` by a credit hold is returned by the credit desk, moving `HELD → STAGED`; this requires the owning order's `credit_status` to be `APPROVED` again and re-runs the zone admission test over the load's original window, so a release can be refused with `ERR_ZONE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` if the zone filled up while the load was held. On success the load re-occupies its `pallet_count` when the current logical clock is inside its window. This `STAGED → HELD → STAGED` loop is the revision cycle of the reefer machine; `DELIVERED`, `EXCURSION_FLAGGED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal. An operation attempted from the wrong reefer status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SHIPMENT_STATE`.

**Telemetry.** The warehouse appends readings to a load in `IN_TRANSIT`, each naming the sampled `temp_c` between -60.00 and 60.00 degrees Celsius at two decimal places and the `recorded_at` instant the probe sampled. A reading is stored against its `cold_chain_shipment_id`, and unlike every other resource in this system a reading carries only its `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because the series is append-only and a sample is never revised. A reading's `recorded_at` must be at or after the load's `last_reading_at`; equal timestamps are accepted and contribute an `interval_minutes` of 0, and anything strictly earlier is refused with `ERR_READING_OUT_OF_ORDER`.

Each reading is assessed in `recorded_at` ascending order. Its `in_violation` is true when `temp_c` is strictly above `reefer_setpoint_c` plus `reefer_tolerance_c`, or strictly below `reefer_setpoint_c` minus `reefer_tolerance_c` — a sample exactly on either band edge is in tolerance. Its `interval_minutes` is the whole minutes elapsed between the previous reading's `recorded_at` and this reading's `recorded_at`, truncated toward zero, and is 0 for the first reading on the load. Its `cumulative_excursion_minutes` is the previous reading's `cumulative_excursion_minutes`, plus its own `interval_minutes` when and only when **this** reading is `in_violation`. **The attribution is normative**: the interval is charged to the reading that *closes* it, so the time elapsed before the first out-of-tolerance sample is not penalised, but the whole gap leading up to a violating sample is charged in full. Charging the interval to the reading that opens it is wrong and shifts every accumulation by one sample. The load's `excursion_minutes` is then the latest reading's `cumulative_excursion_minutes`, and its `reading_count` and `last_reading_at` track the series.

**Cascade C.** When a load's `excursion_minutes` after applying a reading is **strictly greater** than 30 minutes, the load is flagged, moving `IN_TRANSIT → EXCURSION_FLAGGED` — exactly 30 accumulated minutes does not flag. That flag is link 1 of cascade C. Link 2 follows from the flag: every propagation job referenced by a `YEAST` line of the owning order whose `status` is `READY` moves `READY → FAILED` with `failure_reason` set to `COLD_CHAIN_EXCURSION`, and every line of the order with `cold_chain_required` true and `item_type` `HOP` moves `OPEN → QUARANTINED`, dropping out of the order's roll-up input set. Link 3 follows from the job failing: a replacement propagation job is created in `REQUESTED` with its `replaces_job_id` pointing at the failed job and its `pitch_date` pushed out by the strain's `propagation_lead_time_days`. The chain is therefore three links deep — reading, to load, to propagation job, to replacement job. Once flagged, the load stops accumulating and further readings are refused with `ERR_INVALID_SHIPMENT_STATE`; the flag is also what blocks the owning order from being shipped or delivered.
